## M1 — "The Kick at the Docks" — the hate beat

**The character.** The leader of the Hacker cell holding the
Stoneport docks. Canon citation: Region 1's locked dialogue file
(`src/lib/wiring/dialogue/region1-dialogue.json`, R1-BEAT4, Stoneport)
already seats him as the beat's antagonist, in his own words: *"The
docks are ours now. The data here flows where we say it flows."* His
personal name is a NAME GAP in that file — this moment does not
invent one (owner slot, LANDING-RECORD).

**The licence.** Exception shape 4 — cruelty of opportunity, under
the organised-villain clause. He is not strong. Against a creature
that could fight back he is nothing, and the moment is careful to
happen against one that can't: a creature already caught.

**The creature.** A Tiidebiite pup (species verified in the roster,
`species.json`) — the harbour's own small tide-biter, snared in a
cargo net his crew strung between crates to keep the dock "clear."
It is tangled, exhausted, and snapping at air.

### Show version — Season 1

*Setting: the Stoneport docks, working hours, gulls and forklifts.
The cell leader is mid-conversation with a dock foreman — pleasant,
reasonable, a man explaining new management.*

1. The netted pup finds one more burst of strength and lunges in
   its net; the net bites; it cries out — a real cry, frightened,
   not cute.
2. **Without stopping his sentence, without looking down, he kicks
   it.** Not a football kick — a short, practised, sideways boot,
   the way a man moves a crate that is in his way. The pup hits the
   crate stack and goes quiet. Not dead-quiet. *Stopped* quiet. The
   dock sounds go on around the silence like nothing happened,
   because for him, nothing did.
3. The foreman's face does the audience's work. He looks at the pup,
   then at the man, and something in him finishes changing sides —
   the scene does not underline it; his next line is half a second
   late, and everybody on the dock heard why.
4. Mochiko (present with the delivery party) does not fight him —
   she *can't*, and the show knows its own law. What she does is
   the Ranger's verbs: she puts herself between the man and the
   net, takes the pup's panic-snapping on her own sleeve while she
   works the net loose with her teeth in the cord, and lets it bolt
   past her into the harbour water. It does not thank her. It is
   hurt and terrified and it bites her hand on the way out, and she
   lets it. That is the scene's moral arithmetic, done in action:
   the creature bites the person who helped it and goes quiet for
   the person who hurt it, and the audience understands both.
5. Button: the cell leader, watching her bleed lightly on the dock,
   mildly impressed — *"It likes you."* He thinks it's a joke. It is
   the most hated line in the episode, and he never knows why.

*What it changes:* the audience's hatred of the docks cell becomes
personal and permanent — every later appearance of this man is read
through the kick. The foreman becomes a quiet ally from this scene
on (his grudge is data, see game version). Mochiko carries a small
scar and a new fact about herself: she will spend her own skin
before a creature spends more of theirs.

### Game version

A **witnessed event** in the living-world system, seated at the
Stoneport docks on the Sequence road to Dojo 2 (the R1-BEAT4 stretch
— the docks cell is already that beat's antagonist). The player
arrives mid-event: the net, the pup, the kick about to become
inevitable. Outcomes follow the living-world event grammar:

- **Intervene** (the Ranger's verbs only — step between, cut the
  net under the pup's panic, take the snap): the pup escapes; the
  cell marks you; the foreman's private read of you changes and his
  later help unlocks.
- **Ignore / walk past:** the kick happens where you can hear it.
  No one stops you leaving. The dock remembers that too.
- **Wrong side** (help the crew "clear" the dock): available,

The cell leader's NPC record carries `hostileHuman: true`
(ranger-defense lane's flag) — his in-battle direct attack on

**Honor consequence:** the kick is a **witnessed deed** written

dock's cooling toward the cell are derived reads of that deed —
felt in dialogue and access, never displayed as a number.

## M2 — "The Flare at the Ford" — Demonio's guilt

**The character.** DEMONIO. Canon citations: transplant roster
§2.1 — *"DEMONIO — The Demon Soul, vessel of RIN; the protagonist
soul. A named demon who fights like a saint; bows to every
opponent, never to Diablo or Dio. Earth."* Role brief
(`human-design-lane/wave-1/transplants/DEMONIO-ROLE-BRIEF.md`,
owner-ruled 2026-10-27): in Mochiichao he is the **Season 4 dojo
leader**; a **person possessed by a demon**; the world mistakes his
demon form for a creature and battles him as one a couple of times
before the reveal. The brief flags, and this moment respects: the
demon's nature in Mochiichao canon is **not ruled** — so this
moment never names what rides him, never explains it, and never
shows the full demon form.

**The licence.** Exception shape 2 — the possessed. The strength
that does the harm is the passenger's, in a flare that lasts
seconds. The man is the one who stays for the guilt.

*(Amendment note, 2026-10-29, harvest lane: Demonio also stands
in the hybrid class's extended family — THE-RULE shapes 1–2, as
amended; HYBRID-CLASS-CANON.md. His Season 1 speech below is
class-legal: it is spoken in his human form, and the no-human-words
rule binds creature form only. The flare is the class surfacing
through him — which is why, in S4, the world will file the demon
form as a creature. Nothing in this moment's text changes.)*

**Placement decision (made here, justified).** Demonio's season is
S4, and the gimmick — demon form mistaken for a creature — must not
be spent early. So the Season 1 use is **human form only,
unnamed on screen, under ten seconds of strangeness**: no
transformation, no demon form, nothing that reads as anything but
a quiet traveller whose hands were wrong for one moment. The
audience files it as a mystery about a man. In S4, the reveal
re-reads this scene retroactively — *that was him; that was the
first time we saw it hold his hands* — which is the payoff the
rationing buys. His name is withheld in S1 not as a trick on the
audience but because nobody in the scene knows it.

### Show version — Season 1

*Setting: a river ford on the road, late light. A wild Mochii —
frightened by a Syndicate snare-drone's noise off-screen — has
bolted into the shallows and is thrashing, tangled in weed and
snare-line, screaming in cries. Travellers back away. A quiet man
(the audience has seen him once before, chapters back, bowing to a
gate-keeper he had no reason to bow to) wades in.*

1. He speaks to it the way he speaks to everyone — low, formal,
   a man asking pardon in advance. He reaches for the line.
2. The Mochii, drowning-panicked, rakes him — a real hit, blood
   in the water, the kind of wound the base law says a creature
   gives a feeble human who gets close.
3. **The flare.** His posture changes before his face does —
   something stands up inside his silhouette, and the hand that
   comes down is not a frightened man's hand. One blow: flat,
   enormous, final. It smashes the snare-line's anchor stone,
   frees the Mochii — **and catches the Mochii itself across the
   ribs on the follow-through.** The cry it makes is different
   from the panic cries. Everyone on the bank hears the difference.
4. It is over in the same breath. The man is back — staring at his
   own hand like evidence. The Mochii drags itself to the far
   bank, alive, free, and limping, and does not look back.
5. **The guilt, staged his way:** he does not chase it, does not
   call out. He kneels in the shallows — in the water, trousers
   soaked, bleeding from the rake it gave him and not tending it —
   and **bows to the empty bank where it vanished.** To the
   creature. The way he bows to opponents. He holds it far too
   long. Then he leaves his own travelling food on the flat stone
   at the ford — all of it, not a token piece — and walks on the
   wrong side of hungry.
6. No music swell, no explanation, no second mention this season.
   The travellers' whispers give the audience the only label it
   gets: *"strong, that one"* — said the way you'd say weather.

*What it changes:* quietly, everything about how S4 will land. The
audience has already watched this man hurt a creature by accident
of possession and pay for it in the only currencies he has —
reverence and food — years before they learn what he is. When the
S4 reveal comes, the question the audience brings is not "is he a
monster?" but "how long has he been carrying that?" And Demonio
himself is established, before he ever leads a dojo, as the
doctrine's proof from the inside: the strength is real, the harm
is real, and the man fights *like a saint* precisely because he
knows what his hands can do when they aren't his.

**Honor consequence:** the deed written is not cruelty — the

Standing derives the nuance; no number shows.

## M3 — "The Footstool Court" — Diablo's cruelty

**The character.** DIABLO. Canon citation: transplant roster
§2.1, The Seven — *"DIABLO — The Devil Soul; an intruder, not one
of the nine. Conquering Fire; believes he is the final boss."* That
is the verified whole of him for this purpose: a devil-soul who is
not native to the order he intrudes on, fire-as-conquest, and a
man whose self-image is *final boss*. His exact Mochiichao seat
(region, role, arrival) is **not owner-locked in the sources
checked** — this moment is written to his verified traits and its
placement is flagged as an owner slot (LANDING-RECORD), proposed
for the later-season arc in which he arrives, not asserted as
scheduled.

**The licence.** Exception shape 4 in its purest form — cruelty as
worldview. Diablo does not lose his temper. Temper would be
honest. His cruelty is doctrinal: creatures are *furniture in the
story of him*, and he treats them exactly that way, in public,
as a teaching.

*(Classification, amended 2026-10-29, harvest lane: Diablo is
also of the hybrid class — the Devil Soul vessel of the roster's
Seven, the class's person-form face (HYBRID-CLASS-CANON.md). That
alignment sharpens rather than softens this moment: his spoken
line below is class-legal — he holds a person's form and a
person's speech, unlike Pantera's full creature body (M5) — and
his cruelty is a person's chosen cruelty, which is the whole
indictment. The licence stays shape 4; the class is his nature,
not his excuse.)*

### Show version — a later season (his arrival arc)

*Setting: wherever Diablo holds court — a taken place, other
people's home repurposed. He is receiving a delegation. He is
charming. That is the horror of the scene: everything is pleasant
except the detail your eye keeps returning to.*

1. A young Pupbble (roster species; the small water-pup kind that
   follows warmth) — a stray that's been living around the place,
   half-tame from the previous occupants' kindness — wanders
   through the court mid-audience, the way a cat crosses a room.
2. Diablo, mid-sentence, still smiling at the delegation, sets
   his boot across its tail. Not a stamp. A *placement*. The
   Pupbble freezes, then pulls once, cries once — small — and
   then does the thing prey does: it goes still and waits for the
   world to finish.
3. **He keeps it there for the entire audience.** Talks over it.
   Gestures with the free hand. Twice, when it shifts, the boot's
   weight adjusts — not harder, just *informed*: I know you're
   there; that's the point of you. The delegation watches a small
   creature learn stillness under a boot while a devil explains
   the future to them, and the scene's real subject becomes which
   of the delegates can keep smiling.
4. His line, delivered to the room, mild as weather: *"Everything
   in this world is either standing on something, or being stood
   on. I am simply honest about which I am."* — the final-boss
   belief stated as table manners.
5. The release is worse than the hold: when the audience ends he
   lifts the boot *absently*, already turning away, and the
   Pupbble flees — not to the door. To a corner, and then, hating
   itself in the way of small tame things, it creeps back toward
   the warm side of the room an hour later in story time, because
   the kind people who fed it used to sit there. Diablo's cruelty
   is shown to have *rewired* it. That beat — the return — is the
   one the season keeps.
6. Contrast the show has already earned by then: Demonio bowed to
   a creature he hurt by accident (M2). Diablo, told secondhand
   about creatures being hurt in his name later in the season,
   does not bow. He asks what it cost him. (Dialogue note only —
   not a second moment; no second slot spent.)

*What it changes:* Diablo becomes un-redeemable in the audience's
ledger *before* any battle — his evil is not power, it's posture.
It also sharpens the doctrine by opposition: Demonio's guilt and
Diablo's ease are the same world's two answers to the same deed,
and the show means the audience to feel the difference in their
spine.

**Honor consequence:** witnessed by a full court — the deed is

towns near his territory long before anyone explains honor to
them.

travels with his arrival arc — owner slot). Game: placed cutscene,

## M4 — "The Reserve" — Luigi's negligence, and the Giovanni seat

**The character.** LUIGI CASTAGNOLI. Canon citations: game data
(`four-fixes/tree/src/world/FullWorldMaps.ts`) — Castagnoli Island,
map label *"Castagnoli Island — Luigi's Reserve,"* his mansion,

(`persona-dialect-game/CURRENT-STATE.md`): Luigi Castagnoli is
**Dojo 8's leader**, the Syndicate register's fictional owner, and
the man shown to the world pre-reveal only as **"Mysterious
Figure."** Region 1's locked dialogue (R1-BEAT7) seats **"Syndicate
boss as dojo leader"** at Dojo 8.

**The Giovanni reconciliation (in the open).** The owner asked for
"a Giovanni-type" as a separate seed. Verified against the cast
docs: our canon's Giovanni-*seat* — the polished boss at the top
of the criminal organisation who is also, impossibly, a legitimate
league authority — **is Luigi himself.** There is no second boss
in the seat and none is invented. His moment therefore carries
both halves of the seed: the negligence the owner named for Luigi,
and the Giovanni-shape of cruelty committed entirely by signature,
at a distance, in a beautiful room.

**The licence.** Exception shape 4, at one remove. Luigi never
touches a creature in this moment. That is the cruelty: a reserve
is a promise with his name on the gate, and he has never once
walked it.

### Show version — Luigi's season (pre-reveal discipline kept)

*Pre-reveal discipline: per the persona package, before the reveal
Luigi is "Mysterious Figure" — silhouette and shard, no face, no
name on screen. The show version of this moment is built to work
under that discipline: the audience sees the office, the seal, the
signature — never the man whole.*

*Setting: Castagnoli Island. A private reserve — good land, sea
light, enclosures built beautifully and expensively, the way money
builds when it wants to be photographed.*

1. The surface: a junior Syndicate administrator is doing the
   season's paperwork tour with a local keeper the organisation
   inherited with the island. The keeper is old, careful, and
   speaks about the creatures by their habits, the way people do
   who actually feed them.
2. The truth, discovered one enclosure at a time, never in a
   speech: the feeding system is automated and has been faulting
   for a season; the fault reports went *up* and were *signed off*
   — the audience sees the signature flourish on a tablet, three
   times, three reports, never read. A water line runs dry two
   days in seven. The creatures have learned the schedule of their
   own neglect: they gather at the dry trough at the hour it used
   to run. They still gather. That image is the moment.
3. One enclosure holds a creature too weak now to have survived
   the winter without what it was promised. The keeper says the
   line the season will quote: *"He keeps a reserve the way he
   keeps everything. Beautifully. From very far away."*
4. Intercut, once only: the office. A hand — the audience knows
   whose office this is without a face — signs the renewal that
   funds the photographs and not the plumber. The pen is lovely.
   The signature is lovely. Nobody in the room is cruel, and that
   is precisely the indictment: Luigi's harm runs on rails of
   other people's adequacy, and his one contribution is that he
   never, ever goes and *looks*.
5. What his negligence costs, on screen, in the doctrine's terms:
   no blow is struck, so the show gives the cost to the creatures'
   behaviour — the gathering at the dry trough, a creature that no
   longer comes when called by the keeper it trusts because trust
   has stopped predicting food, the quiet arithmetic of animals
   revising a person downward. Cries only. No subtitles. The
   keeper's face does the translating, exactly as M1's foreman did.

*What it changes:* when the reveal eventually puts a face and a
name to the office, the audience doesn't meet a stranger — they
meet the signature. Luigi is hated differently from the docks
leader: not for what his hands did, but for the distance he
engineered so his hands would never have to. And it arms the
Dojo 8 confrontation with a question no battle can answer: *what
else has he signed?*

## M5 — "The Hold" — Pantera in the wild

**The character.** PANTERA. Canon citations: Western Heart record
(transplant roster §1 Anchor 2 + §2.1) — *"PANTERA — The Panther
Soul, vessel of TOU (battle itself)"*; black panther aspect,
yellow eyes; a pro-wrestling grappler in his home canon (chain
throws, multi-part holds — a donor-style grappling art; the
philosophy is his own, verbatim): ***"The hold is honest.
Everything else is negotiation."*** — carried in this package as
his saying, rendered by the people around him (see the dawn beat:
he does not speak it; he never speaks). He is the richest art base
of any transplant (roster card, models, T3 animation set).

**RESOLVED — the owner's clarification (2026-10-29 ~21:54 CDT;
amendment by the show-side harvest lane, same evening).** The
earlier draft of this section flagged a tension — roster Anchor 2
lands Pantera as a Region 2 *species*; the owner's violence-beats
framing read as a *man* in the wild — and held the reconciliation
as an owner slot. **The owner resolved it: there was never a
contradiction.** In his words' substance: *Pantera is a creature
by canon AND anthropomorphic like a human — a human-type PERSON
also seen as a Mochi; he cannot communicate in human words.* He is
the primary carrier of the **hybrid class — the Mochi Rangers**
(THE-RULE shape 1, as amended; class document:
`~/workspace/show-side-harvest/HYBRID-CLASS-CANON.md`). The roster
record already said it, once read correctly: black panther head,
humanoid body, a wrestler's gear — an anthropomorphic panther
person, whom Region 2 files as a species because that is how the
world files his kind. The species reading and the person reading
are the same fact from two sides. This moment is therefore not
"a strong man" at all: it is the hybrid class's own moment, and
the staging hedge in the show version below (station-keeper's
telling) is kept as pure framing — it is how the episode is told,
no longer a shelter against an unresolved ruling.

**The licence.** Exception shape 1 — the hybrid — with survival
as its fence. He is not hunting, not proving, not punishing. The
wild has penned him in a territory that isn't his, at night,
unarmed, and the creatures who own that ground are within their
rights. His strength is grappling strength: holds, pins,
endurance — **and it is creature strength, by anatomy**: a
panther-bodied person's body is a creature-class body, which is
why a Ranger could not do this and he can. He wins, where he
wins, by *holding* — and by letting go first.

**The creatures.** Strikechamp-class: a territorial wild
**Strikechamp** (species 030, Core/Strike, verified in
`species.json`) and, in the second movement, its younger
companion — a Kickmaster (031) that comes at the noise. These are
not villains. They are what the wild is supposed to be: strong,
at home, and unimpressed.

### Show version — a later season (the Region 2 road)

*Framing (as amended 2026-10-29): the beat is told as it is
lived — Pantera arrives at a Ranger way-station at dawn, wrecked,
carrying no creature, having caught nothing, having killed
nothing, and the episode shows the night in flashback **as the
station-keeper pieces it together afterwards** — from the state
Pantera arrives in, his sounds and gestures, and what the gully
itself shows. Pantera tells it in no words at all, because he has
none: that wordlessness is not a gap in the scene, it is the
scene's grammar — the audience learns him the way the world
learns his kind, by watching what he does. The writing below is
the night itself.*

*Setting: a rockfall gully in wild country, storm coming,
night.*

1. The rockfall takes the path behind him and the light with it.
   Pantera — panther-headed, man-shaped, alone, no pack creature,
   no cube in hand, because the point of him is that the wild does
   not care what he brought — picks his ground the way wrestlers
   do: back to stone, feet honest.
2. The Strikechamp comes first, territorial, all business: a
   rush that would end the argument with any ordinary human.
   Pantera does not strike it. **He catches the rush the way his
   whole art exists to catch it — chain grappling, body to body,
   and takes it down into a hold.** The scene is shot as wrestling
   craft, not a fight scene: leverage, breath, the tail-tell his
   own canon taught the audience (*"the tail tells you when the
   hold is coming"* — here it is the creature's tension that tells
   *him*), both of them straining in the mud while the storm
   arrives over the gully.
3. He does not finish anything. He **holds it until it
   understands the negotiation is over** — until the Strikechamp's
   struggle changes key from killing to leaving — and then he
   lets go *first*, open hands, and backs off into the rain with
   his ribs audibly wrong. The creature watches him go with the
   exact expression of a professional who has lost a match to
   another professional.
4. Second movement, the Kickmaster: younger, faster, comes at
   him while he's favouring the ribs — and this one he cannot
   hold clean. It is the honest cost movement: he is clipped,
   downed, nearly finished; he survives by *enduring inside a
   bad position* until the storm breaks the gully's edge and the
   falling water gives them both a bigger problem than each
   other. They separate the way weather separates things. No
   winner's pose. He laughs once, in the rain, at himself — the
   only laugh in any of these five moments, and pointed at his
   own expense, which is why it's allowed.
5. Dawn button, the way-station: the keeper asks what happened
   out there. Pantera — wrapping his own ribs with the competence
   of one who has done it alone for years — answers the way his
   kind answers: a low sound, neither growl nor word; a paw
   pressed once to his own wrong ribs; then the same paw opened,
   flat, toward the gully — *I held; I let go; it's done.* The
   keeper, who has known him long enough to read him, says it
   aloud for the room, and the rendering is marked as the
   keeper's: *"Nothing, he says. They talked."* — and then,
   quieter, the saying the keeper attributes to him and the
   series will too: *"The hold is honest. Everything else is
   negotiation."* Before breakfast, Pantera is at the door
   looking toward the gully, making the small sound that means
   *are they well* — he asks after the gully's creatures before
   he asks after food. The keeper will tell this story for the
   rest of the series.

*What it changes:* Pantera enters the audience's ledger as the
doctrine's first shape made flesh — **the hybrid class itself**,
whose strength is anatomy and whose *ethic* is the restraint: he
could have fought dirtier, longer, with intent to maim, and the
story is that he treats a wild Strikechamp with the respect of a
fellow worker — and that the audience has understood a person all
night without his speaking one word. It also sets the wild's
price honestly for the whole series: out there, even the
exceptions bleed.

A **placed road event** on the wild road toward Region 2 (seat
