## The base law (unchanged, not softened by anything in this package)

Rangers are too weak to really damage a creature, and they know it.
A human body next to a creature is a feeble thing — our own battle
math already says so in the ranger-defense lane's code: an unflagged
human strike is a punch against a creature's defences, and it does
almost nothing because it *should* do almost nothing.

So a Ranger never attacks a creature. Not in anger, not in a panic,
not as a joke, not "just this once because the scene needed it."
The Ranger's verbs with creatures are: **distract, draw attention,
run interference, defend with his own body, and heal afterwards.**
That ruling (2026-10-29 ~21:21 CDT) stands untouched. Everything in
this package is an authored exception layer built *on top* of it —
the exceptions exist to prove the rule, not to erode it.

## The exception: an individual trait, always with a story reason

A human can do real harm to a creature in this world only when the
capacity belongs to **that one person**, for a reason the story has
already taught the audience, in one of four shapes:

1. **The hybrid — the MOCHI RANGER class.** *(Amended 2026-10-29:
   the class is named and defined by the owner — see the amendment
   note at the head of this file and HYBRID-CLASS-CANON.md.)* A
   Mochi Ranger is a **person who is also creature-class**: a
   person in mind and standing whose body (or second form) is a
   Mochiichao's. Not built like a human — the creature is in the
   body, and the strength is anatomy, not attitude. The world
   files them as creatures; people who know them treat them as
   people. In creature form they have **no human words** — cries,
   sounds, gesture only. Distance from Rangers is definitional:
   Rangers are feeble humans; this class is not human-frail at
   all. *(Verified carriers: PANTERA — the primary carrier, an
   anthropomorphic panther person, vessel of TOU; DIABLO — the
   class's person-form face; DEMONIO crosses this shape with
   shape 2 — a person whose possessed second form is
   creature-shaped. The wider vessel set of the transplant roster
   is the class's extended family.)*
2. **The possessed.** A human carrying something that is not human —
   a demon, a soul, a passenger — can strike with the passenger's
   strength in the moments the passenger holds the hands. The
   licence belongs to the possession, never to the man, and the man
   is answerable for what he does with the guilt afterwards.
   *(Verified carrier: Demonio, vessel of RIN.)*
3. **The exceptionally strong.** A rare human whose strength is
   itself the character trait — written into who they are before
   the moment arrives, paid for in the writing (a life of the wild,
   a wrestler's body, a survival history). Strength like this is
   grappling strength first: holds, pins, endurance. It holds
   ground; it does not hunt. *(Carrier note, amended 2026-10-29:
   this shape's long-standing example was Pantera — he is
   re-homed to shape 1, where he belongs: he is not a strong man
   but the hybrid class itself, an anthropomorphic panther person
   whose grappling strength is creature anatomy (MOMENTS.md M5,
   as amended). Shape 3 stands for genuinely human carriers, none
   named yet — it is not weakened by losing him; it is clarified.)*
4. **The exceptionally cruel.** Cruelty does not make a human
   stronger than a creature. It makes him willing to do the small,
   cowardly version: strike the creature that is tied, young,
   cornered, trusting, or unable to answer — or harm by paperwork,
   neglect, and orders, where the creature never even gets a
   villain it can face. Cruelty in this canon is a character
   indictment, never a power-up. *(Verified carriers: the Hacker
   cell at the Stoneport docks — cruelty of opportunity; Diablo —
   cruelty as worldview; Luigi Castagnoli — cruelty at one remove,
   by signature and neglect.)*

Villain humans of the organised kind — Syndicate operatives, hacker
grunts, the Team-Rocket-shaped crews — sit under shape 4 and the
ranger-defense lane's data flag: their direct attacks on creatures
are **evil story behaviour**, deliberately feeble against a creature
that can fight back, and only ever ugly against one that can't.

## The dose budget (hard, counted, and kept in DOSE-LEDGER.md)

These moments are rationed because rationing is what makes them
land. A world where humans hurt creatures every episode is a
different, worse world — and it would silently repeal the base law.

| Surface | Budget |
|---|---|
| Show — Season 1 | **1–2 moments, total.** |
| Show — each later season | **A couple (2) per season**, no carry-over banking of unused slots without the owner's word. |
| Game | **Placed moments only** — each one authored, seated in a named sequence or event, and tied to the ranger-defense lane's `hostileHuman: true` data flag on the NPC record. No random encounters, no ambient cruelty, no repeatable farm of it. |

Every written moment spends a named slot in DOSE-LEDGER.md the
moment it is drafted — not when it airs, so the count can never
drift ahead of the paperwork.

## The lines never crossed

- **No Ranger ever does this.** Not the player, not a companion, not
  a "good" character having a bad day. If a Ranger raises a hand to
  a creature in a draft, the draft is wrong.
- **No cruelty played for laughs.** A human hurting a creature is
  never a gag, a button on a scene, or a cute beat. The camera does
  not wink. (The ending-gag register and this register never touch.)
- **The creature's experience is on screen.** The moment is never
  only about the human's feelings or the plot's needs. What it costs
  the creature — the flinch, the cry, the going-quiet, the limp, the
  trust that does not come back — is shown, in cries and body only.
  Creatures speak in cries, always; no creature ever gets a word,
  a subtitle of its thoughts, or a name-tag to explain its pain.
- **No escalation creep.** Each exception is licensed per person,
  per trait, per moment. One villain's kick does not license the
  next writer's grunt, and a possessed man's flare does not make
  possession a general combat system.
- **Survival is not cruelty.** One fighting to live in the wild
  (Pantera's situation, M5) is written as survival: he is trying to *stop*
  being hurt, he lets go first, and the writing never celebrates
  what it cost the creature either.
- **Consequences are real and remembered.** Where witnesses exist,
  cruelty to a creature is a **witnessed deed** in the honor ledger
  and the world answers it — in who will speak to the actor, in
  which creatures will come near a place afterwards, in grudges
  that persist as data. An unwitnessed cruelty is still a debt the
  story itself remembers and collects later.

## Who owns what

- **The ranger-defense lane owns the mechanic**: the
  `hostileHuman: true` flag, the feeble human damage math, the
  defend/intercept system on the Ranger's own health bar.
- **This lane owns the fiction**: which named people carry a licence
  under the four shapes, the moments themselves, and the dose count.
- A moment in MOMENTS.md that needs a flag the mechanic doesn't
  carry yet is a spec note to that lane — never a claim that the
  wiring exists.
