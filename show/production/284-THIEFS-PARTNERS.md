# #284 — THE THIEF'S LONG-TERM PARTNERS → the ones who stayed, and what their records say

**The human fact under this spec.** #273 (List 28)
built the stolen Mochi's side of the theft: the bond
to the true keeper, the refusal, the road home — and,
at the end of its hardest paragraph, the other
possibility: that a stolen Mochi, over time and
treatment, **genuinely bonds with the thief**. A part
that runs for years (#277) meets the end of that road:
Mochi who were stolen long ago and are now, in every
fact that matters, **his partners**. This spec is how
the systems and the show hold that without lying in
either direction — because there are two lies
available, and both are forbidden below.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/thiefs-partners/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The record keeps both facts, in that order.** A
  long-term partner's #80 record shows, first, the
  theft: the #268 stealing event and its history
  (theft is a history stamp that never launders —
  #274's law, inherited whole: a fence could not wash
  it, and *time does not wash it either*). Second,
  layered after it by the ordinary machinery, the
  bond: the care and history facts their shared years
  have actually written, read by the same bond systems
  that read any partnership (#180's discipline — a
  bond effect exists only where it is declared and
  visible; the bond here is as real as any, because
  its facts are as real as any). **Neither fact edits
  the other.** The record is not "stolen, but…" and
  not "bonded, despite…"; it is a life, in order.
- **Lie one, forbidden: the retcon of innocence.** No
  system, ceremony, or story beat may re-found the
  partnership on a cleaner origin — no adoption record
  backdated over the theft (#217's road exists and was
  not taken), no "as if he caught them fairly" framing
  in any record the game keeps or the show states. If
  the thief wants a clean page with any of them, the
  road is #273's recovery run in reverse — return, and
  whatever happens after a return is a new fact,
  written after, in order. The record's order is its
  honesty.
- **Lie two, forbidden: the permanent sentence.** The
  theft in the record does not cap the bond. A partner
  who stayed is not rendered as damaged goods by any
  system: their bond reads, honours (#104 — a stamp
  earned beside the thief is earned), battle conduct,
  and #136-staged trust are read from their facts like
  any individual's. The show may not stage them as
  secretly miserable by default either — their present
  is played from their present facts. **The arc never
  has to pretend either fact away** — that sentence is
  the portrayal guidance the brief asked for, and it
  cuts both ways on purpose.
- **The choice, where choice is expressible, is the
  Mochi's.** The programme's agency doctrine holds at
  its hardest here: a trade a Mochi refuses never
  happens; a recovery a partner refuses is likewise
  not a rescue but a second taking. If the true keeper
  ever returns (#273's road), a long-term partner's
  staying is a *fact the story must stage as theirs* —
  behaviour, cry, the #136 reads — not a flag the
  humans argue over. Years change what the right
  answer is; the systems' job is to have kept the
  facts straight enough that the scene can be honest.

## Show lane

Portrayal guidance, binding on his part's writing
(#82 plans cite this spec for any partner beat): the
bond is shown the way all bonds are shown — conduct,
care, the small trusts — and the origin is shown when
the story touches it, in flashback or record, without
flinching and without dwelling. A partner's first
meeting with their former keeper is among the heaviest
beats the part owns, and it is staged from the record
(#283's citation discipline at individual scale: who
they were, cited; who they are, stated).

## Game mirror

A thief-route player's long- kept stolen Mochi read
identically: history intact, bond real, any endgame
content (recovery offers, the keeper's return as an
event, #275's restitution — which pays most precisely
because these are the ones it costs most to return)
reads the same two-fact record. Restitution involving
a bonded partner is #275's summit case; this spec
supplies the record it is priced over.
