### Plowpus — B1 · single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B1 Plowpus — a platypus whose bill is a seed-drill and whose claws are ploughshares; it tills the festival fields in spring and is paid in river pebbles it doesn't need.
- **Types:** Terra/Aqua
- **Category:** the Plough Mochi
- **Job:** plough
- **Size class:** Small — a low, long-bodied creature at sturdy farm-cat scale, built close to the ground with its weight carried forward into the bill and foreclaws.
- **Temperament:** methodical — it works a field in straight, patient passes, finishes a row before it starts the next, and treats a skipped patch as a personal debt.
- **Habitat:** Festival fields and the riverbanks beside them in Region 3 ranch country; it sleeps near the water it works beside and returns to the same fields each spring.
- **Diet & behaviour:** Eats riverside grubs, worms, and soft roots turned up by its own ploughing. It accepts river pebbles as payment, keeps them in a neat bankside pile, and has no use for them beyond the keeping. In spring it is all work; out of tilling season it is a slow, unhurried swimmer.
- **Evolution:** Single-stage — does not evolve.
- **Discovery:** Common — met at work. Method: festival fields and adjoining riverbanks, tilling alongside ranch hands. Time/season: spring, when the festival fields are being prepared; quieter and mostly in the water the rest of the year. Level band: early. A Ranger first plausibly meets it by being handed a row to work next to it, pebble payment pile and all.
- **Cry (full spec):** Source character: low, earthy bill-clacks and soft churring grunts, with a splashy snort when the bill comes up wet. Pitch contour: low and flat, dipping lower at the end of a pass. Duration class: short bursts, repeated evenly. Syllable/rhythm pattern: an even, row-like *clack — churr — clack*, paced like work. Variations — idle: a slow contented churr while it re-sorts its pebbles; call: a louder bill-clack volley across the field; hurt: a sharp squawk and bill-snap; faint: a tired snort that trails into snoring; affection: a soft, rapid bill-nibbling chatter against a trusted hand; evolution moment: none — single-stage, the cry never marks a change.
- **Anatomy notes (animation-variance):** Four short webbed feet; the foreclaws are broad ploughshare blades and the hind feet steer like rudders. The bill is a working seed-drill, not a duck bill: it opens only a little, and the drama is in the drilling push, not a bite. Locomotion is a low waddle on land and a smooth, whole-body swim in water. Manipulators: the foreclaws and bill only. Proportion-licence feature: the oversized drill-bill and ploughshare foreclaws. An animator must never give it a headache-psychic pose or a duck's quacking gape.
- **Palette seed:** river-mud brown, wet slate, seed-green, pebble grey, bill ochre
- **Bio (Mochiioteca):** Plowpus tills the festival fields each spring, drilling seed rows with its bill and turning the soil with ploughshare claws. It is paid in river pebbles, which it does not need and keeps anyway in a tidy pile. A field it has finished is known by its ruler-straight rows.
- **Footprint:** Print shape class: webbed. Each print is a broad, fan-shaped webbed impression with ploughshare claw grooves scored ahead of it; the hind prints are narrower steering fans. Toe/claw count and arrangement: webbing makes a single fan rather than separate toes, with the forefoot's blade-claws leaving a short row of deep parallel scores at the front edge. Gait pattern: alternating steps in a low waddle, in long straight passes. Print size class: small. Distinctive marker: twin furrow-lines dragged between the rows of prints, and seed holes drilled at even intervals beside the track — no other ranch track comes with its own planting.
- **Silhouette:** Outline class: low horizontal torpedo. Proportions in words: a long flat body, most of its length in the torso and tail, almost none in leg height. Key visual masses sit at the front (drill-bill and blade foreclaws) and the back (flat paddle tail), with a smooth unbroken back between them. What reads at sprite size: the flat bill, the paddle tail, and the shine on the foreclaws. Identifying negative space: the low wedge of open air under the bill, between the drill tip and the foreclaws, and the notch where the tail meets the waterline when it swims.
- **Stat profile (words only):** A steady workhorse profile — solid defence and stamina for long field work, modest attack delivered through the bill and claws, unremarkable speed on land and better in water — plus "⚑ numbers unassigned (owner/game-design)".
- **DESIGN BRIEF:** Silhouette is a low, flat platypus body carried on short webbed feet, read instantly by the seed-drill bill and ploughshare foreclaws. Key features: drill-bill, blade foreclaws, paddle tail, a bankside pebble pile as its signature prop. Must-never-change list: the bill stays a farm implement, never a duck bill; no headache-psychic read; the body stays a digging farm animal's body; no baked-in shadow.
- **Flags:** ⚠ Shares the platypus with Psyduck (Psyduck is widely documented as platypus-based, §5.3) — concept kept but flagged (B1): the seed is a digging farm animal, no duck, no headache-psychic read (see §5).

### Sentripea — B2 · single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B2 Sentripea — a pea-pod on a stalk with one large eye at the top; the ranch lookout, it rustles the pod to sound alarms (the peas are the rattle).
- **Types:** Flora
- **Category:** the Lookout Mochi
- **Job:** lookout
- **Size class:** Small — mostly stalk and eye; the pod body itself is small, light, and held high, so the creature reads taller than it is heavy.
- **Temperament:** nosy — it watches everything, leans toward gossip at the fence line, and reports movements nobody asked it to track with exactly the same rattle as a real alarm.
- **Habitat:** Ranch fence lines, garden gates, and lookout posts across Region 3 ranch country, wherever a stalk can root in sight of a road, a pen, or the festival grounds.
- **Diet & behaviour:** A plant creature that feeds on sunlight, rain, and rich garden soil, with a taste for being where it can see. Its whole behaviour is watching: the eye tracks passers-by, and any excitement sets the pod rattling — the loose peas inside are the alarm.
- **Evolution:** Single-stage — does not evolve.
- **Discovery:** Common — met at work. Method: ranch lookouts, garden gates, and fence lines, where it is already on duty. Time/season: year-round, most vocal in the busy festival season when there is the most to watch. Level band: early. A Ranger first plausibly meets it by being rattled at, thoroughly, for approaching a gate it has decided is its gate.
- **Cry (full spec):** Source character: no voice-box cry in the usual sense — a dry pod-rattle, leaf-rustle, and a small peeping squeak from the pod's seam. Pitch contour: the squeak sits high and rises when it is excited; the rattle has no pitch, only speed. Duration class: short rattles, very repeatable. Syllable/rhythm pattern: *shaka-shaka … peep* — rattle first, squeak as the verdict. Variations — idle: an occasional lazy half-rattle at passing clouds; call: a full fast rattle that can carry across a ranch yard; hurt: a single hard shake and a thin squeak; faint: the pod droops and the peas settle with one last soft tick; affection: a gentle, purring rustle and the eye leaning close; evolution moment: none — single-stage.
- **Anatomy notes (animation-variance):** No limbs and no hands: the body is a rooted or tiptoeing stalk, a hanging pea-pod, and one large eye at the top. The mouth, if shown at all, is the pod's seam — expression lives in the eye and in how the pod swings and rattles. Locomotion is a stalk-lean, a hop of the root, or a carried sway; it never walks. Proportion-licence feature: mostly eye and stalk — the eye is the head, the face, and most of the acting. An animator must keep it a plant with peas, never an eyeball monster.
- **Palette seed:** pea green, stalk sage, pod-lime, eye white, iris amber
- **Bio (Mochiioteca):** Sentripea keeps watch over the ranch from its stalk, one large eye turning to follow every cart, creature, and cloud. When it spots trouble — or anything it finds interesting — it rattles the peas in its pod to raise the alarm. The peas are the rattle, and the rattle is rarely wrong about something having happened.
- **Footprint:** Print shape class: none — it has no feet. Toe/claw count and arrangement: none; in place of a pad print, the stalk's root-tip leaves a single small round dimple, ringed by fine rootlet scratches. Gait pattern: when it moves at all, the dimples appear in a slow single file with long gaps, each one leaned in the direction it was looking. Print size class: tiny. Distinctive marker: the dimple trail itself, plus shed peas and pod husks near lookout posts — and the fact that the dots keep stopping in clusters wherever there was something worth watching.
- **Silhouette:** Outline class: vertical exclamation mark. Proportions in words: a thin stalk carrying almost all the height, a small pod hanging to one side, and the eye crowning the top like a flag. Key visual masses: the eye at the summit and the pod at mid-height; everything else is line. What reads at sprite size: the single big eye and the pod's curved bulge. Identifying negative space: the tall open triangle between the stalk, the pod's hanging curve, and the ground — the emptiest silhouette in the bucket, by design.
- **Stat profile (words only):** A lookout's profile — keen perception and just enough speed to rattle first, very little bulk or attack; it wins by being noticed early, not by fighting — plus "⚑ numbers unassigned (owner/game-design)".
- **DESIGN BRIEF:** Silhouette is a stalk, a pod, and one huge eye — the proportion licence taken to its limit. Key features: single eye on top, pea-pod body with visible peas as the rattle, rooted stance at gates and fences. Must-never-change list: it stays a plant with peas, not an eyeball monster; the eye is one, large, and on top; the peas must be readable as the alarm rattle; no limbs; no baked-in shadow.
- **Flags:** ⚠ The one-eye-stalk echoes Monster Rancher's Suezo signature — a donor-side proximity flagged in §5 / §5.4 for the owner's eye rather than hidden: the seed here is a plant with peas, not an eyeball monster.

### Cinderewe — B3 · crossing parent, single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B3, the Stormewe crossing — Cinderewe, one of two ewe species designed as a Rancher crossing pair, whose wool smoulders softly and is sheared for winter kindling. Cross: Cinderewe × Rainewe → Stormewe.
- **Types:** Flame (the crossing is recorded in the source as Flame × Aqua → Volt)
- **Category:** the Kindling Mochi
- **Job:** winter kindling — its wool is sheared for winter kindling; the ewe is the ranch's stored warmth.
- **Size class:** Medium — an ordinary ewe's scale, made to look larger and rounder by a full fleece that never quite stops smoking at the edges.
- **Temperament:** ⚑ No one-word temperament is given in the source for this crossing — owner-class, unassigned in the source. Its colour, from the seed alone: a creature whose warmth is a service, shorn each year so other creatures' fires can start.
- **Habitat:** Ranch pastures and shearing sheds in Region 3, kept near barns and winter stores; it favours dry bedding well away from haystacks — including away from Shagdox.
- **Diet & behaviour:** Grazes pasture grass and favours dry, resinous herbs at the field edges. Its wool smoulders softly and continuously, never flaming; at shearing time the fleece comes off as winter kindling, and the ewe spends a short while looking surprised and much smaller.
- **Evolution:** Single-stage — does not evolve. Crossing note: Cinderewe is a designed crossing parent — Cinderewe × Rainewe → Stormewe (see the Stormewe sheet, B3). Stormewe is the cross of the two species, not an evolution of Cinderewe alone.
- **Discovery:** Uncommon — met at work. Method: ranch pastures and shearing sheds, where it is kept for its fleece. Time/season: most sought and most shorn in the run-up to winter; its smoulder is easiest to spot at dusk in any season. Level band: early. A Ranger first plausibly meets it at shearing time, carrying away a bundle of its warm wool.
- **Cry (full spec):** Source character: a soft ewe's bleat with a fireside crackle woven through it, and a faint hiss when rain surprises it. Pitch contour: mid and warm, falling gently. Duration class: short bleats, often doubled. Syllable/rhythm pattern: a plain *baa-baa* with a crackle tail. Variations — idle: a low crackling hum from the wool under an occasional bleat; call: a louder, sharper bleat that makes nearby kindling smoke; hurt: a startled bleat and a puff of sparks; faint: a small bleat that dies into embers-quiet; affection: a warm, sleepy bleat with the crackle turned soft as a hearth; evolution moment: none of its own — but at the crossing moment with Rainewe its crackle is the sound Stormewe's first thunder answers.
- **Anatomy notes (animation-variance):** A four-legged ewe: cloven hooves, a wool-swollen body, a small head that peers out of the fleece. No manipulators; expression is in the ears, the head tilt, and how much the wool is smoking. The jaw is a grazer's jaw — side-to-side cud motion, no bite read. Locomotion is an unhurried ewe's amble and trot. Proportion-licence feature: the fleece itself — a body that is mostly wool, with head and legs as accessories. Shearing must visibly shrink the silhouette.
- **Palette seed:** ember orange, ash grey, cream wool, soot charcoal, hearth red
- **Bio (Mochiioteca):** Cinderewe's wool smoulders softly all year, too gentle to burn and too warm to ignore. At shearing time the fleece is gathered as winter kindling, and one ewe can keep a ranch house's fires starting through the cold months. It is one of the two parents of the Stormewe cross.
- **Footprint:** Print shape class: hoof. Each print is a clean cloven impression, two pointed halves side by side. Toe/claw count and arrangement: two hoof toes per foot, no claws, the halves slightly splayed under the fleece's weight. Gait pattern: alternating steps in a placid walking trail, four prints to a group. Print size class: medium-small. Distinctive marker: in dry grass the edges of its prints are faintly singed and smell of woodsmoke, and a little fallen ash drifts inside the trail — a hoofprint that has been warmed.
- **Silhouette:** Outline class: round cloud on legs. Proportions in words: fleece first, everything else second — a big soft oval of wool with a small head poking out at the front and four thin legs below. Key visual masses: the fleece dome, with smoke wisps rising off its top edge. What reads at sprite size: the round wool mass, the small dark face, and the smoke. Identifying negative space: the narrow gaps between the four legs under the fleece's low hem, and the notch between the head and the fleece where the neck disappears into wool.
- **Stat profile (words only):** A cosy defensive profile — bulky through the fleece, slow, with a modest Flame presence that serves warmth before battle — plus "⚑ numbers unassigned (owner/game-design)".
- **DESIGN BRIEF:** Silhouette is an ewe-shaped bonfire that never quite lights: a dome of smouldering wool, small face, thin legs, permanent smoke wisps. Key features: gently smoking fleece, shearable kindling wool, ember glow at the fleece's deep places. Must-never-change list: the wool smoulders, it never erupts into open flame as its resting state; it is an elemental weather-sheep, not wool livestock; the cross with Rainewe must be noted wherever it is presented; no baked-in shadow.
- **Flags:** ⚠ Sheep are Pokémon-occupied (Wooloo) — these are elemental weather-sheep, not wool livestock (see §5 / §5.4, B3 sheep). ⚑ Additionally: no one-word temperament is given in the source for the B3 crossing — flagged open, owner-class, not invented here.

### Rainewe — B3 · crossing parent, single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B3, the Stormewe crossing — Rainewe, one of two ewe species designed as a Rancher crossing pair, whose wool is a permanent personal raincloud. Cross: Cinderewe × Rainewe → Stormewe.
- **Types:** Aqua (the crossing is recorded in the source as Flame × Aqua → Volt)
- **Category:** the Raincloud Mochi
- **Job:** raincloud-bearer — its wool is a permanent personal raincloud; where Rainewe stands, it is raining, and the ranch plans around that.
- **Size class:** Medium — an ordinary ewe's scale, its fleece a dark, heavy, rain-swollen cloud that drips as it walks.
- **Temperament:** ⚑ No one-word temperament is given in the source for this crossing — owner-class, unassigned in the source. Its colour, from the seed alone: a creature that carries its own weather with it and is rained on even at its own celebrations.
- **Habitat:** Ranch pastures, water meadows, and garden plots in Region 3 that benefit from a resident rain; it is kept away from Cinderewe's winter stores, for both their sakes.
- **Diet & behaviour:** Grazes wet pasture and lush water-edge grass, and drinks a great deal, as if refilling itself. Rain falls from its wool continuously onto whatever is directly beneath it; it naps standing in its own puddle and seems to find the sound restful.
- **Evolution:** Single-stage — does not evolve. Crossing note: Rainewe is a designed crossing parent — Cinderewe × Rainewe → Stormewe (see the Stormewe sheet, B3). Stormewe is the cross of the two species, not an evolution of Rainewe alone.
- **Discovery:** Uncommon — met at work. Method: ranch pastures and garden plots, found by following the sound of rain on a clear day. Time/season: year-round; most valued in dry spells and at festival time, when its rain is borrowed for the grounds. Level band: early. A Ranger first plausibly meets it by getting rained on from a cloud no bigger than a sheep.
- **Cry (full spec):** Source character: a soft ewe's bleat under a constant patter of rain, with drips and small splashes keeping time. Pitch contour: mid and mild, with the patter flattening everything. Duration class: short bleats over a continuous rain bed. Syllable/rhythm pattern: *baa* … *baa*, spaced like drips from a gutter. Variations — idle: sparse bleats through steady patter; call: a louder bleat and a sudden heavier shower; hurt: a startled bleat and a scatter of drops; faint: the bleat fades and the rain thins to a drizzle; affection: a gentle bleat in warm rain, the patter turning rhythmic and calm; evolution moment: none of its own — but at the crossing moment with Cinderewe its rain hiss is the sound Stormewe's first thunder rolls over.
- **Anatomy notes (animation-variance):** A four-legged ewe built exactly as Cinderewe is — cloven hooves, grazer's jaw, no manipulators — with the fleece as a living raincloud. Expression is in the ears, the head, and the rain's intensity, which is an acting dial: drizzle for calm, downpour for feeling. Locomotion is an ewe's amble that splashes as it goes, because it is always standing in its own puddle. Proportion-licence feature: the cloud-fleece, a body that is mostly weather.
- **Palette seed:** raincloud slate, wet wool grey-blue, puddle silver, fresh green, brand blue `#70b0d0`
- **Bio (Mochiioteca):** Rainewe's wool is a permanent personal raincloud, and it rains on Rainewe without pause. Gardens it is led through flourish, and dry pastures are measured out so it can stand in them. It is one of the two parents of the Stormewe cross.
- **Footprint:** Print shape class: hoof. Each print is a cloven impression, two pointed halves side by side, identical in shape to Cinderewe's. Toe/claw count and arrangement: two hoof toes per foot, no claws. Gait pattern: alternating steps in a walking trail. Print size class: medium-small. Distinctive marker: unmistakable — every print is a small full puddle, dark and wet even at the end of a dry day, and the trail between them is damp. A Rainewe trail can be followed by touch, eyes closed, in a drought.
- **Silhouette:** Outline class: round cloud on legs, darker and lower-slung than Cinderewe's. Proportions in words: a heavy horizontal oval of cloud-wool, head low at the front, legs short below, and a visible curtain of rain falling from the fleece's hem to the ground. Key visual masses: the rain-dark fleece and the puddle it stands in. What reads at sprite size: the dark wool mass, the falling rain lines, and the pale face. Identifying negative space: the bright gaps between the rain lines under the fleece, and the dry notch of air either side of its personal downpour — rain here, none there.
- **Stat profile (words only):** A sustaining profile — bulky and slow like its cross-partner, with a steady Aqua presence built around endurance and field control rather than impact — plus "⚑ numbers unassigned (owner/game-design)".
- **DESIGN BRIEF:** Silhouette is an ewe wearing its own weather system: dark cloud-fleece, permanent falling rain, a puddle that travels with it. Key features: rain that never stops, a fleece that reads as cloud before it reads as wool, a calm wet face. Must-never-change list: the rain is personal — it falls on Rainewe, not on the whole scene; it is an elemental weather-sheep, not wool livestock; the cross with Cinderewe must be noted wherever it is presented; no baked-in shadow.
- **Flags:** ⚠ Sheep are Pokémon-occupied (Wooloo) — these are elemental weather-sheep, not wool livestock (see §5 / §5.4, B3 sheep). ⚑ Additionally: no one-word temperament is given in the source for the B3 crossing — flagged open, owner-class, not invented here.

### Stormewe — B3 · cross result, single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B3, the Stormewe crossing — Stormewe, the designed cross of Cinderewe × Rainewe, wears a thunderhead: wool that crackles, rains on one chosen flowerbed at a time, and is the festival's official weather.
- **Types:** Volt (the crossing is recorded in the source as Flame × Aqua → Volt)
- **Category:** the Thunderhead Mochi
- **Job:** the festival's official weather
- **Size class:** Medium — an ordinary ewe's scale in the legs and body, made imposing by a towering thunderhead fleece that doubles its read height and crackles at the edges.
- **Temperament:** ⚑ No one-word temperament is given in the source for this crossing — owner-class, unassigned in the source. Its colour, from the seed alone: weather with a sense of occasion, choosing one flowerbed at a time to favour with its rain.
- **Habitat:** The festival grounds of Region 3 above all; between festivals, high pastures where its thunderhead can grumble without alarming the stock. Its year turns around the Volcano Oni's Mochichao Festival.
- **Diet & behaviour:** Grazes festival-ground grass and flowerbed edges, carefully — its rain is aimed, never careless. The wool crackles with static, rains on one chosen flowerbed at a time, and builds to a dramatic rumble whenever a crowd gathers, which at festival time is always.
- **Evolution:** Does not evolve, and is not evolved into by either parent alone. Stormewe is the designed CROSS of Cinderewe × Rainewe (Flame × Aqua → Volt): the crossing is its origin, recorded here in full — Cinderewe contributes the smoulder, Rainewe contributes the rain, and Stormewe is what the two weathers make together. Combination is flavour and silhouette, per the R3 law; any breeding-system mechanics stay owner-side. Crossing note carried on both parents' sheets (Cinderewe, B3; Rainewe, B3).
- **Discovery:** Event — very rare outside its occasion. Method: festival, as the festival's official weather; it is met on duty, not caught at random. Time/season: the Mochichao Festival itself; between festivals it keeps to high pastures as an uncommon sight. Level band: mid. A Ranger first plausibly meets it when the festival programme calls for weather and the weather walks in on four legs.
- **Cry (full spec):** Source character: an ewe's bleat inside a thunderhead — deep rumble, electric crackle, and rain-patter braided together, the bleat arriving a breath after a small flash in the wool. Pitch contour: low rumble under a mid bleat, crackle spiking high at the edges. Duration class: longer than its parents' cries, built to roll. Syllable/rhythm pattern: *flash — baa — rumble*, in that order, every time. Variations — idle: a distant, contented grumble with occasional single raindrops; call: a full crack-and-bleat that makes the flowerbeds jump; hurt: a sharp thunderclap bleat, then sulking drizzle; faint: the rumble winds down like a storm leaving the valley; affection: warm rain and a low, pleased bleat with the crackle turned to fizz; evolution moment: its arrival moment is the crossing itself — first a hiss (Rainewe's rain meeting Cinderewe's smoulder), then the first small thunder, then the bleat.
- **Anatomy notes (animation-variance):** A four-legged ewe on the same body plan as both parents — cloven hooves, grazer's jaw, no manipulators — carrying a thunderhead instead of a fleece. Acting lives in the head and ears and, above all, in the wool: flash, crackle, rain-aim, and rumble are its expression system. Its rain is targeted locomotion-adjacent acting — the body turns so the chosen flowerbed is under the cloud. Proportion-licence feature: the thunderhead fleece, taller than the ewe under it. An animator must keep the storm readable as wool, not as a special effect pasted over a sheep.
- **Palette seed:** thunderhead charcoal-violet, lightning white, rain silver, storm blue, flowerbed green (accent, where the rain lands)
- **Bio (Mochiioteca):** Stormewe, the cross of Cinderewe and Rainewe, wears a thunderhead for wool. It rains on one chosen flowerbed at a time, crackles when it is pleased, and serves as the official weather of the Mochichao Festival. The festival programme does not list a forecast. It lists Stormewe.
- **Footprint:** Print shape class: hoof. Each print is a cloven impression, two pointed halves side by side, as both parents leave. Toe/claw count and arrangement: two hoof toes per foot, no claws. Gait pattern: alternating steps in a deliberate, processional walk — festival pace. Print size class: medium-small. Distinctive marker: each print is at once damp and faintly scorch-cracked, a wet hoofprint with a lightning-fern of tiny cracks in the mud around it, and only the prints — never the ground between gatherings — carry both marks together.
- **Silhouette:** Outline class: anvil cloud on legs. Proportions in words: a modest ewe body almost lost under a huge, flat-bottomed, billowing thunderhead mass that rises well above the back and overhangs the head. Key visual masses: the thunderhead first, the face lit from inside it second. What reads at sprite size: the dark anvil wool, an internal flash, and the thin legs. Identifying negative space: the straight-edged gap between the thunderhead's flat base and the ewe's back, and the single column of open air where its aimed rain falls beside it, not on it.
- **Stat profile (words only):** The cross out-powers either parent — a dramatic special-attacking weather profile, crackling offence and festival presence, still on a slow ewe's legs — plus "⚑ numbers unassigned (owner/game-design)".
- **DESIGN BRIEF:** Silhouette is a sheep under a personal thunderhead: anvil wool, internal lightning, aimed rain, thin legs in procession. Key features: thunderhead fleece that flashes, rain that chooses one flowerbed, the visible inheritance of both parents (smoulder at the wool's roots, rain at its hem). Must-never-change list: Stormewe is a cross, never presented as an evolution of a single parent; the wool stays wool, not a pasted-on storm effect; it is an elemental weather-sheep, not wool livestock; no baked-in shadow.

### Stackid — B4 · single-stage
- **Line / concept:** B4 Stackid — a goat made of stacked flat stones, cairn-bodied; it stands at trail turns as a living signpost and resents being decorated, except at festival, when it allows ribbons.
- **Types:** Terra
- **Category:** the Cairn Mochi
- **Job:** signpost/guide
- **Size class:** Medium — a goat's worth of stones stacked to signpost height; it reads less as an animal that stands and more as a landmark that happens to breathe.
- **Temperament:** stubborn (literally and otherwise) — it will not be moved, redirected, or redecorated, and the one exception it grants, festival ribbons, is granted the way a mountain grants weather.
- **Habitat:** Trail turns, passes, and crossroads across Region 3's ranch and festival country, on routes walkers actually need marked — the same stones, in the same places, year after year.
- **Diet & behaviour:** A stone body with an animal's habits: it licks mineral salts from roadside rock and browses the moss and lichen that grow between its own slabs. It stands at its post through all weather. Decoration is shaken off within the hour — except at festival, when ribbons are permitted, and worn with enormous dignity.
- **Evolution:** Single-stage — does not evolve.
