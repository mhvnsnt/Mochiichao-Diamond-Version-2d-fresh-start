### The method, as rules a brainstorm must follow

1. **The name is the type tell.** Where a holder has an element, the name
   *means* that element in plain vocabulary — iron, droplet, blaze, sea,
   silver, night. A player who learns the word has learned the matchup.
2. **Short and dense.** One to two syllables dominate (Lin, Falk, Gin,
   Umi, Hono, Yoru, Hex). Three syllables is the exception and is spent
   on softness or ceremony (Shizuku, Homura, Kenji, Mochiko).
3. **A plausible personal name first, a meaning second.** No puns, no
   compounds, none of the move-renaming style (Blast/Burst/Wave). Human
   names in this canon are quiet; the meaning is discovered, not worn.
4. **Japanese carries the elemental ranks** (every elemental dojo leader
   and all four Elite Four). **Outlier languages mark outlier roles:**
   Falk's Germanic falcon for the wind wanderer, Lin's Chinese calm for
   the machine-dojo, Greek reserved for the professor generation (Aeon).
5. **Concept word-names are rationed to concept roles** — Phantom (the
   Umbral leader is a presence, not a person) and Hex (the champion is a
   system-word). At most one or two per region, or the register breaks.
6. **Each rank has its own compression.** Dojo leaders: elemental
   meaning-names. Elite Four: a *matched set* — even shorter, and in
   Region 1 they mirror the dojo type spread (Water/Chrome/Flame/
   Umbral). Champion: a concept, not an element. Nobody in canon carries
   a surname.

**Confidence discipline:** meanings above are marked certain / high /
unverified. Where the owner's intent is unrecorded (Lin, Kenji), this
document says so rather than retrofitting a derivation.
