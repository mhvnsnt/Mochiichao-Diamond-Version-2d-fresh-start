# #281 — DOUBLE LIFE → respected in the square, known in the alley, both at once

**The position this spec designs for.** The thief
(#278) does not live in the underworld and visit the
surface; he lives in **both, at the same time** — a
respected Ranger in public and a known thief among
thieves. Lesser systems make that impossible: one
reputation score means one world always knows what the
other did. Mochiichao's doesn't, and the reason is
already built: **#270's reputation is per-group, and
fame and infamy are separate tracks.** This spec is
the double life read over that architecture — the
system work is not making two reputations possible
(List 28 did that); it is pricing what it costs to
**keep them from colliding**.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/double-life/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The two worlds are two readings of one record.**
  The respectable world reads his public facts: the
  trust classes of #280 (he is, in fact, a helpful,
  present, decorated Ranger — the facts are real).
  The underworld reads #274's facts: fences dealt
  with, goods moved, a name that opens those doors.
  Neither reading is a disguise stat. He is not
  "pretending" in either room, in data terms — both
  rooms are judging true subsets of one history, and
  the subsets do not overlap, because respectable
  people and fences do not compare notes. **That
  non-overlap is the whole asset, and it is social,
  not systemic: this spec founds no concealment
  mechanic, no disguise field, no secrecy score.**
- **Collision risk is derived from bridging facts.**
  The mask does not slip by script fiat; it slips by
  **data** — a fact that exists in both worlds at
  once. The bridging classes, closed list:
  - **Witnesses (#272):** someone who saw the deed
    and also moves in respectable rooms. A witnessed
    theft is a fact with a carrier.
  - **Travellers (#145):** the people whose schedules
    span both worlds by profession — a traveller who
    met him at a dojo and again at a fence is one
    record holding both meetings (#145's continuity is
    exactly the danger, working as designed).
  - **The Mochi themselves (#273/#284):** a stolen
    Mochi recognised in his party by its former keeper
    or their friends is a bridging fact with a face.
    Creatures cannot testify — the voice law — but
    recognition is behaviour and scene, and a keeper
    seeing their lost partner at his side needs no
    words from anyone.
  - **His own honours (#104):** a public stamp and an
    underworld deed attached to the same licence
    identity (#98) — the two worlds' records of him
    are one record, and anyone who ever reads across
    (the law, at #272's higher wanted levels) collapses
    the distance instantly.
  Collision, in system terms: **a bridging fact reaching
  a group that did not hold it.** Reputation
  consequences are #270's to apply; this spec supplies
  the event class and the derivation (risk rises with
  bridging facts held by mobile carriers — travellers
  and witnesses — never with time alone).
- **The ongoing cost is behaviour, and it is his.**
  Keeping the subsets apart costs the character real
  choices the systems can see: not fencing in a town
  where he is decorated; not travelling with a stolen
  partner through its home sector; owing #145
  travellers a careful distance. None of these are
  mechanics imposed on him — they are the route's
  texture (#278), and in the game they are the player-
  thief's own risk management, which the collision
  derivation makes legible without ever showing a
  "exposure meter" (no display: standing in this
  programme is played, not rendered — #201's law,
  inherited by List 28's ladder).

## Show lane

The double life is the part's dramatic engine at room
scale: the audience holds both readings (audience-
knows, #280) while each room holds one. Scenes are
built on near-collisions staged from the bridging
classes — the traveller entering the dojo while he is
in it; the stolen partner's keeper at the festival —
and the arc plan (#82) tracks bridging facts like
continuity, because they are: **a bridging fact staged
in episode 3 is still true in episode 9**, and the
season that forgets one has broken its own record
(#82's conservation discipline, applied to secrets).

## Game mirror

A thief-route player (#278) lives the same derivation:
per-group standing (#270) keeps the worlds apart;
their own bridging facts — fences visited in home
sectors, stolen partners kept in the active party,
witnesses left talking (#272) — are the risk, all of
it readable from the world (who is where, #93; who
travels, #145) rather than from any hidden counter.

## Open slots

None that are mechanics. **Which collisions his story
actually suffers — which traveller, which witness,
whether the mask ever fully comes off — are the part's
story, the owner's and the writers'.** The classes
above are closed; the instances are unwritten on
purpose.
