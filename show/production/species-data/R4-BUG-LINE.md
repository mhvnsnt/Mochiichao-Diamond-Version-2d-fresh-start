# R4 BUG LINE — arthropod design grammar (List 7, #62)

Design notes for the design lane. Register law: Region 4 = the
full mix + originals — animals with no counterpart yet, bugs and
insects, mythical creatures, yokai, oni, spirits, demons,
cryptids, folklore broadly — all original work with inspirations,
rendered in the one Mochiichao style. These notes govern the
arthropod slice of that register; they design nothing themselves.

## What is actually queued (sources, not invention)

- **Termight → Cathedrall** (C3 small bucket) — the termite line;
  the colony-architecture read is already in the names: a mound
  that becomes a cathedral. Social-insect structure is the design
  brief the names carry.
- **Mothlet → Omenmoth** (C5+C6 cryptid/folklore bucket) — a moth
  line filed under folklore: the omen register, not the field-
  guide register. It obeys this grammar *and* the folklore method
  (#63) — dual-register lines take both notes.
- Wave-2 plans name more insects (waiting on data — not designed,
  not specced here).

## The grammar

1. **Segmentation is the silhouette.** An arthropod reads by its
   segment rhythm (head/thorax/abdomen or its invented analogue),
   not by surface detail. The design gate's silhouette test is
   run on the segment shapes alone first: if the segments don't
   read, no antenna or wing saves it.
2. **Limb count is a dial, not a fact.** Real arthropods run
   6/8/many; Mochiichao lines choose per stage and hold the count
   across the line unless the sheet says the evolution changes
   it. Count changes are evolution events, recorded in the sheet —
   never animator drift. Extra limbs must *do* something in the
   read (carry, brace, signal) — decorative legs read as noise at
   Palm scale.
3. **Exoskeleton = cel discipline.** Chitin is the Mochiichao
   style's hard-cel grammar at its most natural: flat fields, one
   hard facet tone per plate, plates separated by the decisive
   contour. Soft rendering on a shell is the preschool failure in
   a new costume (Style Spec anti-preschool rules apply verbatim).
4. **Small is not weak.** Bug lines sit at the Palm end of the
   silhouette staging ladder; the fight-read is carried by
   *equipment and posture* — mandibles, stingers, horns, braced
   stance, colony mass (a Termight's fight-read is partly its
   mound: the line fights as a structure). A lone cute beetle
   with no equipment fails the fight-read test like any mascot.
5. **Wings are state, not decoration.** Folded/flight is a held-
   pose state change (the state language: changes land only on
   held poses) — wing position is staging information, drawn
   deliberately, never fluttering ambience on a principal. (Ambient
   background insects, if a location ever stocks them, are
   LIVING-WORLD population, a different system and a lane call.)
6. **Eyes stay family.** Compound-eye *patterning* may live on the
   surface field, but the eye construction is the family's —
   large, placed for the gaze line, temperament in the lid. A
   realistic compound eye breaks the universe read (the Style
   Spec's eye grammar governs; cf. the approved Pupparmor lids).

## Per-line notes discipline

When an arthropod line is designed, its sheet gains a `bug_notes`
annex: segment map (one line), limb count per stage, shell-plate
rhythm, wing states. The annex is what the rig lane builds from —
arthropod rigs instance repeated limb components (the open-movie
teardown's instanced-leg finding, List 1 #5, is the rig-side twin
of rule 2).

## Coverage

Grammar: standard creature-design practice for arthropods,
publicly documented, re-expressed in house rules. Contents: only
the two queued lines are named, from the queue itself; wave-2
insects are named in plans only and stay undesigned until their
sheets exist (data-first law).
