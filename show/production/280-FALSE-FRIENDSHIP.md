# #280 — FALSE FRIENDSHIP & BETRAYAL → trust built on purpose, and what it costs to burn it

**The brief (owner, 2026-10-27).** The thief's method
is the friendship itself: he **makes friends with
people in order to steal their Mochi** (#278). For that
to work as a story and as a route, friendship in this
world cannot be a cutscene flag — it has to be a real
thing the systems keep, that he can build *on purpose*,
and that the world can watch him spend. This spec is
that thing, and the betrayal rule that prices it.

## What friendship is, in data (verified joins only)

- **The facts already exist, scattered; this spec names
  their join.** A Ranger's history with a named NPC is
  written where this programme already writes such
  things: #93's schedule/interaction layer (meetings,
  where and when), #12's story facts (beats earned
  together), #164's mission facts (work done for and
  with people), #104's honours where an occasion
  stamps service. **Trust, in this spec, is a read over
  those facts** — per (Ranger, NPC) pair, derived at
  read time like every standing in this programme
  (#201's derivation discipline), never a stored
  "friendship meter" and never displayed as a number
  in either lane. The read's shape: has-met → known →
  trusted, as presentation classes over the density
  and kind of the shared record (help given weighs
  more than greetings exchanged — the class rules are
  data on #93's layer, landed there with citation,
  not asserted here).
- **The thief builds it the same way anyone does.**
  That is the mechanic, and it is deliberately
  unglamorous: the route to a mark's trust is the
  ordinary road — show up (#93), help (#164), be there
  for the beats (#12). There is no "deceive" verb and
  no false-intent flag on any interaction: **the
  record of a false friendship is indistinguishable
  from a true one, because in every fact the systems
  keep, it was one.** His intent lives in the writing
  (show) and the player's head (game) — the systems
  keep only what he did. This is what makes #281's
  double life hold together and #272's witnesses so
  dangerous to him: the data never betrays him; only
  events do.

## The betrayal rule (the one hard rule this spec adds)

- **A theft from a friend is priced as a betrayal, not
  a theft.** Input rule into List 28, stated here for
  landing into #270/#272's homes: when a #268 stealing
  event's victim stands in the **trusted** class of the
  trust read above, the event carries the betrayal
  weight — reputation damage lands harder and wider
  (the victim's groups hear it as a betrayal, #270's
  per-group tracks reading a worse deed than a stranger
  theft), and #272's witness handling treats the
  victim's own testimony as certain (a stranger might
  misidentify a thief; a friend cannot unknow him).
  The ladder consequence (#269) is List 28's to price;
  the classification — *this theft was from a friend* —
  is this spec's output, and it is a derivation, not a
  flag the thief can scrub: the trust facts are the
  same facts that opened the victim's door to him.
- **The victim's Mochi is not part of the price.**
  Its side of the theft is #273's, entire: bond,
  refusal, recovery. The friendship the thief burned
  was with the human; the Mochi owes him nothing and
  its record says so.

## Show lane — dramatic irony as structure

The arc-plan (#82) gains one annotation for his beats:
**audience-knows** — whether the audience has been
shown what the friendship is for. The season's suspense
engine is the gap between the two reads of the same
scenes: the mark reads a friendship (#93's warmth,
played straight); the audience, where audience-knows
is set, reads a preparation. Production discipline:
a betrayal beat may only spend trust the audience has
watched being built (in either read) — a theft from a
friendship the show never showed is a cheat against
the audience, and the arc plan's trust citations (the
#12/#164 facts the season staged) are how planning
proves it didn't. When the theft lands, the season
does not cut away from the victim: the betrayal is a
two-sided beat, and the mark's side is owed the same
screen weight as the thief's (the anti-hero is followed;
he is not cosigned — #278).
