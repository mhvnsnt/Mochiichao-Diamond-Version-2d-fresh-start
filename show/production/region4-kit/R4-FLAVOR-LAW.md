# REGION 4 FLAVOR LAW — THE MECHA / TOY-BATTLE REGISTER
Owner-directed 2026-10-27. Status: design law for Region 4 (the Rift
region), to be read with the Regional Flavor Progression (R1 Pokémon-
like → R2 +Digimon drift → R3 +Monster Rancher drift → R4 fusion).
Everything below is original Mochiichao machinery — each tradition's
*contribution* is named for the record; no source names, designs, or
vocabulary are carried into canon text.

## The region in one line
Region 4 is where the world's wild things meet the world's made
things: creatures that transform and combine, Rangers who ride and
pilot, battle as public ritual — and an Institute that studies the
cracks in the world with clean hands and a second set of books.

## The six contributions

### 1. BIONICLE — masks, tribes, and the world-as-body
- **Power worn on the face.** R4 introduces *crafted* power: carved
  face-plates (the Dark Forest mask-craft grown industrial) that grant
  a Mochi or Ranger one defined technique while worn. A plate is
  earned, forged, traded, and can be lost — R4's prestige economy.
  (Design note: Wispkin's skull mask is the wild ancestor of the
  craft; R4 is where the Garden learns the Forest had the idea first.)
- **Elemental tribal structure.** R4 settlements organise by element-
  houses with distinct customs and rivalries; the region's politics
  are house politics.
- **The body under the world.** Bionicle's world-is-a-sleeping-giant
  is the strongest published rhyme to THE SECRET: a machine-world
  whose people worship it as a god and must not know. R4 is where the
  show may *stage the rhyme* — ruins that are clearly joints, a
  "sleeping giant" folktale children are told — while no character
  ever states the truth. Aeon's slips get more dangerous here.

### 2. TRANSFORMERS — two-form bodies, faction philosophy
- **Living two-form Mochi.** R4 species include true transformers:
  one creature, two complete forms (a travel/beast form and a battle
  form), rearranged at will. Form is social strategy — the battle
  form is not worn at market.
- **Two philosophies, one people.** R4's made-creature population is
  split by doctrine (guard the wild vs. order the wild) — a civil
  argument, not good tribe/evil tribe, and Rangers get caught in it.

### 3. BEYBLADE — ritual combat, built fighters, spirit vessels
- **The stadium custom.** Public ritual battles in bounded bowls with
  a counted launch — R4's civic sport, the dojo system's public face.
- **Three-part built gear.** Ranger battle-gear in R4 is assembled
  from three swappable parts (a face/contact part, a weight part, a
  movement part) — the Ranger-as-builder: gear is chosen before the
  match and is signature, like the R3 Susano Oni's festival regalia.
- **Spirit in the vessel.** The gear's crest houses the partner
  Mochi's *regard* — in big moments the partner's form wreathed over
  the gear (show visual; game: a bond-gauge effect). Burst-stakes:
  gear can come apart under a true hit — losing by *burst* (gear
  scattered) outranks losing by ring-out in R4 scoring.

### 4. BAKUGAN — card-gated ritual, partner agency
Full mapping in BAKUGAN-DEEP-USE-MAP.md: field-opening ritual, chosen
ground, declared trios, intervention cards (General/Rare moves as the
hand), grounds-capture scoring, closed/true forms, and the Institute's
"instruments" doctrine as the region's moral fight.

### 5. GUNDAM — war-machine politics, cockpit intimacy
- **Machines are logistics.** R4's large frames need crews, fuel,
  repair yards, and weather windows; the Institute and the houses
  fight over *supply*, not just duels. The airship (owner's defining
  sentence pending) belongs to this register: a crewed machine, not a
  fast-travel menu.
- **The pilot bond.** A frame answers its own Ranger best: sync is a
  relationship with history — grief when a frame is lost, a frame
  that keeps a former Ranger's habits (imprint, in game terms a
  legacy bonus). Aces are political assets: the Institute cages its
  best pilots "for their safety."
- **The new sense.** Long exposure to the Rift is changing some
  Rangers — an expanded awareness near cracks (they *feel* a seam
  before it shows). The Institute studies it, measures it, and files
  it under a word nobody outside hears. (Show: staged as perception,
  never explained.)

### 6. EVANGELION — bio-mecha horror, the institute with floors
- **The frames are alive.** R4's largest frames are grown, not built:
  living beings under restraint plating, ridden by synchronisation —
  the Ranger feels the frame's pain; a frame whose rider's sync
  breaks can act *on its own*, and everyone nearby learns the control
  was a lease. This is the autonomous-action doctrine at mecha scale:
  the frame is a Mochi, with a Mochi's agency, at a size that makes
  agency terrifying.
- **The Institute has floors.** Evangelion's front-institute design
  is the direct template for R4's Institute as already written in
  canon ("We research. The Rift is research."): a public research
  mission, honest staff serving it in good faith, and a lower floor
  running a second agenda on a written timetable. What the lower
  floor wants with the Rift is the region's buried question — and it
  rhymes with THE SECRET by design: the Institute is the one body in
  the world *institutionally organised around almost-knowing*, which
  is why Aeon — the one man who knows — reads as their natural
  opposite number. Luigi's hint, in R4, would make him the most
  dangerous tourist alive.
- **Only the same can fight the same.** What comes through the Rift
  can only be met by what the Rift has touched — the region's frames
  are Rift-grown for a reason the Institute does not publish.

## Design rules (binding on R4 species/gear/episode work)
1. Every mecha element must pass the *Garden test*: could this exist
   in the world Mochiko keeps, grown from R1–R3's drift? If it needs
   the region to become a different show, it doesn't ship.
2. Transformations and combinations are *creature* events first,
   machine events second — the Mochi chooses (autonomous-action
   doctrine applies to frames and two-form species without exception).
3. Ritual is custom, not UI: field-openings, launches, and ground-
   settings are things people *do* in-world, with elders, children,
   and cheating — never a menu animation pasted on.
4. The Institute is written at two floors in every document that
   mentions it; writers must know which floor a scene is on.
5. Nothing from the six traditions enters canon under its source
   name, design, or vocabulary — contributions are machinery only.
