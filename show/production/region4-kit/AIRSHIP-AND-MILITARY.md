# THE AIRSHIP AND THE ORDER — REGION 4'S MILITARY
OWNER-DIRECTED DESIGN (2026-10-27). This document discharges the
airship sentence R4-FLAVOR-LAW.md §5 left pending ("the airship…
belongs to this register: a crewed machine, not a fast-travel menu")
and builds Region 4's military on the same foundation. Names marked
TBD are the owner's to give or the brainstorm's to propose — nothing
in this doc coins one. Forks are listed in §5 with recommended
defaults, recommended-not-locked. Construction-kit filter throughout:
inspirations are role, structure, and story function only — no source
names, designs, ranks, or vocabulary are carried into canon.

## For the record — the inspiration, accurately
The owner named the model as "Maxon from Fallout 3." The accurate
record: the aviator-coated commander with the airship is **Elder
Arthur Maxson of Fallout 4**, master of the Brotherhood of Steel's
airship *Prydwen*; Fallout 3's Brotherhood was Elder Lyons' chapter
and had no airship. The design below works from the corrected record
(Maxson register: the young supreme commander, the coat, the doctrine,
the ship as the order's argument) while importing nothing of the
source's names, heraldry, ranks, or designs.

## 1. THE LORD — the airship's owner
**NAME: TBD.** Working reference in documents only: "the Airship
Lord." No candidate is proposed here; naming routes to the owner's
lock, with the name-gap brainstorm as the proposal channel.

- **What he is:** a Ranger-lord of Region 4's machine age — supreme
  commander of the Order (§2), owner and captain of the airship (§3),
  and the most visible man in the region. He is young for supreme
  command, and the region knows it; his authority does not argue with
  the fact, it stands next to it in a long coat.
- **The silhouette.** The coat is the register: a heavy flight coat
  with a high collar, worn in all weathers and all audiences, with
  one crafted face-plate (R4's prestige craft, R4-FLAVOR-LAW §1)
  worn at the throat as a gorget — the region reads the coat before
  it reads the man, and house-town children copy it in miniature.
  Silhouette only; no source garment is reproduced.
- **He IS the argument.** The Maxson register's core: the man is not
  the order's spokesman, he is its *proof*. Disciplined, controlled,
  courteous, and absolutely certain — he speaks in short doctrinal
  sentences, never raises his voice, and has never been seen to
  improvise. Where he stands, the doctrine is present in the room,
  which is why the Order puts him in rooms.
- **The belief is sincere.** He holds the doctrine because he has
  seen the alternative: design seed (not locked) — as a boy he
  survived a Rift-crossing disaster in which powerful things, held
  by nobody, did what uncontrolled power does. The doctrine is his
  scar tissue. Writers must never play him as a hypocrite or a
  fraud; his error, where he errs, is certainty.
- **His own Mochi** are famous in the region as *sworn* partners —
  immaculate, devoted, never seen to hesitate. Whether the swearing
  was the creatures choosing is a question the region asks quietly
  and the show may stage but canon does not answer (the collision,
  §2). His pilot bond with his personal frame follows the Flavor
  Law's imprint rules — the frame keeps his habits; the crew treats
  its moods as weather.
- **Aeon reads him instantly** as a kindred problem: another man
  whose whole life is organised around the world being more fragile
  than it looks — one floor away from almost-knowing everything the
  Institute files and nothing the Secret holds.

## 2. THE ORDER — Region 4's military
**NAME: TBD** (Fork 2). It is referred to here only as "the Order."

### 2.1 What it is
A sworn techno-military order that holds Region 4's great machines —
the large frames, the Rift-touched gear, the airship — mans the Rift
crossings, and answers incursions with organised force. Part army,
part guild, part congregation: it recruits in the elemental-house
towns, schools and feeds its sworn, maintains the repair yards and
mooring masts, keeps the region's incursion ledger, and buries its
dead with the plate of office returned to the forge. To most of
Region 4 the Order is not an army that visits; it is the weather
system the region lives under.

### 2.2 The doctrine — and its two collisions
Doctrine in the Maxson register, three articles:

1. **Power is held, never loose.** Great frames, Rift-touched gear,
   and seam-knowledge belong under oath, in named hands. What tore
   the world can tear it again.
2. **The hand that holds must answer.** Every holder is named,
   ranked, and answerable for what they hold. The Order's deepest
   contempt is for *anonymous* power — power with no face to answer
   for it.
3. **The held thing serves.** Service is purpose; a frame in service
   is fulfilled by being crewed well.

**Collision A — the Institute.** The Institute agrees with Article 1
and reaches the opposite sacrament: it holds power by *hiding* it
(files, floors, unpublished words), which to the Order is anonymous
power wearing a lab coat — Article 2's exact sin. The Order holds
power by *displaying* it under names and ranks, which to the
Institute is a parade where a vault was needed. Same fear, two
religions. (Relationship mechanics in §2.4; the twin reading is
Fork 4's recommended default.)

**Collision B — the creatures.** Article 3 does not survive contact
with the show's autonomous-action law in the Order's own ranks: a
frame is a Mochi, with a Mochi's agency (R4-FLAVOR-LAW §6), and a
battle Mochi is a fighter, not a switch a Ranger flips (the two-lane
battle doctrine). The Order's ceremonial answer is **the Swearing**:
before a creature enters service it is formally *asked*, in a rite
with witnesses, and its response is entered in the ledger as consent.
Rangers who have watched a Swearing disagree — permanently, and
politely, in public — about what they saw: a creature choosing, or a
creature trained to perform choosing on cue. The region's Rangers
answer with the counter-rite (the resistance oath-shape,
BAKUGAN-DEEP-USE-MAP §2): a partner's agency asserted in public,
renewed by the creature's own act. Canon rules on neither rite's
inner truth; the *argument* is the region's moral engine, and every
R4 story the Order touches is standing on it. Note the practical
bend: in a live incursion, Order companies fight inside the living
fight whether they preach it or not — sync breaks, frames act,
doctrine holds its tongue until the debrief.

### 2.3 Structure — ranks and castes
All terms original to Mochiichao, drawn from the ship and the plate
craft; no source rank vocabulary is used.

- **The Airship Lord** — supreme commander; owner of the flagship;
  final court of the Order.
- **Warden** — officer grade: holds a Rift crossing, a crossing
  company, a mooring town's garrison, or a deck of the ship. Wardens
  answer by name for their holding (Article 2 made flesh) and may
  issue the warrant-tokens of §3.3.
- **Bearer** — the plate-bearing field grade: sworn who hold a
  crafted plate of office; frame riders, company leaders, senior
  crew.
- **Hand** — the sworn rank and file: crew, yard hands, company
  fighters, dispatch riders.
- **Hopeful** — the unsworn: recruits under instruction, holding
  nothing, owed food, teaching, and a fair look.

Castes across the ranks: **Gearwrights** (builders and repair — they
make the three-part gear of R4-FLAVOR-LAW §3 and keep the frames;
the Order's true long memory lives in their yards), **Watchers**
(the Rift-sense corps — Rangers whose exposure to the seams has
changed their perception, R4-FLAVOR-LAW §5; the Order tests
house-town children for the sense and calls it a blessing, and a
Watcher is both prized and watched), and **the Ship's Company** (the
airship's own, who hold the Lord's household and the mooring
circuit). Ground force: the **Crossing Companies**, one per major
seam crossing, who fight incursions by the region's field ritual —
the Order observes the Bakugan-gated custom scrupulously, because
ritual *is* control, performed.

### 2.4 Relationship map
- **The Institute — rival twin** (recommended default, Fork 4). Born
  of the same catastrophe generation: one body of survivors took to
  the laboratory, the other to the sky. The entanglement is material
  as well as doctrinal: Institute yards grow and refit some of the
  frames the Order crews (the Gundam logistics of §3), so each side
  needs the other's competence and despises the needing. Each keeps
  a liaison officer the other pretends is unnecessary. Aeon is the
  only man both buildings receive without a warrant.
- **Delta, Region 4's Champion — unaffiliated, honoured,
  perpetually recruited** (recommended default, Fork 5). The
  Champion's seat sits in the dojo league, which predates the Order
  and declines absorption as a matter of its own older custom. The
  Order recruits at Delta openly and is refused, politely, on a
  schedule both sides almost enjoy; the Lord's standing instruction
  is that Delta receives full honours anywhere the ship flies. To
  the Order, Delta is the standing proof that Article 1 has an
  exception with a name; to Delta, the Order is weather.
- **The Rangers — courted, respected, considered unfinished.** The
  Order recruits Rangers harder than any other population and loses
  most of them to the agency argument (§2.2, Collision B). Its
  officers are under standing orders to treat unaffiliated Rangers
  as colleagues in a live fight and as prospects at the mess table,
  in that order. Mochiko's Garden register — creatures belong to
  somebody, everybody belongs to the Garden — is quoted inside the
  Order more often than the Lord knows, usually by Hands.
- **The Rift — the reason and the wound.** The Order exists because
  the crossings do. It holds them, flies the seam, and keeps the
  ledger of what has come through. Founding story (design seed, not
  locked): at the region's first great tearing, the Order's founders
  held a crossing on the ground, with their hands, while others took
  notes — the seed of its contempt for the Institute and of Article
  2 alike. What the Rift is *for* is the Institute's buried question;
  the Order does not ask it. The Order's whole theology fits in one
  of the Lord's sentences: *"We do not study the door. We stand in
  it."*

## 3. THE AIRSHIP
**NAME: TBD** (Fork 6) — the region will know it by one; documents
here call it "the ship" or "the flagship."

### 3.1 What it is
The Order's flagship: a mobile fortress and command carrier, the
largest crewed machine in Region 4 — frame decks and repair yards,
barracks and a muster hall, the Lord's court, the incursion ledger's
master copy, and a silhouette every child in the region can draw.
It almost never lands. It works a **mooring circuit** of mast-towns,
arriving on published weather windows, hanging over a town for days
at a time while climbers and shuttle craft run between its decks and
the mast. For most of the region it is a feature of the sky that
occasionally comes close enough to be a *place* — and to cast the
shadow under which negotiations, prices, and courtships briefly
change.

### 3.2 Why it exists — the owner's mix, unpacked
- **Brotherhood / Maxson storyline — projection and judgment.** The
  ship exists to *watch* every crossing and be *seen* watching: the
  doctrine given altitude. The Lord's court travels with it, so the
  Order's judgment — warrants, honours, the ledger read aloud —
  arrives in person, in the sky, in the coat. It is also the
  Order's recruiting sermon: no pamphlet the Institute ever filed
  has emptied a house town of its seventeen-year-olds the way one
  mooring week does.
- **Transformers — the titan in the room.** One asset whose arrival
  *transforms* whatever moment it enters: a house dispute, an
  incursion, a festival, is a different event with the ship over it.
  The region's politics are conducted in awareness of where it is on
  its circuit, the way coastal towns think about weather.
- **Gundam — logistics, always logistics.** The ship eats: hundreds
  of crew, fuel and stores, frame parts, Gearwright hours, weather.
  Its circuit is a supply chain with a flag on it; towns compete for
  mooring weeks and resent the prices that follow them; sorties off
  its decks are crewed operations with briefings and return windows,
  never a hero launching alone. If a scene aboard forgets the
  manifest, the scene is wrong.
- **Evangelion — the heart (FORK, not decided).** Option, flagged
  for the owner as Fork 1: the ship is built *around* something —
  a Rift-touched living core, grown and held under restraint at the
  hull's heart, to which the Lord himself is the sync. On this
  option the flagship is secretly the Order's largest frame, its
  deepest held secret, and the true content of Article 1: the Order
  does not merely fear uncontrolled power, it *flies* one,
  controlled daily, by one man's attention. This rhymes the ship
  with the Institute's lower floor and, one floor deeper, with THE
  SECRET — and is therefore staged, if chosen, only as machinery
  and unease, never as explanation. If the owner declines the fork,
  the ship is a built machine full stop: its heart is a boiler room
  and the doctrine's chapel, and nothing in this section changes
  except this paragraph.

### 3.3 The visiting Ranger — the `r4_airship_route_open` beat
How travel on it works, for the game's route flag and the show alike:

- **The route opens two ways:** the ship's circuit brings it to your
  town's mooring mast on a published window; or a Warden issues you
  a **warrant-token** — a blank, unworked plate, recognised on sight
  anywhere in the region — good for one boarding and the circuit
  ride to your destination mast.
- **Boarding** is by climber from the mast, through muster: name and
  business entered in the ledger, ship's law read once, aloud, to
  everyone alike. Guests wear the blank plate while aboard.
- **The Weather-Deck Custom:** a guest's Mochi board and travel *on
  the open weather deck*, never closed in a hold — an old concession
  the Rangers won in the Order's first circuit years, kept ever
  since and kept exactly. Closed-form carriage is never demanded of
  a guest's partner: aboard the ship, guest-right quietly overrules
  Article 3, a hypocrisy the crew does not see and the show may.
- **The cost is not money.** A passenger owes the ship a watch
  rotation and obedience to ship's law under way; a Ranger of
  standing may additionally be asked to carry Order dispatches down
  at their destination — the Order's post rides on honour, and
  refusing is permitted and remembered in equal measure.
- **What it feels like:** counted launches, bells, the field ritual
  miniaturised into deck drill; the doctrine with decks. And, on a
  clear night at altitude, the seam of the Rift visible as weather
  on the horizon — the reason, flying beside you.

## 4. Standing discipline for writers of this material
- No source vocabulary in-world: no Brotherhood titles or ranks, no
  Fallout or other franchise terms, no source designs. The coat, the
  court-in-motion, and the doctrine are *functions*, re-housed.
- The Order is never written as the region's villains by default:
  its people keep the crossings held and the towns fed in winter,
  and its doctrine's error is an *argument*, staged fairly (the
  two-lane doctrine binds the writers even when it doesn't bind the
  Order).
- The Institute is written at two floors in any document that puts
  it in a room with the Order (§2.4): state which floor the scene is
  on.
- Nothing in the Order's doctrine, rites, or the ship's heart (if
  Fork 1 is taken) may state or decode THE SECRET in-world; the
  Order almost-knows the *Rift*, which is a different and smaller
  thing, and the distance between the two is the Lord's tragedy and
  his safety.
