## The battle ritual (system skeleton)
1. Battles happen in a STADIUM — a bounded bowl; the boundary is a
   rule (ring-out is a loss condition), so terrain shape governs play.
2. A countdown ritual launches both tops simultaneously — the launch
   is a skill act (angle, power, rip technique) and part of the sport's
   theatre; everyone present counts together.
3. Victory has THREE graded forms: out-spin the opponent (survival),
   knock them out of the stadium (ring-out), or BURST them — a heavy
   hit knocks the locking mechanism loose and the opponent's top comes
   apart in pieces. Burst is worth the most: destruction outranks
   endurance. Scoring escalates with spectacle.
4. The top is a THREE-PART CONSTRUCTION KIT: a top LAYER (contact
   surface, carries the beast motif and the lock teeth), a middle DISC
   (weight/momentum), a bottom DRIVER (movement pattern, houses the
   burst spring). Battlers BUILD their fighter before the match —
   part choice is strategy, identity, and signature. Parts are swapped
   between matches, never during.
5. Later systems add: mode-changing drivers, spring-loaded bound
   attacks, burst-stoppers that only engage on a hard launch (power
   literally locks the machine together).

## World machinery
- BIT-BEASTS: sacred spirits live inside the top's emblem chip; in the
  fiction they manifest during battle as giant beasts wreathed over
  the spinning top — the toy is a vessel, the spirit is the fighter.
  A blader's bond with their bit-beast is the skill ceiling.
- The sport is a full public institution: ranked ladders, point
  trackers/logging devices issued to players, team circuits,
  tournaments as civic festivals. The game is how the society meets.
