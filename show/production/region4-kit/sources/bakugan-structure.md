## The battle ritual (system skeleton)
1. Two battlers agree; a shared spoken trigger opens the FIELD — time
   effectively pauses; an arena-space overlays the ordinary place.
2. Each battler holds a small fixed hand: 3 creatures, 3 GATE cards,
   3 ABILITY cards. Scarcity is the design: the whole match is legible.
3. A battler SETS a Gate Card face-down on the field (the card is the
   ground the battle will happen on — terrain is chosen, not given).
4. Creatures are kept in a CLOSED compact form (sphere) and THROWN;
   landing on a Gate Card's metal triggers the mechanism — the creature
   OPENS into its true, large battling form. Opening is earned by aim.
5. Battle starts only when two opposing creatures stand on the SAME
   Gate Card. Then: card flipped, its written effect read aloud and
   applied; base power + attribute bonus on that terrain counted.
6. Ability Cards are played in turns from the hand — mid-battle
   interventions by the battler, not the creature.
7. Winner captures the Gate Card. First to 3 captured Gates wins the
   match. Everything used goes to a "used pile" — a visible ledger.

## World machinery
- Six ATTRIBUTES (fire/water/earth/light/dark/wind) organised as six
  worlds of one shattered world (Vestroia); two CORES hold the world
  together (a positive and a negative core) — imbalance = catastrophe.
- Creatures are PARTNERS: in the fiction they are intelligent beings
  from the other world who bond one-to-one with a human battler; the
  toy-state (sphere) is a diminished form imposed on them.
- An invading power treats the creatures as mindless game pieces and
  forces them back into ball form; resistance = restoring their true
  forms and their say in battles. (Institutional cruelty vs. partner
  agency is the franchise's moral engine.)
- Later machinery: support pieces, traps that flip a battle's terms,
  evolved forms, and a Life Gauge — spending life-force for power is
  permanent; a creature whose life runs out is lost for good.
