## 1. The ritual is the region's battle custom (the big import)
Bakugan's core invention is that **battle is a formal, gated ritual
with a spoken opening, a chosen ground, and a countable hand** — and
that is precisely what Region 4 needs that Regions 1–3 don't have:
- **The field opens.** A shared call opens an arena-space over an
  ordinary place; time thins; bystanders are outside the moment.
  Mochiichao mapping: in R4, serious battles (dojo-stakes and above)
  are *opened*, not started — a custom the Institute formalised and
  the Rangers keep. This dovetails with the show's autonomous-action
  doctrine: the ritual frames the living fight; once the field is
  open, the Mochi fight with their own agency inside it, and the
  Ranger's cards/commands are decisive beats, not puppet strings.
- **The ground is chosen.** A battler sets the terrain card the fight
  will stand on. Mapping: R4 battle custom includes *ground-setting* —
  the challenger lays the ground (a woven field-mat / ring-stone in
  game terms), and home-ground advantage is a real, legible mechanic
  the show can stage wordlessly.
- **A small fixed hand.** Three partners, three grounds, three
  interventions. Mapping: R4 ritual battles are fought in *declared
  trios* — a Ranger declares three Mochi and three held techniques
  (our General/Rare move economy supplies the interventions; a Rare
  Move played as an intervention card is the region's prestige beat).
- **Capture the grounds to win.** Best-of-grounds scoring instead of
  pure knock-out. Mapping: R4 ritual matches can be *won on grounds* —
  a gentler, more sporting stakes-shape for festival/dojo play, sitting
  beside (not replacing) the game's health-based command battles.

## 2. Closed form / true form — the creature economy
The sphere→true-form machinery maps onto Mochiichao's R2→R4 drift:
- Creatures in R4 are commonly kept in a **resting closed form** (a
  curled, armoured, sleep-like state — travel-safe, Garden-safe) and
  **open** into true form for work and battle. Opening requires the
  ritual ground: a Mochi opens fully only on set ground or in genuine
  need — which gives the show a clean visual grammar (closed = daily
  life, open = stakes) and the game a transformation beat the R2
  Digimon-drift evolutions grow toward.
- The franchise's moral engine — an institution forcing creatures
  into closed form and calling them mindless — maps *directly* onto
  R4's Institute politics: the Institute's R4 doctrine holds that
  Mochi are instruments to be kept closed and deployed; the Rangers'
  counter-doctrine (already canon in the battle doctrine: Mochi are
  fighters with agency) becomes a regional argument, not just a style.

## 3. The two-cores world shape → the Rift's physics
Vestroia's machinery — one world in elemental segments, held by two
opposed cores, catastrophe when they unbalance — is the template for
R4's Rift cosmology, restated in canon terms: Iridescence's regions
are segments of one world; the Rift is where the seams show. R4 story
beats can use the *shape* (a segment slipping out of register; a
stabilising rite; two opposed "weights" the Institute measures) with
no source vocabulary. This also gives the airship-era its stakes:
travel between segments stops being geography and becomes *crossing
between registers* — the airship (owner's sentence still pending)
flies the seam.

## 4. Dialogue functions available for swapped-over adaptation
From the ritual's functional skeleton (per dialogue-library rules —
functions, never lines): the **field-opening call-and-response** (F8/
ceremony function), the **ground-set declaration**, the **counting
beat** (powers tallied aloud by a neutral voice — maps to our arena
announcer function), the **intervention declaration**, the **capture
acknowledgement** (loser concedes the ground, not their worth), and
the **resistance oath-shape** (a partner-creature's agency asserted
in one short beat — in our canon carried by subtitle/action, since
Mochi don't speak). The wiring job adds these as R4 functions when
Region 4's fabric phase opens.
