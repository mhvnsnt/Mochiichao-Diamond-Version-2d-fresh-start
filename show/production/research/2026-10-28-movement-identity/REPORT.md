# Movement Identity — Why Part-Rig Cartoons Become Face-Only Statues, and How Professionals (and Games) Animate the Whole Form

## Summary — verdict in one page

**The current pipeline, on the owner's ground-truth description, sits below BOTH bars:**

| Bar | What it requires, minimum | Current output (ground truth) | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. VIDEO-GAME FLOOR (minimum)** | Every creature on screen has a *continuous, per-species idle* for 100% of its on-screen time; its body — not only its face — changes; secondary actions break the loop; background tiles/elements cycle continuously; no unexplained full-white / missing background mid-scene. Pokémon *Black/White* (2010, Nintendo DS) does this for 649+ species with *part-based stretch/skew rules* — i.e. with essentially the same part-pack technology. | Body rigid for whole shots; only mouth/eye parts swap; no idle, no material behaviour; backgrounds can blink to white. | **NO — below a 2010 handheld game** |
| **2. TV STANDARD (target)** | Whole-form key posing pose-to-pose; squash & stretch with volume preserved *in the silhouette*; anticipation → action → follow-through/overlap → overshoot & settle → *moving hold* (never a dead hold); per-character movement vocabulary signed in a character bible / movement spec before a shot is cued; FX bodies (flame, smoke, water, plant) animated as FX, on cycles, every frame they are visible. | None of these are in the cue vocabulary at all. Timing/easing/camera/grade were added in a prior pass — those polish *how a cue interpolates*, they cannot create motion that was never cued, on a rig that cannot deform. | **NO — not a budget gap, a vocabulary/rig/spec gap** |

The core finding, and the reason this is fixable cheaply: **the failure is not "part packs."** Pokémon *Black/White*'s celebrated continuous idles are themselves part packs — "creating frames for individual body parts as necessary and then making the animations themselves into stretch/skew rules applied to these individual body parts" [Dragonfly Cave, verified live]. Toon Boom TV rigs are part packs too. What the professionals and the games add, and this pipeline lacks, is four things, in order:

1. **A deformation layer on the body itself** (mesh/plastic/bone/envelope deformer, or shape-substitution) so the *silhouette* can change — without it squash & stretch is physically impossible, however good the timing sheet is.
2. **Continuous tracks, not event cues** — an always-on idle / breathing / material loop underneath dialogue and action, i.e. the *moving hold* as default state, with events layered on top.
3. **A per-character movement specification written before production** — material, mass, idle, locomotion, signature motions, reaction timing — the studio "character bible + library of movements" [Animation Mentor, index].
4. **Procedural secondary motion** — spring/Verlet jiggle, flame/smoke/plant cycles driven by parameters (stiffness, damping, phase) tuned *per material*, so "water-ness" is generated continuously for free once tuned, instead of being hand-cued per shot.

Face-only is what you get, inevitably, when (a) the only parts with more than one drawing are eyes/mouth, (b) the only cues an author can write are discrete events at timestamps, and (c) no document ever specifies how the body should behave *between* events. Sections below prove each leg from sources, then give the bill of materials and the gates.

## Findings

### 1. The principles as practised — whole body, not face

Sources: Thomas & Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* (1981), codifying Disney practice; Richard Williams, *The Animator's Survival Kit*, expanding the same 12; Adobe's industry summary; Wave Motion Cannon's sakuga analysis of the principles in anime.

The 12 are: squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight-ahead / pose-to-pose, follow-through and overlapping action, slow in / slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing, appeal [Adobe, index: https://www.adobe.com/in/creativecloud/roc/blog/video/animation-principles.html — read 2026-10-28, index].

Applied as practised, each is a **body** instruction:

- **Squash and stretch — the silhouette deforms, volume preserved.** "Squash and stretch makes a drawing feel elastic rather than rigid and inanimate… Without squash and stretch, animated characters may look and move like lifeless mannequins" [Wave Motion Cannon, verified live: http://wavemotioncannon.com/2017/04/25/animation-principles-squash-and-stretch/]. Crucially it is *material-relative*: "A rock, chair, or giant robot would look less convincing if it warped… When they strike each other they do not bend; they break" (*Patlabor* example, same source); skull is less pliable than jaw/cheek; and "no matter how much an object squashes and stretches, its volume never changes." So the principle is not "deform everything equally" — it is **"deform exactly as much as what the thing IS permits, and no less."** A water drop permits — demands — near-maximum deformation. A shell permits almost none in the shell, and therefore demands it in legs, head retraction, and whole-body recoil instead. Thomas & Johnston's own teaching image is the half-filled flour sack drawn in different shapes/positions to suggest different attitudes [same source] — a faceless sack that *acts purely by form change*. Student flour-sack exercises worldwide still use it precisely to prove emotion/personality can be carried "without a face" via body language, weight and gesture [Behance/Ulster exercises, index].
- **Acting is whole-body or it is ambiguous.** Thomas & Johnston (quoted in *Illusion of Life* excerpts, index: https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/0786860707/ref=pfb_spv01_b5ce88d40005097cb58a9c1669e9ec04): the actor/animator "speaks with his whole body in both attitude and movement"; to show sadness "we droop the shoulders, slump the body, drop the head, add a long face, and drag the feet." Note the order: four body cues, one face cue. A face-only sad cue is, in their own analysis, under-determined — it also reads tired/discouraged/listless.
- **Anticipation is a body counter-move:** crouch before jump, draw back before kick/throw [Adobe; Williams summaries, index]. On a rigid torso with no crouch drawing/deformation, anticipation cannot be shown at all — actions then start from nowhere, which reads as puppet twitching.
- **Follow-through / overlapping action:** parts continue after the main mass stops, and parts move at different rates [Adobe; Williams]. This is the principle that makes tails, ears, hair, cloth, bellies, droplets and flames mandatory movers — they are not decoration, they are where the audience reads weight and material. Williams' canonical example is a dog's tail still moving after the jump [index].
- **Secondary action supports, never replaces, the primary:** arm swing while walking, etc. [Adobe]. In the failed output the relationship is inverted: the only motion present (blink, mouth) *is* secondary action, with no primary body action underneath.
- **Pose-to-pose / strong key poses:** pose-to-pose = block key poses first, then in-between [Adobe]. TV practice adds the **moving hold**: get into a strong pose and *stay there without stopping* — "Keeping the character alive is what the old 2D animators used to call a 'moving hold'. It's also the basis of TV animation… If the character stops moving completely, the animation will die, and the character feels lifeless… at the very least keep the main motion control moving just a little bit — all our motion comes from the core" plus overshoot-and-settle into the pose [Animation Apprentice, verified live: https://animationapprentice.blogspot.com/2020/05/why-animated-characters-need-to-breathe.html]. A held drawing in pro TV is therefore a *redrawn / drifting* hold, never an identical bitmap repeated.
- **Arcs, timing, exaggeration are characterising:** timing = number of frames between poses sets weight/personality — heavy = more frames, slower recovery; light/liquid = fast attack, long wobbly settle. Same action timed differently *is* a different character. This is why a single global easing preset (added in the prior wiring pass) cannot create identity: identity lives in *per-character, per-material timing differences*.

**Movement vocabulary / signature.** Before posing a character, a studio animator studies "the character's personality, physical traits, and usually a library of movements for that character… such as walk cycles, postures, etc, and a character 'bible' that you can access at the studio" [Animation Mentor Tips & Tricks, via index: https://animapp.tw/blog/production/animation-and-rigging/120-am-tt-how-to-put-pose-it-w.html]. That is the institutional form of movement identity: it is *documented, banked, and reused*, not improvised per shot. Pokémon makes the same point by species: in the anime "even if the move is the same, each Pokémon uses different movements" [Namu wiki summary of anime practice, index], and key animator Masaaki Iwane's episodes are singled out precisely because "the Pokémon will move more dynamically than usual" under one animator doing whole-episode key animation [Kanzenshuu Animage interview translation, index: https://www.kanzenshuu.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=42117].

### 2. The VIDEO-GAME FLOOR (addendum) — quantified

This bar matters because games solved *exactly this pipeline's problem* — hundreds of distinct creatures, tiny budgets per creature, part-based art — and still cleared it.

**2a. Per-species continuous idle — Pokémon games**

- *Pokémon Crystal* (2000) was "the first game to include animated sprites for Pokémon" (front sprites; multi-frame, more than two frames — before it, animations were entry-only flourishes) [Bulbapedia, verified live/index: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/?title=Pok%C3%A9mon_Crystal_Version&oldid=3453863]. Emerald reintroduced animated sprites, after which it "became standard"; Platinum fully animated back sprites and Trainers [same source].
- *Pokémon Black and White* (2010): **"sprites remain animated throughout the battle"** [Bulbapedia Generation V / History, index]; *Black 2 / White 2*: "Just like in Pokémon Black and White, the Pokémon sprites are fully animated and move throughout battles… every Trainer in the game has battle animations, as well as more frames" and the page opened live confirms "Every Trainer has their own fully animated sprite" [Bulbapedia BW2, verified live: https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Black_and_White_Versions_2].
- *How* BW does it — the single most relevant production fact in this report: "In an extremely surprising move, the developers decided to implement **idle animations for all Pokémon, played continuously during battles**… Black and White solve the problem ingeniously by **creating frames for individual body parts as necessary and then making the animations themselves into stretch/skew rules applied to these individual body parts**. This allows the Pokémon to look dynamic and move reasonably smoothly without the crazy effort… required to create equivalent animations frame-by-frame for every single Pokémon" [Dragonfly Cave, verified live: http://www.dragonflycave.com/unova/bw-changes/].
- *Anti-monotony design:* BW idles are **not one simple loop**: "giving each Pokémon a couple of different actions such that the normal loop is broken up by the secondary action every now and then… it mostly succeeds in just making them look alive" [same source]. Quantify as a floor rule: **≥2 actions per idle (base loop + intermittent secondary), loop length typically ~1–2 s, duty cycle 100% of battle time.**
- *State is shown in the body/idle, not in UI alone:* in BW, poison = throbbing purple tint, paralysis = yellow, burn = red, freeze = animation **stops** + blue, sleep = animation **slows** + eyes close, and "as a Pokémon's HP goes down, its animation will slow down to signify that it's weakened"; send-out has a **weight-dependent tremor and thud, heavier = bigger thud + dust, flying/levitating = none** [Dragonfly Cave, verified live]. That is movement identity as a system: material, weight and condition modulate the *same* idle continuously.
- *Implementation form worth copying:* in the Crystal disassembly format each species carries two separate scripts — `anim.asm` (entry/main sequence, e.g. 5 frames with per-frame durations 08/08/08/24/08…) and **`anim_idle.asm`** (a short 2-frame idle played in some contexts) [pret/pokecrystal wiki, index: https://github-wiki-see.page/m/pret/pokecrystal/wiki/Add-a-new-Pok%C3%A9mon]. Even in 2000 the data model separated *event animation* from *idle animation* per species. The cue-sheet pipeline under diagnosis has only the first file.
- 3D era: *X and Y* — "Each Pokémon has different animations while idle and after they attack and receive damage" [Nintendo World Report hands-on, index: http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/35215/pokemon-x-and-pokemon-y-nintendo-3ds]; a transcript analysis notes the categorised set every Pokémon carries: **idle, hurt, faint, status, physical, special** (+ run for some physical moves) — a manageable, finite clip set per species, reused across hundreds of moves [index]. *Mystery Dungeon*: "every species… has one [idle]: sit and 'twitch', bark at nothing, splash around in the air, or… hop… thrust" — i.e. idle *verb chosen from body plan* (a fish splashes in air; a quadruped barks) [TV Tropes Idle Animation, index — weaker source, used only for the verb-list illustration].

**2b. Living backgrounds — games never leave the world dead, and never blank it**

- Pokémon's own engine has had **animated tileset functions as a named system** since Gen II: `_AnimateTileset` cycles, per tileset, functions including `AnimateWaterTile` (swap water tile to next frame), `AnimateFlowerTile` (cycle flower graphics), `AnimateWaterPalette` (shift palette to simulate flowing water *without changing tile data* — the cheapest possible ambient motion) and a flickering cave-entrance palette [PokeOctober/DeepWiki analysis of pokecrystal engine, index: https://deepwiki.com/pokeachromicdevs/pokeoctober/5.2-tilesets-and-visual-environment]. Floor rule quantifiable from this: **every water/flower/fire tile on screen advances frames on a fixed cadence (every few engine frames) for 100% of the scene; ambient motion costs palette swaps, not redraws.**
- General game practice visible in *X/Y* reviews: "The trees blow in the wind, the water's waves and ripples could be mistaken as real… idle animations from characters such as stretching and yawning, splashing through puddles" while the player simply sits on a bench watching NPCs pass [Rotoscopers X/Y review, index: https://www.rotoscopers.com/2013/10/18/video-game-review-pokemon-x-y/]. Background NPCs in games universally ship with idle + walk loops (that is the *definition* of an NPC sprite pack) — a background character with no loop is a bug, not a style.
- **The white-blink point:** no shipped game treats "background disappears to white for frames mid-scene" as acceptable; a missing background is a loading/render bug. In animation terms it is a missing-cel / missing-paint error. It must therefore be gated as a *hard fail* (see §6, Gate G0), not a quality preference. Owner statement of the defect is taken as ground truth; no external source is needed to establish that a blank frame is below both bars — BW's palette-cycled water shows even a *zero-redraw* background is expected to keep moving and, first, to *exist* on every frame.

**Position statement:** the pipeline today fails the game floor on all three floor criteria — (i) continuous per-character idle: absent; (ii) body-part motion beyond face: absent; (iii) continuous background presence + ambient cycle: absent (white-outs). It does not merely score low; it lacks the *categories*.

### 3. Liquid and soft-body characters specifically (+ flame, smoke/ghost, plant)

**Liquid / slime / blob / water-drop — the motion vocabulary**

Derived from §1 principles applied to a viscoelastic material, and confirmed by how the canonical slime characters are built:

1. **Locomotion is deformation, not translation.** A blob has no legs to cycle; it moves by *squash → stretch in travel direction → squash on landing → wobble-settle*. The bouncing-ball exercise *is* the character's walk cycle: stretch while falling/moving, squash on contact (volume out sideways), recover through 2–4 diminishing wobbles. Rimuru in slime form (*That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime*, Eight Bit, 2018– ) is the mass-audience reference: a blue, translucent, faceless-in-slime-form blob whose acting is carried by hopping/squashing, stretching to engulf (Predator), and splitting/dripping — the character literally has no arms/face in that form for long stretches, so the show *must* act with form [series premise, Wikipedia index: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Time_I_Got_Reincarnated_as_a_Slime_(TV_series)]. The owner's water drop is in exactly Rimuru's position — which is why face-only is fatal for him and merely weak for a humanoid.
2. **Idle = perpetual wobble/breathing pulse.** Jelly/slime physics in plain terms: a viscoelastic body "temporarily deforms and then slowly springs back, creating that characteristic jelly jiggle"; slime "stretch it slowly, flows; pull quickly, snaps" [WonderLearning summary, index — popular source, physics description only]. As an idle: a 12–24-frame loop of ±3–8% width/height anti-phase scaling (width up when height down — volume), plus a surface highlight that slides. That one loop, tuned soft, *is* "water" to a viewer.
3. **Reaction timing is the signature:** fast squash (2–3 frames in), slow recovery (8–16 frames) with overshoot past rest shape and one smaller undershoot — a damped oscillation. Every stimulus (landing, poke, loud sound, emotion spike) re-triggers it with amplitude ∝ stimulus. Emotion is amplitude/frequency: happy = bouncy high-frequency small hops; sad = slow sag, form droops and *stays* drooped (recovery time → ∞); scared = rapid tremble (1-frame ±1–2% jitter).
4. **Surface events:** drips (a bulge forms at the bottom/side, necks, detaches, falls with stretch, main body recoils up slightly), ripples after any impact (a ring/bulge travelling across the form over ~6–10 frames), merging/splitting for interactions. On a TV budget these are **substitution drawings**: 3–5 pre-drawn body states (rest / squashed / stretched / drip-bulge / flat-sad) swapped like mouth shapes already are — the pipeline already owns this mechanism for faces; it simply never built the body set.
5. **TV-budget method:** 8–12-frame wobble cycle on 2s (i.e. 4–6 drawings), reused as (a) idle, (b) settle after every move, (c) locomotion base with a positional move added; substitution drawings for extremes; smear drawing (elongated drop) for fast moves, held 1–2 frames only.

**Flame (and flame-tailed) characters**

- Anime FX treats fire as a *shape cycle, never a still shape*: Kanada-style FX vocabulary lists fire/water/smoke/energy as stylised, angular, constantly re-drawn effects [anime production vocab, index]; Kanada's "liquid fire" goes further — motion created "only through the interplay of colours," outlines disappearing [Animetudes, index: http://animetudes.com/2021/07/21/yoshinori-kanada-and-the-nature-of-animation/]. Practical vocabulary: 6–10-frame flicker cycle on 1s/2s, tip licking side-to-side, height varying ±20–40%, occasional detached ember/spark rising and fading, shape vocabulary = teardrop tongues, never symmetrical for two consecutive frames.
- The tail flame is a **mood/health meter by canon**: Charmander's tail flame "burning brightly when the Pokémon is strong, weakly when it is exhausted, wavering when it is happy, and blazing when it is enraged" [Bulbapedia Charmander, index: http://m.bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Charmander_(Pok%C3%A9mon)]. So a flame-tailed cat whose flame is a static decal is missing his main acting instrument: flame *size/flicker-rate/colour* must be a continuous track driven by emotion/state, exactly as BW drives idle speed by HP (§2a).
- Cat body underneath: quadruped/biped cat locomotion = spine flex (gather/extend), tail (the non-flame part) as follow-through chain lagging 2–4 frames, ear twitches as intermittent secondary (the BW anti-monotony trick).

**Smoke / ghost characters**

- Vocabulary: rise, expand, dissipate, re-form. Edges are the animation: outline boils/dissolves (2–3 alternating ragged outlines = "boiling line"), bottom edge trails into wisps that detach and fade, body drifts (never locks to ground — a 6–12 px slow sine drift + slight rotation), passing through states = stretch thin → break → re-coalesce. Opacity is a motion parameter (fade to 60–80% on idle exhale, back to full on intent). Stillness is impossible for smoke: a frozen smoke shape reads as a grey rock. Cycle: 12–16-frame drift/wisp loop + boil.

**Plant characters**

- Vocabulary: sway from the *base* (root fixed, tip amplitude largest — an inverted pendulum / curve-deformer motion), leaves/petals as overlapping-action chains each lagging the stem by 2–6 frames in sequence, phototropism (slow lean toward light/speaker — acting!), wilting (whole-form droop over 12–24 frames, recovery slower), bloom/open-close as anticipation + overshoot, root/leg shuffle if mobile. A plant's "breathing" is a 24–48-frame ultra-slow sway; its "flinch" is leaves folding. If a plant character stands bolt upright and rigid, he reads as a plastic prop — the one thing a plant never is outdoors.

**Shelled biped (contrast case)**

- The shell is the Patlabor case: rigid, *correctly* undeforming. Identity must come from everywhere else: heavy timing (slow anticipation, weighty plant of feet, body pitch/roll into steps, head-and-limbs recoil on landing while shell stays true), shell as a *separate lagging mass* (it settles 2–3 frames after the body stops — follow-through of a carried weight), retract/pop-out substitution states (head/limbs in vs out — a turtle's signature gag and emotion), and knock response = wobble *of the whole body on its feet* (rotation oscillation), not shell deformation. This proves the system is material-honest, not "wobble everything."

### 4. Why face-only reads as preschool — limited animation, done badly vs done well

**History.** Limited animation was an economic invention: full *Tom & Jerry* animation ≈ 20,000–30,000 drawings for a short (Barbera: ~5,000 drawings per episode at "a drawing for every other frame," and limited cut that to "only two or three drawings per foot of film"); Hanna-Barbera TV needed 30 minutes a week on half the budget of one short, so "planned" animation used **2,000–3,000 drawings** [Seibert/Burnett, verified live: https://fredseibert.com/post/71257648/limited-animationunlimited-imagination; Cartoon Research, index]. Its named devices are, verbatim, the defect under diagnosis: reusable backgrounds, **"characters with 'muzzles' so only their mouths had to be animated; characters that blinked a lot, to enhance the illusion of motion"** [Seibert, verified live]. Face-only on a held body is not a new amateur mistake — it is the *documented 1957 cost-cutting device itself*, which is why audiences instantly file it as cheap/preschool: they have 70 years of training in reading it that way.

**But good limited animation never stopped there.** The compensations, from the sources:

- Hanna-Barbera themselves compensated with writing, voice, sound FX, camera shakes/truck-ins/quick cuts — Barbera: "we had to use every trick to put over the feeling of motion… camera shakes, truck-ins, dissolves, quick cuts" and a major SFX library [H-B roundtable, index: https://fredseibert.com/post/69009471/hanna-barbera-sound-effects-roundtable-1995-2]. Criticism that H-B shows were "simplified and… limited to the point of stiffness" is recorded as the *failure mode* of the same system [Lambiek, index].
- TV anime's version: hold on 3s/2s, spend on **strong key poses, impact frames and sakuga bursts**; smear frames (one distorted drawing, 1–2 frames) sell speed for almost no drawings; bank/cycle reuse; FX cycles keep frames alive even in dialogue shots [anime production sources, index, §1/§3]. Anime critics name the *dead* version exactly: "closeup shots with dialogue and the main animation is the mouth moving between 2 to 4 different poses with no secondary action and very little any other movement" is called "an animation cheat… not at all a decision" [Grinning Tiger sakuga essay, index]. Note: that sentence describes the pipeline's output shot-for-shot.
- The moving hold (§1) is the bridge: TV animators "stay inside a pose for as long as possible because of the sheer volume of work," but the hold *moves* — breath, drift, settle [Animation Apprentice, verified live]. **Preschool/dead limited = hold + face flap. Professional limited = moving hold + strong pose changes + secondary chains + timing contrast.** Same drawing count, different result.
- **Modern puppet-rig TV (the structural difference):** a Toon Boom Harmony cut-out rig is not a stack of rigid bitmaps. Official docs: deformers "can substantially increase the range of movements… a cut-out character rig is able to perform without having to draw new poses… animated in a way that likens the fluidity and flexibility of traditional animation"; Bone for limbs/torso articulation, **Curve for flexible parts (hair, mouths, torsos, rubber-hose limbs), Envelope for fluid shapes (hair, cloaks, shoulders), Free Form to stretch/squash all artwork between points** [Toon Boom Harmony 25 docs, verified live: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-25/premium/getting-started/deformation.html]. Plus **drawing substitutions** on every part (swap drawings mid-animation — hands, body states, smears), pose libraries, and peg hierarchies keyed pose-to-pose with the deformers keyed *with* the pegs [Toon Boom cut-out docs, index: https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-20/advanced/getting-started/cut-out.html]. A naive part pack lacks all three layers: no deformer (silhouette frozen), no body substitutions (states frozen), no pose library (acting frozen). It retains only the fourth layer — face substitutions — which is precisely the layer H-B invented as a *substitute* for animation.

### 5. Diagnosis — structural causes, cause by cause

Each cause is stated as a mechanism: remove the cause and the symptom becomes impossible; leave it and no amount of polish fixes the symptom.

| # | Cause | Mechanism → symptom |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Rig has no whole-form deformation channel.** Body/head are rigid transforms (position/rotation/scale of a bitmap). | Squash & stretch, wobble, droop, swell are *unrepresentable*, not merely uncued. Uniform scale is the only "deformation" and it preserves shape — a scaled drop is a bigger statue. This is the root cause for the water drop specifically. |
| D2 | **Substitution sets exist only for the face.** Mouth shapes, eye/blink/look sets were authored; no body-state set (squash, stretch, droop, drip, recoil, retract…) exists for any character. | The swap mechanism — the cheapest animation device the pipeline owns — can only ever swap face parts. Authoring effort followed the asset list, so the face got 10+ drawings and the body got 1. |
| D3 | **Cue sheet is event-based, with no continuous tracks.** Cues fire at timestamps (say line, blink, look, camera move); between cues the system's default is *hold last state identically*. | Time between events — typically 80–95% of runtime — is by construction dead. Games/TV invert the default: idle/material tracks run continuously and events *interrupt/modulate* them (BW: freeze stops the idle, sleep slows it — the idle is the thing being modulated). |
| D4 | **No per-character movement specification exists.** No document states material, mass, idle, locomotion, signatures, or reaction timing for any character. | Even a willing animator/cue-author has nothing to implement: "water drop" appears in the design as a *drawing*, never as a *behaviour*. Studios prevent exactly this with the character bible + movement library (§1). Absence of spec = every character inherits the default humanoid-face behaviour = identity erased by default. |
| D5 | **Cues are written for plot events, not for physics/acting beats.** No cue types for anticipation, follow-through, settle, secondary chain, or state change (tired/excited/wet…). | The action grammar has no verbs for the principles. A jump cue without a preceding crouch cue-type and a following settle cue-type will always read weightless, however eased. |
| D6 | **No banked cycles.** Breathing/wobble/sway/flicker loops were never authored as reusable assets per character. | Continuous motion, if attempted per shot, costs per shot — so it is rationally skipped per shot. Games solved this by banking *one* idle per species and amortising it over every appearance (BW's part-rules per species). One-time cost, infinite reuse — the economics point the other way once banked. |
| D7 | **Secondary/appendage parts are not chained.** Tails, ears, leaves, flame, wisps, shells are either fused into the rigid body drawing or static attachments, with no lag/phase parameters. | Follow-through/overlap — the cheapest liveliness signal — cannot occur. A flame tail fused into a body bitmap can never flicker; it must be a separate part with its own cycle (D2/D6 again). |
| D8 | **Background is a single static plate with no ambient track and no presence guarantee.** No animated-tile/palette-cycle equivalent (water, foliage, light), and a compositing path that can emit frames with the background missing (white). | Fails the game floor twice: world reads dead even when characters move, and white-out frames are hard render errors leaking to output because no gate checks "background present on every frame." |
| D9 | **Review has no motion metric.** Acceptance is by eye, shot by shot, with no per-character checklist or measured threshold. | Face-only passes review because *something* moves in every shot (the mouth). §6's gates exist to make "the body didn't move" a measurable fail, not a taste argument. |
| D10 | **(Contributing, from prior pass) Polish was layered on the wrong level.** Timing sheets, easing, camera, grade were added to a system lacking D1–D6. | Easing a rigid translation makes a statue glide smoothly; camera moves over a dead hold are the exact anime "cheat" shot (§4). Polish amplified the readability of the defect. The fix order in §7 therefore puts rig/spec/cycles *before* any further polish. |

### 6. Measurable gates — "is the character actually being animated, not just his face?"

No published standard gives a ready-made pass mark for this exact question; the defensible method below builds the metric from a published measurement technique and sets thresholds *relative to the two bars* (calibrate: measure a BW battle clip and a TV dialogue clip with the same code, set the floor gate at the game value, the target gate at the TV value). Tool: **OpenCV** (Apache 2.0, index: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenCV) + Python/NumPy — free, scriptable, already the standard for frame-difference motion measurement: the peer-reviewed Motion Tracker method computes, per frame, a binary motion silhouette from frame differencing and reports "**the proportion of pixels in Ft that have been displaced, i.e. the index of the amount of movement in the frame**" [PLOS ONE, index: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0130293]; dense flow via `calcOpticalFlowFarneback` gives per-region magnitude [OpenCV docs, index].

Compute, per character per shot, from the character's alpha mask (the pipeline knows it exactly — no segmentation guesswork):

- **G0 — Background presence (HARD FAIL, game floor).** 0 frames per scene where background region mean ≈ white/blank or background layer alpha/coverage < 99% of frame, *except* a whitelisted intentional flash of ≤2 frames with a cue ID. Also: background ambient motion > 0 in every 2-s window where an ambient element (water/foliage/fire/light) is on screen (palette/tile cycle counts).
- **G1 — Body-motion coverage (game floor).** In every rolling 1-s window, ≥60% of frames must show body-region (character mask *minus* face sub-mask) pixel change above noise. Face-only output scores ≈0–10% (blinks) — it cannot pass by accident. BW idles score ~100% (continuous).
- **G2 — Silhouette deformation (TV target, the water test).** Per second, silhouette shape must actually change, not merely translate: measure mask area, width/height ratio, and contour (e.g. IoU of mask vs. mask 6 frames earlier after centroid alignment). Gate: aligned-IoU < 0.97 for at least 30% of frames in any action/idle second for soft characters (liquid/ghost/plant-tip), and width/height ratio range ≥5% per 2 s for liquid idle (his wobble *is* the gate). Shelled character exempt on contour, gated instead on whole-body rotation/position oscillation after impacts.
- **G3 — Dead-hold limit (TV floor).** No run of >12 consecutive frames (0.5 s at 24 fps) with body-region change = 0 while the character is on screen and awake/alive, except a whitelisted dramatic freeze ≤24 frames. This is the moving-hold rule made numeric.
- **G4 — Per-region coverage.** Divide mask into regions (body core, appendages [tail/ears/leaves/flame/wisps], face). Per scene, each region must move in ≥40% of the character's shots (appendages via their cycles count automatically once D7 is fixed). Prevents "body moves but flame never does" partial fixes.
- **G5 — Anticipation/settle presence (checklist + spot metric).** For every cued major action (jump, throw, hit, entrance): a counter-pose or squash ≥2 frames before, and a settle (overshoot + ≥4 frames decaying motion) after — verified from the cue sheet mechanically (cue types must exist, §7) and sampled visually at review.
- **G6 — Identity checklist (per character, per episode, human-signed).** Spec (§8) lists 3–5 signature motions; ≥1 must appear per appearance, idle must match spec verb (hop/wobble/sway/flicker/drift — not generic bob), and flame/smoke/plant/liquid state tracks must respond to at least one story state change (emotion/health/weather) per episode. Checklist fail = shot returned, same as a model-sheet violation in a studio.
- **Calibration note (honesty):** thresholds above are *engineering starting values derived from the principles and the BW/TV reference behaviour*, not published constants — no source publishes "IoU < 0.97" as an industry standard, and the report does not pretend otherwise. The defensible procedure is: run G1–G3 on (a) current output, (b) a BW battle capture, (c) a TV dialogue scene; publish the three numbers in the pipeline docs; set floor = (b), target = (c). The current output's G1/G2 ≈ 0 makes the diagnosis independent of exact threshold choice.

### 8. Per-character movement-spec templates

Common fields for every character (the character-bible page): `material / mass & weight class / volume rule / rest shape / idle (cycle, frames, fps, amplitude) / breathing equivalent / locomotion (verb, cycle, ground contact) / anticipation pose / settle behaviour (frames, overshoot %) / secondary chains (part, lag frames, spring params) / signature motions (3–5, with triggers) / emotion map (happy/sad/angry/scared/sleep → body change, not face change) / state map (tired/hurt/wet/hot/cold → idle speed/amplitude/shape) / forbidden motions (what this body must NEVER do) / substitution set list / smear drawing / sound-linked motions`. Filled per character:

#### 8.1 Liquid / water-drop body
- **Material/mass:** water in a surface-tension skin; light-medium; volume strictly preserved (wider ⇔ shorter).
- **Idle:** continuous wobble-breathing: 16-frame loop on 2s, width +6% / height −5% anti-phase, highlight slides 2–3 px; every 3rd–4th loop a secondary action (small hop or side bulge) breaks the loop (BW rule).
- **Locomotion:** hop/slide by deformation — squash 3f → stretch toward travel 2–3f → land squash 3f → wobble-settle 10–14f diminishing. No sliding at constant shape (that is a statue on a conveyor).
- **Anticipation/settle:** anticipation = squash-down; settle = damped oscillation, overshoot +4%, two visible cycles.
- **Secondary/surface:** drips (bulge→neck→detach→recoil) after big squashes and when sad/nervous; ripple ring after impacts; spring params: low stiffness, low damping.
- **Signatures:** (1) victory double-bounce with drip flicked off; (2) puddle-flop (goes almost flat, 12f, then re-inflates) for exhaustion/defeat; (3) stretch-peek (tall thin, to look over things); (4) shiver-tremble when cold/scared.
- **Emotion map:** happy = bouncier, higher frequency; sad = sag −15% height, slow/no recovery, drip rate up; angry = rapid small-amplitude vibration + surface boil; scared = tremble + shrink.
- **Forbidden:** rigid translation; shape-identical frames >12f; walking with legs (he has none); deformation that changes volume.
- **Substitutions:** rest, squash-wide, stretch-tall, flat-puddle, drip-bulge, droop-sad, smear-elongated.

#### 8.2 Flame-tailed cat body
- **Material/mass:** mammalian quadruped/biped cat, light, agile; **tail flame = separate FX part, never fused into body art.**
- **Idle:** cat breathing (flank/chest ±2%, 24f loop) + tail-base sway with flame flicker cycle 8f on 1s/2s (height ±25%, tip lick, non-symmetrical consecutive frames) + intermittent ear twitch / tail-tip secondary every few loops.
- **Locomotion:** spine gather–extend walk/trot cycle; run = extended stretch poses + smear on legs for fastest beat; landing = body squash *in the legs/spine only* (cat-soft), flame lags/streaks backward then recovers (follow-through).
- **Flame track (continuous, state-driven):** size/flicker-rate/colour = health+mood meter (Charmander canon, §3): strong/bright = healthy, low/slow = exhausted, wavering = happy, tall/blazing = enraged, sputtering + steam wisps = wet/rain, near-out = danger (story-critical, never decorative-off).
- **Signatures:** (1) startle-arch (back arches, flame flares, 3f in); (2) pounce crouch–wiggle anticipation (hindquarters wiggle 6–8f before leap); (3) content curl-up sleep with flame dimmed to ember pulse; (4) flame-lick groom.
- **Emotion map:** body posture per Thomas & Johnston (droop vs arch) + flame params above; face is third, not first.
- **Forbidden:** static flame in any frame; flame symmetrical/identical 2 frames running; rigid spine in locomotion.
- **Substitutions:** arch, crouch, stretch-run, curl-sleep, flinch; flame set: ember / normal / tall / sputter / smear-streak.

#### 8.3 Plant body
- **Material/mass:** rooted or pot-rooted stem body; flexible stem, heavy head/leaf mass at top (inverted pendulum).
- **Idle:** ultra-slow sway 32–48f loop, base fixed, tip amplitude 4–8 px, leaves lagging stem in sequence (2f per leaf outward); subtle growth-pulse (height +1–2%) as breathing equivalent.
- **Locomotion:** if mobile — root-shuffle / pot-hop with full sway counter-swing; if rooted — locomotion is *leaning* (reach toward target over 12–24f, overshoot, sway-settle). Never rigid slide.
- **Anticipation/settle:** anticipation = lean *away* + leaf fold; settle = sway oscillation decaying over 16–24f (plants settle slowest of the cast — timing contrast vs the drop).
- **Secondary:** leaf/petal chains (curve-deformer motion), bloom open/close, falling leaf/seed FX cycle occasionally.
- **Signatures:** (1) phototropism lean toward whoever speaks (acting while listening — his moving hold); (2) wilt (whole-form droop over 24f) for sad/tired/thirsty, recovery slower still, water cue → perk-up with leaf shiver; (3) bloom-flare for joy/surprise; (4) leaf-fold flinch.
- **State map:** thirst/light/weather modulate sway amplitude and droop baseline continuously.
- **Forbidden:** upright rigidity; tip moving before base (motion must originate at base/stem); fast humanoid gestures.
- **Substitutions:** wilt, bloom-open, bloom-closed, lean-L, lean-R, flinch-fold.

#### 8.4 Smoke / ghost body
- **Material/mass:** gas; no fixed volume, no ground contact; edges unstable by definition.
- **Idle:** perpetual drift (slow sine float 6–12 px, slight rotation ±2°), bottom wisps detach and fade on a 12–16f cycle, outline boil (2–3 ragged variants alternating), opacity breathing 100%→80%→100% over ~32f.
- **Locomotion:** stretch thin in travel direction → flow (position move with trailing wisps) → re-coalesce with overshoot swell; passing obstacles = split around and rejoin (substitution pair).
- **Anticipation/settle:** anticipation = compress/darken (density up, size down); settle = expansion puffs ×2 decaying.
- **Secondary:** wisp chain (3–5 trailing elements, increasing lag/fade), occasional detached puff that rises and dissipates (his "secondary action breaks the loop").
- **Signatures:** (1) scare-swell (sudden ×1.6 expansion + boil speed-up, 4f); (2) thin-slip (goes ribbon-thin to pass/peek); (3) dissipate-exit / re-form-entrance (never walk off rigid); (4) sad-rain-down (drifts downward, edges raining off, opacity falling).
- **Emotion map:** density/opacity/height carry emotion (high+light = buoyant, low+dense+dark = gloomy); face optional in several states.
- **Forbidden:** hard unchanging outline >8f; ground-locked walking; cast-shadow solidity (shadow, if any, soft and lagging); appearing/disappearing by white flash.
- **Substitutions:** normal, swell, ribbon-thin, compressed, dissipating (3-stage), re-forming.

#### 8.5 Shelled biped body
- **Material/mass:** rigid shell (NO shell deformation — the honest exception), fleshy limbs/head, heavy for his size; total mass reads in timing.
- **Idle:** breathing in the *exposed* flesh (neck/limb sway ±1–2%), weight shifts foot-to-foot every few seconds (whole-body rock ±1.5°, shell included as rigid mass), head look-arounds as intermittent secondary; shell settles with a 2–3f lag after each shift (carried-weight follow-through).
- **Locomotion:** lumber — slow anticipation (lean + shell pitch), deliberate steps with body pitch/roll, foot-plant squash *in the legs*, head bob counter-phase; fastest possible move is still slower than every other cast member's normal (timing contrast is his identity).
- **Anticipation/settle:** anticipation = retract slightly + lean; settle = whole-body rotational wobble on the feet (2 cycles, shell true) + shell knock-shiver if impacted.
- **Signatures:** (1) full retract (head/limbs in, shell only, 3f in — his flinch/hide) and pop-out (overshoot) return; (2) shell-knock reaction (rock + wobble, offended posture); (3) tip-over and rock-back-up struggle (or fail → stuck, a gag state); (4) proud puff (chest/head high, shell gleam — posture, not deformation).
- **Emotion map:** posture + retraction depth + step tempo; sad = low head, slow drag steps (Thomas & Johnston's drag-feet, literally).
- **Forbidden:** shell squash/stretch in any frame (breaks material honesty and the cast's contrast); bouncy/light timing; sliding without steps.
- **Substitutions:** retracted, pop-out, tip-over, rock-L, rock-R, proud, droop.
