# Video of a Specific Flat 2D Cartoon Character with Exact Likeness — Free/Open Tools on Kaggle T4 (16GB)

*Research date: 2026-10-28. Flags: "verified live" = the primary page (repo / model card / paper / trainer docs) was read directly; "index" = fact comes from search-result text or a secondary source and was not confirmed on the primary page.*

## Summary

**Bottom line: do not try to make a big Wan model "hold" the character. Determinism beats conditioning.** On the evidence found, the highest exact-likeness route on a T4 is the lane the pipeline already owns — canon art, puppet/cue-sheet drawing, matting, compositing — augmented with (1) a **purpose-built cartoon interpolator, ToonCrafter** (Apache-2.0), which inbetweens *two pieces of canon art* rather than inventing frames, and (2) a **still-image character LoRA on SDXL/Illustrious** to generate *new* locked keyframes that are then animated with short, heavily constrained video passes (Wan 2.1 VACE 1.3B — already in the pipeline — with reference image + canny/pose control, short clips, frame-1 likeness gating). A **Wan 2.1 1.3B character LoRA trained with musubi-tuner on stills + short clips** is the best *pure-diffusion* option and is T4-feasible in VRAM; **Phantom-Wan-1.3B** is the best no-training reference-conditioning comparator. Neither has *any* published evidence on flat Western cel cartoon — all cartoon-adjacent evidence is anime. Large routes (Wan 2.2 14B training, Wan-Animate 14B, S2V, SkyReels-A2, HunyuanVideo) are effectively out of T4 reach or photoreal-only.

**The hardware fact that changes several plans: the T4 is a Turing GPU.** Tensor cores are optimized for FP16/INT8/INT4 (65 TFLOPS FP16 mixed precision, 16GB GDDR6); **BF16 arrived with Ampere and FP8 with Hopper**, so the T4 has no native BF16 or FP8 acceleration (NVIDIA T4 datasheet, `https://bostondownloads.blob.core.windows.net/downloads/6/5/3/65397b64-cdff-4ef3-a73b-49db471fe966/nvidia-t4-datasheet.pdf`, index; tensor-core generation table, `https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/tensor-cores-explained-ai-training`, index; "T4… No bf16 support", `https://carpentries-incubator.github.io/ML_with_AWS_SageMaker/instructor/instances-for-ML.html`, index). Most Wan training/inference recipes (musubi-tuner `--fp8_base`, ai-toolkit) were developed on RTX 3090/4090/A100 and assume FP8/BF16 paths; on a T4 those paths will be slow, emulated, or fail. Favour FP16-native components: Wan 2.1 1.3B, CogVideoX-2B (FP16-trained), SDXL, ToonCrafter FP16 pruned, LTX-Video 2B. Also note 2×T4 does not pool VRAM in most trainers/ComfyUI setups; multi-GPU paths in the Wan repos are documented for 8-GPU runs.

## Findings

### 1. Per-character LoRA training on the video model (Wan 2.1/2.2 1.3B, 14B, 5B TI2V)

**musubi-tuner (kohya-ss) is the reference trainer, and its docs were read in full** (`https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/blob/main/docs/wan.md`, verified live, crawled 2026-10-28):

- Supported Wan tasks: Wan2.1 `t2v-1.3B, t2v-14B, i2v-14B, t2i-14B` plus Fun-Control `-FC` variants; **Wan2.2 support is experimental and covers only the 14B models** (`t2v-A14B, i2v-A14B`). **There is no Wan2.2 5B TI2V task in musubi-tuner** — so the 5B, often suggested as the T4-friendly Wan 2.2, has no musubi training path; ai-toolkit does list Wan2.2-TI2V-5B among its supported video models (repo README, `https://github.com/ostris/ai-toolkit`, verified live; licence MIT, verified on the repo page).
- With FP8 + block swap, musubi claims inference of 720×1280×81-frame video in 24GB VRAM and **training with 720×1280 images in 24GB VRAM**. For VRAM below ~16GB it recommends `--fp8_t5` (cache/offload the text encoder). A typical 1.3B command uses `--task t2v-1.3B --fp8_base --optimizer_type adamw8bit --learning_rate 2e-4 --gradient_checkpointing --network_dim 32 --timestep_sampling shift --discrete_flow_shift 3.0`. Caveat per §Summary: `--fp8_base` has no native acceleration on Turing T4; expect the 1.3B to *fit* in 16GB but train slowly in practice. This specific FP8-on-Turing behaviour could not be verified from any source (see Could not verify).
- Wan2.2 dual-expert training requires both `--dit` (low-noise) and `--dit_high_noise` with a `--timestep_boundary` (default 0.9 I2V / 0.875 T2V); `--offload_inactive_dit` is incompatible with `--blocks_to_swap` and on Windows needs ~42GB shared VRAM / ~96GB RAM for both models — i.e. 14B LoRA training is a big-hardware activity, not a T4 one.
- Trained LoRAs "seemed to be compatible with ComfyUI (may depend on nodes)" — acceptable for the pipeline's ComfyUI base.

**Training data: stills-only is a supported, documented mode.** The musubi dataset-config doc (`https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/blob/main/docs/dataset_config.md`, verified live) shows image datasets and video datasets can be mixed in one TOML; image datasets use a caption `.txt` per image; video targets must be N×4+1 frames for Wan/HunyuanVideo, with 16fps source recommended for Wan2.1. So a canon-art corpus of stills (plus any existing puppet-lane clips) is a legal training set. The one-frame training doc (`https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/blob/main/docs/wan_1f.md`, verified live) is "highly experimental" and uses I2V-14B / FLF2V-14B only — not a T4 route.

**Anime character LoRA evidence — the strongest cartoon-specific evidence that exists (all index, Civitai model pages):**

- "Yuzuki Roa" (Nijisanji anime character), Wan2.1 & 2.2 14B T2V LoRA: "Trained: musubi-tuner, **30 images**, Caption: adjusted caption from Qwen2.5-VL-3B, lr 2e-4, flow_shift 3.0, epoch 200, train_step 6000, lora_strength 0.8" — `https://civitai.com/models/1593980/yuzuki-roa-or-wan21-and-22-14b-t2v` (index).
- "Igarashi Rika", same recipe (30 images, 6000 steps); its author notes setting `--timestep_boundary 885` in training "increased character reproducibility" for the Wan2.2 version — `https://civitai.com/models/1522679/igarashi-rika-or-wan21-and-22-14b-t2v` (index).
- A retro-anime *style* LoRA for Wan2.2 T2V (Kawajiri) took ~36 hours (low-noise) + ~8 hours (high-noise) **on a 6000 Pro Blackwell** — `https://civitai.com/models/1833906/wan22-yoshiaki-kawajiri-retro-anime-style-t2v` (index). Extrapolating 14B training time to a T4 gives days; treat 14B training as out of scope.
- A community Wan2.1 LoRA training note advises: "If you have a single 24GB VRAM, use 1.3B T2V for training… in my tests it didn't even use half of the available memory" (<12GB, so it fits a T4 in VRAM), while 14B on 24GB needs ~36 blocks swapped and is "painfully slow — 5 to 10 times longer. Training just 16 images for 100 epochs can take days" — `https://civitai.com/articles/12980/wan21-lora-training-note` (index).

**ai-toolkit (ostris) low-VRAM claims are contested — flag honestly.** Its low-VRAM documentation (via DeepWiki, `https://deepwiki.com/ostris/ai-toolkit/6.3-advanced-training-features`, index) describes strategies to fit 40GB+ models "into 16GB or less" by trading compute. But user reports contradict the optimistic reading: a GitHub issue comment puts safe Wan2.2 usage at ~26–27GB VRAM in ai-toolkit at 1024/1280px, and an NVIDIA-forum user reports even an RTX 5090 (32GB) had to drop to Wan2.2 **5B at 512px** (768px OOM) for 109-frame training, at 6.57 s/it on the 5090 (`https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/ostris-ai-toolkit-on-dgx-spark/355277/12`, index). Civitai's hosted Wan training recipe page reports ~1–3 min per 100 steps / 20–60 min per 2000 steps for 2.1/2.2 (`https://developer.civitai.com/orchestration/recipes/training-wan`, index) — hosted hardware unspecified; order-of-magnitude only, not a T4 figure.

**Does a character LoRA beat reference conditioning on likeness?** No controlled study was found (see Could not verify). The indirect evidence is: (a) the anime LoRA community trains LoRAs precisely because reference conditioning alone does not reproduce a character; (b) reference methods' published subject-consistency scores are far from exact even on photoreal humans (§2). Treat "LoRA > reference conditioning for a *specific* character" as well-supported practitioner consensus, not a measured result.

### 2. Subject-reference conditioning (Phantom-Wan, VACE ref, Wan-Animate, SkyReels-A2)

**Phantom-Wan** (`https://github.com/Phantom-video/Phantom`, verified live; licence **Apache-2.0**, verified on repo page; paper arXiv:2502.11079, ICCV 2025). A unified single/multi-subject reference framework built on Wan2.1 T2V: reference images are injected into the DiT alongside text (cross-modal alignment trained on text–image–video triplets), up to **4 reference images**. Checkpoints **Phantom-Wan-1.3B and 14B** on HF (`bytedance-research/Phantom`); a single-GPU inference command at 832×480 is documented for the 1.3B. The repo's own framing is human-first: it "emphasizes subject consistency in **human** generation while enhancing ID-preserving video generation." The 14B examples do include stylized demos — Nezha/Ao Bing anime characters in a classroom, a "cartoon old man" in a "cartoon-style café" — so anime/cartoon is not absent, but there is **no flat Western cel-cartoon demo**, and **training code is still an unchecked TODO in the repo** (no way to fine-tune Phantom itself).

**VACE** (`https://github.com/ali-vilab/VACE`, verified live; licence **Apache-2.0**; paper arXiv:2503.07598, ICCV 2025). All-in-one reference-to-video / video-to-video / masked editing: inputs are organized into a Video Condition Unit (VCU) and injected via a Context Adapter; reference images are passed as `src_ref_images`. Model table on the repo: **Wan2.1-VACE-1.3B** (~81 frames × 480×832, Apache-2.0), **Wan2.1-VACE-14B** (~81 × 720×1280, Apache-2.0), and VACE-LTX-Video-0.9 under the more restrictive **RAIL-M** licence. The pipeline already runs VACE-1.3B; its reference-image path is the natural "reference + control" slot for canon art.

**Wan-Animate / Wan2.2-Animate-14B** (paper arXiv:2509.14055; `https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2`; HF card `https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-Animate-14B`, licence **Apache-2.0**, verified live). Two modes — animation and replacement. It enforces appearance via spatially aligned **skeleton signals** for body motion plus **implicit facial features extracted from source images** for expressions, and ships an auxiliary **Relighting LoRA** for the replacement mode — which *deliberately changes appearance lighting*, a direct hazard for two-tone cel shading. The official HF card/repo also warns: **"we do not recommend using LoRA models trained on Wan2.2" with Wan-Animate** — so the character-LoRA + Animate stack is officially discouraged. It is 14B-only; the repo gives a single-GPU command but no VRAM figure, so T4 use would rely on unverified ComfyUI FP8/GGUF offload builds. All demos are photoreal humans. Related: **Wan2.2-S2V-14B**'s official single-GPU command "can run on a GPU with at least 80GB VRAM" (Wan2.2 repo, verified live) — not a T4 model in official form.

**SkyReels-A2** (`https://github.com/SkyworkAI/SkyReels-A2`, verified live; paper arXiv:2504.02436). Elements-to-video: an image–text joint embedding injects multiple element references (characters, objects, backgrounds) into a Wan2.1-14B base. Only **A2-Wan2.1-14B-Preview** (~81×480×832) is actually released; 14B, 14B-Pro and Infinity are listed "To be released." The repo's own low-end path is to set `offload_switch=True` in `infer_MGPU.py` to run on an **RTX 4090 (24GB)** — above T4 class. **Licence could not be cleanly verified: GitHub shows "Other (NOASSERTION)"** on the repo page.

**How good is reference conditioning, measured?** A comparison table in the HuMo paper (arXiv:2509.08519, index) reports photoreal-human subject consistency: Phantom ID-Cur 0.649 / ID-Glink 0.674, CLIP-I 0.677, vs HunyuanCustom 0.309/0.335. Two lessons: even the best reference method is far from "exact" on its home turf (photoreal humans), and all of these metrics are face-embedding based, so they cannot even be computed meaningfully on a flat cartoon face. **Failure mode to expect with flat 2D art:** reference encoders trained on photoreal/anime distributions treat bold outlines and flat colour fields as noise to be "rendered" — consistent with the pipeline's observed failure (frame 1 re-imagined as a smooth 3D toy) under plain I2V.

### 3. Two-stage approaches (still-image locked keyframes + FLF2V / VACE / FramePack)

This is a documented, practised route, and for cartoon it has a purpose-built model:

**ToonCrafter** (`https://github.com/Doubiiu/ToonCrafter`, verified live; licence **Apache-2.0**; SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) is the only model found that was built for exactly this job: it **interpolates two cartoon images**, with sparse sketch guidance and reference-based sketch colourisation. Hard limits: **max 16 frames at 512×320**. The official checkpoint needs ~24GB on an A100 (24s, DDIM 50 steps, `perframe_ae=True`); the repo acknowledges user reports of 24–27GB, and points to a **community ComfyUI pruned FP16 model at 12GB** (`ComfyUI-DynamiCrafterWrapper` by kijai) — that 12GB figure is the T4-relevant one (index for the pruned figure). The repo's own disclaimer: "success rate is not guaranteed… open-source research exploration, instead of commercial products." Role in the pipeline: inbetweening between two canon keyframes for short segments, at modest resolution, then upscale/composite.

**Wan FLF2V (first+last frame).** Wan2.1's FLF2V is **14B-only, 720P** (`https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1`, verified live, Apache-2.0); the repo notes the FLF model was trained primarily on Chinese text–video pairs and recommends Chinese prompts. ComfyUI's FLF tutorial (`https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan-flf`, verified live) claims a "matching rate of first and last frames reaches 98%" and "jitter reduced 37%" — **these numbers appear only in Comfy documentation; no paper source was found, so treat them as unverified vendor claims.** The workflow uses `wan2.1_flf2v_720p_14B_fp16.safetensors` (or FP8) + `umt5_xxl` + `wan_2.1_vae` + `clip_vision_h`, and the docs themselves warn the 720×1280 default "may cause users with lower VRAM hard to run smoothly and will take a long time." Wan2.2 has no separate FLF checkpoint; ComfyUI's native `WanFirstLastFrameToVideo` node uses the I2V-A14B models (docs.comfy.org Wan2.2 tutorial, index). Community guidance (HF forums, index): use FLF "when you want to constrain where characters end up… avoid the model drifting into random poses by the end; make last frame only slightly different to keep motion subtle." That is the correct mental model: **FLF pins both endpoints, so drift is bounded by the segment length — but the middle frames are still generated, and the endpoints being 14B makes it a poor T4 citizen.** At 1.3B scale, VACE (reference + control video) is the practical FLF substitute.

**FramePack** (`https://github.com/lllyasviel/FramePack`, verified live; licence **Apache-2.0**; NeurIPS 2025 paper "Frame Context Packing and Drift Prevention in Next-Frame-Prediction Video Diffusion Models"). It predicts next frame-sections with context compressed to a constant length, so memory does not grow with duration: the repo states that to generate a 1-minute video (60s, 30fps, 1800 frames) with the 13B model, "minimal required GPU memory is **6GB**." Speed on RTX 4090: 2.5 s/frame unoptimized, 1.5 s/frame with TeaCache; laptops (3070Ti/3060) run 4–8× slower. The repo warns TeaCache/quantization "is not really lossless and sometimes can influence the result a lot." FramePack is built on HunyuanVideo 13B and is **I2V-only from a single starting image**: its anti-drift machinery fights *temporal degradation over long clips*, not *identity departure from a reference*. It does not fix the pipeline's observed failure (frame 1 already wrong) — the first generated frames are still the base model's re-imagining.

**The still-image stage: prefer SDXL over FLUX for cel art.**

- **FLUX.1 [dev] is under the FLUX.1 [dev] Non-Commercial License**; FLUX.1 [schnell] is Apache-2.0 (secondary sources agreeing, e.g. `https://www.anygen.io/showcase/flux1/index.html` citing the official HF cards — index; licence not re-verified on the HF card in this session). FLUX is photoreal-first and its ControlNet/IP-Adapter ecosystem is weaker: a Runflow guide notes "FLUX is slower than SDXL here and its ControlNet is less reliable… SDXL version is faster and more consistent" (index).
- **SDXL anime route:** Illustrious XL (SDXL-based, illustration-first) + character LoRA + IP-Adapter (1–2 anchor frames) + ControlNet lineart/pose is described as the "practical SDXL stack for TV-cel stability" in an HF discussion (`https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/best-approach-in-2026-to-reproduce-a-specific-cartoon-style/173784/2`, index). Illustrious runs in 6–8GB VRAM; SDXL LoRA training minimum is ~8GB VRAM (gradient checkpointing, cached latents, batch 1) per `https://techtactician.com/how-to-train-stable-diffusion-lora-models/` (index). Data norms: FLUX character LoRAs are commonly trained from **10–15 clean images** (Runflow, index); ai-toolkit's Wan character tutorial used ≥10 images + a single trigger word (index). **Illustrious carries its own "Illustrious License" per checkpoint — check the specific HF card before any commercial use; do not assume Apache.** (The Illustrious-XL-v2.0 HF card was opened, `https://huggingface.co/OnomaAIResearch/Illustrious-XL-v2.0`, verified live, but the licence text was not in the rendered portion read.)
- Why the still stage matters: IP-Adapter's mechanism is a CLIP image-embedding adapter (22M parameters) with a FaceID variant that swaps in an InsightFace ArcFace ID embedding (IP-Adapter wiki, `https://github.com/tencent-ailab/IP-Adapter` wiki via search result, index; HF diffusers docs, `https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/v0.35.1/using-diffusers/ip_adapter`, index). The non-FaceID (CLIP) variants are style/appearance adapters that work on illustrated faces; the FaceID variants depend on photoreal face detection (§6).

**AnimateDiff** (index) deserves one line: it bolts a temporal motion module onto existing SD1.5/SDXL checkpoints, **so a still-image character LoRA carries over into video unchanged** — its key advantage for cartoon. Costs: SDXL animation ~14GB minimum VRAM, 16 frames typical (up to 24), SD1.5 10–13GB at 512×512; known flicker, worst on faces/fine detail (`https://insiderllm.com/guides/local-ai-video-generation/`, index).

### 4. Other open video models (HunyuanVideo, LTX-Video/LTX-2, CogVideoX, Mochi, SkyReels-V2)

- **HunyuanVideo** (`https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo`, verified live): 13B; official requirements table: **60GB peak VRAM for 720×1280×129f, 45GB for 544×960×129f** — the original is not a T4 model. Licence is the **Tencent Hunyuan Community License** (GitHub shows "Other (NOASSERTION)"; secondary sources state it excludes EU/UK/South Korea and caps usage at 100M MAU — index). **HunyuanCustom** (arXiv:2505.04512, index) is the subject-consistency variant: LLaVA text–image fusion plus an **image ID enhancement module using temporal concatenation** to reinforce identity across frames; supports image/audio/video conditions and human and non-human subjects — architecturally the most interesting identity mechanism in this survey, but built on the 13B base, so it inherits the 45–60GB problem. HunyuanVideo-1.5 (8.3B) is reported at 14GB with offloading by a secondary comparison site (`https://singularitybyte.com/tutorials/ai-video-generator-comparison-2026-open-source-models-tested.html`, index) — watch-list only, licence still Tencent community.
- **LTX-Video 0.9.x** (`https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-Video`, verified live; code Apache-2.0 per the GitHub licence field, but **model licences differ — v0.9.5 release notes state a new "OpenRail-M" licence for commercial use**). Features: **multi-keyframe conditioning**, keyframe animation, video extension; **IC-LoRA control models (depth, pose, canny) for the 13B released 2025-07-08**. The 2B distilled models are the low-VRAM path; the 13B dev model "requires more VRAM." No subject-reference encoder or character-consistency evidence beyond generic LoRA training support.
- **LTX-2 / 2.3 / 2.5** (index, multiple agreeing sources): 22B-class joint audio+video. **Licence is the LTX-2 Community License — free commercial use only under $10M annual revenue; it is NOT Apache-2.0** despite frequent misattribution (`https://aivideobootcamp.com/blog/ltx-2-complete-guide-2026/`, index). LTX's own blog puts minimum local VRAM for LTX-2.5 at 16GB and calls it "the floor, not a recommendation" (index); trainer docs report 80GB standard / 32GB low-memory for LoRA training (`https://www.therundown.ai/tools/ltx-2`, index). Verdict: too heavy to train on T4, marginal to run.
- **CogVideoX** (`https://github.com/zai-org/CogVideo`, verified live; code Apache-2.0). **Model licence split: the 2B is Apache-2.0; the 5B is under the bespoke CogVideoX License.** Diffusers memory table in the repo: CogVideoX-5B BF16 from **5GB minimum**, INT8 from 4.4GB; 2B FP16 from 4GB; 1.5-5B BF16 from 10GB — but the repo warns these were tested only on A100/H100 and the optimizations "can be adapted to all NVIDIA Ampere architecture and above" (the T4 is pre-Ampere Turing), while also noting TorchAO quantization "allows the model to run on **free T4 Colabs**." Official finetune hardware (via DeepWiki of `finetune/README.md`, index): **2B LoRA rank-128, DDP FP16, 49×480×720 = 16GB VRAM; 5B LoRA = 24GB**. So CogVideoX-2B LoRA is the only CogVideoX training config that fits a T4 — at the cost of a much weaker base model than Wan for character work.
- **Mochi-1** (repo README via `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/genmoai/mochi/main/README.md`, index): Apache-2.0, 10B, ~60GB single-GPU official, <20GB in optimized ComfyUI builds. Its own limitations section states it is "**optimized for photorealistic styles so does not perform well with animated content**" — disqualifying for this mission, in the vendor's own words.
- **SkyReels-V2** (`https://github.com/SkyworkAI/SkyReels-V2`, verified live; licence shown as "Other"): Wan-based, 1.3B and 14B across T2V/I2V/Diffusion-Forcing. HF card figures (index): **540P generation with the 1.3B ≈ 14.7GB peak VRAM; 14B ≈ 43.4GB (T2V) / 51.2GB (DF)**. The 1.3B is borderline T4-feasible, but V2 has no subject-reference mechanism beyond I2V — its contribution is Diffusion-Forcing long-form generation, not likeness.

### 5. Pose/motion control as likeness aid

**What exists:** Wan2.1-Fun-Control 1.3B/14B accepts **Canny (line art), Depth, OpenPose, MLSD and trajectory** control videos, up to 81 frames at 16fps, at 512/768/1024 (ComfyUI docs, `https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/fun-control`, index). For flat cartoon, **canny/lineart is the most relevant control signal of all** — it speaks the character's own language (bold outlines). VACE likewise accepts pose/depth control videos through its preprocessors, and musubi-tuner can even *train* Fun-Control (`-FC` tasks). Wan-Animate's skeleton stream is the 14B version of the same idea.

**Does it reduce identity drift? No source demonstrates that, and two primary papers cut the other way:**

- X-Portrait (arXiv:2403.15931, index): landmark/ControlNet-style driving "tends to copy the driving structure entangled with identity features such as facial shapes and ratios. As a result, **undesirable identity drift to the driving subject occurs** during cross-identity animation." Pose control fixes *motion*; driven naively it can *import the driver's proportions*.
- Animate-X (ICLR 2025, index): "**since DWPose is trained on human data, it does not accurately extract every pose in the dancing video of the anthropomorphic character**" — the authors manually screened 100 videos. Pose *detectors* are unreliable on non-human-proportioned cartoon characters.

**Conclusion:** use control signals to remove *motion* randomness (one less degree of freedom for the model to invent, and end-poses can be pinned), and source those signals from the pipeline's own drawn cue sheets/puppet lane rather than DWPose-on-canon-art. Identity must come from LoRA / reference / locked keyframes — control is a complement, never the identity mechanism.

### 6. Post-hoc correction for cartoon characters

- **No credible open-source cartoon character-swap exists.** The entire open faceswap stack — InsightFace/ReActor/FaceFusion, IP-Adapter FaceID, PuLID — is built on photoreal face detection + ArcFace embeddings trained on human faces: FaceID "swaps CLIP for an InsightFace ArcFace embedding… encodes facial geometry" and needs a companion LoRA (`http://comfy.icu/node/IPAAdapterFaceIDBatch`, index); PuLID uses InsightFace + EVA-CLIP with face masking (index). On flat cartoon faces with non-human proportions these will fail to detect, or embed garbage. This is a reasoned conclusion from their documented mechanism, not a tested claim. The one "comic faceswap" product found (Segmind FaceSwap Comic v1) is a **paid hosted API — excluded by the mission's rules.**
- **EbSynth** (official site `http://ebsynth.com/?ref=futureen`, index): example-based synthesis that propagates a painted/corrected keyframe across a shot via optical flow. The official FAQ says the free plan has "no time or functional limitations" (Pro adds high-res render / PNG sequences). **It is freeware, not open-source — labelled as such.** Official guidance warns propagation quality depends on optical flow and that **flat/shiny materials are hard to track** — a real caveat for flat-colour cartoon; the official tips recommend per-part tracks (background/face/eyes separated, alpha keyframes). Best-fit role: propagate *hand-corrected canon-style keyframes* over puppet/VACE output and repair individual bad segments.
- **RIFE / Practical-RIFE** (MIT, `hzwer/Practical-RIFE`, index): deterministic frame interpolation — smooths locked-keyframe sequences (and doubles fps) without re-imagining the character. Safe as a final pass.
- Unproven-for-this options, listed so they are not oversold: low-denoise per-frame img2img with an SDXL cartoon LoRA (reintroduces flicker/drift — the thing being fought), and deterministic palette quantization + outline re-inking (consistent with the pipeline's existing matting/compositing; this is pipeline engineering, not a model claim, and needs no citation).

### 7. Avatar-generator benchmark — techniques and open equivalents

**What the best hosted system actually does (HeyGen Avatar V**, arXiv HTML `https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13872`, read via search): the central insight is **video-reference conditioning — not per-identity fine-tuning and not a bottleneck face embedding.** The model attends directly to the **full token sequence of a reference video via Sparse Reference Attention**, capturing static appearance *and* dynamic behaviour (talking rhythm, habitual expressions); plus a dedicated motion representation stream, an **identity-aware super-resolution refiner DiT**, and LLM voice cloning from short audio. It was trained on 50M+ raw videos → 100M+ pretraining clips and 10M+ avatar fine-tuning clips, in five stages including >10× distillation and RLHF, and is deployed on thousands of GPUs. HeyGen's own page: Avatar V "resolves identity drift… **without any additional fine-tuning or extra reference input**" compared with single-frame systems.

Mapping to open equivalents wireable into the local ComfyUI pipeline:

| Hosted technique | Open equivalent | Status for flat cartoon on T4 |
|---|---|---|
| Per-subject fine-tuning ("Digital Twin") | Character LoRA — musubi-tuner (Wan 1.3B) / SDXL LoRA for keyframes | ✅ Feasible; strongest likeness lever available |
| Full-reference-token attention | Phantom-Wan (reference images injected as tokens into the Wan DiT); VACE reference images (`src_ref_images`) | ⚠️ Same mechanism family, but image- not video-reference, far smaller scale, human-first training |
| Face-ID embeddings | IP-Adapter FaceID / PuLID / InstantID | ❌ Photoreal only; no cartoon equivalent exists |
| Identity-aware SR / refiner pass | Per-frame SDXL-LoRA img2img detailer; EbSynth propagation of corrected keyframes | ⚠️ Partial, unproven equivalents |
| Audio-driven performance | Wan2.2-S2V (14B; official single-GPU path needs 80GB) / HunyuanVideo-Avatar / SkyReels-A1 | ❌ Not T4-practical at quality; keep dialogue shots short / puppet-driven |

**The honest asymmetry:** hosted avatar tools are also *narrower* than this mission — talking head, near-static camera, photoreal human with a face-recognition prior doing much of the work. They do not solve full-body flat-cartoon action. Beating them *on this character* is achievable precisely because the target is deterministic flat art: a pipeline whose frames originate from canon art (puppet lane, ToonCrafter between canon keyframes, matting/compositing) can be *exactly* right in a way no generative avatar tool attempts.

### Bill of materials — top recommendation, end-to-end (canon art → gated clips)

**Lane A — locked keyframes (identity source of truth)**
1. Canon art corpus: all existing character sheets/poses/expressions, white-field, as already produced by the pipeline.
2. Still model: **Illustrious XL v2.0** (SDXL-based; check the checkpoint's Illustrious License on its HF card before commercial use) in ComfyUI.
3. **SDXL character LoRA** trained on the canon corpus (kohya sd-scripts or ai-toolkit — ai-toolkit is MIT, `github.com/ostris/ai-toolkit`, verified live; ~8GB-class training, batch 1, cached latents; trigger word per character; caption pose/expression/background, not identity).
4. **ControlNet lineart + OpenPose** (SDXL) fed from the pipeline's drawn cue sheets — pose comes from drawings, not DWPose on canon art (Animate-X caveat, §5).
5. **IP-Adapter Plus (CLIP variant, not FaceID)** with 1–2 canon anchor frames as a soft appearance anchor on top of the LoRA.

**Lane B — motion (short segments only)**
6. **ToonCrafter** via `ComfyUI-DynamiCrafterWrapper` (kijai), pruned FP16 checkpoint (~12GB): inbetween Lane-A keyframe pairs, ≤16 frames @ 512×320, sketch guidance from cue-sheet line art.
7. **Wan2.1 VACE-1.3B** (already in pipeline; Apache-2.0): shots ToonCrafter can't cover — canon keyframe as `src_ref_images` reference + canny/pose control video from the puppet lane; keep segments short; optional **Wan2.1-1.3B character LoRA** (item 9) loaded for these passes.
8. **Practical-RIFE** (MIT) for interpolation/fps-doubling of locked sequences.

**Lane C — the video-model LoRA (progressive enhancement, train once)**
9. **musubi-tuner** (`github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner`): train a **Wan2.1 T2V-1.3B character LoRA**, dataset TOML mixing canon stills (caption `.txt` each; precedent: 30 images) + short clips cut from approved Lane-B output (targets N×4+1 frames, 16fps); `--network_dim 32 --learning_rate 2e-4 --optimizer_type adamw8bit --gradient_checkpointing --fp8_t5` for the text encoder; **do not rely on `--fp8_base` speed on T4** — budget wall-clock generously and validate a 200-step smoke run first. Recipe precedent (anime, index): lr 2e-4, flow_shift 3.0, ~6000 steps, inference strength ~0.8.

**Lane D — correction & finishing (already mostly owned)**
10. **EbSynth** (freeware, not open source — optional, replaceable by hand inbetweening): propagate hand-corrected canon keyframes over bad segments; per-part tracks (face/eyes separated) per official guidance.
11. Existing **BiRefNet + distance-from-white matting**, compositing over painted backgrounds, palette/outline cleanup.
12. **Gate (unchanged, strict):** frame-1 and keyframe likeness check against canon art; any segment whose first frame departs = reject and re-run via Lane A/B, never "fix in the video model."

**Explicitly not in the BOM:** FLUX.1-dev (non-commercial licence, photoreal bias), IP-Adapter FaceID/PuLID (photoreal face embeddings), Wan-Animate/S2V 14B (VRAM class + LoRA warning), any hosted API.

## What will NOT work / unproven / photoreal-only claims

- **Plain Wan 2.2 I2V with a start frame + prompt** — already falsified by the pipeline's own experiments; nothing in the literature contradicts that result for flat cel art.
- **"IP-Adapter / ControlNet for Wan 2.2"** — they do not exist for Wan (confirmed by prior research and consistent with everything found here); the Wan-native equivalents are VACE, Fun-Control, Phantom-Wan.
- **Photoreal faceswap/restoration as cartoon correction** (InsightFace, ReActor, FaceFusion, PuLID, IP-Adapter FaceID): all presuppose a detectable photoreal human face and an ArcFace embedding space. Flat cartoon faces are out-of-distribution for both stages. No open cartoon character-swap tool was found at all.
- **Wan-Animate as the likeness saviour:** 14B-only, photoreal demos only, its Relighting LoRA actively fights two-tone cel shading, and its own card discourages stacking Wan2.2 LoRAs. Unproven for cartoon; impractical on T4 in official form.
- **SkyReels-A2:** only a 14B *Preview* is released, the vendor's own low-VRAM path targets a 24GB 4090, and its licence could not be verified (GitHub: NOASSERTION).
- **FramePack as an identity fix:** its drift prevention is about long-clip temporal degradation from a *given* start frame; it does not constrain generation toward a reference character, and its HunyuanVideo base shares the photoreal lean.
- **ComfyUI's FLF2V "98% first/last-frame matching, 37% less jitter" figures:** documentation-only claims with no paper or reproducible source found — do not plan around them. FLF2V is also 14B-only in Wan2.1.
- **ai-toolkit "trains Wan 2.2 in 12–16GB" claims:** contradicted by user reports of 26–27GB typical usage and a 5090 being forced to 5B@512px. SEO pages claiming 12GB Wan2.2 14B character-LoRA settings (e.g. wan27.org) are unverified and were not used as evidence.
- **Mochi-1 for cartoon:** its own README says it "does not perform well with animated content."
- **The anime≠cel gap:** every positive cartoon result found (Civitai Wan LoRAs, Phantom's Nezha demo, ToonCrafter's demos) is anime or anime-adjacent. **No published result on a flat Western cel-shaded character (bold outlines, two-tone shading) was found for any method.** Anime evidence transfers imperfectly: anime faces are closer to the human proportions these models and detectors expect.
- **Hosted-avatar comparisons:** HeyGen-class systems solve a narrower problem (photoreal talking head) with datacenter scale (100M+ clips, thousands of GPUs). "Beating" them is only meaningful on this pipeline's own ground — exact flat-art likeness — which is what the deterministic-first ranking exploits.
