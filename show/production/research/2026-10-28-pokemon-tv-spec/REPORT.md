# Pokémon TV Anime Production Spec — Gap Analysis vs a Small Automated Open-Source Pipeline

## Summary

**Pokémon is not "drawn at 24 fps." It is *timed* at 24 fps and *drawn* at 8–12 unique drawings per second, selectively.** The single best Pokémon-specific volume source found — OLM animation producer Hiroyuki Kato, quoted in Animation World Network's Celsys/OLM Asia report (2019) — puts a Pokémon episode at **"over 300 shots or 4,000–5,000 individual drawings per episode."** Everything else in the TV-anime system — settei (model sheets), layout → genga → douga → shiage → background → satsuei (compositing), animation-director correction passes, afureko voice recording, bank/held cels — exists to make those ~300 cuts / ~4–5k drawings, produced by dozens of people in parallel rotations, look like one coherent show.

**The compared pipeline (Part 2, taken as ground truth) already has the *skeleton* of that system** — a timed cue sheet (= a timesheet), part-pack puppets (= cel layers), compositing over background plates, and Rhubarb lip-sync (= kuchipaku mouth flaps). What it lacks is almost everything that sits *between* the skeleton bones: pose/expression libraries built from a formal settei package, ease/spacing (timing charts) instead of linear puppet moves, secondary motion, a dedicated effects/shadow-highlight layer pass, a real compositing grade (not just matting + drop shadow), and a retake/QC loop that functions as its "animation director."

**Highest-leverage closers, all free/open-source and verified below:** rebuild the cue lane on an explicit 24 fps timesheet in **OpenToonz/Tahoma2D (BSD)** or **Synfig (GPL-3, bones + automatic vector tweening)**; add mesh deformation via OpenToonz Plastic tool / Synfig Skeleton Distortion / **Inochi Creator (BSD-2)** instead of rigid part translation; do compositing/effects in **Blender (GPL) or Natron (GPL-2)** with reusable node presets; keep **RIFE (MIT) / FILM (Apache-2.0) OFF by default for cel work** — the research literature (AnimeInterp, CVPR 2021) shows natural-video interpolation fails specifically on flat, textureless cel colour and large exaggerated motion. There is **no automated equivalent** for: key-pose acting choices, animation-director model correction, and effects-animation design. Those remain human-eye steps, and the honest pipeline below says so.

Confidence: Pokémon volume/software/schedule numbers = **verified from opened primary/industry sources** (AWN/OLM, Animage Iwane interview translation, Kanzenshuu Toei guide, Jarrett Martin industry guide, ANN Answerman). Exact per-episode percentages of ones/twos/threes for Pokémon specifically = **could not verify** — no source found publishes a Pokémon exposure-sheet census; the twos/threes split given is the documented TV-anime norm, flagged as such.

## Findings

### 1. Frame rate and timing: ones, twos, threes, timesheets, holds, bank, limited-animation economics

- **Production timing base is 24 fps.** The Japanese timesheet (タイムシート, Western "X-sheet/exposure sheet") has one row per frame, 24 rows per second; it records, per cut: which drawing number is exposed for how many frames, dialogue timing, camera instructions (pan/zoom/TU/TB), cel-layer order (A, B, C…), and compositing/effects instructions. Source: Kanzenshuu production guide — "Each line on the timing sheet represents a single frame. With the film being played at a frame rate of 24 frames per second (fps), it takes 24 frames/photographs to create a single second" — https://www.kanzenshuu.com/animation-production/process/ (opened 2026-10-28, index/crawled copy). Jarrett Martin's guide confirms the layout/genga animator authors the X-sheet with "camera notations, and compositing instructions" — https://www.jarrettmartin.jp/how-anime-is-made-republished/ (opened 2026-10-28).
- **Drawing rate ≠ frame rate.** TV anime default is **on twos (12 drawings/sec) and on threes (8 drawings/sec)**, mixed *within a single cut* — a cut may start on 1s, settle to 3s, return to 1s for an accent. Ones (24/sec) are reserved for fast action, camera-following motion, and sakuga accents; fours and longer holds are used for slow drift, dialogue holds and backgrounds. This mixed-timing practice is the documented industry norm (Production I.G guide summary: "especially when it comes to Japanese TV-anime, the animation is performed with 2:s… Sometimes… with 1:s and sometimes even with 3:s, and an animation can consist of all those" — search index, https://www.huitula.com/productionIG2_page2.htm). ⚠️ **Flag:** any claim of a fixed Pokémon-specific split (e.g. "Pokémon is 70% threes") is **commonly repeated but poorly sourced** — no OLM timesheet census was found. Do not quote one.
- **Spacing, not count, is the craft.** The key animator draws a *timing/spacing chart* (a small diagram beside the key drawing) telling the in-betweener where the in-betweens cluster: clustered near keys = ease-out/in (slow), evenly spaced = constant, clustered mid-way = snap. Kanzenshuu: the timing chart "indicates how many in-between frames will be needed… and the timing of these in-between frames in relation to the key frames," with non-linear elements (arm, tail, head) charted separately — https://www.kanzenshuu.com/animation-production/process/
- **Held cels / holds.** A single drawing is routinely held for seconds while only (a) the mouth layer, (b) a blink layer, (c) the camera (pan/zoom over an oversized drawing/background), or (d) a background/effects layer moves. This is not a defect — it is the grammar. The storytelling load shifts to voice, framing and cutting.
- **Bank system (バンク).** Reusable finished sequences — in Pokémon: Poké Ball throws, evolution, attacks, Team Rocket motto/blast-off in the Ash era — are animated once (often lavishly) and re-used, freeing the drawing budget for new cuts. Bank economics originate with Tezuka/Mushi Pro's *Astro Boy* weekly system and remain standard TV practice. Iwane interview corroborates the inverse for XY: director Yajima deliberately *reduced* stock/effect-background use and redrew backgrounds per scene — a quality choice that cost drawings (search index, Yajima interview translation, https://truthordeal.neocities.org/translations/yajimainterview).
- **Limited-animation economics are a budget line, literally.** Iwane (Animage, Mar 2015): each outsourced episode came with a **drawing allotment**; exceeding it lost the studio money, undershooting angered OLM. From *XY* (2013) the allotment effectively rose — "each episode uses about 20% to 30% more drawings than before" *Best Wishes*. Source: https://dogasu.bulbagarden.net/features/translations/animage_masaaki_iwane.html (opened 2026-10-28).
- **Delivery/master rate note:** production is timed at 24; home-video masters surface at 23.976p and Japanese broadcast was converted for 1080i terrestrial. Treat "24 fps timeline, 23.976 delivery" as the safe formulation; a Pokémon-specific OLM delivery spec sheet was **not found** (see Could not verify).

**Staffing pipeline (per episode, industry-standard titles):** Series director (kantoku) → episode director (enshutsu: directs acting/framing, checks every stage, can order retakes) → storyboard (e-conte, often by enshutsu) → production desk / production assistants (seisaku shinkō: schedule, cut bags/files, tracking every cut daily) → chief/general animation director (sō-sakkan, often the character designer: final model polish) → animation directors (sakkan: layout check + genga check, redraw corrections, "match the rest" notes) → key animators (genga, in sections of ~15–20 cuts) → second key/cleanup → in-betweeners (douga) + douga checker → colour design (shikisai sekkei) / colour specification per cut (iro-shitei) → finishing (shiage) + cel checker → art director (bijutsu kantoku) + background studio (Pokémon credits historically e.g. Studio Jack) → CG department → compositing director (satsuei kantoku) + compositing → editor → sound director + afureko → dubbing mix → online edit/delivery (V-hen). Role definitions: Martin guide, staff sections — https://www.jarrettmartin.jp/how-anime-is-made-republished/

### 3. Character construction: settei and consistency enforcement

A settei package is not one turnaround. Per Martin guide (settei + kouban-hyou sections), a working package contains, per character/production:

- **Character settei:** front / 3⁄4 / side / back turnarounds at consistent scale, **height/proportion comparison chart** (characters lined up to a shared baseline), costume/detail callouts, and clean (kiyokaki) versions approved from roughs — adaptation designs go straight to settei; original designs add a concept (gen'an) → animation-design adaptation step.
- **Expression sheet (hyōjō-shū)** — a named, separate deliverable, listed alongside per-episode settei orders.
- **Colour models / iro-shitei:** colour designer defines base cel colours, then **separate colour sets per location and time of day** (day/night/sunset variants are *painted colour choices*, not just a compositing filter), plus per-cut colour specification telling shiage which model to use; a cel checker verifies.
- **Art settei + art boards (bijutsu bōdo):** line designs for every location + painted key boards, again with time-of-day variants.
- **Prop/mecha settei, mob (crowd character) settei**, per-episode settei ordered from the storyboard + kouban-hyou (a per-cut sheet: who/where/when, lighting, season, special needs).
- **Sakkan correction collection (sakuga kantoku shūsei-shū) / precaution sheets:** compilations of past corrections — "draw it like *this*, not like *that*" — distributed so the same model errors stop recurring. This is the closest analogue to a style-guide/linter for drawings.

**Who maintains them:** character designer + sō-sakkan own the model; settei producer (settei seisaku) manages ordering/production of design materials; sakkan enforce per episode.

**Consistency enforcement = layered human correction, not the sheet alone:** layout enshutsu check (acting, framing, continuity) → layout sakkan check (model/quality redraws) → genga enshutsu check → genga sakkan check → sō-sakkan check (final say; may fix only eyes/jawline or redraw everything) → douga check → cel check → retakes (a cut can be bounced at any gate; rushes are reviewed as all-rush (in edit) and bara-rush (cut apart, sent back to departments)). Sources: Martin guide, layout-check/retake sections; Sakuga Wiki layout checks (search index, yellow sakkan paper / green sō-sakkan paper, "ta awasete — match the rest").

**Pokémon-specific:** designs ultimately derive from game character design (Sugimori/Nishida lineage) adapted for animation; per-series redesigns are production decisions with animation consequences — e.g. *Sun & Moon*'s rounder, simpler models were chosen to permit more fluid/expressive movement, and *XY*'s sharper models + 3D camera work pushed drawing counts up. Treat any single "official Pokémon settei book" claim cautiously: settei sheets circulate via production-material books/auctions (e.g. Heritage production-drawing lots show layout + expression + mouth drawings + exposure sheet for a single Pokémon cut), not as a single public spec.

### 4. Drawing pipeline and software; compositing; resolution history

**Chain (modern, digital):**
`script (3–4 drafts) → e-conte storyboard (cut numbers, seconds+frames per cut) → layout (exact framing, character + background line art, X-sheet started) → [enshutsu + sakkan + sō-sakkan checks] → genga (keys + timing/spacing charts, shadow/highlight markup in coloured pencil/layers) → 2nd genga/cleanup → douga (in-betweens + cleanup, douga check, X-sheet finalised) → shiage (digital ink & paint per iro-shitei, cel check) ‖ backgrounds in parallel from approved layouts (art boards → painted BGs, art-director check) ‖ CG elements → satsuei/compositing (assemble per X-sheet via time-remapping; camera moves, multiplane depth, effects, lighting/grade) → offline edit → afureko/dubbing → online edit/master.`

**Software actually used (industry, and Pokémon/OLM specifically):**
- **RETAS Studio (Celsys)** was the industry backbone: Stylos (drawing), TraceMan (scan/vectorise paper), PaintMan (ink & paint), CoreRETAS (compositing). ANN Answerman (2018): "nearly the entire industry relies on the RETAS Studio Suite" — https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/answerman/2018-03-30/.129615 (opened 2026-10-28).
- **Clip Studio Paint (Celsys)** — OLM's documented Pokémon tool: "Clip Studio Paint is used in the genga (keyframe) and [douga] stages" at OLM Asia, adopted to digitise a still largely paper-based workflow (Kato, AWN). Martin guide (2023): douga "generally created in RETAS Stylos, or Clip Studio Paint… Paper douga is still the norm at many studios as of 2023"; shiage in PaintMan or CSP.
- **Toonz lineage:** Toonz (Digital Video) → Studio Ghibli's customised version → open-sourced as **OpenToonz** (2016, Dwango/Digital Video/Ghibli). It is the direct ancestor of the free tool recommended in Part 3 — not an imitation of the pipeline, a branch of it.
- **Compositing: Adobe After Effects dominates** ("Almost all of anime compositing is done in After Effects; though some productions will do compositing in RETAS" — Martin guide), with proprietary/studio plugins (ANN). Photography/compositing today adds: time-remapped cel timing (not baked PNG sequences — so a timing fix or cel swap is cheap, a retake-friendly design), pans/zooms/rotations, multiplane parallax and pseudo-3D space from 2D layers, blur/depth-of-field, glow/bloom, colour grade/"anime filter," shadow & highlight treatment, dust/particles, and integration of 3DCG (traditionally 3ds Max/Maya + proprietary cel-shading plugins; Blender is now common in the wider industry). Kanzenshuu modern section confirms compositors build "3D spaces using 2D drawings and backgrounds" with acceleration/motion-path camera work.
- **Backgrounds:** painted from layouts under the art director; modern practice is digital (Photoshop most common, also CSP, or traditional-then-scan) with **stock textures/assets reused for speed** (Martin guide) — ~300 BGs/episode is painting *plus* a library, not 300 from-scratch paintings.

**Pokémon format history (verified points):**
- 1997–Aug 2002: hand-painted **cel**, 4:3 SD. Digital ink-&-paint transition during *Advanced Generation* — series switched to digital production in **August 2002** (Bulbapedia/Namu corroboration via search index; the last cel episode is commonly identified as the end of Master Quest — treat the exact episode title attribution as fan-wiki grade, the 2002 date as solid).
- Early AG onward: broadcast digitally but **not produced in full HD** (Bulbanews, citing TV Tokyo) — i.e. SD production upscaled for HD broadcast.
- **2 Apr 2009, DP121 "The Lonely Snover!": first HD, 16:9 episode** — https://bulbanews.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_to_begin_HD_broadcast_in_April (search index) and episode listing note "Starting with this episode, the Pokémon anime is now produced in high definition widescreen."
- Modern series: full digital production incl. storyboards in at least some OLM work (ANN on the 2019 *Pokémon Masters* trailer: producer Katō described "a full digital production, including the storyboards," OLM Team Kato) — https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2019-06-28/pokemon-masters-smartphone-game-trailer-was-solo-key-animated-by-masaaki-iwane/.148389
- Working/drawing resolution is larger than delivery (animators draw at production size above 1080 lines so camera moves/upscales in comp don't soften lines); an exact current OLM Pokémon pixel spec was **not found** — do not quote the "1684×1190" figure that circulates in compositing tutorials as a Pokémon spec; it is a tutorial project size.

### 5. Sound-side conventions relevant to picture

- **Afureko (after-recording) is the Japanese default — the opposite of US pre-scoring.** Picture (often *not final* picture) comes first; the ensemble cast records together in one room, watching the cut. Kanzenshuu: dialogue for an episode records in **2–3 hours** incl. break/retakes, cast around 3–4 shared microphones, after a test reading + test recording against projected picture. Martin guide sharpens the modern reality: afureko is **usually done before animation is finished — often over the e-conte/CT movie — "There are no lip flaps at this time, and the lip flaps will be adjusted based on the voice recording."** Actors speak when their character's name marker appears in the CT movie. So the flow is: *storyboard timing sets line length → voice is recorded to storyboard/animatic timing → mouth flaps are fitted to the recorded voice.* (US dubbing is the reverse constraint: fit words to existing flaps.)
- **Mouth flaps (kuchipaku) are a 3-drawing system in TV anime:** closed / half-open / open, cycled roughly in time with syllables, typically on a separate cel layer from the head/body (so a held head + flap layer = a talking character for the cost of 3 small drawings). Tezuka's animation textbook states the TV convention explicitly: "they usually make do with just three positions: an open mouth, a mid-position mouth, and a closed mouth" (via Scribd copy of Tezuka Animation Vol. 1 — **flag: low-grade scan source**, but the 3-shape convention is universally corroborated in practice). Refinements that matter on screen: mouth changes land on **twos** (every 2 frames) not every frame; hold the last open shape **2–3 frames past the end of the audio** at sentence ends or it reads as abruptly cut off (Animator Island, limited-animation lip-sync note); jaw/head do *not* move with each flap in TV style.
- **Battles/action timing:** built in the storyboard + genga, not in comp. Vocabulary: anticipation pose held (sometimes on threes) → explosive movement on **ones/twos with smear drawings** (a limb drawn elongated/multiplied for 1–2 frames) → **impact frame** (1–3 frames of high-contrast/inverted/abstract flash drawing at contact) → held aftermath pose with effects/background motion carrying the energy. Speed lines / focus lines are drawn background or FX layers, not character motion. Kanada-lineage FX animation (angular flame/lightning shapes) is a specialist skill. In *XY*, Iwane/Yajima-era Pokémon added: dynamic **3D-camera moves around 2D action** ("the camera moves around a lot… that's thanks to the hard work of the 3D staff" — Iwane), fewer effect-background shortcuts, backgrounds redrawn to the actual location, and longer continuous action cuts (one Mega Evolution special cut >1,000 drawings). CG in modern Pokémon is primarily camera, stages, crowds/objects and some Pokémon/effects — the characters' acting remains 2D genga.
- **Sound is mixed last (dubbing/DB):** voices + SE + BGM balanced against final picture; music/SE placement is decided against timecode by sound director + recording director (Kanzenshuu Stage 9).

### 6. What separates "TV anime standard" from amateur output, on screen

1. **Pose quality over motion quantity.** Every held drawing is a *designed* pose (line of action, silhouette, expression) chosen by a key animator and corrected by a sakkan. Amateur puppet output holds *neutral* poses and moves them linearly — the eye reads "nothing is being acted."
2. **Variable timing.** Accents land on 1s/2s inside otherwise 3s/hold material; spacing charts create ease-in/out and snap. Constant-rate interpolation (or a cue every N frames at uniform speed) reads as "Flash/webtoon," not TV anime.
3. **Secondary motion everywhere, cheaply:** hair tips, clothing hems, tails/ears, props settle 2–6 frames *after* the body stops (follow-through), on their own layer/timing — often a 2–3 drawing cycle or a shifted copy of the main timing.
4. **Eyes do the acting in holds:** blinks (with eyelid squash + slight head dip on important blinks), gaze shifts before head turns, pupil/highlight changes. A dialogue hold in Pokémon is mouth + blink + occasional gaze — never a frozen face.
5. **Shadow/highlight are drawn shapes, not lighting effects:** a hard-edged shadow cel layer (kage) and highlight layer with animator-chosen shapes that *move with the pose*, plus per-scene colour models (night/interior variants). A soft post drop-shadow reads as video compositing, not cel.
6. **Backgrounds are paintings with lighting direction**, foreground/mid/background separation, and "book" (foreground occlusion) elements; characters' colour model is chosen to sit *in* that light.
7. **Compositing finish:** unified grade, slight blur/glow on highlights, camera moves with acceleration curves and parallax (BG moves slower than FG), occasional focus pulls. Ungraded, equally-sharp, single-plane comp reads as collage.
8. **Cutting rhythm:** ~300 cuts/21 min ≈ a cut every ~4 seconds, with cut length *varied* by intent (long holds for dialogue beats, rapid cuts for action). Uniform scene lengths read amateur regardless of drawing quality.
9. **Off-model discipline:** characters look identical across cuts because three layers of humans redrew them until they did. Consistency *is* a visible quality signal.

### 7. Gap analysis — the main deliverable

Pipeline as given (Part 2) is abbreviated: **CUE** = cue-sheet puppet lane + Rhubarb + TTS/auditions; **COMP** = white-field matting + BG plates + post shadows; **BOARD** = script/storyboard drafts; **NO** = generative video (likeness drift, excluded), no auto-inbetweening, no pose/expression libraries, no FX layer, no formal settei for most characters, no manual frame-by-frame (by design).

| G12 | **Backgrounds: plates without art direction system** | Art boards + BG settei, ~300/ep, FG "book" occlusion layers, time-of-day variants, stock textures | Painted plates, switched by cue | Scenes feel like backdrops; switches (incl. the 1-s-early defect) are naked cuts | BG *package* per location: wide master (for pans) + day/night variant + FG occlusion strip + detail inset. | G13 | **No sakkan function (no correction/retake loop)** | 3 check gates + retakes; one role owns "on-model" | Owner eyeballs final render | Errors (timing, drift, wrong character layer) ship; quality varies by scene | Formalise the owner as sakkan with a **checklist pass at animatic stage** (not render stage): model check vs settei sheet, timing check vs timesheet, continuity check. Retake = re-cue one cut only, enabled by time-remapped comp (G11). This is a *process* gap, closable for free, and it is where the BG-switch defect actually gets caught |
| G14 | **No CT/animatic discipline** | CT movie updated continuously; used for voice, SE, director checks; afureko is shot off it | Storyboards exist as drafts; voice is TTS scratch | Timing errors discovered at final render (most expensive point) | Assemble a timed animatic for *every* scene (boards + scratch voice + cue timings in **Kdenlive**) and lock it before puppet work. Lock = no cue may deviate from animatic frame numbers without a retake note |
| G15 | **Camera is static / moves linearly** | Camera work is directed: pans/TU with ease curves, multiplane, occasional 3D moves in modern Pokémon | Position moves in cue sheet | "Stage play" framing | Blender as camera stage: lay BG + character planes at different depths, animate a real camera with eased curves; or Natron transform nodes with curve editor. Even a 5% slow push-in on dialogue holds is a TV signature |
| G16 | **Cut rhythm unplanned** | ~300 cuts/21 min, lengths varied by intent; storyboard *is* the timing document | Scenes built beat-by-beat | Uniform pacing reads amateur even if a scene looks fine | Board with cut durations in seconds+frames (e-conte format), target varied lengths; count cuts per minute as a QC metric |
| G17 | **No CG/3D assistance for hard moves** | 3D staff do camera, stages, vehicles, crowds; 2D acting stays 2D | None | Walk cycles, turns, perspective moves are the puppet system's weakest points | **Blender Grease Pencil + 3D blocking**: block hard shots in crude 3D (turns, walks toward camera, pans across a room), use as timing/perspective guide under the puppet, or render cel-shaded 3D for props/vehicles only. Never for faces |
| G18 | **Sound finish** | SE/BGM placed to timecode; dubbing mix after picture | TTS + auditions (voice side is comparatively strong) | (Lower priority) picture cuts land without SE punctuation | **Audacity/Ardour (GPL)** mix pass against locked animatic timecode; SE stock library (whoosh/hit/ambience) placed on the timesheet like an FX cue |

**What is NOT a gap:** the 24 fps timeline (correct), part-pack architecture (that *is* cel layering — A/B/C layers in the analog system worked exactly this way, with mouths on a separate layer from heads), Rhubarb (a superset of kuchipaku — it needs quantising, not replacing), and the refusal of manual frame-by-frame (TV anime itself is an industrial system for *minimising* unique drawings; the pipeline's instinct is aligned with the medium — it is missing the *systems* TV uses to hide the minimisation).

**The cel-vs-video distinction, stated plainly:** interpolation models (RIFE/FILM) estimate motion from *texture*; cel art deliberately has no texture inside a colour region, and its motion is pose-to-pose, not continuous. That is why "AI in-betweening" demos look magical on live action and smeary on anime, and why the deterministic puppet tween (Synfig/OpenToonz/Inochi) — which moves *known parts along known paths* — is the genuinely applicable technology for this show.

### 9. One-pass scene pipeline (script beat → finished scene)

Legend: **[A]** automated · **[E]** human eye required · **[A/E]** automated draft, eye approves.

1. **Beat → e-conte board [E]** — Board the beat in Krita/Blender with cut number, framing, action, dialogue, **duration in seconds + frames**. (Replaces "storyboard drafts" with a timing document.)
2. **Timesheet [A/E]** — Expand the board into a frame-numbered X-sheet (OpenToonz/Tahoma2D scene, or a spreadsheet in the same format): per cut — drawing/pose IDs, hold lengths, spacing type, mouth track, FX cues, camera, BG ID. *This sheet is the single source of truth; the cue sheet is generated from it, not written independently* — this structurally prevents the 1-second-early BG class of error.
3. **CT animatic [A→E lock]** — Assemble boards + TTS scratch voice in Kdenlive at exact frame counts. Owner (as enshutsu) locks timing. **No production before lock.**
4. **Voice [A/E]** — Final TTS/audition take selected to the locked animatic (afureko order: voice to storyboard timing, flaps fitted after).
5. **Asset pull [A]** — Per cut, pull from libraries: settei-checked poses (G2), expressions, BG package (master + variant + FG strip), FX stock. Missing asset = a *settei ticket*, drawn once in Krita, added to the library forever.
6. **Puppet performance [A/E]** — Rig in Synfig (bones/tweening) or Tahoma2D/OpenToonz (Plastic mesh) or Inochi (mesh+physics). Author *key poses only*, in 3-cue action phrases (anticipation/action/settle, G7); software tweens the in-betweens; follow-through tracks auto-generated as delayed, damped copies (G6) **[A]**, eye adjusts amplitude **[E]**.
7. **Mouth pass [A]** — Rhubarb → quantised to closed/half/open on twos + sentence-end hold-over (G8).
8. **Spot sakuga allowance [E, rationed]** — Per scene, *one* beat may be escalated: smear/breakdown poses from the library, impact frame, ones-timing. This rationing is exactly how TV spends its fluidity budget.
9. **Satsuei comp [A preset / E check]** — Natron or Blender preset chain: BG parallax → FG book → character (with per-pose kage/highlight layers, G10) → contact shadow → FX stock (G9) → camera move (eased) → grade/bloom. Time-remapped, so retakes swap single poses without re-rendering the cut's logic.
10. **Sakkan pass [E]** — Owner checks the comp against a printed checklist: on-model vs settei, timing vs timesheet, continuity, flap sync. Fail = retake *that cut only*, back to step 6/9.
11. **Sound finish [A/E]** — SE from stock library placed at timesheet frame numbers; mix in Audacity/Ardour; dubbing-style balance against picture.
12. **V-hen delivery [A]** — Kdenlive/FFmpeg render at **1920×1080, 24 fps master** (23.976 variant only if a distributor demands it), archive scene with its timesheet + libraries versioned.

**Honest remainder — Pokémon steps with NO automated equivalent:**
- **Key-pose acting invention** (choosing *the* pose that sells a line) — libraries amortise it, nothing generates it on-model.
- **Sakkan judgement** (is this on-model / is this funny / is this timed right) — checklists assist, the eye decides.
- **FX animation design** for a *new* effect (stock covers repeats only).
- **Storyboard direction** (shot choice, cut rhythm) — the animatic process disciplines it, software doesn't do it.
- **Voice performance direction** (TTS casting/take selection is the owner's sakkan-for-ears job).

### 10. Frame-rate / timing guidance for the cue/puppet system

Master rules (all derived from §1):

1. **Timeline stays 24 fps. Nothing is ever authored "at 12 fps"** — drawings/poses are *held* for 2 or 3 frames on a 24 fps sheet. This keeps mouth, camera, FX and BG tracks commensurable.
2. **Default dialogue acting = threes for body, twos for mouth, holds for everything else.** Body pose changes on 3s (8/sec) or held entirely; mouth changes only on even frames; blinks every **2–5 seconds** (irregular — a metronome blink is a tell), blink itself = 3 drawings over ~4–6 frames (open → half → closed 1f → half → open), with a 1–2 px head dip on emphatic blinks.
3. **Movement accents = twos; impacts = ones for at most 4–8 frames.** A punch, snap-turn, or take reads *faster* if the anticipation before it was on threes/hold and the settle after returns to threes. Uniform twos everywhere reads *slower* (no contrast) and costs double.
4. **Never tween linearly across a long move.** Minimum viable spacing: ease-out (fast start, settle in) for entrances/points, ease-in-out for pans, and a **2–4 frame overshoot/settle** on stops. In Synfig/Blender terms: no linear interpolation mode on character tracks, ever; linear is for camera pans on BG only — and even those get eased ends on TV.
5. **Hold is a performance, not an absence:** a dialogue hold must contain at least two of {mouth, blink, gaze shift, secondary sway (2–3f loop at low amplitude), camera push ≤5%}. A hold with zero of these is the #1 amateur tell.
6. **Pose-to-pose, not path-to-path:** characters should *arrive* at a held pose, not continuously travel. If a part moves for >12 frames without a hold or accent, it will read as puppet slide — insert a mid-pose hold of 3–6 frames (a "moving hold" with secondary motion only).
7. **Bank your cycles:** walks, runs, laughs, nods = authored once as timed cycles (walk on twos, 8–12 frames/loop typical TV economy), reused via the bank — and vary them with camera/BG, as TV does, not by re-authoring.
8. **Sound leads picture by design:** mouth opens on the frame the syllable starts (on the nearest even frame), and the final open shape survives **+2 frames** past line end (G8). BG/FX cues are specified by *absolute frame number from cut start* on the timesheet and verified in the animatic — the specific fix for the known ~1 s-early BG defect (at 24 fps, 1 s = 24 rows of the sheet — impossible to miss in a frame-numbered check, invisible in a seconds-based cue list).
