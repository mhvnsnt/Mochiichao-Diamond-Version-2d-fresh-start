# Production research — Dragon Ball / Dragon Ball Z (Toei Animation)

Study layer for the Mochiichao show build. Sources: the five-series
production-research report compiled 2026-10-27. Prices and dates are
**as reported 2026-10-27**; the print core is Japanese.

## Production method (the headline)
**No rigs, and none shared — there is nothing to download.** DB
(1986) / DBZ (1989–96) are **hand-painted cel** production: animation
character designs by Minoru Maeda (early), then Katsuyoshi Nakatsuru
and chief designer Tadayoshi Yamamuro adapting Toriyama; key
drawings on paper, cels hand-painted, backgrounds hand-painted in
gouache, shot on film directed by **exposure sheets**. Later digital-
era Dragon Ball (Kai/Super/Daima) is still hand-drawn key animation
with digital paint. Game models (FighterZ/Kakarot) are game assets,
not the TV show's rigs, and are not officially shared.

## Free / public study sources
- ★ **Kanzenshuu** — https://www.kanzenshuu.com — the exemplary fan
  scholarly archive: complete Databook Guide, **free English
  translations of Daizenshuu Toriyama/staff interviews and the
  "Shenlong Times" pamphlets** (scenario writer Takao Koyama,
  producer Kōzō Morishita, composer Shunsuke Kikuchi, Yamamuro…),
  episode guides, and an **Animation Styles Guide** identifying which
  supervisor/animator drew which episode — essential for studying
  why DBZ's look shifts episode to episode.
- **Sakugabooru** — extensive DBZ `genga / production_materials`
  posts incl. supervisor-correction comparisons (Masayuki Sato,
  Katsuyoshi Nakatsuru, Masahiro Shimanuki) that teach the
  genga→correction workflow.
- **Heritage Auctions** — freely viewable lots pairing hand-painted
  production cels with matching animation drawings, gouache
  backgrounds and **studio exposure sheets** (Toei, c.1989–96).

## Study route (from the report)
1. Chōzenshū 2–3 (in print, Japanese) for anime design/episode
   material; Daizenshuu 3/5 second-hand for the original model-
   sheet pages.
2. Kanzenshuu translations + Animation Styles Guide (free, English).
3. *Golden Warrior* for character-designer method.
4. *A Visual History* (English) for Toriyama's manga art evolution —
   don't mistake it for anime settei.

**Bottom line (report): HIGH in Japanese print, MEDIUM in English.**
Published model-sheet material exists and is well documented;
English readers get superb translated *interviews* but not
translated settei volumes.
