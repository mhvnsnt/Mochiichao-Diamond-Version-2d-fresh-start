# #321 — THE COVERED FACE IN STORY → the shield is the face, and the camera knows it

**The gap this fills.** #317 made the covered face
anatomy; #320 made it expressive. This spec is the
**narrative** law — how stories and cameras are
allowed to treat a species whose face is partly
covered, before a writer reaches for the cheapest
structure in fiction: the unmasking.

**The plain law, first.** A grown face shield was
never *hidden*. It grew there, in the open, as the
individual grew. So: **you cannot reveal what was
never hidden.** No story structure may be built on
taking a grown covering off, peeking under it, or
treating what lies beneath as a secret the audience
is owed. The face beneath a muzzle-plate is a
muzzle. That is the whole mystery, and it is not
one.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/covered-face-story/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The camera treats the shield as the face.**
  Framing, eyelines, reaction shots, and close-ups
  address the covered face exactly as they address
  an open one — same shot grammar, same held beats
  (#320). A camera that lingers on a shield *as an
  obstacle* (shooting around it, treating the
  character as visually incomplete) violates this
  spec; so does lighting that hides covered faces
  in shadow as a default mood.
- **Forbidden structures (closed list):**
  - the **unmasking** — any beat whose payoff is a
    grown covering coming off (#317: the vocabulary
    has no such verb; a story may not invent one);
  - the **tease** — a covering presented as a
    mystery about identity or appearance ("what is
    he hiding?") — identity in this world is carried
    by the record and the deviation register (#318),
    never by a face;
  - the **reveal substitute** — a *different*
    individual's uncovered face used as a stand-in
    revelation for the species. The one lawful
    exception is the register: the maskless
    Verdilance's face is shown because it is **his
    face** — a named individual's own anatomy,
    presented as ordinary to him, never staged as
    the species' secret finally exposed. His scenes
    are shot on his face the way Bastihound's are
    shot on Bastihound's plate.
  - the **ceremonial removal** — helmets-off
    respect beats borrowed from human armour
    grammar. A Verdilance shows respect the way his
    line does (#265's formal etiquette: the bow,
    the stood-aside) — with the shield on, because
    the shield is him.
- **What a covered face may carry in story:**
  recognition (#319 — the shield is *how* a standard
  individual is generically known, and its absence
  is how one individual is specifically known),
  craft and care (a shield is groomed, polished,
  mended-in-prose like any grown part — #162's care
  layer touches it as anatomy, never as equipment:
  it is not taken off to be cleaned), age and
  history written on it as prose (a crack, a polish
  worn bright — an individual's history under #80's
  discipline, the Wispkin crack being the standing
  example of a mark that is meaning, not damage).
- **The human mirror, stated once:** humans in this
  world wear removable gear (#116's wardrobe sets).
  The contrast is available to stories — a human
  takes a hat off; a Verdilance *is* his crown and
  shield — but the contrast may never be staged as
  the covered character lacking something the human
  has.

## What the shield *means* — deliberately not here

Whether Verdilance's shield is honour, office,
adulthood, or simply what a finished Verdilance
looks like is **episode writing and canon**, owned
by the owner and the show lane. This system is the
staging law that any such meaning must be told
inside of; it instances no meaning.

## Wiring

Narrative rules seated with #82's arc planning and
#53's board review; keys off #317 (anatomy), #318
(the register exception), #320 (shot grammar),
#265 (etiquette forms). A board or script that
contains a forbidden structure fails review on this
spec's citation — the check is a read, not a tool.

## Open slots

Story/canon facts only: **the in-world meaning of
each covered species' covering** (Verdilance's
shield first) — the owner's and the episodes' to
write; any individual prose-history marks (cracks,
polish) on named covered individuals — prose under
#80, ruled case by case.

## Coverage — honest
