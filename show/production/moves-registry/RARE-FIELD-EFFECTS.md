# Rare Move Field Effects — what the scarce moves do outside battle

## ⚠️ DRAFT — OWNER REVIEW

**Nothing in this sheet is locked.** The source-games column (§a) is fact
carried from the master registry and the games it records. The game-lane
column (§b) states the games-model default and is flagged wherever it goes
beyond the owner's stated model. The show-lane column (§c) follows the
completed anime study and the locked two-lane doctrine. The owner locks or
amends any line with one word.

**The two lanes, in one breath (locked doctrine, 2026-10-27):** the GAME
handles moves the way the Pokémon *games* do — a machine system, found and
fought through the world, with field gating. The SHOW handles moves the way
the Pokémon *anime* does — field capability is body + job, obstacles are
puzzles solved by a move, a tool, or ingenuity, and **no route is ever hard-
gated on a field move**.

**Owner's stated acquisition model (the part that is already his):** General
Moves are found by searching and fighting through the world. Rare Moves are
the scarce class — they can also arrive pre-known on a creature, or through
alternate routes entirely (his Pokémon GO comparison). Everything in §b that
goes beyond that — obstacle types, party requirements, re-usability — is
**DRAFT default**, written so he has something concrete to amend.

**Source-games gating, system level (applies to every §a below):** in
Generations 1–6, using an HM move in the field required the matching gym
badge; Generation 7 retired HMs in favour of Ride Pokémon; Generation 8's
BDSP moved the field moves into Pokétch apps with no badge gate; Generation
9 has no HM system (the moves survive as ordinary TMs). Machine history per
move below is quoted from its registry row.

## 1. Slice (source: Cut) — NORMAL, Physical, 50 / 95 / 30

**§a Source games.** Cuts down small trees blocking paths (and tall grass in
later generations). HM01 in Gens 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and BDSP (Gen 8) — an HM in
every generation that had one, which is why rare-wins classes it Rare without
needing the note.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Slender trees and brush-walls are Slice gates: the
party needs a Mochi that knows Slice to pass. Acquisition per the owner's
scarcity model; once known, the gate stays open permanently. *Beyond the
stated model:* the specific obstacle set (trees vs. brush) and permanent-open
rule are draft defaults.

**§c Show lane.** Cutting is a working action, not a key. A Mochi with
cutting anatomy does it as a job — and the party's first answer to brush is
whatever is at hand (the anime solved brush with Razor Leaf, not Cut).
*Scene use:* a fallen, overgrown path to the Garden Gate needs clearing
before a delivery arrives — Sproutle's Leaf Blast trims it back in a working
montage, and Slice is never named, because the scene is about the work, not
the lock.

## 2. Mist Clear (source: Defog) — FLYING, Status, — / — / 15

**§a Source games.** Clears fog from foggy areas (and, in battle, hazards
and screens). HM05 in Gen 4 (Sinnoh) and BDSP only — the joint-narrowest HM
run of the eleven; Rare on that history alone.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Fog banks are Mist Clear gates in the regions that
have them: visibility, not passage, is what's gated — *draft default:* a fog
area can be entered without it at reduced visibility / higher encounter
pressure, and Mist Clear makes it fully navigable. That soft-gate shape is
this sheet's own draft proposal, flagged for the owner precisely because it
differs from the other ten.

**§c Show lane.** Mist is mood before it is mechanics. A winged Mochi's
wingbeats thin it because wings move air — capability, not a taught field
move. *Scene use:* in the Dark Forest, a Mochi's wingbeats open a brief
window in the mist that lets the party glimpse a landmark and get their
bearings — then the mist closes again. The Forest's dread is never dispelled
by a technique; the window is all anyone gets.

## 3. Deep Dive (source: Dive) — WATER, Physical, 80 / 100 / 10

**§a Source games.** Dives at deep-water spots to reach underwater areas,
and surfaces again. HM in Gen 3 (HM08), Gen 5 (HM06), Gen 6 (HM07); a TM
listing in BDSP (TM45) does not change its class (rare-wins).

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Deep-water dive spots are Deep Dive gates: a party
Mochi that knows it can take the Ranger down to underwater map pockets.
Acquisition scarce per the owner's model — *draft default:* this is a prime
candidate for his "arrives pre-known" route (a water Mochi joins already
knowing it).

**§c Show lane.** Nobody orders "Dive" — water creatures dive because they
are water creatures (the anime study found no verified named field use of
Dive, ever). *Scene use:* something precious drops into the deep end of the
Pond; Dorowani is simply gone after it, no command, no move name — the
camera holds on the ripples, and the scene plays on what he brings back up.

## 4. Illuminate (source: Flash) — NORMAL, Status, — / 100 / 20

**§a Source games.** Lights up dark caves. HM05 in Gens 1, 2, 3; demoted to
TM70 in Gens 4, 5, 6 — the textbook rare-wins case: HM history wins over the
later TM listings, and Illuminate is doubly anchored because the name is
already canon in Mochiichao as the Flash-equivalent technique.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Dark areas (caves, the Dark Forest's deep paths)
are Illuminate gates in the games-model sense: *draft default:* entry is
possible in darkness with a shrunken view radius; Illuminate restores full
vision. Like Mist Clear, this is drafted as a soft gate (vision, not
passage) for the owner's decision — the source games hard-gate nothing with
Flash either; they only darken the screen.

**§c Show lane.** The anime used Flash for its field purpose exactly once
(Clair's Dragonair lighting the way to the Dragon's Den) and otherwise
reached for light sources — a flashlight, a Braixen's flame. Our lane keeps
both: a Mochi's glow is a presence, and Mochiko's lamp is a tool, and either
can carry a scene. *Scene use:* the EP01 stinger's held darkness in the Dark
Forest is broken not by a technique-as-key but by Wispkin's own faint
presence — light as character, not as unlock.

## 5. Ascend (source: Fly) — FLYING, Physical, 90 / 95 / 15

**§a Source games.** Flies the player to any previously visited location —
the fast-travel HM. HM02 in Gens 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and BDSP; TM listings in
Gens 7 and 9 stay in its history without touching its class.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Ascend is the fast-travel unlock: once a party
Mochi knows it, the Ranger can return to any visited Garden/town node.
*draft defaults beyond the stated model:* the visited-node restriction and
the one-Mochi-knows-it party rule. Acquisition: the owner's model makes this
a signature scarce find — fought through to, or arriving pre-known on a
flying Mochi.

**§c Show lane.** Flight is riding and partnership, never a named travel
move: the anime's Ash travels on Charizard's and Pidgeot's backs, and Sun &
Moon formalised it as Ride Pokémon (Kiawe's Charizard flies deliveries as a
family job). *Scene use:* a Ranger and a flying Mochi make a dawn delivery
run together — the flight is shot as shared work and trust, the destination
reached because this Mochi *does this*, not because a move was spent.

## 6. Crag Climb (source: Rock Climb) — NORMAL, Physical, 90 / 85 / 20

**§a Source games.** Scales marked rocky walls. HM08 in Gen 4 (Sinnoh) and
BDSP only — with Defog, the narrowest HM run; Rare on that history.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Marked rock faces are Crag Climb gates to ledges,
shortcuts, and high pockets. *Draft default:* gates are one-way-up; descent
is free by other paths. Acquisition scarce per the owner's model.

**§c Show lane.** Climbing is a body doing what it is built for — the anime's
first use of a then-current HM move by a main character's Pokémon was
Grotle's Rock Climb, arriving with no teaching scene and no item, a full
decade in. *Scene use:* a sure-footed Mochi scrambles up a cliff face
carrying a rope, then braces it so the humans can climb — capability plus
ingenuity, the anime's two-step in one beat.

## 7. Boulder Break (source: Rock Smash) — FIGHTING, Physical, 40 / 100 / 15

**§a Source games.** Smashes cracked rocks blocking paths (sometimes
revealing items or wild encounters). A TM in Gen 2 (TM08), then HM06 in Gens
3, 4, 6 and BDSP, a TM again in Gen 5 (TM94, B2/W2), Gen 6 (TM94 alongside
the HM) and Gen 9 — the busiest dual history of the eleven, and the owner's
own worked example of rare-wins.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Cracked boulders are Boulder Break gates: breakable
only by a Mochi that knows it; *draft defaults:* broken rocks stay broken,
and some hide items or encounters, as in the source games. Acquisition per
the owner's scarcity model.

**§c Show lane.** An ordinary move a strong Mochi simply knows, used
utilitarianly (the anime's Gible smashed a cage open with it — a breakout,
not a route key). *Scene use:* storm debris — a cracked boulder — blocks the
Garden path at dawn rounds; Gabu breaks it up as morning work, half play,
and the smaller pieces get stacked into the path edging by the others. Work,
not gating.

## 8. Mighty Push (source: Strength) — NORMAL, Physical, 80 / 100 / 15

**§a Source games.** Moves heavy boulders — onto switches, into holes, out
of the way; the classic HM puzzle verb. HM04 in Gens 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and
BDSP.

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Heavy boulders are Mighty Push objects: movable
only by a Mochi that knows it, in push-puzzle configurations (block a vent,
weigh a switch, clear a lane). *Draft defaults:* boulders reset when the
area reloads (the source games' puzzle behaviour); whether Mochiichao keeps
that or makes pushes permanent is an owner call, flagged here.

**§c Show lane.** Strength on screen is a strong creature being strong — the
anime's Happiny lifts boulders as a character trait and "Strength" is never
even named. *Scene use:* Gabu shoulders a boulder aside to free a trapped
smaller Mochi — shot as effort and gentleness (he sets it down carefully),
and nobody calls it a move, because for him it isn't one; it's just Gabu.

## 9. Wave (source: Surf) — WATER, Special, 90 / 100 / 15

**§a Source games.** Crosses water, carrying the player on a Pokémon's back
— the defining travel HM. HM03 in Gens 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and BDSP; TM
listings in Gens 7 and 9 recorded, class unchanged (rare-wins).

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Open water is a Wave gate: shores and docks are
entry points, and a party Mochi that knows Wave carries the Ranger across
lakes and along rivers to otherwise unreachable banks. Acquisition: scarce —
*draft default:* a flagship "search and fight through the world" reward or a
pre-known arrival, per the owner's model.

**§c Show lane.** The anime's answer is the ferry, not the move: Lapras
carries the party for an entire arc and Bulbapedia's editors insist it
"never used Surf" — it swims, and the humans ride; Sun & Moon made it a
service (Ride Pokémon). *Scene use:* Dorowani ferries Mochiko across the
Pond on morning rounds as an established routine — the crossing is
companionship and local geography, and Wave the name belongs to battle.

## 10. Cascade (source: Waterfall) — WATER, Physical, 80 / 100 / 15

**§a Source games.** Climbs waterfalls in the field. HM in Gens 2 (HM07), 3
(HM07), 4 (HM07), 5 (HM05), 6 (HM05) and BDSP; TM listings in Gens 7 and 9
recorded, class unchanged (rare-wins). Registry stats are carried from the
Black 2 decomp (see the completion audit, §4).

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Waterfall faces are Cascade gates: climbable only
with a Mochi that knows it, opening the high ground above falls. *Draft
default:* paired with Wave — most falls sit in water that already needs
Wave to reach — mirroring the source games' one-two lock; whether
Mochiichao keeps the pairing or lets Cascade stand alone is flagged for the
owner.

**§c Show lane.** The anime's waterfall episode is the doctrine in miniature:
stranded Wailmer, tall waterfall, and nobody teaches or uses Waterfall —
Ash engineers an **ice ramp**, puts Wailmer on a sled, and the whole party
pushes. *Scene use:* the party needs to get above the falls at the Pond's
edge; the solution staged is ramps, ropes, and a water Mochi swimming the
shallows alongside — the falls are a puzzle the group solves, never a door
Cascade unlocks.

## 11. Vortex (source: Whirlpool) — WATER, Special, 35 / 85 / 15

**§a Source games.** Crosses and calms whirlpools blocking water routes.
HM06 in Gen 2 (Johto), HM05 in Gen 4 (HGSS); TM listings in BDSP-era Gen 8
and Gen 9 recorded, class unchanged (rare-wins).

**§b Game lane — DRAFT.** Whirlpools are Vortex gates on water routes: a
Mochi that knows it stills or parts them for safe passage. As with Cascade,
*draft default:* most whirlpool routes presuppose Wave for the water itself;
the pairing is flagged for the owner rather than assumed silently.

**§c Show lane.** A whirlpool on screen is water behaving dangerously, and
the party reads it, routes around it, or waits it out — seamanship, not a
counter-move. *Scene use:* a whirlpool guards a drifting, lost object the
party needs; a strong swimmer times the rotation and rides the edge in and
out — the beat is nerve and timing, and Vortex is never spoken.

1. The §b obstacle sets (which world objects gate on which Rare Move).
2. The two soft gates (Mist Clear, Illuminate — vision gated, passage not)
   versus hard gates like the other nine.
