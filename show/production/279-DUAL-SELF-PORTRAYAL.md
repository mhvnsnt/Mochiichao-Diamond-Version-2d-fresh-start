# #279 — DUAL-SELF PORTRAYAL → one person, two modes, shown and never explained

**The brief (owner, 2026-10-27).** The thief lead of
#278 carries a **split personality** in his inspiration
mix (Yugi's two selves — inspiration only; the
character, his sides, and what they are is original
Mochiichao canon, and its nature is the owner's story
fact, OPEN below). This spec is not the psychology; it
is the **portrayal grammar**: how a show and a game
present one character with two modes so the audience
always knows which mode they are watching, the world
reacts to the difference, and nothing ever has to stop
and explain it.

**The voice law binds this spec first.** Creatures are
pure cries and sounds only — no word dialogue, no
name-saying, in any lane. A dual-self story is exactly
the kind of story that tempts a production into letting
a creature "react in words" to the change. Forbidden,
at format level: the Mochi's reading of the two modes
is staged as **behaviour and cry** — posture, approach,
refusal, #136's readable states (`distress`, `warning`,
`refusal`, `contentment`…) — read by humans and
audience the way #136 taught them to read. If a beat
needs a creature to *say* which self it fears, the
beat is rewritten, not subtitled.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/dual-self/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **One character record, two presentation modes.**
  The character is ONE person in every system: one #98
  identity, one licence, one List 28 ladder state, one
  #108 personality seed if he is ever an individual in
  game data — **the mode is never a stat split, a second
  record, or a second reputation.** What he does in
  either mode is done by him: #272's witnesses saw *him*;
  #270's groups judge *him*. (This is the design's whole
  moral weight, kept mechanical on purpose: the ladder
  does not accept "the other one did it.")
- **Mode is a production annotation.** In show planning
  (#82's arc-plan family), a beat or scene that features
  him carries `mode` — which presentation is on screen.
  Mode is written like #82's entry/exit state: the beat
  record states the mode it opens in, any switch inside
  it is a staged beat (a visible transition — the
  production chooses his tells: posture, dress cue,
  staging, sound — authored design, OPEN as content),
  and the mode it hands on. A switch that happens
  off-screen between beats is a record error, the way
  an unrecorded state change is an error everywhere
  else in this programme: **the audience may be
  surprised by a switch; they may never be confused
  about which mode they are looking at afterwards.**
- **The world reads the difference.** Humans respond to
  the mode they meet (a #93 interaction fact: the same
  NPC's greeting texture differs by mode — the record
  keys the interaction to the person, the staging keys
  its tone to the mode). **Mochi read him plainly:**
  creatures do not know his story; they know his
  behaviour — a Mochi that trusts one mode's hands and
  flinches from the other's is stating a #152-class
  behaviour fact (reads from conduct, the #136
  discipline), and production must earn it the same
  way: the flinch is staged only where conduct has been
  shown to differ. The creatures are the part's honest
  mirror precisely because they cannot be told which
  self is which.
- **What a mode may change, closed list:** presentation
  (staging, tells, interaction tone) and **which doors
  List 28 opens** — an underworld contact who deals
  only with the mode he knows is reading #270/#274
  facts about the person through the mode the contact
  has met; the record underneath is one. What a mode
  may never change: stats, seed, ladder position,
  record history, creature data of any kind.

## Game mirror

If the lead is playable (itself an open canon fact,
#278), mode is a **presentation state of the player
character** with the same closed effects list: staging
and door-reads, never a build, never a second save
identity — #146's one payload holds one person, and a
mode toggle writes nothing the ladder hasn't already
written by deed.

## Wiring

Show: #82 (mode annotated in arc/episode planning),
#136 (the readable-states vocabulary his Mochi answer
in), #152 (behaviour reads), #93 (interaction tone).
Game: #98/#108 (one identity, one seed), List 28
#270/#272/#274 (one ladder, witnesses and doors read
the person). Explicit non-joins: no creature dialogue
machinery, no second reputation track for a mode, no
#149 screen treatment of mode (the device identifies
the person, once).

## Open slots

**What the two selves ARE — their nature, their names
if they carry separate ones, which is "his" — story/
canon facts, the owner's and the show lane's writing,
entire.** His switch tells and mode designs are
authored production content. No mechanic is open: the
one-record rule and the closed effects list are the
system, and they are complete with the canon slot
empty.

## Coverage — honest

- Architecture: portrayal grammar only, seated on
  verified show machinery (#82/#136/#152) and the
  one-record laws (#98/#108/#146).
- Contents: zero modes designed. This package rules
  HOW a dual self is shown; it does not decide what
  his two selves are — that decision is visibly,
  deliberately, not here.
