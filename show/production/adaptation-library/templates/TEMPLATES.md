# TEMPLATE EXTRACTION — reusable show templates, Mochiichao-mapped

*Every template: SOURCE → STRUCTURE → MOCHIICHAO MAPPING.
Canon enforced in every mapping: creatures make PURE CRIES only
(no words, no name-saying); meaning via performance + rationed
silent-film SUBTITLES (max ~3/episode, spent at earned moments);
humans (Mochiko, Prof. Aeon, Hex) speak; Hex is a Ranger and
Mochiko a keeper; the Secret stays buried (Aeon knows; written
material never teaches it to the audience as exposition — it is
rationed per the bible's reveal-order); names are Dark Forest /
Iridescence canon. Settings: Garden Gate, Garden Green, Garden
Pond, the Treeline, the Dark Forest (presence only — camera never
enters), Aeon's lab, the First Route.*

---

## I. EPISODE SHAPES

### E1 — THE SIX-BEAT MYSTERY (garden-sized)
- **Source:** Scooby-Doo 1969 6-beat formula, documented in
  mystery-coast-ghosted/docs/research/SCOOBY-DOO-1969-PIPELINE.md;
  already Mochiichao's locked engine (EP01, EP05).
- **Structure:** 1 Arrival (the day begins ordinary) → 2 Oddity (one
  wrong detail, small and specific) → 3 Investigation (the keeper
  asks, looks, counts) → 4 False scare (the wrong explanation,
  staged at full menace) → 5 Reveal (the truth is a resident doing
  a job / a need unmet) → 6 Board/Tag (the keeper board records it;
  a rule is learned).
- **Mochiichao mapping:** EP01 (Wispkin) and EP05 (the cold spot)
  are the reference builds. The barometer (from the bible-craft
  study): *the reveal must prove the creature was never the
  problem.* Any Season-1 episode can be poured into this shape;
  the Forest thread advances ONLY inside beat 2 or beat 6, never
  as the reveal.

### E2 — THE FLAW-FORMULA EPISODE
- **Source:** cape-jones SHOW-BIBLE (the owner's own engine):
  premises printed as **want → attempt → flaw-failure → tag**;
  Johnny Bravo S2 bible's story barometer is the same law at
  network scale (story must be driven by the lead; every action
  traces to base motives).
- **Structure:** A character wants one concrete thing → pursues it
  with their signature method → the method's built-in flaw is what
  stops them (not an obstacle, not a villain) → tag: they get a
  smaller, truer version of the want.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The creature episodes. Gabu wants to be
  the escort/hero → Gabu's method (all confidence, chest out) is
  what strands him → tag: the day needed exactly his stubbornness
  after all. Sproutle wants the long way round to count as the
  short way. Kittember wants to be taken seriously as the Garden's
  sensor. Formula notation goes in the beat sheet header so any
  writer can check a premise in one line.

### E3 — THE CREATURE-HOUSE DAY
- **Source:** Foster's Home episode engine, catalogued in
  TRIPPEDD's public-source animation-study CATALOG (the house IS
  the cast's society; episodes generate from house rules, new
  arrivals, and resident friction).
- **Structure:** A house rule / arrival / rota change perturbs the
  household → each resident's way of life collides with it in one
  scene apiece → the keeper must choose between the rule and the
  resident → the house absorbs the change; the rota/board shows it.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The Garden is the house; the keeper
  board is the rota. Natural home for equal-shine rotation
  episodes (bible §4): the featured species is the resident whose
  way of life the change touches. Pairs with E1 when the change
  arrives as an oddity.

### E4 — THE GARDEN-ECONOMY SCHEME (scam-of-the-week, softened)
- **Source:** Ed, Edd n Eddy engine (binder outline format: one
  defining mechanism per character; a scheme in three escalating
  attempts) — re-skinned from "scam" to "scheme," because the
  Garden's economy is care and trade, not marks and quarters.
- **Structure:** Somebody wants a thing the Garden doesn't simply
  give → a scheme with a printed plan (the plan is the comedy:
  it is always one step too many) → three attempts, each failing
  BIGGER and more mechanically → collapse → the thing arrives by
  the Garden's own logic (Rule 12 staged: it belonged to somebody
  all along).
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Gabu + Kittember as scheme partners;
  Mochii's bounce as the uncontrolled variable; Mochiko never the
  mark — she is the economy's referee and learns the scheme last.
  Outline discipline transfers verbatim: all-caps location
  headings, present-tense action, SFX words inline.

### E5 — CHORE → COSMIC ESCALATION
- **Source:** Regular Show engine (production-docs in the owner's
  repo; structure confirmed from public transcripts — see
  sources/gag-engines/).
- **Structure:** A mundane chore stated in one line → done lazily
  or competitively → ONE surreal jump (no ladder of weirdness —
  a single cut from garden-scale to cosmic-scale stakes) → the
  chore completed at cosmic scale → a song or a held quiet beat
  as the button.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Watering Sproutle → the sprinkler
  becomes a weather event over the Pond. Ration: ONE per season
  band — escalation is a spice; the Garden's default register is
  small and true. The Forest NEVER supplies the surreal jump
  (Forest = dread, not comedy escalation).

### E6 — THE LAB BACKFIRE / TESTS ON THE TESTER
- **Source:** Dexter's-Lab invention-backfire grammar + the
  owner's johnny-test-parody inversion (the tests are run ON
  the tester).
- **Structure:** An experiment with a stated, sensible goal → the
  invention works EXACTLY as designed, which is the problem →
  escalation by literal-mindedness → the authority figure ends
  as the test subject → reset, with one souvenir kept.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Aeon's episodes. His data tablet and
  the keeper device are the invention surface; the creatures'
  literal-mindedness is the engine; Aeon ends wearing the
  experiment. Doubles as the show's way to teach a game system
  (care loop, ring game) without a tutorial scene.

### E7 — THE INTERVIEW SEGMENT (in-episode talk format)
- **Source:** Space Ghost Coast to Coast production format
  (studied via mystery-coast research; production files in the
  owner's space-ghost repo): held poses, mouth-only animation,
  the host's questions are the writing; the guest's answers are
  recontextualized.
- **Structure:** Desk → guest entrance with ceremony → three
  questions, the third one wrong in a way that reveals → a
  musical/sting break → the guest leaves having changed nothing
  and revealed everything.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** A segment format INSIDE episodes, not
  a standalone show: Dorowani (Manual in hand, grave courtesy)
  interviews a resident at the Pond desk-stone; the resident
  answers in cries; Dorowani's follow-ups translate by reaction,
  never by subtitle — except the segment's ONE allowed subtitle,
  which is the punchline and the feeling at once. Cheapest
  producible format in the library (HB method: holds + mouths).

### E8 — THE COMPETITION / TOURNAMENT DAY
- **Source:** OK K.O. hero-plaza tournament grammar + Powerpuff
  11-minute rescue format (owner's repos) + the show's own EP03
  ring game and EP04 dojo sparring drafts.
- **Structure:** The contest is announced with its custom (rules
  stated as CUSTOM, not exposition) → entrants' styles contrasted
  in warm-ups → the contest in three exchanges, each exchange
  teaching one rule → the result is a draw or a redefinition of
  winning → the board records names, not scores.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Already the EP03/EP04 spine — this
  template codifies it for reuse: ring game, dojo sparring, a
  Garden race day. The Garden's law travels with it: stakes are
  honour and a board mark, never harm; nobody keeps score because
  the board has no column for wins.

### E9 — THE SERIALIZED WEAVE (arc rationing)
- **Source:** Scooby-Doo: Mystery Incorporated (owner's repo is
  his HB template base; structure also sampled publicly — see
  sources/anime-mystery/) + Amphibia's reveal-order discipline
  (canon truth vs. air order, from the TVA pitch bibles).
- **Structure:** A complete standalone episode (any shape above)
  + exactly ONE arc ration, placed in beat 2 (as an oddity) or
  beat 6 (as a tag), sized so a viewer who skips it loses
  nothing this week and a viewer who keeps it feels the season
  breathing.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The Forest thread and the Mochiioteca /
  keeper-device thread. EP05 is the reference build: three nights
  of small wrongness, one dividend of dread, no answers. The
  bible's reveal-order page governs placement; the ration is
  never the episode's climax in Season 1.

### E10 — THE MIXED-MEDIA EPISODE (one-off)
- **Source:** Gumball's mixed-media format (bible + comp guide
  in owner's repo): every character keeps their own medium and
  the format never apologizes for it.
- **Structure:** An ordinary episode shape (usually E3) played
  straight, with ONE production rule changed: each species
  rendered in a different medium for the whole episode (pixel,
  generative render, clay-photo, paper cutout).
- **Mochiichao mapping:** A special, rationed like E5. Wispkin
  as paper cutout and Kittember as clay-photo are the proven
  pairings from the pilot work. The episode's story must be
  about being seen differently, or the device is decoration.

## II. SCENE TEMPLATES

### S1 — RIVAL CONFRONTATION (the face-off)
- **Source:** cape-jones bible's rival slot + the show's EP04
  draft (Hex's formal challenge) + JoJo menace grammar as
  sampled publicly (sources/anime-mystery/) — threat is staged
  as STILLNESS and framing, not volume.
- **Structure:** Arrival exactly on time, kit laid out in a line
  → the challenge stated formally, in few words → the mirror
  shot (same location grammar as the hero's arrival, opposite
  keeper) → PRESENT poses exchanged (Menace held a beat too
  long) → the confrontation defers to the custom (the bout, the
  contest, dinner) — the face-off is a promise, not the fight.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Hex and Dorowani at the Gate. Hex
  speaks; Dorowani answers in a cry and perfect form. Mochiko's
  side is staged looser on purpose — the Garden's looseness is
  its style of confidence.

### S2 — THE QUIET TWO-HANDER (keeper + one creature)
- **Source:** The two-hander mechanics sampled from Avatar /
  Gravity Falls (sources/mystery-serialized/) + the owner's own
  EP05 "ask the night keeper" build.
- **Structure:** One location, dusk or night → the human asks a
  real question in plain words → the creature's answer is a
  GUIDED ACTION (it shows, leads, demonstrates — never tells) →
  the episode's one subtitle is spent HERE if it is spent
  anywhere → the human repeats the creature's action badly,
  then adequately → held quiet beat; the scene ends on the
  shared action, not a line.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Mochiko + Wispkin (night rounds),
  Mochiko + Sproutle (the dawn sequence), Aeon + Dorowani (the
  Manual read together). This is the show's prestige scene —
  budget silence for it: two-handers carry the fewest cuts in
  any episode they appear in.

### S3 — CHASE MECHANICS (three-attempt geometry)
- **Source:** Looney Tunes / PD shorts gag construction
  (animation-research-library TECHNIQUES.md: hold-then-snap,
  smear frames, ones-vs-twos) + Pink Panther pantomime archive.
- **Structure:** The chase is a GEOMETRY, not a scramble: a
  declared route → attempt one (direct) fails by the route's
  own rule → attempt two (clever) fails by the same rule read
  differently → attempt three succeeds by ACCEPTING the rule →
  freeze or snap-cut on the acceptance pose.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Garden-scale only: the First Route,
  the Green, around the Pond. Mochii's bounce vs. Gabu's straight
  line vs. Kittember's shortcut over the wall — each species'
  movement grammar is a chase rule. No chase enters the Forest.

### S4 — THE REVEAL SCENE (never the problem)
- **Source:** Beat 5 of E1, specified by the Johnny Bravo
  barometer as filtered through the bible-craft study.
- **Structure:** The accused/strange resident is found IN the
  act → the act is re-framed by ONE new piece of staging (a
  chalk line, a painted name, a kept roster) → the keeper says
  the small true sentence (humans) or the subtitle lands
  (creature) → the board is updated in view of the audience.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Wispkin's night-keeper reveal (EP01)
  is the reference build. Every later reveal rhymes with it
  staging-wise: same board, same hand, new name.

### S5 — TRAINING MONTAGE (music-led)
- **Source:** Dojo-preparation grammar (EP04 draft) + training
  structure sampled from Avatar / Pokémon (sources/) + HB
  timing charts.
- **Structure:** A stated drill → three rounds, each round one
  gag or one grace (alternate) → the creature's cry becomes the
  rhythm track (cries as percussion, per the show's sound
  design) → final round played straight, no gag → the drill
  done imperfectly and sufficiently.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Dorowani's form drills (grace rounds)
  vs. Mochii's water play (gag rounds) in the same montage —
  two training philosophies intercut, neither mocked.

### S6 — THE PRESENTATION (ceremony of equals)
- **Source:** The owner's own EP02 build (the Choosing), codified.
- **Structure:** Candidates presented one at a time, each given
  the SAME full measure — same framing, same held beat, a real
  PRESENT pose and a real moment → the chooser's reaction is the
  only asymmetry allowed → the choice lands on fit, stated in
  as few words as possible → the unchosen keep full dignity and
  a place in the episode's tag.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Reuse shape for: new resident arrivals,
  dojo entrants, Garden race line-ups. The law travels: *nobody
  is presented as the wrong answer.*

## III. GAG MECHANISMS (mechanisms, not quips — house law)

- **G1 HOLD-THEN-SNAP** (Dover Boys / PD study): a pose held two
  beats past comfort, then movement in a single snap. The hold
  is the joke's engine; the snap is its release. Default timing
  for PRESENT poses.
- **G2 SMEAR + 2s/3s** (HB limited-animation method): fast
  action on smears, dialogue and reaction on 2s/3s, never
  blanket-interpolated. Also a budget law: holds are cheap and
  funny; motion is spent where it means.
- **G3 THE SILENCE GAG** (owner's own EP01 cricket beat): after
  an earned line or subtitle, a held beat of garden ambience —
  no music sting. Rationed: silence only works if the show is
  usually alive (Living World law).
- **G4 PROP GAGS WITH RULES** (keeper board, Gabu's net-hat,
  Dorowani's Manual, the gate bell): a recurring prop accrues
  ONE rule per appearance (the board has no column for wins;
  the bell rings for arrivals, so a bell with no arrival is an
  oddity). The rules are the running gag's ledger.
- **G5 THE RUNNING-GAG LEDGER** (cape-jones canon-gag log
  pattern): every running gag is logged with its appearances
  and its payoff owed. A gag may repeat three times as pattern,
  the fourth must turn (payoff, inversion, or retirement).
- **G6 CUTAWAY RATIONING** (Kim Possible S4 memo's pacing law,
  inverted for our register): network memo says faster gags,
  more cutaways; Mochiichao's cutaways are rare and always to a
  REACTION (a resident watching), never to a reference. If a
  cutaway needs explaining, it is cut.
- **G7 PANTOMIME FIRST** (Pink Panther archive): any gag that
  can be played wordlessly by a creature IS played wordlessly.
  Words are the humans' tool; creature comedy is physical or
  it is nothing.
- **G8 THE BAROMETER TEST** (Johnny Bravo bible): every gag is
  checked against the character's base motives — a gag the
  character wouldn't generate is rewritten, whoever wrote it.

## IV. POP-CULTURE REFERENCE SLOTS (owner/archive-derived)

*A slot is a famous beat SHAPE re-skinned: the reference gives
the audience the pleasure of recognition; the scene must play
perfectly for a viewer who has never seen the original.*

### R1 — THE LATE-NIGHT DESK
- **Reference:** the late-night talk-show desk interview (via
  Space Ghost C2C's recontextualized-interview format, owner's
  archive).
- **Why it lands:** ceremony + incongruity — a grave host, a
  guest who cannot be interviewed in any normal way, questions
  that miss with total confidence.
- **Mochiichao version:** Template E7 — Dorowani at the Pond
  desk-stone; the guest answers in cries; one subtitle per
  segment, and it is never the answer to the question asked.

### R2 — THE UNMASKING THAT ISN'T
- **Reference:** the Scooby unmasking (the monster is a person
  in a costume) — inverted.
- **Why it lands:** the audience's genre expectation does the
  work; the inversion pays it off as warmth instead of capture.
- **Mochiichao version:** Beat 5 of E1 — the "monster" is
  unmasked as a resident doing necessary work (Wispkin's night
  rounds). Never played with an actual villain in Season 1.

### R3 — THE FACE-OFF / WEIGH-IN
- **Reference:** the sports/wrestling pre-match face-off —
  two camps, a measured space, staredowns, the crowd's hush.
- **Why it lands:** ritual seriousness applied to play stakes
  is inherently funny and inherently stirring at once.
- **Mochiichao version:** Template S1 at the Pond ring —
  Hex's measured strides vs. the Garden's looseness; the hush
  is real (G3 silence), the stakes are a board mark.

## V. FROM THE PUBLIC GAG ENGINES (SpongeBob / Regular Show transcripts)

### E11 — THE ESCALATION LADDER (counted)
- **Source:** Regular Show "Just Set Up the Chairs" + "High Score";
  SpongeBob "Pizza Delivery" (sources/gag-engines/). All three
  mundane-premise episodes climb exactly **7 escalation steps**;
  the competition episodes climb 6 with a montage as step 5.
- **Structure:** Step count is the template. Each step must (a)
  raise the cost, (b) be caused by the previous step's solution,
  (c) keep the ORIGINAL task visible (the missing chairs are
  spotted mid-battle; the cargo is never eaten). Final step:
  **flaw-as-weapon reversal** — the trait that caused the trouble
  is the trait that ends it.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The precise form of E5. Garden ladders
  run 5 steps, not 7 (our runtime and register are gentler), and
  step (c) is canon law: the care task stays in frame — Mochii
  finishes the watering DURING the weather event, not after it.

### E12 — THE BROADCAST FRAME (competition variant)
- **Source:** "The Fry Cook Games" — the whole episode is staged
  as a sports broadcast: torch ceremony (fails twice, "please
  stand by" card, announcer unbothered), events scored by a
  HOUSE RULE (presentation as food), deadpan commentary over
  catastrophe, a tie delivered by montage, and a final stopped
  not by victory but by a **sentimental wardrobe reveal**.
- **Structure:** Frame first (ceremony + commentator grammar) →
  insult-duel cold break between friends → bosses cross-cut
  radicalizing each side → event ladder → montage tie → final
  event interrupted by sentiment → pair exits together, no
  medal.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The EP04 dojo-sparring upgrade path:
  Dorowani can carry deadpan "commentary" as reaction staging
  (it narrates by Manual, in cries + exactly one subtitle);
  the house scoring rule = FORM (Dorowani scores by finished
  pictures; Mochii scores by joy, and the Garden's custom counts
  both). The sentimental stop replaces any win: the bout ends
  because somebody's person is cold / hungry / called in —
  the Garden's priorities interrupt the contest, which is the
  show's thesis in miniature.

### Additional gag mechanisms (gag-engines cluster)
- **G9 STATUS STACKING (three-beat):** a respect deficit is
  stacked in exactly three quick beats (authority → stranger →
  peer) before the premise launches — humiliation, not a
  monster, is the inciting incident. For Hex: the stack is what
  his formality defends against.
- **G10 SONG-AS-PROGRESS-BAR:** one tune re-sung in decaying
  styles to mark exhaustion/time (Pizza Delivery). Mochiichao
  form: a creature's cry-motif re-performed slower/rougher
  across a long task — sound design carries the clock.
- **G11 CARGO-INTEGRITY RULE:** a stated rule ("the customer
  gets it whole") is tested at every step and NEVER broken —
  the tension is the rule's survival. Garden form: the berry /
  the board / the lantern — a fragile charge carried through
  an episode.
- **G12 WARNING-IGNORED RULE:** an explicit, legible warning
  (a note, a chalk line, a painted sign) is shown to the
  audience BEFORE it is ignored — the laugh/dread is prepaid.
  EP05's chalk line at the Pond is already this mechanism in
  the dread register.
- **G13 ANTI-CLIMAX AUTHORITY TAG:** after the climax, the
  authority figure nearly grants the reward, then revokes it
  for a petty visible reason — the tag lands on the revocation,
  not the victory. Aeon variant: the revocation is kind, and
  the reason is a Garden rule the audience already knows.
- **G14 RESPECT-AS-CURRENCY:** esteem is tracked like score —
  won in public, lost in public, spent at the climax. The
  keeper board is the show's ledger prop for exactly this
  (G4's rules accrue: the board can record REGARD, not wins).

### Reference slots (gag-engines cluster)
### R4 — THE OLYMPICS BROADCAST, DOMESTICATED
- **Reference:** Olympic broadcast grammar (torch, cauldron,
  podium, scored dives) with workplace apparatus substituted 1:1.
- **Why it lands:** the grammar is universally legible; the
  substitution is the joke and the sincerity at once.
- **Mochiichao version:** E12's frame at the Pond ring — a
  lantern instead of a torch, the board instead of a podium,
  PRESENT poses scored as pictures. No event names, no anthems,
  no source apparatus beyond the grammar itself.
### R5 — THE RECORD-HOLDER (abstract slot ONLY)
- **Reference:** the arcade record-chaser documentary archetype
  — an obsessive holder who treats a leaderboard as a life's
  work; stakes inflation (world → universe) belongs to him.
- **Why it lands:** monomania played totally straight.
- **Mochiichao version:** a creature who holds the Garden's one
  unofficial record (longest balance on the gate post) and
  defends it with cosmic seriousness. ABSTRACT ONLY — the
  sampled source's holder is a parody of a real, litigated
  person; the figure, name, and design are on the do-not-adapt
  list. Only the archetype's SHAPE transfers.

## VI. FROM THE POKÉMON ANIME (verified transcripts)

### S7 — THE SHELTER OFFER (bond turn)
- **Source:** Pokémon EP001 — the hero offers the partner its
  ball as SHELTER, not control, then body-blocks the threat;
  the partner chooses the shoulder itself. EP039 adds the
  negative form: the hero can be the DISTURBANCE, and the bond
  is proved by who the clan follows, not by any battle.
- **Structure:** Threat or weather closes in → the keeper offers
  protection with NO command attached (an open door, a held
  capsule, a turned back that shields) → held beat — the
  creature decides → the creature's choice is physical
  (climbs, settles, leans) → the keeper's relief is played
  small.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The post-Choosing proof beat for any
  new pairing (Mochiko/Mochii first; reusable for Hex/
  Dorowani in reverse — Hex offers formality as shelter and
  Dorowani accepts by relaxing one fin-streak). Subtitle is
  usually WRONG here: the choice must read in staging alone.

### S8 — BATTLE AS THREE LESSONS
- **Source:** Pokémon EP058 — the gym battle's tail is staged
  as three discrete lessons (type advantage refuted; the
  disobedient ace forfeits without attacking; the underdog
  wins by an anatomy exploit), then a freeze-frame on the
  unbeaten fourth problem.
- **Structure:** Each exchange teaches ONE rule of the show's
  own battle custom; the commentator/sideline names the rule
  AFTER the exchange, never before; the sequence ends on an
  unanswered problem, not a victory lap.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Dojo sparring grammar for EP04 and
  all later bouts: exchanges = lessons in the Garden's custom
  (form, joy, patience). Aeon is the sideline who names rules
  late and briefly. The anatomy-exploit win is legal ONLY when
  the anatomy was planted earlier in the episode (GF clue law).

### G15 — GAG WITHDRAWAL (the emotional tell)
- **Source:** Pokémon EP039 — jokes front-loaded; the final
  third goes gag-free, music thins, and the ABSENCE of comedy
  tells the audience the beat is real.
- **Structure:** Comedy density is a dial the episode turns
  DOWN on purpose at the turn; silence and plain staging take
  its place; the first small sound after the quiet (a cry, a
  bell) lands at full weight.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Already kin to G3, but stronger:
  EP05's night beats run it — vignettes comic-then-not across
  three nights. Standing rule: any episode may spend gag
  withdrawal ONCE, at its true turn; spending it twice teaches
  the audience to ignore it.

### R6 — ONE REFERENCE, THROWAWAY SLOT
- **Source:** The Pokémon house pattern, confirmed across
  EP001/029/039/058 samples: exactly one pop-culture reference
  per episode, placed in a throwaway slot (a cold-open beat, a
  battle command, a villain aside) — never load-bearing, never
  explained.
- **Mochiichao version:** A standing ration for the whole
  library: max one reference slot per episode, chosen from
  §IV/R-templates, logged in the cue sheet like a subtitle —
  spent or held, never improvised in the edit.

## VII. FROM MYSTERY + SERIALIZED (Gravity Falls / Avatar)

### S9 — THE RE-SEE REVEAL
- **Source:** GF "Headhunters" — the reveal is an old clue
  re-read (the victim's own shoe carries the hole), the
  culprits were background furniture all along, and the killing
  trap spends a weakness planted as an Act-1 joke.
- **Structure:** Plant a two-data-point clue early → eliminate
  the wrong frame honestly (each elimination teaches a rule) →
  at the reveal, return to the SAME staging as the plant →
  the truth was visible the whole time.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The upgrade to E1's beat 5 for any
  Season-1 mystery above garden scale: EP05's chalk line is
  planted to be re-seen (a hand's width — measured against
  whose hand?). Pairs with the duplicate test below for
  identity puzzles: the real one proves itself by a small
  private gesture from an earlier episode; the impostor uses
  the generic signal.

### E13 — ARC AS INVENTORY (rationing, calibrated)
- **Source:** GF "Into the Bunker" (arc = objects in rooms:
  dated supplies, an opened can, a page that is a trap key;
  a FALSE-ANSWER climax that advances the arc by eliminating
  a fake; the salvage ending = one broken device is the whole
  forward payload) + Scooby-Doo MI S1E1's measured ratio.
- **Structure:** The standalone shell closes 100%. The arc
  appears as (1) one found object whose disconnection is
  DRAMATIZED (the culprit is asked about it on-screen and has
  never seen it), (2) one audience-only reaction shot (an
  authority figure shaken, unnoticed by the cast), (3) one
  post-case contact or coda. Nothing else.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** E9 with numbers: the Forest ration
  per episode = 1 object (a chalk line, a cold spot, a bell
  rope filmed with dust) + 1 reaction (Aeon, once, unnoticed)
  + 1 coda. The false-answer rule protects open item #1: the
  show may eliminate wrong explanations of the Forest for a
  whole season without ever spending the true one.

### S10 — THE UNANNOUNCED EXAM (training payoff)
- **Source:** Avatar "Bitter Work" — drill ladder isolating
  one sub-skill each, scored by a repeated barked cue → the
  formal test is FAILED on nerve → the real exam arrives
  unannounced as a rescue, and the stance WAS the skill.
  Mirror plot: a counter-technique taught dry, deliberately
  never fired live — capability as arc ration.
- **Structure:** Expectation deflation (fancy named moves →
  "move a rock") → one-sentence embodied doctrine → drills →
  failed formal test → avoidance valley where the reason is
  named → real exam, unannounced, watched by the teacher all
  along.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The dojo strand's full grammar,
  upgrading S5: Dorowani's training pays off when form becomes
  rescue (holding a pose = holding a gate against the wind);
  Mochii's "failed test" is standing still, and its exam is
  the moment the Garden needs it still. Hex's counter-technique
  equivalent: a Garden custom (draw-by-custom) taught dry in
  EP04, fired for real a season later.

### S11 — THE ROTATING TWO-HANDER CHAIN (bottle episode)
- **Source:** Avatar "Zuko Alone" — a lone-rider episode with

- **Mochiichao mapping:** The Season-2 shape for a Hex-alone

## VIII. FROM ANIME BOND/MENACE + MI (Digimon / JoJo / Mystery Inc.)

### E14 — THE FORCED-EVOLUTION BOND CRISIS (keeper's-flaw shape)
- **Source:** Digimon Adventure EP16 — the evolution rule is
  stated (partner fed + human in danger), then ABUSED by the
  leader's flaw (force-feeding, manufactured danger) → the
  power fires WRONG (corrupted form) → it cannot be beaten,
  only outlasted (burnout, regression) → the partner
  apologizes, the group absolves, the leader names his own
  flaw in one line.
- **Structure:** Rule stated early and plainly → the human
  games the rule → corrupted payoff worse than losing → the
  creature pays the cost → absolution scene → one-line
  self-diagnosis as the tag.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** The "keeper's flaw hurts the
  creature" episode: Mochiko, sleepless over the Garden,
  games the CARE LOOP (double rounds, doubled checks — care
  as control) and the Garden's rhythm corrupts: residents
  over-tended, Wispkin's round double-walked and wrong. No
  evolution horror — the corruption is RHYTHM, and the fix is
  the apology + the one line: she was keeping the garden FROM
  something instead of FOR somebody. Rule 12's shadow side,
  staged, never quoted.

### S12 — THE SEATED CONFRONTATION (violence disabled)
- **Source:** JoJo SC EP34–35 — a gambler confrontation at
  one cafe table across two episodes: the ability's rules
  disable violence in the first five minutes (souls die with
  the gambler), the trigger is psychological (inwardly
  ADMITTING defeat), tension carried by inserts (hands, chips,
  sweat) and monologue; the opponent's deteriorating face is
  the damage bar; stakes escalate priced in PERSONS; the hero
  wins the people and loses the objective (no information —
  the search restarts from zero).
- **Structure:** Rules stated by demonstration (a trivial bet
  first, rigged before it was offered) → calibration loss →
  anomaly (the hero never looks at his cards) → escalation
  ladder in persons → impossible price → the opponent breaks
  on what he INFERS, not on what is shown → post-reveal: the
  winning hand was worthless; not-knowing kept the bluff
  honest.
- **Mochiichao mapping:** Hex's formal challenges, upgraded:
  a dojo-sparring negotiation or a keeper-board dispute
  played SEATED, at the Gate table, with the board between
  them. The "damage bar" is staging: Hex's kit line, laid out
  perfectly at the start, is disturbed item by item as his
  certainty thins. Stakes priced in persons = in NAMES on the
  board (whose name is risked is the escalation). Mochiko's
  bluff equivalent: she never reads the Manual — not-knowing
  the custom keeps her honest, and Dorowani (who carries the
  Manual) simply trusts her. Props/prices from the source
  (chips, cheats, the final raise) are do-not-adapt; the
  STRUCTURE is the kit.

### R7 — THE MUSEUM OF YOUR OWN CASES
- **Reference:** MI S1E1's museum of the town's past monsters

- **Why it lands:** the show's own history as set dressing
  tells new viewers the world is old, and rewards returning
  viewers with recognition.
- **Mochiichao version:** From Season 2, the keeper board's
  wall accrues the season's solved oddities as painted marks
  and kept props (the net-hat, the chalk line's board). One
  homage FRAMING per mystery episode is permitted inside a
