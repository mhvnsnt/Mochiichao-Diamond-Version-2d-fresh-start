# EP058 — "Riddle Me This" (TB058 / EP058)
## Type: (b) gym-battle episode — first half of a two-part gym arc (concludes EP059)
First broadcast: Japan Aug 13, 1998 / US Sep 18, 1999 (Bulbapedia EP058).
Sources: Bulbapedia EP058 (full plot + trivia + dub edits), Pokémon Wiki (Fandom)
episode references, and Pokémon Wiki **International transcript** (full transcript
exists — see SOURCE.md). Structure extraction only; no transcript reproduced.

## Cold open shape
- Narrator status-report over a ferry: hero is one badge from league entry; each
  companion states a *different* reason for wanting the island (scenery / research
  lab / badge). Rival boards the same boat and deflates the premise before the
  title card: there is no gym anymore; the island is a resort.

## Act structure
- **Act 1 is a quest chain, not a battle** — the gym must be *found* before it can
  be fought:
  1. Arrival deflation: ruined gym building, explained by a disguised local who
     speaks only in riddles. Companion (not the hero) solves riddle #1
     (hot + cool = hot springs).
  2. Deflation montage #2: the famous research lab is also a tourist trap.
  3. Lodging rejection montage: centre full, every hotel full — a run of "no"s
     played for rhythm, ending with the party camping while the rival dines.
  4. Rival contrast scene ends in a hazard-cameo: the recurring singing creature
     appears as hotel entertainment; heroes flee the room before it can sing —
     everyone else falls asleep. The gag is staged as an *evacuation*, not a song.
  5. Riddle #2 is printed on the innkeeper's card (clock hands/face); companion
     solves again → free rooms. **Eyecatch falls here** — the midpoint reward is
     a bed, not a battle beat.
- **Act 2:**
  1. Villain trio heist at the lab, staged with an **abbreviated, impatient
     version of their own motto ritual** — the joke is that they skip their
     signature ceremony to get to work. Balloon heist foiled by a two-creature
     retrieval (flyer + electric), ending in the standard sky-exit.
  2. **The rescue earns the secret:** the innkeeper rewards the hero with riddle
     #3 — the hidden gym is where firefighters can never win. Hero cannot solve it.
  3. **Discovery by accident, not deduction:** in the hot springs, the baby
     companion wanders onto a novelty tap, trips a hidden switch, drops the
     dividing screen, opens the cave. The environment solves the riddle.
  4. Identity reveal via riddle #4 (the wig riddle): the riddle-man *was* the gym
     leader all along, in disguise — every gatekeeping puzzle was an entrance exam.
- **Battle occupies only the episode tail** (see below) and does **not resolve**.

## Battle staging — three lessons, then a hard stop
Formal 3-on-3, staged as three distinct teaching beats plus a counter:
1. **Type chart fails:** hero leads with the type-advantaged starter; it is knocked
   out in *one* hit by the leader's opener. Leader states the lesson aloud:
   choosing is more than selecting the right type.
2. **No battle at all:** hero fields his disobedient ace; the ace flies off and
   naps; leader *disqualifies it for leaving the ring*. A loss with zero attacks —
   the bond problem from EP001's lineage, still unpaid.
3. **Anatomy exploit wins:** underdog's electric attacks do nothing to a
   Ground-type — until the hero targets its **horn as a conductor**, bypassing the
   immunity. (This is the beat the episode is famous for; see DO NOT ADAPT.)
   Companion exposition + a pun from the other companion mark the turn.
4. **Hard counter / cliffhanger:** leader's signature creature debuts; its own body
   heat bends the air so electric attacks refract away (explained mid-fight by the
   companion-analyst). Relentless close attacks push the underdog to the platform
   edge, lava below. Episode **freezes on the incoming ultimate attack**; narrator
   asks the audience a direct question; "To be continued." Resolution is sold to
   the next episode.

## A/B weave
- A-plot: find and fight the hidden gym (riddle chain → battle).
- B-plot: rival's resort holiday (taunting counterpoint; he has badges to spare)
  and the villain heist — the B-plot is *load-bearing*: foiling the heist is what
  earns the clue that unlocks the A-plot's second half.

## Running gags (mechanisms)
- **Riddle-as-gate:** every scene transition is locked behind a riddle; the hero
  never solves one — the companion is the solver, the hero is the applicant.
- **Singing-creature evacuation:** recurring sleeper hazard used as a scene-ender.
- **Rival's cheerleader chorus:** chants agreement after his lines, a Greek-chorus
  humiliation device.
- **Villain motto, truncated:** the ritual itself becomes the joke when rushed.
- **Post-win companion pun:** tension released by a groan-pun from the sideline
  immediately after the exploit lands.

## Emotional turn
- Deliberately thin — this is a *competence* episode. The one emotional beat is
  professional respect: the leader only reveals himself after the hero proves he is
  "not a tourist" by rescuing the lab's creatures for no reward.

## Pop-culture / reference slots (shape only)
- **Puzzle-villain host archetype:** the entire episode is hung on a riddling
  host whose catchphrase-shape is the episode title (a famous comic-book
  puzzle-villain's signature challenge phrase). Shape: cast the gatekeeper as a
  *genre archetype*, let the title announce the archetype, never name the source.
  Lands because the audience solves along with the companion.
- **Disguised-authority reveal:** the eccentric local who "helps" the hero all
  episode is the boss in a wig — the riddle that reveals him is *about* the wig.
