# EP039 — "Pikachu's Goodbye" (TB039 / EP039)
## Type: (c) quiet / character episode — the fake-release episode
Japanese title translates as "Forest of Pikachu." First broadcast: Japan Apr 16, 1998 /
US Nov 20, 1998 (Bulbapedia EP039). Notable context from Bulbapedia: first episode to
air after the EP038 incident hiatus; aired as part of the first one-hour special
(with EP040). Sources: Bulbapedia EP039 (full plot + trivia + dub edits) and Pokémon
Wiki **International transcript** (full transcript exists — see SOURCE.md).
Structure extraction only; no transcript reproduced.

## Cold open shape
- The quietest opening in the sample: narrator states the party has *stopped to
  rest*; companions praise the fresh air. Only incident before the title card is a
  slapstick vine-swing into a tree (in the dub the hero adds a jungle-king yell —
  see reference slot). Tone contract: nothing is at stake, which is the point.

## Act structure
- **Act 1 — discovery and exclusion:**
  1. Partner spots a small wild member of its own species; pursuit reveals a whole
     hidden clan in a clearing.
  2. Acceptance beat is *ritualised, not verbal*: the small one greets the partner
     tail-to-tail; the clan follows. (For a cries-only cast, this is the model:
     belonging shown as a physical greeting protocol.)
  3. Hero runs in wanting to join — clan scatters. **The hero is the disturbance.**
     Companion explains: wild creatures fear humans they have never met.
  4. Rescue set-piece earns what intrusion couldn't: the small one falls in a
     river; partner dives in; both are swept toward a waterfall; the clan forms a
     **tail-chain** and hauls them out. Partner is rewarded with food and lifted up
     cheering. Hero is physically held back by his companion and told to *just
     watch* — the episode's thesis in staging.
  5. Villain trio observes from cover and plans a mass capture (their profit reading
     of the same scene).
  6. Night: the clan's communal chant. Over dinner the hero loses his appetite;
     companion remarks, in passing, that being with its own kind must be the best
     thing in the world for the partner. **Eyecatch falls on that line.**
- **Act 2 — rumination, interruption, performed rejection:**
  1. Dinner silence; hero denies anything is wrong. The companion's line **replays
     verbatim as an internal loop** (the transcript repeats the exchange) — guilt
     staged as an echo, not a speech.
  2. Villain capture interrupts the rumination and accidentally supplies the proof
     the hero needed: netted partner chews through the capture net first, the clan
     copies it, and the partner bites the balloon to free the rest — sky-exit for
     the trio, with a volley of species-name puns on the way up.
  3. **Fake ending:** danger over, group restored — then the hero walks away from
     the celebration. Campfire confrontation: he announces the release, claims it
     is what the partner wants, and when the partner follows, he *shouts at it to
     stay* and runs. (Bulbapedia dub notes: in the Japanese version there is **no
     music from the campfire extinguishing until the run**, and his voice trembles;
     the dub scores it.)
  4. Insert-song flashback montage of the pair's history carries the turn (JP and
     dub use different songs — see DO NOT ADAPT).
  5. **Sunrise resolution:** the clan chants a farewell; the small one shakes tails
     with the partner one last time; the partner runs to the hero and into his arms.
     Hero cries. Narrator closes with the bond thesis: the adventure brought them
     closer. No button joke — the episode ends on the embrace.

## A/B weave
- No true B-plot. The villain capture is a *device inside the A-plot*: it delays the
  release decision by one night and demonstrates the partner's leadership of the
  clan — i.e., it strengthens the case *for* leaving, which makes the final choice
  cost more.

## Battle staging
- None, formally. The only "combat" is capture/escape physics (insulated net vs.
  teeth; balloon vs. bite). Power is expressed as *who the clan follows*.

## Running gags (mechanisms) — and their deliberate withdrawal
- Gags are **front-loaded and then withdrawn**: vine crash (pre-title), villain
  species-puns during the balloon escape. The final third has no gag beats at all;
  silence/music replaces them. The withdrawal itself signals "this one is real."
- **Meta gag:** after their motto, the trio's leader thanks their fans for loyalty
  and support — a fourth-wall break Bulbapedia links to the show's return from the
  post-EP038 hiatus. Shape: use the villains' ritual slot for a one-off direct
  address when the *real world* has interrupted the show.

## Emotional turn — mechanics worth stealing (structure only)
- The decision is planted by a *friend's casual line*, not by a villain or a loss.
- The hero never asks the partner what it wants (it cannot answer in words); he
  performs rejection so the partner's return is unambiguously a *choice*.
- The clan's farewell chant mirrors the night chant: same ritual, opposite meaning.

## Pop-culture / reference slot (shape only)
- **Jungle-king vine yell** (dub-only, per Bulbapedia dub edits): a one-second
  borrowed adventure-hero yell dropped into an otherwise silent cold open. Shape:
  single-beat genre quote as a cold-open laugh, immediately dropped.

## DO NOT ADAPT (flag by name/description only)
- The dub insert song for the flashback montage (titled for this episode's goodbye
  premise) and the Japanese insert song it replaced.
- The performed-rejection release scene as scripted here (shouted stay command +
  run + sunrise return) — it is this franchise's signature fake-release staging;
  Mochiichao needs its own mechanics for the same beat (cf. EP001's body-shield,
  which is the *earlier* and more reusable shape).
- The tail-shake greeting as this species' acceptance ritual — invent a per-species
  Mochiichao protocol instead of borrowing this one.
- The trio's fourth-wall fan-thank-you line (tied to a real broadcast incident).
