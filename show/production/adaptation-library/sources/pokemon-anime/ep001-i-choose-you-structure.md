# EP001 — "Pokémon – I Choose You!" (TB001 / EP001)
## Type: (a) bond / first-training episode — series premiere
First broadcast: Japan 1997 / US 1998 (per Fandom episode page). Sources: Bulbapedia
EP001 material (via Bulbapedia user-archive mirror + trivia), Pokémon Wiki (Fandom)
episode page, and Pokémon Wiki **International transcript** (full transcript exists —
see SOURCE.md). Structure extraction only; no transcript reproduced.

## Cold open shape
- No villain, no in medias res danger. **Spectacle prologue borrowed from another
  medium:** a stadium battle shown first as a game-style animation, then revealed to
  be a TV broadcast a kid is watching in his bedroom.
- The prologue doubles as a tutorial: off-screen announcer names attacks, explains a
  recall/switch, and models what "good training" sounds like — the hero (and viewer)
  learn battle grammar by watching, not by being told.
- Title card lands *after* the night-before setup (mother's bedtime order, a TV
  lecture introducing the three starter options), not at the top.

## Act structure
- **Pre-title:** theme → TV battle prologue → bedroom night-before → title card.
- **Act 1:** dream montage — the hero sleep-fantasises each of the three starter
  choices in turn (rule of three #1). The dream-throw becomes a real throw: he
  smashes his own alarm clock in his sleep. Oversleeping is therefore *caused on
  screen*, not announced.
  - Late arrival → rival already celebrated by crowd/cheerleaders (status humiliation
    before any failure of skill).
  - Rule of three #2: three starter balls opened, all empty, each with the same
    deadpan verdict from the professor.
  - Leftover partner revealed as a problem case; the warning is delivered, the hero
    accepts anyway.
  - Departure set-piece: repeated electric-shock gag (hug → shock; gift handover →
    double shock; crowd compliment → mass shock). The gag *is* the character sheet.
  - Road test: partner refuses its ball (ball bounced back with tail, played as a
    catch game the adults misread as friendship), refuses a battle, climbs a tree,
    yawns. Eyecatch falls exactly on the refusal peak.
- **Act 2:** solo capture attempts escalate as slapstick: thrown ball (escape) →
  ambush with clothing (blown back by the target's wind move) → thrown rock
  (hits the *wrong*, aggressive species). A reference-device voice inserts exposition
  throughout as comedy beats — it explains the rules a beat too late, and once
  insults the hero, which the partner finds hilarious.
- **Climax is a survival chase, not a formal battle:** the provoked flock wounds the
  partner; hero carries it, jumps a waterfall, steals a bystander's bike (creating a
  debt-character who joins the story later), crashes in a storm.
- **Emotional turn:** hero offers the ball as *shelter, not control*, steps in front
  of the flock, and challenges it to take him instead. The partner — injured,
  previously disobedient — climbs his shoulder under its own power and discharges,
  amplified by the storm. Bond is proved by reciprocal sacrifice, not by obedience.
- **Ending / tag:** soft button, no joke. Storm clears; an **unidentified golden
  flyer crosses a rainbow**; the reference device admits no data. Narrator reframes
  the day as the start of a long journey. Hero carries the sleeping partner toward
  medical help; partner licks his scratch. "To be continued."

## A/B weave
- **Single A-plot only.** Bulbapedia/Fandom trivia confirm this is the **only
  episode of its era with no villain trio appearance at all** — the template's usual
  B-plot slot is deliberately empty in the premiere, so every beat is one causal
  chain: oversleep → leftover partner → refusal → provocation → injury → sacrifice.

## Battle / training staging
- Formal battle grammar appears only in the TV prologue (announcer-led).
- Real "training" beats are negative examples: command ignored, capture attempted
  without weakening, wrong target engaged. Competence is never what saves the day;
  body-blocking is.

## Running gags (mechanisms, this episode)
- **Shock-on-contact loop:** same setup, escalating victims (hero → hero+mentor →
  whole crowd). Establishes the partner's refusal language for the whole series.
- **Partner laughter as bond barometer:** partner laughs at every failure in Act 2 —
  mocking early, but the laughter stops the moment the hero is actually hurt.
- **Dream-throw / sleep-action:** what the hero rehearses asleep, he does awake,
  with worse objects (clock for ball).

## Pop-culture / reference slot (shape only)
- **Medium-echo cold open:** the episode opens by recreating the source *game's*
  opening duel, then pulls back to reveal it as TV. Shape: "the adaptation watches
  its own source for one beat before starting." Lands because it flatters players
  and needs no dialogue.

## DO NOT ADAPT (flag by name/description only)
- The episode's exact title phrase / hero's selection catchphrase.
- The unidentified golden legendary flyover + rainbow as a bond omen (this specific
  species' silent debut, before its own generation existed).
- The starter-trio selection ceremony and the professor's TV lecture format.
- The destroyed-bike debt as the mechanism that attaches the female lead.
- The "Who's That Pokémon?" eyecatch identity (this episode's answer is the partner).
