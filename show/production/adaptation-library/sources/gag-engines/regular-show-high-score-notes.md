# Regular Show — "High Score" — Structure Notes
Cluster: 11-minute gag-engine cartoons | Type: (b) competition / game episode
Season 2 (per transcript episode list). Source: transcript wiki, see SOURCE.md.
STRUCTURE ONLY — no transcript reproduced.

## Cold open
- Payday: the boss pays the heroes in sandwich bags of loose coins.
  Humiliation, not a monster, is the inciting incident. A three-beat
  respect deficit is stacked fast: boss -> street kids -> workplace.

## Premise
Nobody respects the duo. Thesis stated on screen: people respect
people who are good at something. They choose the nearest arena —
a cafe arcade game — to manufacture respect.

## Escalation — 7 steps after setup
1. Public challenge, public failure: hero scores negative points
   (a score below zero played as a physics impossibility joke).
2. Accidental technique: splitting one control set between two players
   (one on stick, one on buttons, both flailing) outperforms solo play.
   Co-op incompetence is codified as method.
3. Respect montage: licensed pop song; strangers, then a self-important
   fan, validate them. A business-card gag objectifies "respect"
   as a collectible.
4. Rematch won: the original street kids concede; the pair notice they
   are near the machine's world record.
5. Record-holder materialises: breaking the record summons its holder
   cosmically (lightning, floating giant head). He reveals a universe
   record exactly 1 point above their new score — status can never
   quite be caught.
6. Final head-to-head: holder deploys a fake sympathy backstory
   (life devoted to the game, wife left) to induce a throw; duo throw,
   he confesses the lie, citing "no rules."
7. Loophole mirrored: duo use the same no-rules licence — crowd chant
   as sabotage, then physical interference at the cabinet — retake
   the lead; holder explodes, covering everyone in brain goo.

## Act shape (~11 min)
- Act 1 (~0-3.5m): pay humiliation, respect thesis, challenge, negative
  score.
- Act 2 (~3.5-7.5m): co-op discovery, montage, job abandoned by tossing
  the walkie-talkie, rematch, world record broken.
- Act 3 (~7.5-11m): cosmic holder, universe-record reveal, sympathy
  con, no-rules endgame, explosion, boss tag.

## Gag mechanisms (abstract)
- Respect as currency: an abstract social good is tracked like score —
  stated, lost, farmed in montage, wagered in the final ("winner gets
  all the respect").
- One-point gap gag: the ultimate target is the achieved score + 1.
- Sympathy fake-out: a villain's tragic monologue is staged with crowd
  murmurs as a laugh track; the confession lands the instant mercy is
  granted.
- Rules-lawyering symmetry: the antagonist's loophole is quoted back
  and used harder by the heroes — no new power is introduced.
- Split-controls co-op: two bodies, one player — visualises the duo's
  whole dynamic in a single cabinet tableau.
- Anti-climax boss tag: the real authority arrives AFTER the cosmic
  victory, almost grants the original mundane request (pay by check),
  then revokes it because he is covered in goo, tearing the checks up
  while shouting that he respects their decision.

## Ending tag
Victory is total in the arena and worthless at work. Heroes accept the
boss's furious "respect" at face value; credits.

## Reference / parody slots (shape only)
- Arcade record-chaser documentary archetype (the King of Kong shape):
  a bearded, obsessive record-holder who treats a leaderboard as a
  life's work and a title to defend by any means. Lands because the
  stakes inflation (world -> universe record) is his, not the heroes'.
- 1980s arcade-film dialogue beat: a bystander's "you got some skill"
  exchange is restaged around the cabinet (sourced in the show's own
  pop-culture reference list as a nod to the 1989 film The Wizard).
- Licensed-song training montage (boy-band track) as instant era glue.

## Do-not-adapt flags (identification only)
- The record-holder character entirely: name, initials joke, floating
  bearded-head design, and any likeness to the real record-holder he
  parodies — this character was litigated in real life (suit dismissed
  as parody, 2015). Abstract slot only; never the figure.
- The in-show game name, the universe-record figure, and the licensed
  montage song.
- Character names/designs and the cafe/park cast as staged here.
