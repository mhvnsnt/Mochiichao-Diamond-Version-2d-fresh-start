# SpongeBob — "The Fry Cook Games" — Structure Notes
Cluster: 11-minute gag-engine cartoons | Type: (b) competition / game episode
Season 2, aired 2001-09-28 (per transcript header). Source: transcript wiki, see SOURCE.md.
STRUCTURE ONLY — no transcript reproduced.

## Cold open
- Fake sports broadcast, in progress: torch ceremony fails twice
  (wind, then a "Please Stand By" card, then the torch-bearer catches
  fire). Announcer continues unbothered. The broadcast frame, not the
  heroes, opens the episode.

## Premise
Annual workplace Olympics. Hero represents his boss; his best friend is
insulted that the job is called skilled, gets hired by the rival boss
purely to prove it is easy, and the friendship becomes the real contest.

## Escalation — 6 steps
1. Training-ground insult duel: compliments curdle into a rapid single-word
   insult volley (attributes neither duelist actually has — ears,
   fingernails — escalating to bare colours). Friendship formally breaks.
2. Rival bosses recruit and radicalise: cross-cut pep talks where each
   boss finishes the other's sentences while poisoning each friend
   against the other.
3. Event 1 (pole vault over a fryer): hero clean; challenger accidentally
   converts the crowd into a food product, instantly sold by a vendor.
4. Event 2 (high dive into syrup): hero's entry is judged on food
   presentation; challenger wins by literally becoming the finished
   dish — including a cut to a live-action photo of the real food.
5. Highlight montage: many events, score tied. Competition grammar
   (announcer, replays, medals talk) does the compressing.
6. Final event (wrestling on a giant bun): pre-fight body-inflation
   poses, identity-erasure attack (erasing part of a name tag), foot
   gag — then both stop on a wardrobe reveal (see Ending).

## Act shape (~11 min)
- Act 1 (~0-3m): broadcast opening, rivalry history in fake archive
  clips, hero's oath, insult duel.
- Act 2 (~3-8m): rival entrances (a giant carrier misdirect reveals the
  tiny challenger), manipulation cross-cut, Events 1-2.
- Act 3 (~8-11m): montage to a tie, final wrestling, reconciliation,
  bosses left demanding violence as the pair exit holding hands.

## Gag mechanisms (abstract)
- Sports-broadcast deadpan: catastrophic visuals narrated as routine
  results; the announcer never breaks format.
- Archive-clip history: backstory delivered as fake "who could forget"
  highlights, each ending in petty sabotage on a podium.
- Single-word insult volley: dialogue strips to nouns/colours at speed;
  grammar collapsing = anger rising.
- Entrance misdirect: scale build-up (shadow, stomps, warnings) pays off
  in a small, cheerful reveal strapped to the giant.
- Food-ification rule: every athletic feat is re-scored as food prep;
  the arena, apparatus, and injuries are all kitchen items at scale.
- Live-action insert: one cut to a photographed real object as the
  "perfect form" punchline.
- Sentimental wardrobe reveal: matching hidden colours instantly end
  the fight — emotion, not a winner, resolves the contest.

## Ending tag
Reconciled pair exit arm-in-arm; final line is a gross throwaway about
the revealed clothing's original colour. Bosses' demand to resume
fighting is ignored — no medal is awarded.

## Reference / parody slots (shape only)
- Summer Olympics: torch relay/lighting, cauldron, podium, clean-and-jerk,
  gymnastics rings, diving commentary. Lands by substituting fast-food
  apparatus 1:1 for sporting apparatus.
- Pro-wrestling / sports-rivalry promo grammar: heel managers, entrance
  spectacle, "this is personal" escalation in the final.

## Do-not-adapt flags (identification only)
- The colour-insult volley wording and the name-erasure "not Rick" beat.
- The swapped-underwear reconciliation device as staged here.
- Torch-lit giant burger cauldron, event names, and venue/brand names.
- Character and announcer identities/designs from this show.
