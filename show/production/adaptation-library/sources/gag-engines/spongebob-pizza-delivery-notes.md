# SpongeBob — "Pizza Delivery" — Structure Notes
Cluster: 11-minute gag-engine cartoons | Type: (a) mundane-task-escalates + two-hander
Season 1, aired 1999-08-14 (per transcript header). Source: transcript wiki, see SOURCE.md.
STRUCTURE ONLY — no transcript reproduced. One short excerpt max, where noted.

## Cold open (no separate teaser)
- In medias res at work, after closing. A single incoming order interrupts the
  "trying to go home" beat. Cold open IS the premise launch: < ~1 min.

## Premise
Deliver one food item to one nearby customer. Two mismatched co-workers:
the eager volunteer + the reluctant draftee. Boss invents both the product
and the delivery service on the spot to chase the sale.

## Escalation — 7 steps after setup
1. Competence failure: learner driver reverses all night; pair stranded,
   out of fuel, "middle of nowhere."
2. Reversal gag: kicking the vehicle resets the fuel gauge to full — and
   the vehicle drives off without them.
3. On foot: hitchhiking set-piece fails; helper is nearly run over, rescuer
   is buried in dust instead.
4. Weather escalation: wind -> cargo used as sail/parachute -> tornado.
   One partner's line flips mid-beat from "let go" to "hang on" (rule:
   survival instantly rewrites the argument).
5. Survival/hunger: folklore "pioneer tricks" are tried literally and fail
   (ground-listening, inedible food). Temptation beat: hungry partner
   seduces the guardian into "just checking" the cargo.
6. Absurd solution made literal: a claimed ancient vehicle (a boulder)
   actually works, and delivers them instantly. The joke premise, believed
   hard enough, becomes the transport mechanic.
7. Delivery succeeds — customer rejects it over a missing add-on that was
   never ordered. Effort is invalidated on a technicality.

## Act shape (~11 min)
- Act 1 (~0-2.5m): order, invention of product/service, vehicle-check gag
  list, driving failure.
- Act 2 (~2.5-8.5m): stranded trek as linked set-pieces (hitchhike / wind /
  hunger), each ending on the reluctant partner worse off.
- Act 3 (~8.5-11m): boulder ride -> doorstep rejection -> defender beat ->
  instant return home -> exhausted one-line tag over black.

## Gag mechanisms (abstract)
- Checklist gag: a formal inspection list where each item harms/insults
  the partner (final item is a body-inflation visual).
- Repetition-with-rally: one instructional phrase repeated until it becomes
  a chant, then a song. Short proof of mechanism: the delivery jingle is
  re-sung in decaying styles (cheerful -> beatbox -> blues -> exhausted
  mumble) as energy drains — same tune = progress bar for exhaustion.
- Running lore gag: "the pioneers..." precedes every bad idea; the
  straight partner's late rant lists the lore's failures back.
- Cargo-integrity rule: the item must not be eaten/opened; every escalation
  tests that rule, never breaks it.
- Held reaction: blank stare from the straight partner held after each
  absurd claim, before any reply.
- Role reversal at climax: the reluctant partner, not the eager one,
  delivers the punishment/defense beat — earned by accumulated suffering.

## Ending tag
Instant return, straight back to work; exhausted complaint over black.
No reset speech — fatigue is the punchline.

## Reference / parody slots (shape only)
- Frontier-survival folklore / western stranded-trek: lore is quoted like
  scripture, then physically disproved each time.
- Prehistoric-vehicle gag shape: stone-age transport treated as a
  well-maintained classic car. Lands because the believer's confidence
  never wavers and the world finally agrees with him.

## Do-not-adapt flags (identification only)
- The delivery jingle (melody + wording) and its branded product name.
- The boulder-ride line and "pioneers" catchphrasing as worded here.
- The customer's missing-drink rant and the face-slam delivery payoff.
- Character names, employer/product names, and the sponge/starfish designs.
