# ATLA Book 2 E9 "Bitter Work" — Structure Notes (training episode)

Sample type: (c) Avatar: The Last Airbender training episode. Sources: transcript page, Avatar Wiki (Fandom) — see SOURCE.md. Structure only; no transcript saved.

## Cold open
Sunrise, camp asleep. Student wakes *before* everyone, bursting with anticipation — training begins from eagerness, not obligation. Teacher makes a destructive entrance (bursts out of a rock tent, catapults a sleeping teammate into the sky) — teacher's pedagogy (force, noise, no coddling) is demonstrated before a word of curriculum.

## Training-montage structure (the reusable spine)
Built as **two mirrored training plots cross-cut**, plus a trapped-comic-relief C-plot that turns out to be the exam:

### A-plot: student vs. his opposite element
1. **Expectation deflation**: student fantasises named spectacular moves; teacher assigns the smallest possible unit ("move a rock").
2. **One-sentence doctrine, embodied**: stance first; the element is stubborn, so the student must *be* like it. Doctrine is a posture, not a lecture.
3. **First attempt fails by reflex**: student applies his native element's logic (yield/deflect) and is knocked backward. Failure is *diagnosed as mindset*, not talent.
4. **Pedagogy argument (the scene inside the montage)**: a second, gentler teacher pulls the harsh teacher aside and prescribes encouragement / "a gentle nudge." Harsh teacher accepts verbally, then literalises it abusively — montage resumes at higher intensity. Two teaching philosophies are contrasted without stopping the training.
5. **Montage proper — short, varied, scored by a repeated barked cue** (one short phrase, transcript marks it twice):
   - carry a heavy rock while the ground is made unstable underfoot;
   - dig through rock with bare fingers (student injures hand);
   - blindfolded, smash rising rocks with a borrowed weapon (~5 tries to first success — progress is counted);
   - push a teacher-made rock shield past a marked boundary line (first clean success);
   - balance on two shaking pillars while tossing/catching a rock (mastery image: stillness under provocation).
   Each drill isolates one sub-skill: load, contact, sensing, pressure, balance.
6. **Formal test, failed on nerve**: stop a boulder rolled downhill at him — blindfolded, to force sensing. Perfect stance/form, then he *jumps aside* at the last second. Teacher's verdict reframes the whole episode: the deficit is guts/commitment, not technique.
7. **Avoidance valley**: student retreats to his *old* element with the gentle teacher, who names the structural reason — he is learning his element's natural opposite — and proves his existing mastery with a reflex test (slicing a thrown reed). Confidence is restored in the old skill so the new-skill failure reads as specific, not global.
8. **Provocation beat**: harsh teacher attacks something the student values (uses his antique staff as a nutcracker) — engineered to make him finally *stand his ground against her*, verbally.
9. **Real exam, unannounced**: a teammate is trapped and a large protective mother animal charges. Student holds ground, repels the animal with his *old* element, refuses to leave — then, on the teacher's command in the moment ("Do it now!"), performs the new element for the first time, because the *stance* (not leaving) was the skill all along. Teacher reveals she was present and watching the whole time.
10. **Button**: first success repeated casually for the group; gentle teacher credits "positive reinforcement"; harsh teacher agrees, lying cheerfully — both pedagogies claim the win.

### B-plot: parallel training that fails upward (mirror)
- Wounded mentor + student in a ruin. Student requests advanced training *to fight a specific stronger opponent*.
- Mentor first teaches **generation** of an advanced technique: theory (separate positive/negative energies; guide, don't command), demonstration, student attempt → explosive failure in his face.
- Failure is diagnosed psychologically: an internal state (shame/pride — mentor's formulation: pride is the *source* of shame, not its opposite) blocks a technique that requires calm.
- **Pivot, not repetition**: mentor abandons generation and teaches a *defensive counter-technique he invented himself by studying a different element's practitioners* — delivered with a four-elements lecture drawn in dirt (four symbols, then a circle around them: wholeness through borrowing). The technique is taught as dry physical choreography (pathway through the body, with a lethal wrong route explicitly warned against), practised without the live element.
- Student demands a live test; mentor refuses absolutely. Student rides into a storm alone and begs the sky to strike him; **nothing happens** — episode ends on his tears, unresolved. The A-plot's success and B-plot's non-event are the same lesson from opposite ends: standing still works; demanding the test doesn't.

### C-plot: the hole
One teammate spends the whole episode physically stuck in a crack in the ground, bargaining with the universe (escalating vows: give up meat, then sarcasm — "pretty much my whole identity"), befriending the animal he was hunting. Pure stasis as comedy — and he is the *reason* the exam happens: rescue requires the new skill, in front of the creature's mother.

## Arc rationing
Two arc payloads, both planted as tools, neither used: (1) a counter-technique that will matter in a later confrontation — taught, practised dry, deliberately *not* fired; (2) the cross-element borrowing doctrine, stated once, visually (circle in dirt). Training episode = arc advances by *capability and doctrine*, plot stays local.

## Homage / reference shapes (shape only)
No direct film homage identified in the sources sampled; the reference layer is internal — drill-sergeant vs. nurturer teacher archetypes, and the "opposite element" schema. Candidate slot for Mochiichao: any training episode can carry a *borrowed-from-another-discipline* counter-move as its homage-shaped beat.

## Do-not-adapt flags (brief identification only)
- Element system names/terms, the mentor's four-elements lecture wording and dirt-diagram as drawn, lightning-redirection pathway wording, teacher nicknames for the student, the trapped teammate's animal name, and the storm-yelling closing scene as staged. Adapt the slots (opposite-discipline block, dry-run counter-technique, refusal-of-live-test ending), not these identities/wordings.
