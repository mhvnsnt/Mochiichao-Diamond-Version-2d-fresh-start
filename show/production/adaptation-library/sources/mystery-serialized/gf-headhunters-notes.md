# GF S1E3 "Headhunters" — Structure Notes (mystery-of-the-week)

Sample type: (a) Gravity Falls mystery-of-the-week. Sources: episode page + transcript page, Gravity Falls Wiki (Fandom) — see SOURCE.md. Structure only; no transcript saved. One short excerpt shape noted, not reproduced at length.

## Cold open (pre-title, ~2 beats)
1. In-world TV detective show watched by the leads — sets the genre contract ("this week is a whodunit") and plants the running gag that the child detective will be dismissed as cute, not competent.
2. Inciting discovery *during a mundane chore*: a helper cleaning finds a hidden door behind wallpaper. Mystery space is inside the home base, not a trip away.
3. Fake scare in the room itself (a still figure that is a real person) → title. The room's contents are the suspect pool, introduced before any crime.

## Setup before the crime (act 1, no mystery yet)
- Inventory tour of the collection — every later suspect/weapon gets a visual introduction.
- One item already damaged by an *ordinary* cause (sun through blinds melts wax) — establishes the material's weakness (heat/sun) long before it becomes the solution/weapon.
- A replacement item is *made on screen in a mini-montage* by the comic-relief sibling — so the victim-object has emotional value, and its making (materials, glitter, effort) is witnessed.
- Public unveiling goes wrong for a *non-mystery* reason (promised free food was a lie) → a room full of angry people = a ready-made motive pool. No villain needed yet.

## Crime + investigation beat sequence
1. **Crime off-screen, in a gap in attention**: caretaker leaves the room for minutes; returns to find the figure beheaded. No witness, no forced entry.
2. **Authority refuses the case** ("unsolvable") — licences the child detectives and sets the ending button (proving the authority wrong).
3. **Clue 1 found by accident, not deduction**: footprints in carpet. Two data points in one clue: (i) prints lead to the weapon, (ii) one print has a **hole in the shoe**.
4. **Weapon misread**: an axe → assumed owner is the town lumberjack-type (motive: he was furious at the unveiling — shown, not told, in one punch-a-pole beat).
5. **Elimination by handedness**: suspect proves he is exclusively right-handed; the weapon is left-handed. Suspect list is physically crossed off — progress is visual.
6. **Elimination narrows to one**: the remaining left-handed suspect with a holed shoe.
7. **Red-herring collapse**: final suspect has an embarrassing, verifiable alibi (timestamped camera footage of a private, humiliating activity at crime time) **and** the weapon has *no fingerprints at all*. The "no prints" beat is the hinge: it retroactively invalidates the human-suspect frame.
8. **False-resolution trough**: detectives publicly embarrassed, authority mocks them. Story appears over; funeral for the victim-object follows.

## Reveal mechanics
- **Reveal clue is a re-see, not a new clue**: at the funeral, detective notices the *victim's own* shoe has the holed sole. Footprints were the victim-class figures' own — the suspect pool was in the room, posed as furniture, in the background of the whole episode (and of the funeral scene itself).
- Culprits self-reveal the moment they are named: they drop the pose and explain motive in one speech — locked away/forgotten for years after stopping being profitable; revenge attack that hit the *replica* instead of the owner by mistake (mistaken-identity explains why a "murder" of an object, not a person).
- Rules stated at reveal, all pre-planted: they animate only at a stated time condition; they are vulnerable to heat (planted in act 1 by the sun-melted figure).

## Climax mechanics
- Improvised household heat sources as weapons (fire irons, candles, fireplace, coffee).
- One-on-one duel with the leader on a high sign/roof; detective *deliberately lures* him outside so sunrise does the killing — the act-1 weakness, used as a trap, not a new power.
- Sibling disposes of the rest in parallel, off-duel, in the fireplace — clean division: hero duels leader, comic sibling mass-clears minions.

## Ending buttons (three, stacked)
1. **Status button**: detectives report success; authority figure literally sprays coffee and crashes — competence gag closed.
2. **Owner indifference button**: owner thanks them for the recovered head, unbothered — resets home-base status quo.
3. **Sequel-hook button (credits)**: disposal was "99%" complete; one severed head survives hidden in the vents and speaks in the credits gag, unheard/misattributed by the character on screen. End-credits cryptogram (see Do-Not-Adapt) reinforces the hook textually.

## Arc rationing in this episode
Almost zero. Arc material is limited to: background sighting of a future character at the unveiling, and (in hindsight only) an owner's grief beat that foreshadows a later family reveal. Lesson: an early-season MOTW can carry arc as *texture in crowd/background*, with no plot time spent on it.

## Homage / reference shapes (shape only — see SOURCE.md for source identifications)
- Closed wax-museum figures kept at home, "accidents" around them — Twilight-Zone-style wax-museum horror premise.
- Axe-brandishing sibling mimics a famous shower-scene string cue with vocal sound effects.
- Detective-leader figure is a classic literary detective (public-domain character used as a wax figure).

## Do-not-adapt flags (brief identification only)
- Named wax figures of real/fictional persons; the in-world TV detective-duck show and its name; the end-credits cryptogram text; the surviving wax talk-show-host head in the vents; the "sneeze like a kitten / adorable" running gag wording. Adapt the *slots* (celebrity-figure collection, in-world detective show, one-survivor credits hook), not these identities.
