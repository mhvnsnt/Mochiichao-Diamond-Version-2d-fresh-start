# Scooby-Doo! Mystery Incorporated S1E1 — "Beware the Beast from Below"
## Structure only — no transcript saved. Short excerpt only where noted.

Sample rationale: Series pilot — the cleanest possible exhibit of the show's
invention: a complete classic standalone mystery with a serialized arc clue
rationed in at ~2 touches (one object, one phone call), plus a villain reaction
shot that costs one second.

## Cold open (inverted)
- Opens AFTER a solved mystery: gang celebrate, then are JAILED by the sheriff and
  lectured for meddling; parents summoned. The franchise's usual ending (thanks +
  unmasking) is replaced by punishment — status quo established as HOSTILE TOWN.
- Worldbuilding tour beat doubles as thesis: a gang member guides tourists through
  a museum of the town's past "monsters," cynically noting they were all criminals
  in costumes — and is silenced by her own parents, whose business sells the curse.
  Town economy DEPENDS on unsolved mysteries. Every later obstruction has a motive.

## Character-arc seeding (parallel to, not inside, the mystery)
- Each member gets a 1-scene parent conflict (image / new friends / the leader's son)
  — the gang is framed as each other's "bad influence."
- Two slow-burn relationship threads planted in throwaway beats: one secret couple
  hiding from the team pet/friend; one one-sided crush the target cannot perceive
  because he thinks only about traps. Neither affects this episode's solution.

## Mystery beat sequence vs. the classic 6-beat formula
Classic formula (from prior Mochiichao research on the original series):
(1) legend/monster intro, (2) monster attack, (3) investigation + clues,
(4) chase, (5) trap, (6) unmasking + motive explanation.

This episode, beat for beat:
1. LEGEND INTRO — EXPANDED: Museum tour recites centuries of disappearances
   (conquistadors, missionaries, a vanished family). Legend is the TOWN, not the
   monster; the week's monster has no legend at all.
2. MONSTER ATTACK — DISPLACED: First attack happens OFF the gang (sewer workers
   breach a cave, open hazard barrels, are hit by green burst). Second attack is the
   cold-open-style hit on the gang's van (manhole erupts, creature emerges from fog,
   vanishes). Victims are found COCOONED ALIVE to a cave ceiling — attack leaves
   evidence-bodies, not just scares.
3. INVESTIGATION — TWO-TRACK: (a) Lab track: gang STEALS a cocooned body and takes
   it to a school science teacher for analysis (he notes victim is alive, in stasis).
   (b) Material track: a slime sample survives; the team pet EATS it and it is
   identified by taste as a specific new dessert shop's product. Clue chain is
   sensory/gag-driven, not forensic.
   FALSE SUSPECT built fairly: dessert's owner is a recent arrival, no background,
   controls the substance. Infiltration beat: two members in disguise-comedy
   service jobs get the team inside after hours.
4. CHASE — SHORT + FUNCTIONAL: Locked-closet discovery -> monster chase that exists
   to lead one member to a HIDDEN ENTRANCE back into the caves, where a FRESH TUNNEL
   is found running under the town bank. Geography is the real clue.
5. TRAP — FAILS, THEN IMPROVISED: The leader's set-piece cage trap misfires and
   captures the GANG (monster seals them in with their own clue-substance); they
   EAT their way out and improvise the capture with a hose of the same dessert,
   cocooning the monster to a wall. Signature competence beat: trap obsession fails,
   appetite + improvisation succeed.
6. UNMASKING — DOUBLE TWIST: Gang confidently accuse the dessert owner of planning
   a bank robbery — and he walks in, having called the police himself. Actual culprit
   is the LAB TEACHER from beat 3a: he faked his own cocooning mid-episode (a second
   "attack" the audience saw only by sound — a scream heard by a janitor), planned to
   tunnel to the bank and FRAME the dessert owner. Motive stated on unmasking: money,
   and resentment at a teacher's pay (raised in an awkward earlier beat).

## Arc-clue rationing (the serialized layer)
- TOUCH 1 (mid-investigation, inside the standalone clue space): In the caves, one
  member finds a magnifying-glass-shaped locket holding a photo of an unknown teen
  couple. It is handled as if it were a case clue — even linked to the crush thread
  (she wishes someone would give HER such a gift).
- PLACEMENT TEST (at the unmasking): She asks the culprit about it. He has never seen
  it. The object is thereby FORMALLY DISCONNECTED from the solved mystery on-screen.
- SILENT REACTION SHOT (same scene, 1 beat): The town mayor — present for the
  arrest — is visibly surprised/upset by the locket. Unnoticed by the gang.
  Arc information given to the AUDIENCE ONLY.
- TOUCH 2 (post-case coda, new location, relaxed scene): An anonymous distorted-
  voice phone call from an unseen informant: the gang is "doomed" for removing the
  locket; they have uncovered a truth about the town's curse that should have stayed
  buried; (short excerpt, <40 chars) "the real mystery has just begun."
  No name for the couple, no explanation of the locket, no second call.
- RATIO: ~2 arc touches + 1 reaction shot in a fully closed 22-minute case.
  Arc never withholds the standalone answer; standalone never explains the arc object.

## Ending hook
- Case closed, culprit arrested, authorities STILL resentful (solved case = lost
  tourist attraction). Hook is purely the coda call + the unanswered object now in a
  protagonist's pocket — a portable mystery the cast carries into every later episode.

## Homage / reference beats (shape only)
- Museum statues: the franchise's own 1969-era monsters displayed as in-universe
  tourist exhibits (self-homage as set dressing; identities flagged, see SOURCE.md).
- A tourist extra drawn as a lookalike of a famous 1970s guest-star comedian from
  the franchise's guest-star era (no lines).
- One horror staging homage, confirmed in Scoobypedia notes: monster cornering a
  heroine face-to-face framed after a well-known sci-fi horror sequel's alien/
  heroine close-up (Alien 3, per the wiki's cultural-references list).
- Horror-host likeness in the culprit's character design (noted on wiki; shape only).

## Adaptation mechanics (what transfers)
- Solve 100% of the week's case; ration the arc at 1 found object + 1 anonymous
  contact + 1 authority-figure reaction the heroes miss.
- Prove an object is NOT part of the case by asking the culprit on-screen — the
  disconnection is dramatised, not left ambiguous by omission.
- Town/institution profits from mystery itself, so solving is socially punished:
  a renewable reason for authority obstruction every week.
- Culprit is a trusted helper met mid-investigation, and fakes his own victimhood
  off-camera (heard, not seen) — fair-play because the "attack" was never shown.
- Trap fails, improvisation with the clue substance itself captures the monster.
