# Digimon Adventure Ep 16 — "The Arrival of SkullGreymon" (JP: "Dark Evolution! SkullGreymon")
## Structure only — no transcript saved. Short excerpt only where noted.

Sample rationale: Cleanest partner-bond crisis / forced-evolution episode in the
series. The human's flaw, not an external villain's power, causes the bad evolution.

## Cold open / act setup
- No monster cold open. Opens IN EXHAUSTION: party crossing a desert, morale low.
- New power object (a crest, won previous episode) is introduced as FALSE CONFIDENCE:
  the leader reassures everyone *because* they hold it, before anyone knows its rule.
- Exposition is delivered as a working hypothesis from the group's analyst character:
  evolution needs (1) partner fed/full of energy + (2) human in danger. Both halves are
  planted here so both can be abused later in the episode.

## Bond crisis — build (escalation in 3 visible steps)
1. INSTRUMENTALISATION: Leader force-feeds partner the GROUP's shared food supply
   despite partner being full/slowed. Care inverted into resource extraction.
   Onlookers, not the partner, voice the worry — partner stays loyal/silent.
2. CHARACTER WITNESS: A teammate gives a benign backstory reading (school sports team:
   "he pushes hard because he wants the team to win"). Same trait, kind framing —
   so the audience, not a narrator, must decide when help became harm.
3. DISPLACEMENT: A low-stakes play beat (an impromptu ball game at the venue) —
   leader angrily kicks the ball away / scolds play during a crisis. The flaw is now
   social, not just tactical: the group, not only the partner, is being spent.

## Venue / trap mechanics
- Destination is led by a GLOWING MACGUFFIN PAIR (tag seeks its crest) belonging to a
  DIFFERENT, less-confident member — the B-plot object resolves while the A-plot rots.
- Venue is a repurposed arena (colosseum fitted with sports goals) — familiar game
  space turned gladiatorial. Villain appears ONLY on the big screen, absent in body,
  and locks the whole party inside a goal-net that shocks anyone who touches it.
- Separation device: the one partner who matters is the ONLY one who didn't make it
  into the trap — he must fight alone, which is exactly what the leader wanted.

## Confrontation beat sequence
1. THREAT DISPLAY: A mirror opponent — an enemy version of the partner's own evolved
   form, with a control collar implication — rampages in. Mirror-match, not a new monster.
2. UNDERPOWERED FIGHT: Hero partner evolves to the SAME form but loses visibly:
   sluggish, reflexes dulled — payoff of the force-feeding plant (his power move even
   misfires as a belch in the dub gag beat). Cause-and-effect, not random weakness.
3. ESCAPE B-PLOT INTERCUT: Trapped party tunnels out; the glowing object turns out to
   be set INTO the blocking stone itself — B-plot member's crest found by the group
   helping each other, in contrast to the leader forcing his own crest's power.
4. FORCED TRIGGER: Leader deliberately runs into the arena and draws the enemy's
   attention — manufacturing the "human in danger" condition from the cold-open rule.
5. WRONG PAYOFF / REVERSAL: Crest + device glow, evolution fires — but into a WRONG,
   skeletal, out-of-control ultimate form. It defeats the mirror enemy in seconds,
   then turns on the friends. Allied evolved partners (3 of them) cannot stop it.
6. EXHAUSTION, NOT DEFEAT: Nobody beats it. It burns out, runs out of energy, and
   reverts ALL the way past rookie to the baby/in-training stage — regression as cost.

## Payoff / repair
- Reverted partner apologises first; group absolves him ("not his fault").
- Leader takes named responsibility in one sentence of self-diagnosis: holding the
  crest made him think he had to fight ALONE. Apology directed to partner first.
- No punishment, no lost power object: the lesson is stored as RULE KNOWLEDGE for the
  audience — correct evolution later in the series must be earned by the opposite
  behaviour (self-sacrifice / trust), which this episode defines by negative example.

## Ending hook
- SOFT hook only: second member's crest acquired (collection advances 1 step),
  villain still at large and remote, party resumes the journey chastened.
  The hook is emotional debt, not a cliffhanger.

## Homage / reference beats (shape only)
- Gladiatorial arena duel: hero's creature vs. collared mirror creature for a
  remote, screen-watching tyrant's entertainment.
- Sports-ground-in-ancient-arena incongruity gag (the party's shared hobby from home
  literally built into the villain's venue).
- Short excerpt (structure example, dub gag timing, <60 chars): the evolved partner's
  signature blast "comes out as a belch" after overeating — plant/payoff in one gag.

## Adaptation mechanics (what transfers)
- Evolution/power-up gated by the HUMAN's emotional conduct, not partner's stats.
- Abuse a stated power rule literally (feed + endanger) -> get a corrupted result.
- Mirror-match antagonist; victory that is worse than losing; burnout regression.
- B-plot object found by cooperation while A-plot forcing fails — thematic counterpoint
  staged in parallel, never stated in dialogue.
