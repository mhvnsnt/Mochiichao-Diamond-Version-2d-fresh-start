# JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Stardust Crusaders Ep 34-35 — "D'Arby the Gambler, Part 1+2"
## Structure only — no transcript saved. One confrontation, two episodes.

Sample rationale: The task asked for ONE episode built around a single opponent
confrontation. This confrontation is a single continuous scene (one cafe, one table)
split across Ep 34 (setup + two preliminary losses) and Ep 35 (the poker payoff).
Ep 34 is the primary sample; Ep 35 is included only because the confrontation's
reversal/payoff lands there. No physical fight occurs at any point.

## Cold open / act setup
- ARRIVAL + CLOCK: Heroes reach the destination city at last, but the goal is a
  needle in it (one photographed building among millions) and a dying-relative
  deadline has shrunk to days. Search montage of failure establishes they NEED
  information more than they need to win a fight.
- Opponent is found BY ASKING AROUND: he is the one person who volunteers that he
  knows the location. Help is offered only as a wager, never for money.

## Opponent / ability reveal structure (staged, 3 locks)
1. LOCK 1 — TRIVIAL BET, LETHAL PRICE: A throwaway bet (which of two food scraps a
   nearby cat takes first). The impulsive teammate accepts, jokingly "betting his
   soul," treating the opponent as a madman. He loses on a last-second animal swerve.
2. ABILITY REVEAL: The opponent's power manifests and converts the loser's soul into
   a small collectible token (a poker chip). Rules stated BY DEMONSTRATION:
   - Power triggers not on losing the game but on the loser inwardly ADMITTING defeat.
   - Killing the opponent would destroy the trapped souls — violence is ruled out
     in one line, before anyone tries it.
   - He displays a COLLECTION of prior victims' tokens: threat display = back-catalogue.
   - He openly boasts that cheating is legitimate if uncaught: his ethic, stated.
   - Reveal beat: the cat was his pet all along — the "chance" bet was rigged before
     it was offered. Nothing in his arena is neutral, including bystanders.
3. LOCK 2 — THE CRAFTY ELDER LOSES AT HIS OWN KIND OF GAME: A surface-tension game
   (coins into a brim-full glass; whoever spills loses). Elder cheats visibly-to-us
   (secretly tops up the liquid on his turn; tries psychological destabilising by
   deliberately misnaming the opponent). Opponent out-cheats invisibly: post-loss
   DEDUCTION beat — hero spots a melted-chocolate shim tilting the glass, recalculating
   its capacity. Elder, believing his own arithmetic, admits defeat inwardly -> soul taken.
   Lesson stored: this opponent beats specialists at their specialism.

## Main confrontation — poker (Ep 35)
### Threat display / anti-cheat ritual
- Hero demonstrates his power's precision VISION (calls a shuffled deck's order)
  explicitly as a warning, not an attack.
- Opponent counters with his own sense display (touch-memorised deck).
- First deal: hero catches a "second deal" cheat by perception and his power breaks
  the opponent's finger — the ONLY physical violence in the whole confrontation,
  used as a rules-enforcement gesture, then never repeated.
- A neutral child dealer is recruited — later revealed (to audience/opponent's side)
  to be on the opponent's payroll, as is the entire cafe. Arena is wholly owned.

### Beat sequence
1. CALIBRATION LOSS: Hero loses hand 1 and half his stake. Opponent relaxes completely.
2. ANOMALY: Hand 2 — hero refuses to look at his own cards, keeps them as dealt.
   Opponent is dealt a near-maximum hand (four kings) and still destabilises,
   because an unread hand cannot be modelled.
3. ESCALATION LADDER (each raise is a person): hero raises with (a) rest of his own
   soul, (b) a present teammate's soul — teammate publicly consents on pure trust,
   (c) an ABSENT teammate's soul (proposed by the opponent as a mind-game; hero
   accepts without hesitation), (d) his own MOTHER's soul — the person the whole
   journey exists to save.
4. MICRO-TELLS AS WEAPONS: Between raises, hero's power produces small impossible
   comforts at unseen speed — a lit cigarette appears in his mouth; a cocktail is
   suddenly in his hand, half-drunk. He never claims anything. The opponent INFERS:
   if the power is that fast, it could have swapped his cards unobserved.
5. IMPOSSIBLE PRICE: To call the mother's-soul raise, opponent must stake the one
   thing he cannot pay: his master's secret ability — leaking it means execution by
   his own side. Every option is now loss: fold = defeat-admission = power backfires;
   call = possible loss + death sentence.
6. REVERSAL / PAYOFF: Opponent breaks down psychologically, subconsciously admits
   defeat, folds without a showdown. His own power releases ALL trapped souls
   (including his whole prior collection). He is left mentally destroyed and CANNOT
   answer the heroes' questions — the heroes win the people but gain ZERO information.
   Victory with the objective still unmet: the search plot does not advance an inch.
7. POST-REVEAL: Hero's hand is shown to the audience/teammate to have been WORTHLESS
   all along. He never looked because not-knowing kept his face, and therefore his
   bluff, perfectly honest. Power was used only for props; the weapon was composure.

## Posing / menace grammar (structural, not visual copying)
- Menace is STATIC: seated opponent, still camera, long takes at one table;
  tension is carried by inserts (hands, chips, sweat, glass) and internal monologue.
- Posing functions as punctuation at rule-changes (power manifestation, a raise, a
  breakdown), not continuously — each exaggerated tableau marks a stake change.
- Sweat/bleach-out visual deterioration of the OPPONENT is the damage bar: his face,
  not a health meter, tracks the fight.

## Ending hook
- ANTI-HOOK: Souls restored, villain neutralised, but no location, no secret, and
  the deadline is shorter. Next-episode engine is the SAME search, resumed from zero.

## Homage / reference beats (shape only)
- Casino-film / western-saloon high-stakes poker showdown shape, transplanted into
  a street cafe; professional-magician handwork was consulted for the card handling
  (per director commentary noted on the wiki page).
- Gambler-with-a-collection-of-souls as a folk-devil-at-the-crossroads wager shape.

## Adaptation mechanics (what transfers)
- A "battle" with violence contractually disabled in the first 5 minutes by the
  ability's own rules; deduction replaces damage.
- Ability with a PSYCHOLOGICAL trigger (admitting defeat) so the climax must be a
  mental break, not a knockout.
- Bluff built from TRUE micro-feats (speed props) implying a FALSE macro-feat
  (card swap) — opponent defeats himself with correct reasoning from real evidence.
- Escalate stakes in PERSONS, not points; final raise prices the opponent's life
  against the hero's dearest person.
- Win condition achieved, mission objective denied — victory that costs screen time.
