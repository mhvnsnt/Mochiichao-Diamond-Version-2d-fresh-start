# #226 — MIGRATION SPECTACLES → the world, moving, witnessed

**Seat in the doctrine:** #144 founded cross-region
migration as data: **paired #113 overlays** sharing a
`migrationId` (a departure overlay and an arrival
overlay, a window, a border between them) —
populations in timed presence, derived, never stored
as journeys. #144's subject was *where species are*.
This spec's subject is what that movement is **like
to stand in front of**: the migration as a spectacle
— a seasonal mass movement a Ranger can travel to,
watch, and remember. The spectacle is a presentation
and event layer over #144's pairs; it founds no
second migration data, and where the two could
disagree, #144's pair is the fact and this is the
view from the ground.

**What was studied.** Mass-migration events in the
publicly documented record (natural and fictional):
the spectacle's grammar (a route, a season, a
density that peaks and passes); witnessing as the
core interaction (observation posts, counts, the
etiquette of not interfering); the anti-pattern —
spectacle as a harvest (a rare-spawn bonanza with a
festival skin) — refused here in favour of the
documented naturalist pattern: the animals are going
somewhere; you are watching them go. The kit taken:
the witnessed event record, overlay-keyed encounters,
and transit behaviour as the spectacle's texture.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/migration-spectacle/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The spectacle record.** One record per migration
  event, keyed to #144's pair:

  ```
  { spectacleId, migrationId (#144's pair),
    window: <from #144/#19>, witnessPoints: [<area refs>],
    chronicleClass: "phenomena" }
  ```

  The record adds **witnessing** and nothing else:
  `witnessPoints` are the areas from which the
  movement is presented at spectacle density (a
  crossing, a strait, a pass — geography is content,
  OPEN). The spectacle's timing is #144's window read
  through #19's clock and #195's seasonal rotation
  (its package was not in the workspace at build
  time — the rotation join is registered UNVERIFIED
  in STAGING; the window itself is #144's and is
  verified).
- **Witnessed, not farmed — the load-bearing law.**
  During a spectacle, encounters in the affected
  areas are **the migration overlay's own** (#113's
  discipline, verbatim: an overlay is a roll-time
  transform over the area's #7 table; the tables on
  disk never change). The overlay's rows are what
  they are — the spectacle record contains no
  encounter table of its own and boosts nothing: no
  rate multiplier, no rarity window, no spectacle-only
  species. What the spectacle adds is *density of
  presentation* — the visible, audible mass of the
  movement (#215's beds and #152's profiles at crowd
  scale) — not density of opportunity. A Ranger who
  travels to a spectacle to *watch* is rewarded with
  the watching; a Ranger who travels to exploit it
  finds the ordinary overlay, honestly labelled.
- **Transit behaviour.** The spectacle's Mochi are
  **passing through**: their #152 behaviour profiles
  are read in the wild view's *transit* register —
  movement-directed, low curiosity, threat response
  keyed to the route, not the witness. (The transit
  register is a read proposed into #152's vocabulary
  in its home; until accepted there, spectacle
  presentation cites the wild view and notes the
  register as a proposal.) Individuals encountered
  from the overlay are ordinary wild individuals
  (#80) whose home is elsewhere — the dex and habitat
  consequences are #144's projections, unchanged.
- **Announcement and memory.** A coming spectacle is
  #90/#172 media matter and a #224 `phenomena` item
  (sourced from #144's pair — the paper prints the
  expectation, labelled as projection per #142's
  law). A witnessed spectacle may stamp a #104
  occasion (a proposal into #104's home — the honour,
  if any, is for *witnessing*, never for a take).
  The spectacle leaves the world exactly as #144
  said it would: the departure area empties on the
  window's close because the overlay lifts, not
  because anything was stored or spent.
- **Show lane.** A migration is a ready-made episode
  set-piece under #82: the cast joins the road as the
  movement passes — cast **from the same overlay
  data** the game rolls (one canon of populations;
  the show's spectacle and the game's are the same
  event in two media). Conservation reads cleanly:
  the spectacle changes no one's state; it is the
  world being large, in view, for one episode — and
  the Forest clause holds on screen as everywhere:
  no spectacle route crosses the depths (#163), and
  what migrates past the Forest goes *past* it.
