# #216 — COMPLETION CEREMONY SYSTEM → the Hall of Fame, presented

**Doctrine seat:** #126 founded the Hall of Fame:
append-only snapshot records written once per
capstone clear at the moment #100's
`story.champion_defeated` flag (#12) sets —
snapshots precisely because history must not change
when the living record does. And #126's own
fragment lists, among its open slots, **"ceremony
presentation"** — named and left open. This package
is that slot's spec: the ceremony that presents the
record the moment it is written. It is proposed
into #126's home per the proposal discipline; until
accepted there, the ceremony is spec-level. The
governing law is stated first because everything
follows from it: **the ceremony writes nothing but
the #126 record itself** — it is the presentation
*of* the write, never a second write.

**What was studied.** Induction/Hall-of-Fame
ceremonies in the publicly documented record (the
reference games' Hall of Fame room: the team
presented one by one, the portrait taken, the
record read back) and ceremony grammar generally:
a ceremony presents facts that already exist to the
people they belong to, in a fixed order, and its
power is that it adds nothing.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/completion-ceremony/v1` (presentation grammar over #126's record; no new stored shape)

- **Trigger and content (all read from the record
  being written).** The ceremony fires on the
  #126 write (first clear and every rematch clear,
  per #126's fragment) and presents exactly the
  snapshot's contents: the sequence as run (dojo/
  elite ids in order — #100's composition, copied
  as ids), the team rows (species via #23 lookups;
  individuals as they were *at the clear* — the
  snapshot, not the living record, so a teammate
  since traded (#68) or released (#106) stands in
  the portrait as they were, which is the whole
  point of #126's design), and the Ranger's licence
  standing line (#98's derived view, read live).
  The Fragment case renders full beside it (#213's
  `case-complete` state) — the case is the run's
  portrait in object form, presented by the grammar
  #213 specified.
- **The portrait.** The ceremony's centre is the
  team portrait: the team as recorded, posed as a
  group. In-world, the sitting joins #196's portrait
  & studio system (the ceremony portrait is a
  studio sitting with the ceremony as its occasion —
  the join is proposed to #196, whose spec was still
  building at this build; see STAGING). The portrait
  as an *asset* obeys #156's currency law when it
  travels (a promo may cite the portrait; its pieces
  cite their marked-current versions).
- **Two forms.** *First clear:* the full ceremony —
  sequence read, team presented member by member
  (each member's honours-at-clear from the snapshot
  rows are shown with them, #104's stamps as facts
  already held), portrait, case. *Rematch clears*
  (#87/#100 ladders write #126 records too): the
  short form — portrait + the record's read-back,
  no re-induction theatre; a second full ceremony
  for the same Ranger would devalue the first, and
  the grammar says so by shape. Durations and
  staging values are production data, OPEN.
- **Writes nothing else.** No honours are granted
  by the ceremony (honours shown were earned in the
  run and stamped by their own systems), no flags
  set beyond the clear flag that triggered it, no
  items, no unlocks performed by the ceremony
  itself — post-game doors read #12's flags
  (#127's discovery model), and if a ceremony scene
  *shows* a door opening, the flag did it, not the
  party. A ceremony implementation that writes
  anything but presenting #126's record fails this
  spec's one law.

## Show lane — the finale mirror

A season's completion ceremony is finale grammar
under #82: the ceremony is a canon beat the arc
plan names (entry ≠ exit is the *point* of a
finale — the conservation checklist records the
champion beat as the named change). The show
ceremony presents the season's team as the game
ceremony presents the snapshot's: member by
member, as they were — including anyone the season
parted from, which is the snapshot doctrine wearing
story clothes. The closing image is the team
portrait (the show's own asset, built from current
pieces under #156's law when it travels to promo).
The show ceremony, like the game's, *adds* nothing:
no new member, no return, no revelation that the
season didn't earn — a finale that invents is a
conservation defect.
