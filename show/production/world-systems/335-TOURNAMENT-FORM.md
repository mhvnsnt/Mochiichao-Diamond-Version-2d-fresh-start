# #335 — THE TOURNAMENT FORM → the Tenkaichi staging, dressed over #151's core

**First, what already exists — because the real-code
law forbids building it twice.** #151 already built
the tournament's *core*: one bracket/record system
(`mochiichao/tournament/v1` — entrants, fixtures as
ordinary battles with a bracket address, named
rounds), a closed list of what a tournament may
change (records, #104 honours, licence-standing
inputs — nothing else), a game lane that runs
brackets and a show lane that runs an arc under
#82's conservation, with #64's boss-intro grammar at
the late rounds. And #151 left the honest open slot
this package exists to serve: **whether any
canonical tournament exists, where, and its name —
owner's, entire.**

**The gap, precisely.** #151's form is a league
conference seen from the record office. It has no
*stage*: no single arena the world gathers in, no
announcer, no crowd at full size (it predates
#242's dojo crowd), no spectacle grammar (it
predates List 32's doctrine). DBZ's Tenkaichi
Budokai is the documented master of exactly that
layer — one ring, one voice, a crowd that is half
the show, honour as the only real stake. This spec
builds that layer as the **candidate form**: the
dressing a Mochiichao tournament wears *if* canon
ever holds one, seated over #151's core, forking
nothing.

## Native spec — the Tenkaichi layer over `mochiichao/tournament/v1` (#151)

- **The arena is a place.** A tournament under
  this form names one arena (a #119 interior/
  place record, authored): the ring, the stands,
  the backstage. The form is place-shaped because
  Tenkaichi is — the crowd comes *somewhere*, and
  #306's living-world standard (commissioned
  citation, List 31 mid-build) can then hold the
  town around it: a tournament weekend changes the
  host town's #93 life, and that, not the bracket,
  is most of what residents remember.
- **The announcer is a role, not a narrator.** One
  human voice owns the ring: introductions,
  calls, the count of the crowd's mood. Game lane:
  the announcer is #301's conversation family
  pointed at a crowd — authored lines as content,
  triggered by fixture beats (#10's queue). Show
  lane: the announcer is the arc's one licensed
  near-narrator, and the subtitle law still binds
  — patter is ambience-class sound; only the calls
  that *matter* (a name, a fall, a champion) earn
  the important-moment subtitle. Creatures at a
  tournament are cries only, as everywhere.
- **The crowd runs at full size.** #242's crowd
  system staged at arena scale: cast from the
  host town's facts (#93/#145), reactions keyed to
  #10's tempo and to *stakes the crowd can see* —
  and #242's chorus law rides in verbatim: applause
  is remembered, never scored; no honour, outcome,
  or price reads the crowd. Tenkaichi's crowd is
  the loudest thing in the building and decides
  nothing, and ours keeps that honesty.
- **Honour stakes, at maximum visibility.** The
  form's prize fiction is #104's honours and
  #131's signature register: a tournament is where
  signature performances are *seen* (List 32's
  spectacle doctrine applies at full budget in the
  late rounds — the arena is one of the places the
  doctrine's big moments are *for*). Placement
  honours land in #104's catalogue when the owner
  builds it; this form asserts no catalogue entry.
- **Ring-out — proposed, not founded.** Tenkaichi
  fixtures can end by the ring's edge. Our fixture
  battles are ordinary battles in #30's wrapper
  (#151), and battle ends are #17's country: this
  spec therefore **proposes** a fixture-context
  rule into #151's file — a tournament fixture may
  carry an arena-edge condition whose satisfaction
  ends the fixture as a *result fact*, the battle
  underneath untouched — and founds nothing here.
  If the proposal is refused at landing, the form
  stands without it; Tenkaichi colour is not a
  load-bearing wall.
- **Backstage grammar.** #151 already seats the
  episodes between matches; this form names the
  Tenkaichi backstage beats as arc production data
  (the draw, the waiting room, the rival met in a
  corridor — contents are writing, the *seats* are
  the form).

## The fence, stated once and plainly

Building this form asserts **nothing** about canon
holding a tournament. #151's WHETHER slot — whether
any canonical tournament exists, where, and its
name — stays the owner's, untouched by this
package. If canon never holds one, List 34 will
have built a dressed stage nobody performs on, and
the register (#327) will say so. That is the honest
price of having the form ready, and the owner
ordered the form, not the fact.
