# #314 — YOKAI & SPIRIT SPECTACLE → the uncanny, done properly: power staged as ritual and custom

**The gap this fills.** The yokai family's grammar:
how Region 4's folklore powers — oni, spirits,
yokai, the old things — are staged when they perform.
Every other family in this list stages power as
*physics* (mass, pressure, voltage, height). This
family stages power as **etiquette**: a spirit's
spectacle is ritual-shaped, done in the old way, in
the proper order, with the proper objects — and the
terror and the wonder both come from how *correct*
it is. Region 4's flavour law (the folklore method is
already on file: list7's R4-BUG-LINE/R4-FOLKLORE-
METHOD working papers) makes this the region's native
staging language, not an imported one.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
documented folklore staging in the reference record —
from the games' shrine-and-festival grammar to the
anime's spirit etiquette — repeats one discovery: the
supernatural that follows *rules* is more wondrous
and more frightening than the supernatural that
doesn't. A spirit who must be greeted correctly, who
performs its power as a ceremony performed a thousand
times, carries history in every gesture. The kit
taken: custom is the special effect. (#244 built the
customs system for Rangers; this family is what
custom looks like when the *other side* keeps it.)

## Staging grammar — `mochiichao/spectacle-yokai/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The proper order (ritual shape).** A yokai
  performance has a *form*: an opening propriety
  (arrival acknowledged, a greeting-shape, an object
  placed or a threshold respected), the working
  itself performed as a sequence that is visibly
  *practised* — gestures in their inherited order,
  never improvised — and a closing propriety. Skipping
  a step is itself a staging event: the audience that
  has learned the form *feels* the omission, which is
  how this family stages alarm without a single loud
  cue. The forms are authored content per tradition
  (the content lane writes them under the R4 folklore
  method); the grammar fixes that there *is* a form,
  that it is kept, and that breaking it reads.
- **Objects carry the spectacle.** Where other
  families fill the frame with effect layers, this
  family's frame fills with *things*: masks, bells,
  paper, rope, flame in a row, water in a vessel —
  ordinary sacred objects, correctly used. The power
  is visible in what the objects do when the ritual
  reaches them (the bell sounds without a hand; the
  papers stand), staged with #313's restraint but the
  opposite meaning: not wrongness — *rightness*, one
  degree past possible.
- **The two registers.** The family performs in
  **wonder** (the festival register: colour, music in
  #72's bank keyed ceremonial, a community witnessing
  — spectacle as belonging) and in **dread** (the
  register of #313's fence-line: the same form kept
  in an empty place, at the wrong hour, by something
  whose correctness is the frightening part). Same
  grammar, same forms; the register is carried by
  place, hour (#19's phases), and witness — never by
  making the ritual itself sloppier in dread. Dread
  performed *improperly* is just mess; dread performed
  perfectly, alone, is the family's climax.
- **The Dark Forest borrows this family.** Stated
  plainly, as the brief directs: the depths' dread
  register — the mist, the humming surface, the dread
  underneath (#82's production record, #215's beds) —
  is staged in this family's grammar where the show
  stages it at all: things in the Forest are done in
  an old way, properly, by no one visible. **The
  contents stay canon-open**: what stopped moving,
  what the dread *is* — this grammar writes no word
  of it. It supplies the staging language for a
  mystery it is forbidden to explain (the same fence
  #138's side stories carry: they may not answer it
  either). In the game, the depths keep their
  withholding entire (empty tables, empty plate —
  #7/#128), and this family stages nothing there but
  the surface's edge-moments the beds already hold.

## Game mirror

On #9's tracks: performer track carries the ritual
sequence (gesture order is performance data — the
family's scripts are the list's most *choreographed*,
with the fewest effect layers); sound cues draw on
#72's ceremonial bank and #215's beds; background
track stays near-still — the form plays against a
held world. Battle mirror: a yokai-tradition
performer's battle staging keeps the ritual shape at
battle tempo — the propriety beats compress but never
vanish (a spirit who skips its form to attack faster
has been staged out of its family).

## The discipline

- **Custom is never decoration.** A ritual shape
  borrowed as visual garnish for a non-yokai
  performance is a defect in this family and an insult
  to the region's law: the forms belong to the
  traditions that keep them (R4 folklore method's own
  fence, restated where the staging lives).
- **Never played for laughs at the form itself.**
  Comedy may live around a ceremony (witnesses,
  Rangers out of their depth — #244's etiquette beats);
  the form itself is performed straight, in both
  registers, always.
- **The mystery fence is absolute.** This grammar may
  stage the Forest's dread; it may not stage an
  *explanation* of it. Any script — show or game —
  whose yokai staging answers a canon-open question
  has broken the family's first law, whatever its
  class.
