# #266 — FLOWER & PLANT SYMBOLISM (SHOW PRODUCTION) → what a flower in the frame is doing there

**The gap this fills.** The show now has flowering
bodies everywhere: crowns that open (#257/#259),
gardens that bloom (#264), pollen in the air (#261).
Production needs a shared answer to a director's
question — *what does this flower mean, here, in this
shot?* — that doesn't get re-invented per episode and
doesn't harden into a fake dictionary. This spec is
that shared vocabulary. It is production grammar,
like #50's colour scripts: it instances **no
symbolism assignments at all**, because which flower
means what for which character is episode writing,
and episode writing is not a system.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/flower-symbolism/v1` (prose grammar — no fragment, deliberately)

- **The bloom-state scale.** Five states of a flower
  in frame, each carrying a plain production reading
  the whole crew shares:
  - `bud-closed` — potential, guardedness, a thing
    not yet said. (It is also simply Budguard's head:
    the scale's honesty is that a state can be mere
    anatomy in one shot and a statement in another —
    framing decides, the vocabulary names the choice.)
  - `opening` — change underway; the shot's verb.
  - `full-bloom` — openness, arrival, the kept
    promise; the widest read on the scale.
  - `petal-fall` — beauty in the act of ending;
    transience without tragedy unless staged so.
  - `spent` — rest after flowering; *not* death —
    a plant that has bloomed is a plant between
    blooms, and the grammar refuses the cheap
    equation (canon's plants are perennial people,
    not cut flowers).
  The scale is the layer's whole dictionary. Any
  finer meaning is the episode's to build from
  these, on screen, and not the layer's to store.
- **Three seats a flower can occupy in a shot** —
  the second half of the vocabulary:
  - `species-flower` — a flower that is someone's
    body (a Verdilance crown, a planted resident in
    bloom). Its state reads as that *character's*
    state — the strongest seat; staging it is
    directing a performance, and #82's conservation
    applies (its state across an episode is
    continuity, changed on screen or not at all).
  - `place-flower` — the location's own flowering
    (a garden bed, a route's verge). Emotional
    weather for the scene (#202-adjacent: mood's
    scenic cousin) — it colours, it does not argue.
  - `carried-flower` — a flower a character brings,
    gives, or finds. The only seat where a flower is
    an *object* — and note it is never a Mochi's
    grown part (a picked flower is a plant cutting,
    world flora, not someone's anatomy; the
    grown-not-held law's mirror: what grows on a
    Mochi is never a prop, what is carried never
    pretends to be grown).
- **Two production rules.** (1) **State changes
  happen on screen or not at all** — a species-flower
  closed in scene 2 and open in scene 5 with no
  opening beat is a continuity defect (#53 family),
  unless the episode has honestly passed the time
  and shown the passing. (2) **The game's truths
  still bind the frame** — a bloom shown out of its
  world's season/weather logic (#257's read) is a
  deliberate statement the episode owns, the way
  #200's casting owns a lustre appearance; the
  vocabulary exists so that statement is made
  knowingly, in shared words, rather than by a
  background artist's accident.

## What this layer never does

It never assigns: no flower is entered against a
character, a feeling, or an event anywhere in data.
It never crosses into the game: the game has bloom
*states* (#257) and Garden beds (#264); symbolism
is the show reading its own frames, and no game
record may cite it. And it never narrates: a
symbol the audience needs explained in a subtitle
has failed as a symbol — the subtitle law stands
over this layer like every other.

## Wiring

Production grammar beside #82 (arc/conservation) and
#50 (colour scripts); reads #257 (bloom states as
world truth), #259 (which flowers are bodies),
#264/#261 (place flowering and air), #53-family
continuity. Writes: nothing, anywhere — a vocabulary
the crew speaks, not a record the repo stores.

## Open slots

By design, the only open thing is everything that
should be: every actual assignment of flower to
feeling is episode writing, owned by whoever writes
the episode, forever. There is nothing to land and
nothing to wait for.

## Coverage — honest

- A vocabulary and two rules. Its smallness is the
  point: symbolism machinery that instances
  assignments would be a system pretending to be a
  muse, and this programme has been careful, list
  after list, not to build those.
