# #296 — SONIC GRAMMAR FOR THE SHOW → what makes it beloved, as production rules

**Doctrine seat:** The owner directed (2026-10-27)
that what makes the Sonic shows and games popular be
pulled in 100% and wired into our universe — as
*grammar*: the staging and story habits that make an
audience feel speed, attitude, and adventure. This
package states those habits as production rules on
#82's arc-planning layer. It imports no character,
no place, no brand mark, and no design — grammar is
what remains when every name is removed, and grammar
is all that crosses over (the construction-kit rule).

**The doctrine sentence (owner-ruled, 2026-10-27,
folded in before this package closed):**
**Spectacle is per-power-family.** In the source
world, speed is staged as spectacle because speed is
its hero's thing. Here, *every* character gets their
spectacle: each power family — lightning, water,
strength, rock, earth and ground, speed, the aerial
families (flight, levitation), the ghostly and
phantom families, yokai spirit powers, the
paranormal — carries **its own staging grammar**,
and an episode stages a character's power the way
that power deserves to be seen. Speed is merely one
family among them — historically first in this
package's sources, with no rank above the others.
The deep build — each family's grammar written out
in full — is **List 32's** (#307 spectacle doctrine
and its per-family packages, building separately).
This package states the doctrine, seats it in the
production system, and points: **#296 founds;
List 32 carries.**

## Native spec — `mochiichao/show-grammar-sonic/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

Production rules, each stated as *what it does for
an audience* — the test every rule must pass at
board stage:

- **R1 — Spectacle per family (the doctrine, as a
  rule).** Every principal character's power family
  has a staging grammar (List 32 authors them): how
  the camera moves, what the sound does (#215 beds,
  #51 classes), what the environment gives back.
  A scene that stages a water character with the
  speed family's grammar — all cuts and streaks —
  fails the canon pass the way a wrong cry does:
  the power must *look like itself*. Adventure is
  the register: spectacle serves wonder and daring,
  never power-ranking — a spectacle beat shows what
  a power is *like*, not how strong it is (#70's
  refusal, in staging form).
- **R2 — Zone variety as episode grammar.** The
  source world's episodes are remembered as *places*:
  each arc's sector has one dominant visual idea,
  stated in the episode's colour script (#50) before
  boards open — a new place should be describable
  in one sentence by a child who saw it once. #82
  arc plans carry a `zone idea` line per arc
  (production data, proposed into #82's format);
  two consecutive arcs may not share a dominant idea.
  This is the connected world (#127) made *felt*:
  travelling reads as arriving somewhere different,
  not as the same road continuing.
- **R3 — The mirror-rival structure.** The source
  world's most durable invention is not its hero but
  the hero's **dark double**: a recurring figure who
  is the lead's power turned to the opposite purpose,
  whose scenes are staged as the lead's grammar
  *inverted* (same family, reversed register — per
  R1, the inversion is legible because the family
  grammar is known). Mochiichao already owns this
  structure natively — List 29's anti-hero line and
  #131's signature register are its homes — and this
  rule binds the *staging*: when a mirror pair
  shares a scene, the board must be able to say
  which grammar is whose, and the episode's turn is
  the moment one borrows the other's. No character
  is imported by this rule; it is a shape any canon
  pair can be cast into, by the owner's casting.
- **R4 — Attitude is staging, not dialogue.**
  Confidence, irreverence, warmth — the source
  world's leads read as *cool* because of how scenes
  are built around them: entrances staged as events,
  competence shown before it is discussed, humour
  carried in timing and reaction, and long held
  beats where a character simply *is* in a place.
  Native form: #120's cutscene staging and #82's
  beats carry an `attitude` note per principal
  (production data) — what the staging must make
  undeniable about this person before their first
  line. In this universe the rule splits by the
  voice law, and the split is the point: **humans**
  carry attitude in staging *and* speech; **creatures
  stay cries-only** — a Mochi's attitude is posture,
  timing, and cry colour (#155), never words, never
  name-saying. A board that gives a creature a
  catchphrase fails; a board that gives a creature
  a *way of entering a room* passes.
- **R5 — Music identity (leitmotif discipline).**
  The source world is hummed: its characters and
  places carry themes audiences recognise in two
  bars. Native form on the programme's music layer
  (#72's instrument bank + #215's regional beds):
  a **theme registry** (production data) — a
  principal character, a power family (List 32
  reads it), or a major place may hold a theme: a
  short motif, owned by the show's own music
  production, stated once plainly at its subject's
  defining moment and thereafter *varied*, never
  merely repeated (tempo, instrument, and mood
  variants are the composer's data). The registry
  asserts no compositions; it founds the seat and
  the discipline: themes are assigned sparingly (a
  theme everyone has is a theme no one has), and a
  theme's first full statement is an episode event,
  not background.

## What this package is not

Not a character, place, song, or design import
(above). Not a re-founding of List 32: the
per-family grammars live there; if a List 32 family
grammar and this package's rules ever disagree, the
family grammar governs its family and this package
is corrected by citation — the doctrine (R1) is the
only sentence here that binds List 32, and it binds
by the owner's ruling, quoted above.

## Coverage — honest

- **Architecture: fully specified** — five rules,
  each with its audience test and its board-stage
  failure shape.
- **Contents: zero zone ideas, attitudes, or themes
  instanced** — they are per-production authored
  data. The yield is a show that knows *why* its
  model works: every power spectacular in its own
  grammar, every place one sentence big, every hero
  shadowed by a mirror, every entrance an event —
  and not one borrowed face anywhere in it.
