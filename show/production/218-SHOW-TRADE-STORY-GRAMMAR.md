# #218 — SHOW TRADE STORY GRAMMAR → how a trade is staged as story

**The ruling this spec stands on (owner, 2026-10-27,
RULED — not a proposal):** the show **can** depict
trades. The owner's words, in substance: Pokémon
depicts trades in its show all the time — as stories,
as side stories, and as backstory, where characters
talk about how they got their Pokémon in a trade and
how it evolved and such — "we can have stuff like
that." Scope expressly includes **backstory trades**
(a character recounting that they received their
partner in a trade, including trade-evolution
histories). This ruling **resolves the #68-vs-#168
flag** carried since List 18: #68's sentence "the
show lane never trades" is confirmed as the **system**
rule only — the show never runs the trading mechanic,
no episode state depends on #68's protocol — and it
was never a ban on depiction. #168's narrower mirror
(a depicted transfer is an authored story event
consistent with the record semantics) is the correct
reading, and this spec is its grammar.

**What was studied.** Depicted exchanges in the
documented animated record, read as story structure:
the trade staged as a meeting of two creatures'
inclinations (the canonical pattern — a Mochi who
loves performing and a Mochi who loves battling
discover each other's worlds, and the exchange is
*their* resolution, ratified by their Rangers); the
trade as backstory exposition (a partner's history
told in dialogue, carrying a trade-evolution with
it); the refused trade (the exchange that does not
happen because the creature does not want it — a
complete story, not a failed one). The kit taken:
agency-first staging, the refusal as a valid ending,
and one canon of records under both lanes.

## Native grammar — staging a depicted transfer

- **Creature agency is the centre — the load-bearing
  rule.** Under the autonomous-action doctrine
  (creatures act with their own agency; Rangers
  sometimes command), a depicted trade turns on the
  **Mochi's own wishes**. Stated as law, natively:
  **a trade the creature refuses does not happen, on
  screen, ever.** There is no staging — comedic,
  dramatic, or expedient — in which a Ranger trades
  away a protesting partner, or receives one who is
  protesting the move, and the story treats it as
  fine. Distress at a parting is story; refusal is a
  veto. The distinction is the creature's settled
  will, and the episode must make it legible (cries
  and behaviour only — the voice law holds; #136's
  comprehension grammar is how the audience reads it).
- **Rangers facilitate, never dispose.** On screen, a
  Ranger proposes, arranges, grieves, celebrates —
  and never *disposes of* a partner. The staging
  grammar reflects this: the decision beat belongs to
  the creatures (an exchange of looks, a trial
  journey, a shared task), and the Rangers' beat is
  recognition — seeing what their partners have
  already decided.
- **The classes of depicted trade.**
  1. **Present trade** — the exchange happens inside
     the episode's action; staged per the agency rule
     above; closes under #82's conservation as a
     *named canon change* (the transfer is exactly the
     kind of beat #82's checklist exists to name:
     rosters at exit differ from entry because this
     beat happened, on screen, for this reason).
  2. **Side-story trade** — the exchange is the
     episode's B-plot or a short: same grammar, smaller
     stage; the trade's smallness is the point (two
     strangers at a ferry landing; a market-day whim
     the creatures ratify).
  3. **Backstory trade** — no exchange is staged at
     all: a character recounts a completed trade, and
     the story carries its consequences (the partner's
     temperament in their new life; a **trade
     evolution** remembered — "she changed the week
     she came to me" — told, not shown, unless a
     flashback beat is separately authored). Backstory
     trades still obey the agency centre *in the
     telling*: the recounted trade is remembered as
     one the creature chose or came to choose — a
     backstory of a forced trade is a story about a
     wrong, and must be framed as one.
- **One canon of records, two lanes.** A depicted
  trade writes **the same history the game's system
  would**: the individual's keeper history gains the
  entry (`{met_via: trade, from, to, beat}` — #168's
  stamp, the beat being the episode), and nothing else
  about the individual changes. A partner who arrived
  by trade in the show *has that trade in their
  record* in the game; a trade in the game is eligible
  backstory in the show. The lanes never keep separate
  provenances.
- **What depiction never does.** No protocol on
  screen (no escrow UI, no mechanic language — the
  system stays #68's, invisible); no principal partner
  is ever the subject of a depicted trade (#68's
  `story_locked` has a narrative twin: the partner a
  Ranger's story is *about* is not transferable
  material); no trade is staged to fix a plot problem
  (a creature is not a parcel to be rerouted).
