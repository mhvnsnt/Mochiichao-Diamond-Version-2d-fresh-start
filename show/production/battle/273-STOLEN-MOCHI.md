# #273 — THE STOLEN MOCHI'S SIDE → agency, disobedience, and the road home

**The seat of this spec.** Every other system in
this list looks at the thief or the law. This one
looks at the individual in the middle. A stolen
Mochi is not loot: it is a kept partner whose keeper
seat was rewritten by force (#268's `theft` entry),
and the record knows it — the individual's keeping
state is `stolen`, its **true keeper** is cited on
its own record, and its bond to that keeper is
**frozen, not erased**. What follows is what that
means in play: how a stolen Mochi behaves for the
thief, what its true keeper can do, and the hard
road by which a stolen Mochi can come, in time, to
genuinely belong somewhere new.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/stolen-mochi/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **Obedience — the traded model, darkened.**
  Pokémon's answer for a creature whose keeper is
  not the one who raised it is obedience: above a
  level the keeper's standing hasn't earned, the
  creature may refuse, nap, or turn away. A stolen
  Mochi starts further back than any trade: its bond
  to the thief begins at the floor, and its obedience
  read runs on the *thief's* licence tier versus its
  level (Pokémon's shape, #98's tiers) **minus the
  weight of the bond it still holds to its true
  keeper** — the stronger that frozen bond, the less
  of the Mochi the thief actually has. Disobedience
  is declared and visible, per #180's law — rows in
  a table with on-screen cues (the turned back, the
  ignored call, the move used anyway at half heart),
  never invisible probability bends. The table's
  rows and thresholds are data; the classes are:
  *refusal, distraction, sorrow* (underperformance
  without defiance — it does the thing, badly,
  because its heart is elsewhere), and *homesick
  flight* — outside battle, a stolen Mochi with a
  strong frozen bond may simply leave: slip away in
  the night and travel toward its true keeper's
  last known place by the roaming grammar (#267's
  pool mechanics, wearing a different history entry).
  A fled stolen Mochi becomes a recovery encounter
  for *both* Rangers — the thief has lost it; the
  true keeper may find it on the road home.
- **What the thief's care does — the hard road,
  stated with its cost.** Bond accrues to a thief
  the ordinary way (#39's care acts — feeding,
  tending, fighting beside), because bond is the
  individual's fact, not a moral judgement. As
  thief-bond grows, obedience eases. Two costs ride
  it, always: **#269's daily keeping deed** (−1 a
  day while knowingly kept, capped per individual —
  the meter charges rent on a stolen life), and the
  **frozen bond's slow thaw** — only once thief-bond
  passes the frozen bond does the old bond begin,
  very slowly, to recede in the obedience read (it
  never leaves the record; history is not obedience).
  If thief-bond ever genuinely exceeds the old bond
  and stays there, the keeping state can change —
  `stolen` → `settled` — by a recorded act of the
  individual's own choosing in presentation terms
  (it stops looking homeward). The theft entry
  remains in the history forever. The Mochi may
  forgive; the record does not forget, and #269
  charges the thief for every day of the road. That
  is the hard road, priced honestly.
- **The victim's side — recovery.** The true keeper
  is never left with a shrug:
  - **The record knows.** The victim's Mochiioteca
    shows the individual as *stolen* — the derived
    view over the theft entry — with the taker's
    identity if the theft was witnessed or the
    victim was present (a battle snag is face to
    face; the victim always knows *who*, if not
    *where*).
  - **Tracking.** #89's tracking mechanic is the
    road: sign → direction → identity, read along
    the thief's trail while it is warm; fences
    (#274) exist to make trails cold, which is
    their real product.
  - **The law.** Report fast (#272's victim lane) —
    wanted pressure makes the thief's life smaller
    and recovery likelier: a pursued thief may
    abandon, fence cheaply, or be caught holding.
  - **Confrontation.** Found, the thief can be
    faced: a **re-snag** (#268's lawful mirror —
    restitution, +deed) or the thief's surrender of
    the individual (a history entry, `returned`,
    answering the theft), or refusal — which is
    itself a deed (#269) and grounds for the law's
    escalation. A field duel (#210) may be the
    confrontation's *form* — honour's road to the
    moment — but per #210's own stakes law the
    Mochi itself is never the stake on paper; the
    take-back is the re-snag or the surrender, not
    a prize clause.
  - **Recovery completes the record.** A recovered
    individual returns to its true keeper with its
    history lengthened, not cleaned: the theft, the
    time away, and the return are all in sequence
    (#227's storytelling reads exactly this shape).
    Bond resumes from its frozen value — the thaw
    clock only ever ran in the thief's keeping.

## Show lane

A stolen partner is one of the show's strongest
engines: the audience watches the Mochi's side of
it — the turned back, the looking homeward — in
pure cry-and-behaviour grammar (#136). #276 carries
the mirror, and the anti-hero long game the owner
has named will live inside this spec's rules: a
thief lead's stolen partners are where the audience
keeps score.
