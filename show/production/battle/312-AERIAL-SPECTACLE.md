# #312 — AERIAL SPECTACLE → the sky is a stage, and stillness can fly

**The gap this fills.** The aerial family's grammar:
flight, levitation, and sheer speed staged vertically.
This is the family that inherits the Sonic doctrine
most directly — speed as spectacle AND identity
(List 30's SONIC-CATALOGUE.md, C1, landed research
behind #296) — and it must also hold the family's
opposite pole: the levitator, who does not move fast
at all and is spectacular *because* of it.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
Sonic record stages speed by *framing* — the world
streams past, the camera can barely keep the hero,
set-pieces (the loop, the corkscrew) exist so speed
has architecture to violate. The flying record in the
anime stages height the same way: battles climb, the
ground becomes a map, clouds are terrain. And the
levitating record — psychics and ghosts across the
reference works — stages its power as *exemption*:
everything streams, shakes, and falls except the one
figure hanging calm at the centre. The kit taken:
height is staging, speed is framing, levitation is
contrast.

## Staging grammar — `mochiichao/spectacle-aerial/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **Vertical framing (the sky is a stage).** Aerial
  performances are composed vertically: the frame
  includes the drop. The ground appears as a map far
  below (or the plate tilts to give the height),
  altitude is *kept in frame* — a flyer staged at
  ground level with a blue background has not been
  staged aerially at all. Ascent and descent are
  performances, not transitions: leaving the ground
  is a beat, and **landing is punctuation** — the
  sentence ends with weight arriving (#310's smallest
  honest impact, scaled to the body).
- **Wind made visible.** Air is invisible; the family
  stages it by its evidence: cloud interaction (a
  flyer punches through a cloud layer and the layer
  keeps the hole for a beat), streamers and grass
  bending in the wake, the sound bed's wind family
  (#84's ecology, #215's beds) rising with altitude
  and speed. Speed lines and smears (#49's grammar)
  belong to the *world* streaming, never smeared
  across the flyer's own drawn form at signature class
  — the hero stays readable; the world blurs.
- **Speed's dialect (the fast end).** At the family's
  fast end the grammar is Sonic's, translated: speed
  reads through *scenery turnover* (how much world
  passes per beat), through architecture built to be
  violated (loops, drops, threads between obstacles —
  in battle staging, the plate's own features), and
  through the sound bed's pitch of passage. Speed is
  never staged by shaking the performer.
- **Levitation's dialect (stillness inside motion).**
  The levitator hangs: no wingbeat, no strain, no
  bob-cycle cliché — a held, calm figure while the
  world streams, shakes, or falls around them. The
  spectacle is the *contrast*: debris falls past them
  downward, rain falls, opponents scramble for
  footing, and the levitator's stillness is the
  loudest thing in frame. Their movement, when it
  comes, is translation without acceleration — they
  are simply elsewhere, which borders the ghost
  family's wrongness (#313) and is allowed to: the
  families share a fence here, stated in both specs.

## The discipline

- **Height must be earned in frame.** Cutting to a
  character already high, with no ascent and no drop
  in frame, spends the family's capital for nothing —
  the ascent beat is mandatory at signature class.
- **The flyer stays readable.** World-blur, never
  hero-blur, at signature and climax class (#49's
  smear grammar serves motion *around* the drawn
  form); an unreadable smear where the character
  should be is the family's defect.
- **Levitation is not slow flight.** Staging a
  levitator with flapping, effort, or drift-correction
  breaks the dialect — their power is exemption from
  the effort everyone else is paying.
