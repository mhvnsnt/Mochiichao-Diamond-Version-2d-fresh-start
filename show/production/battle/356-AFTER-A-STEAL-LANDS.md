# #356 — AFTER A STEAL LANDS → the battle does not pretend nothing happened

**The moment after.** #268 wrote what a successful
steal does to the *record* (the keeper seat
rewrites, the `theft` entry appends, the
keeping-state opens). #273 wrote what it does to
the stolen Mochi's *life*. This spec writes what
it does to the *battle it happened in* — the
minutes between the catch and the end of the
fight, which every earlier system walked past:
the victim is still standing there, a partner
down, in front of witnesses, holding a team that
just watched it happen.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/aftermath/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The victim fights shorthanded — plainly.** The
  taken Mochi is gone from the battle: its slot is
  empty, no replacement rule softens it, and the
  battle's arithmetic simply has one less
  combatant on the victim's side (formats per
  #157 — in multi formats the side continues at
  its reduced strength). This is stated rather
  than assumed because it is the theft's whole
  in-battle weight: stealing is not only a crime
  on the ledger, it is a *tactical event* — you
  have removed an enemy combatant by taking them
  alive — and the system does not apologise for
  the double nature. (The conscience layer exists
  precisely because the tactic works.)
- **The remaining Mochi read the theft — visibly
  or not at all.** The victim's fielded Mochi may
  carry the moment in its presentation and, only
  where declared, its behaviour — under #180's
  declaration law, in its battle form: any
  mechanical consequence for the victim's remaining
  team must be a **declared row with a visible cue**
  or it does not exist. The candidate rows are
  named as classes for the data pass, instanced
  *nowhere* by this spec: *rage* (a partner taken
  in front of them — an offensive-temper class),
  *sorrow* (a partner taken — a faltering class),
  keyed to the taken Mochi's bond facts with the
  witnesses-of-the-moment (a Mochi who did not see
  it — switched out, already fainted — carries
  nothing; the state is a read of *having been
  there*, computed from the battle's own facts,
  never a stored flag). If the data pass instances
  no rows, the battle still reads the theft in the
  one channel that needs no row: presentation —
  #152's behaviour family has the grief and the
  fury in it, and a Mochi's face is not a modifier.
- **The prize is not a weapon — not in this
  battle, not yet at all.** The stolen Mochi does
  not join the thief's battle side: it is in the
  device, in #273's keeping-state, and it **cannot
  be sent out in the battle it was stolen in** —
  by the battle's own slot rules (it was never in
  the thief's party for this battle; no send-out
  path exists for it), and afterwards only under
  #273's obedience machinery, which begins at the
  floor. Stated as a battle spec because the
  fantasy it refuses is specific: steal the ace,
  turn it on its keeper in the same fight. The
  record forbids it twice over — the Mochi does
  not know the thief, may not obey the thief
  (#273's rows: refusal, distraction, sorrow,
  homesick flight), and its heart, if theft closed
  or deepened anything in it, is #287's concern,
  not a fresh recruit's. A thief who wants to
  *use* what they took has a long road (#273/#288);
  the battle they took it in gives them only the
  absence they made.
- **The law can arrive mid-battle.** At the top of
  #272's wanted scale — *hunted* and *most-wanted*,
  read from **landed reports**, never from the
  current deed's unlanded noise — a battle in a
  patrolled place can be **interrupted by responders
  arriving**: Rangers of standing (#93/#145 actors,
  #302's escalation ladder) enter the battle's
  edge and the fight's question changes from *win*
  to *get away*. Mechanics, stated plainly:
  - The interruption is a battle event in #10's
    queue (an arrival beat), not a cutscene steal
    of agency: the current turn resolves, then the
    battle converts.
  - Conversion ends the battle *as a battle*: no
    winner is declared, no defeat outcomes write
    (the battle was taken away from both sides —
    #353's write-once discipline reads an
    interrupted battle as *unfinished*, and an
    unfinished battle pays nothing to anyone).
  - What follows is a **pursuit state in the
    world**, not a RUN command — #254 bars escape
    from Ranger battles as a *command*, and an
    interruption is not a command: it is the world
    ending the fight. The pursuit itself (flight,
    capture, surrender to the law) runs on #302's
    escalation machinery and #272's response flow,
    in the field systems, where chases live.
  - A theft *committed in this battle* cannot
    trigger its own interruption — its report has
    not landed yet (#272's flow takes time, and the
    victim's fast lane starts at the battle's end).
    Interruptions answer *old* wanted, arriving at
    the worst possible moment, which is exactly the
    fiction they exist for.

## Show lane

This is the show's third-act machinery in miniature:
the fight that stops being a fight. Episode staging
(#82) takes the same beats — the empty slot, the
team reading the loss, the prize that will not turn
its head toward its new keeper, and far off, the
sound of the law arriving — and the classes here
are its shot grammar. The stolen Mochi's refusal to
fight its keeper in the same scene is the show's
promise kept in the game's rules (and #284's whole
subject, grown long).
