# #316 — SIGNATURE SPECTACLE → the family grammar is the language; the signature is the voice

**The gap this fills.** #308–#314 built seven
vocabularies. This spec owns the thing that keeps
them from becoming templates: the **individual
dialect** — how a named character's performance of
their family's grammar is recognisably *theirs* and
no one else's. #131 owns the signature *move* (the
designation record: this species' signature, with its
locked seed register). This spec owns the signature
*staging*: the performance layer over it, in both
lanes, under the owner's standing law that signature
attacks are per-species canon and templates are
forbidden.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
anime record's most-remembered attacks are remembered
as *performances*, not moves — the same technique in
two characters' hands is two scenes, because the
staging (the tell, the tempo, the aftermath, what the
world does) belongs to the character. The Sonic
record's C1 finding (SONIC-CATALOGUE, landed) says it
in one line: a character's thing must be recognisable
in a half-second, in every appearance. The kit taken:
a dialect is a small set of staging constants, held
across every performance, inside a family grammar
that everyone shares.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/signature-spectacle/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **What a dialect is.** For each row of #131's
  register, a dialect record names the performance
  constants that make the signature *this* signature:
  - its **tell** (the family's charge grammar, in this
    individual's form — e.g. within #309, one
    performer's orb gathers *inward and down*, droplets
    falling up into it; another's would spiral),
  - its **tempo** (where the beats sit: this one's
    pause before release is long; this one's aftermath
    is the long part),
  - its **world-answer** (which of #307's environment
    channels answer *first* and loudest for them —
    one ground-user's Tremor lifts dust before it
    cracks stone; Gabu's is Gabu's, and another's
    must differ — #311 supplies the tempers, the
    dialect picks the order),
  - its **sound mark** (the bed family's treatment:
    which instrument/strike family enters, per #51/#72,
    laid over #215 — a half-second sonic signature,
    the C1 rule in audio).
  Four constants, no more: a dialect small enough to
  hold in every performance is a dialect that survives;
  a page of constants is a template wearing a name.
- **Seated on the register, not beside it.** The
  dialect record hangs off #131's register row (the
  register is the seat; the dialect is its
  presentation annex — proposed into #131's home in
  STAGING, per the proposal discipline). #147's
  spectacle view (#307) reads dialect + class together:
  a signature performance is *covered* only when its
  script performs its dialect (the coverage question
  "does the script exist" grows the question "is it
  *his*"). Rows whose move representation is still in
  #131's reconciliation (Water Orb, Tremor — `moveId:
  null` there) carry dialects all the same: the
  *performance* is canon even where the registry
  representation is pending, and this spec mirrors
  #131's note rather than resolving it.
- **Cast like a signature, presented like one.** In
  the show, a signature spectacle is **cast**: when a
  character joins the cast, their dialect is authored
  into their production record and performed
  consistently (the #82/#120 staging machinery carries
  it; a guest director staging the signature without
  the dialect has made the #307 defect's personal
  form). In the game, the signature's animscript
  (#9, under #147's coverage) performs the dialect as
  scripted constants — same tell, same tempo, same
  world-answer order — so a player recognises the
  move's owner before the name plate does.
- **The register grows by casting, never by
  generation.** A dialect exists because a named
  character was cast with one. No system in this
  package *invents* a dialect for an uncast species,
  a wild individual, or a procedural encounter: wild
  performances use the family grammar plain (#307's
  ordinary class performs the grammar without dialect
  constants). This is the lustre law's shape (#200's
  show rule — cast, never rolled) applied to staging:
  individuality at this level is authored, and the
  authorship is the point.

## The discipline

- **A dialect never crosses.** Performing one
  character's dialect constants in another's signature
  — even of the same species and family — is the list's
  gravest defect: it is the template sin in reverse,
  and it breaks the owner's per-species canon law at
  the presentation layer. (Same-species individuals
  differ *within* the species' envelope, per the
  Individual Variation Doctrine; the dialect is where
  that doctrine becomes visible in battle.)
- **Dialects don't escalate.** A dialect is constants,
  not a budget: meeting a character for the tenth time
  performs the same dialect at the story's class
  (#307), never a bigger dialect. Recognition is the
  reward; inflation is still inflation.
- **Losing is performed in dialect too.** A signature
  performed in a losing battle, a failed capture, a
  farewell (#211's grammar) keeps its constants — the
  tell falters in tempo, perhaps, but the voice is the
  voice. A dialect that only exists in victories is a
  costume.
