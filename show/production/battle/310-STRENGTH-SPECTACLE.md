# #310 — STRENGTH & IMPACT SPECTACLE → mass: the pause before it lands, and the world keeping the dent

**The gap this fills.** The strength family's grammar:
how raw physical power is staged so the audience
believes in weight. This family's law is the shortest
in the list — **mass** — and its commonest failure is
the opposite: cartoon weightlessness, where huge
things move like balloons and hits land like pats.
Strength spectacle is built from three honesties: the
pause, the contact, and the consequence.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
documented strong-hit staging in the action record —
from the anime's impact frames to Buu's Fury's
screen-shaking blows (an essence-law kin) — repeats
one shape: *wind-up you can read, a held beat, contact
in one or two frames, and an aftermath the scene does
not tidy away.* The games encode the same respect in
their heaviest moves' long charge and recoil beats.
The kit taken: weight is time before the hit, truth
at the hit, and memory after it.

## Staging grammar — `mochiichao/spectacle-strength/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The pause (weight needs a beat).** Before a staged
  impact there is a held moment where the mass is
  *committed* — the lift that strains, the draw-back
  that loads, the ground cracking slightly under the
  planted foot. The pause is the spectacle: it is how
  a still-ish medium says *this is heavy.* Its length
  is the class dial (#307): ordinary hits get a breath;
  signature impacts get the full held beat the audience
  leans into.
- **The contact (one frame, honestly drawn).** Impact
  lands in one or two frames, with the family's
  signature effect: the **shockwave ring** — dust,
  air, and loose ground leaving the contact point in a
  visible circle — and the impact frame itself
  (contrast frame, #49's smear grammar at its hardest
  stop). The camera answers with a single shake scaled
  to the mass, then stillness — a camera that keeps
  shaking is lying about how much is left.
- **Debris honesty (things break like themselves).**
  What breaks, breaks the way that material breaks:
  stone cracks and drops, wood splinters along its
  grain, earth thuds and stays. Debris has weight too
  — it falls at the speed heavy things fall, and it
  *stays where it fell* for the rest of the scene.
  The consequence is the third act of every impact:
  the crater, the cracked wall, the tree down. A fight
  staged in a place leaves the place marked, and the
  marks are scene state (the show dresses them; the
  game carries them on the #128 plate for the battle's
  remainder).
- **Environment response (#307's tell, family form).**
  Before contact: dust sifts from ceilings and ledges
  at the pause — the world flinches early. At contact:
  the ring, and every loose thing in frame jumping
  once. After: settling dust in light, and quiet — the
  family's sound design is a *hole* after the impact
  (bed ducks hard, #215) before the world's sounds
  creep back.

## The discipline

- **Never cartoon weightlessness.** A huge body that
  bounces, a boulder that floats, a hit with no pause
  and no dent: each is this family's named defect.
  Comedy is not an exemption — a comic strength beat
  (the show has them) still pays the pause and the
  consequence; the joke is in the reaction, never in
  pretending the mass wasn't there.
- **Scale honesty between bodies.** Impact reads
  relative: the same blow staged against a wall and
  against a small creature are different sentences —
  the grammar scales the ring, the shake, and the
  debris to the *pairing*, not to the move alone.
  (Anatomy variance, #9's law, applied to spectacle.)
