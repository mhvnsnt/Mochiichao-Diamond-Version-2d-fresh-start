# #307 — SPECTACLE DOCTRINE → spectacle is a budget, spent on purpose, or it is noise

**The gap this fills.** List 30's #296 (mid-build at
this writing) states the Sonic grammar: in Sonic,
speed is staged as spectacle because speed is what
Sonic *is*. The owner's doctrine (2026-10-27) widens
it into law for this whole world: **every character
gets their spectacle** — lightning, water, strength,
rock, earth, ground, speed, flight, the ghostly, the
yokai — all of it staged big, all of it adventure.
What no package yet owns is the system underneath the
doctrine: what *earns* scale, how much scale a scene
or a fight may spend, what the audience is actually
looking at when power reads as power, and the rule
that keeps the big moments big. This spec is that
system. Lists 308–316 are its family vocabularies;
this one is the budget they all draw from.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
Sonic games and shows ration spectacle without ever
saying so — the loop-the-loop is a set-piece the
*stage* owns, the hero's ordinary running is fast but
plain, and the transformation is saved for the
climax; the audience can feel the ladder because the
lower rungs stay low. Shōnen staging does the same
with its named techniques: a signature is framed like
an event, a basic attack is not. The kit taken:
**scale is declared, rationed, and legible** — never
applied by enthusiasm.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/spectacle-doctrine/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **Scale is a declared class, not an impression.**
  Every staged performance — an animscript (#9) in the
  game, a staged beat in the show — may carry a
  spectacle declaration with one of four classes:
  - `ordinary` — the default. A move performed
    cleanly, at the size the move is.
  - `set-piece` — the *place or moment* is the star:
    a first arrival, a stage-owned moment, a weather
    event. Set-pieces belong to scenes, not to moves.
  - `signature` — reserved for performances seated in
    #131's signature register, and for a species'
    first-meeting performance (the #207 unseen→seen
    transition — a creature's first full performance
    may be staged one class up, once).
  - `climax` — the top of the ladder. **At most one
    climax-class performance per battle, per scene,
    per episode movement.** A second one is a defect,
    not a treat.
- **The declaration rides machinery that exists.** In
  the game the declaration is a block in #9's
  animscript (header-level: family + class), and its
  staging spends only #9's existing tracks — camera,
  background swap, screen effects, layered effect
  sprites, sound cues. **No new track is founded.**
  #147's coverage ledger gains a derived spectacle
  view (which performances carry which class) the same
  way it derives everything else — regenerated, never
  hand-kept. In the show, the same classes are the
  budgeting vocabulary of #82's arc planning and
  #120's cutscene staging: an episode movement that
  wants two climaxes is re-planned, not re-labelled.
- **Environment response is the universal tell.** In a
  drawn medium, power is legible because *the world
  answers it*: water displaces, dust lifts and hangs,
  light bends or fails, vegetation leans, loose things
  slide, the sound bed ducks or thins. Every family
  spec below defines its own answers, but the doctrine
  is one: **a performance with no environment response
  has not been staged, only drawn.** The response
  vocabulary is presentation data keyed to the place
  (the #128 battle plate in the game; the scene's
  dressed set in the show) — the same strike lands
  differently over water than over stone, and the
  difference is the spectacle.
- **The anti-inflation rule.** Staging an ordinary
  performance above its class — a basic move framed
  like a signature, a routine scene given a climax —
  is a **defect class**, checked where checks live:
  in the game, #147's spectacle view flags any
  performance whose declared class exceeds its seat
  (no register row, no first-meeting read, no
  set-piece scene = no class above `ordinary`); in
  the show, the flag is a planning review under #82.
  The rule exists because spectacle is a currency:
  spend it on everything and the climax buys nothing.
  The biggest moments are protected by keeping the
  small ones honest.
- **Spectacle never touches the numbers.** A spectacle
  declaration is presentation only. It cannot change
  damage, order, accuracy, or any term in #17's
  pipeline (as closed by #248): a cue that would move
  a number is a defect in the script, full stop. It
  cannot grant a move, an ability, or a power the
  record does not hold — the family grammars below are
  vocabularies for staging powers that exist, never a
  source of new ones.

## The discipline (binds every spec in this list)

- **Creatures stay cries-only.** Spectacle is staging:
  camera, environment, sound bed, light. It never puts
  words in a creature's mouth, never adds narration a
  cry would have to carry (#136's closed intention
  vocabulary and #155's vocal layering are untouched —
  a cry *inside* a spectacle is still just a cry, and
  its layering obeys #155's floor).
- **A family grammar is a vocabulary, not a template.**
  #308–#314 define how a *kind* of power is staged.
  Two users of the same family never perform it
  identically: the grammar supplies the words, the
  individual's performance (#316) is the sentence.
  A validator-grade statement of the same law already
  exists in #9: a performance is per-anatomy, with
  mandatory variance notes. Spectacle inherits it.
- **Tone fences are inherited, not re-argued.** A
  frenzy is terrifying, never cool (#289). The ghost
  family is wrongness, never jump-scares (#313).
  Battle in the depths keeps its withholding (the
  #128 depths plate ships empty; spectacle classes do
  not conjure a plate where canon keeps none).

## Show lane

The doctrine is the show's staging budget stated in
the game's vocabulary so both lanes spend the same
currency: #82 plans episode movements against the
four classes; #156's promo currency gate reads them
(a trailer may only cut climax-class shots that exist
as current registered performances — the gate it
already runs, now with a class to read).
