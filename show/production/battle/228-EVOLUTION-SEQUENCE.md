# #228 — EVOLUTION SEQUENCE PRESENTATION → the evolution moment as a designed sequence (GAME lane)

**What was studied.** Evolution sequences in the
publicly documented record: a short, unmistakable
ritual — the creature is held in light, the old
silhouette gives way to the new, the world registers
the change — always the same grammar, always
performed by the species itself. Beside it, this
world's own hatch grammar (#135), which already
solved the same staging problem for a birth. The kit
taken: reuse the house grammar; evolution is
hatching's sibling, not a new invention.

## Canon anchors (verified in the packages)

- **#135's sequence is the house pattern.** READY →
  BREAK → REVEAL → FIRST CRY → FIRST BOND, staged
  over #77's owed data slot. #228 is the evolution
  counterpart over #199's method layer and #8's edge
  model: the edge declares *whether and how* an
  evolution can fire; this spec stages *the moment
  when it does*.
- **#199 + #8 own eligibility.** The edge
  `{from, to, methodClass, trigger, conditions[]}`
  is checked before the sequence may start. A
  sequence that begins for an edge whose conditions
  fail is a data error, not a dramatic choice.
- **#178/#129 own the surrounding arithmetic.**
  Level is derived, never stored (#178); a
  level-class evolution is recognised when the
  derived level crosses the edge's threshold, and
  #129's learning flow runs *after* the sequence —
  the new form's first move-learning is part of
  becoming, sequenced behind the reveal, never
  interleaved with it.
- **The anatomy-variance law.** The sequence is
  performed by the species' own rig: #147's coverage
  ledger counts one performance per anatomy, and an
  evolution sequence exists for a line only where
  #147 shows the performance scripted. A shared
  generic sequence stretched over every anatomy is
  the failure this law exists to prevent.

## Native spec — the sequence grammar (no new format; a beat layer over #8/#199)

- **The beats** (names deliberately parallel to
  #135, so the two rituals read as one family):
  - `ONSET` — the trigger is recognised: the edge's
    conditions are re-checked at this beat and the
    sequence commits to running. Before ONSET, the
    moment can still not happen (a held item
    removed, a move away from the place) — after
    ONSET it is a sequence, and sequences finish or
    are *cancelled* (below), never just dropped.
  - `TRANSFORMATION` — the held beat, #135's
    BREAK-analogue: the old form is held in the
    transformation staging (light/form language is
    the art lane's per-line production; the grammar
    here fixes only that the old form is *held and
    honoured*, never mocked or erased mid-beat).
  - `REVEAL` — the new form, whole, held — #135's
    REVEAL-analogue. **The individual's record
    updates at this beat and not before**: species
    seat, forme, and every derived view (#178 level
    context, #207 discovery state) turn over here,
    atomically. Before REVEAL the record is the old
    species' record, entire.
  - `FIRST CRY` — the new form's first cry, rendered
    from the new species' #2 parameters (#135's
    firstCry discipline, one system later in life).
  - `FIRST STEPS` — the FIRST BOND-analogue: the
    new form's first presented behaviour, read from
    its #152 profile's display behaviours — the
    world (and the Ranger) meets who this individual
    now is. Registration consequences (a newly
    *registered* species in the Mochiioteca) resolve
    here, after the individual, per #135's order:
    the individual is the fact; the dex entry is its
    shadow.
- **Cancellation.** Where canon provides a way to
  refuse the moment (the documented pattern: a held
  item class that suppresses evolution, a Ranger's
  interruption in the game lane), cancelling before
  REVEAL **writes nothing** — no partial record, no
  "almost" state, no mark on the individual. The
  edge simply remains available for its next honest
  crossing. Whether any suppression item exists in
  this world's item layer (#31/#91) is data, not
  asserted here.
- **Once per crossing.** The sequence runs once per
  edge-crossing event. Re-entering and re-leaving a
  threshold does not re-stage a completed evolution;
  the record's form is the memory.

## Show lane

In the show, evolution is the creature's own growth
moment (the autonomous-action doctrine, #199's
mirror): the same five beats, staged by #82/#42
grammar, with no Ranger pulling anything. The
game's ONSET check is bookkeeping; the show's onset
is story — but the REVEAL discipline holds in both
lanes: a show evolution is not "true" in canon
until its reveal beat, and scripts may not treat
the new form as present before it.
