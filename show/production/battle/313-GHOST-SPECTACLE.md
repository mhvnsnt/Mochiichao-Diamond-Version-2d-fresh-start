# #313 — GHOST & PHANTOM SPECTACLE → presence before form: the paranormal staged as wrongness in ordinary things

**The gap this fills.** The ghost family's grammar:
how the phantom is staged so it *haunts* rather than
merely appears. This family's spectacle is the
quietest in the list and the hardest to fake: nothing
explodes. The world is ordinary, and wrong. The
family holds a seed performance: Wispkin's **Dark
Wave** — the shadow-pulse signature of the white
cracked-mask ghost (#131's register; its learnset row
is flagged provisional in the research mapping, which
this spec does not touch — the *performance* is
canon's, whatever the data reconciliation decides).

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
documented ghost staging across the reference works
never leads with the creature: it leads with the
place misbehaving — cold where it should be warm,
sound arriving from the wrong side, a shadow
disagreeing with its owner — and the form, when it
comes, is almost a relief. Horror grammar's cheap
version (the jump-scare) is precisely what the record's
best ghost work avoids and what this family forbids.
The kit taken: haunting is a property of the *scene*,
staged in the scene's ordinary materials.

## Staging grammar — `mochiichao/spectacle-ghost/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **Presence before form.** A ghost performance opens
  with evidence, not entrance: the sound bed grows a
  **cold spot** — a localised wrongness in #215's bed
  (a region of the mix where warmth/reverb drops out,
  breath-fog in summer air, the small sounds of a place
  stopping one by one). Light behaves wrong next:
  flames lean *away* from nothing, shadows hold a
  fraction too long, reflections lag. Only then —
  third beat — form. The order is the grammar: a ghost
  that simply fades in has skipped its own spectacle.
- **Wrongness in ordinary things.** The family's
  effects are corruptions of the scene's existing
  materials, never imported spectacle objects: doors
  in the set breathe, portraits' eyes are wrong in
  one frame and right after, the performer's own
  shadow performs a slightly different action. The
  Dark Wave's staging is the family's release form:
  darkness that moves like a slow fluid with intent —
  it *pools*, reaches, and withdraws, rather than
  blasting (a darkness that explodes is lightning in
  a costume, #308's defect in reverse).
- **The voice rule at its strictest.** Of all families
  this one most tempts words — whispering, moaning
  with meaning. The law does not bend: creatures are
  cries only (#136), and a ghost's cry staged under
  #155's layering keeps the identity floor — a Wispkin
  cry processed until it resembles speech is a defect
  in two packages at once. The paranormal stays
  *paranormal* precisely because it never explains
  itself in words.
- **Temperature of the register.** The family's
  spectacle register is **dread at low volume**: the
  climax class (#307) in this family is not bigger
  but *closer* — the wrongness reaches the camera's
  own materials (the frame's edge darkens wrong, the
  bed's cold spot swallows the mix's centre). Scale
  here is intimacy, and the doctrine's budget reads
  it as climax all the same.

## The discipline

- **Never jump-scare grammar.** No sudden full-frame
  face, no scream sting, no cut-to-horror. A startle
  is a cheap spend of the audience's trust and is this
  family's named defect — the same tone fence List 30
  set for frenzy (#289: terrifying, never cool) in its
  mirror form: here, *dreadful, never startling.*
- **Wrongness must be deniable in any single frame.**
  Each individual staging beat, screenshotted alone,
  could pass as ordinary; the haunting is the
  accumulation. A beat that is only readable as
  supernatural in isolation has over-drawn.
- **The ghost is a character, not a weather.** However
  atmospheric the staging, the performance ends with a
  *someone* — agency, intention, a creature the Ranger
  can meet (#136's staged fluency governs what can be
  understood of them; spectacle never upgrades it).
