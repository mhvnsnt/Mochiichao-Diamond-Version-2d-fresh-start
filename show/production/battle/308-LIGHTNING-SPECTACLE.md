# #308 — LIGHTNING SPECTACLE → the strike is instant; the spectacle is the before and the after

**The gap this fills.** #307 gives every power family
a budget; this spec gives the storm family its
grammar — how lightning is staged so an audience
*feels* voltage. The family's identity is one fact:
**lightning does not travel, it arrives.** Speed (the
Sonic family, #312's fast end and #296's doctrine) is
drawn motion; lightning is a cut. A package that let
a bolt drift across the screen like a projectile
would have built the wrong family.

**How the reference record fills the same gap:** the
documented staging of electric attacks in the anime
record is tell → white frame → aftermath: the air
charges, the frame blows out, and the sound arrives
*after* the light — the flash–thunder delay is how
the medium tells you how far away the power is. The
games keep the mechanical cousin of the same idea:
under storm weather, the great electric and wind
strikes simply do not miss (#252's storm-true seat —
Thunder's class). The kit taken: anticipation and
aftermath carry the spectacle; the strike itself is
one frame and a sound on its way.

## Staging grammar — `mochiichao/spectacle-lightning/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **The tell (charge).** Before any strike: the world
  goes *static*. Fur and hair lift, dust hovers
  instead of settling, loose metal ticks, the sound
  bed thins to a hiss and a rising whine. The tell is
  scaled by class (#307): an ordinary strike gets a
  beat of it; a signature gets the full held charge —
  the audience should fear the strike before it
  exists. A charge the performer *holds* (a stored,
  aimed strike) is staged differently from a wild
  discharge: held = the tell concentrates on the
  performer; wild = the tell spreads across the whole
  plate.
- **The strike (punctuation).** One frame of presence,
  then absence: flash frame, afterimage burned on the
  plate, the target's reaction already happening. The
  camera does not chase — it holds still and lets the
  light do the violence. Branching is drawn only in
  the afterimage, never as a travelling bolt.
- **The thunder (the sound bed's drum).** The strike's
  sound arrives late on purpose: flash and thunder
  separated by a **distance delay** that is a staging
  dial — near-zero for a strike in the room with you,
  long and rolling for a strike across a valley. The
  bed family is #51's strike/crack library over #215's
  regional bed, ducked to near-silence in the tell so
  the crack lands on quiet. Thunder is the only
  family whose sound cue is allowed to *overrun* the
  performance's end — the rumble is the signature's
  tail.
- **Environment response (#307's tell, family form).**
  Water surfaces stipple and jump; metal objects
  spark at their edges; dust hangs lit for a frame;
  in rain (the family's home weather, #252 `RAIN` /
  `STORM`) the whole plate strobes once with the
  strike. Under `STORM`, staged lightning and the
  weather's own lightning are the same grammar — the
  audience should not be able to tell where the sky
  ends and the performer begins; that confusion *is*
  the family's climax register.

## Game mirror

On #9's tracks: screen-effects track carries the
flash frame and afterimage; sound-cue track carries
tell-hiss, crack, and the delayed drum (delay is cue
data, in frames, at #9's frame address); camera
track is *forbidden* a chase move during the strike
beat (the validator-level note rides the animscript's
`variance_notes` until a checker exists — stated
honestly as a note, not a claimed enforcement).
Mechanically the family adds nothing: `STORM`'s
storm-true accuracy is move data under #252, and a
spectacle declaration never touches it (#307).

## The discipline

- **Instant means instant.** A performance that draws
  the bolt travelling slowly, curving toward the
  target like a guided missile, has left the family —
  that is a different power's grammar. (A *held*
  charge travelling with the performer is the tell,
  not the strike; the distinction is written on the
  script.)
- **Flash discipline is accessibility law.** Flash
  frames obey #121's accessibility rules (intensity
  and repetition caps) in both lanes; a spectacle
  class never buys exemption from them. The family's
  power must survive the caps — it does, because the
  spectacle lives in the tell and the thunder, not in
  strobing.
- **No words, no narration of voltage.** The hiss, the
  lift, the crack say it (#307's cries-only rule
  binds; a performer's cry may ride the tell, layered
  under #155, and it is still only a cry).
