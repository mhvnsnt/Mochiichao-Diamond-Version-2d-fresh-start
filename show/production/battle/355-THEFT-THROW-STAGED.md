# #355 — THE THEFT THROW, STAGED → the same skeleton, played as transgression or as relief

**The principle.** A steal attempt and a rescue
snag are the *same physical act* — same device,
same throw, same capture skeleton (#268) — and the
two must never *feel* the same. #349 split them in
the record's vocabulary; this spec splits them on
the screen and in the mix. The staging is not
decoration laid over the mechanic: in a programme
where the audience's witness is a system (#272's
fourth kind, #276), how a throw *reads* is part of
what the throw is.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/theft-staging/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **One skeleton, two registers.** The throw's
  animscript skeleton (#9: the throw, the arc, the
  checks' shake grammar from #38) is shared whole —
  a production economy and a meaning at once: the
  audience learns one motion, and the register
  tells them what it means *this* time. The
  registers are declared presentation data on the
  attempt (family + register per #307's doctrine
  header grammar), never a second animation set:
  - **The theft register — dark, quick, wrong.**
    The throw is staged slightly too fast, cut
    slightly too tight; the room answers it —
    #242's crowd register drops (a held beat of
    quiet where noise was), and the sound bed
    (#215's family) thins and drops a register
    under the throw, so the ball crosses a sudden
    silence. The spectacle grammar is #307's
    doctrine run in negative: ordinary spectacle
    spends scale; theft staging spends *warmth* —
    light and sound withdraw, and the transgression
    is felt as the world going briefly cold around
    one quick motion. The victim's reaction beat
    (#352's `to-attempt`) rides the same segment:
    the staging makes room for the voice by taking
    everything else away.
  - **The rescue register — relief.** The same
    throw staged at honest speed, the bed *holding*
    under it and warming at the catch's resolution;
    where the device read the heart (#349), the
    staging gives the read its own small beat before
    the throw (the pause #349 founded as the hero's
    tell — the device speaks, the Ranger breathes,
    *then* the throw). A successful rescue's
    resolution beat is the warmest frame the battle
    system owns: it is staged as the first moment
    of #288's long road, not as a capture's triumph.
- **What staging may never do.** The registers are
  presentation only, under the programme's standing
  fence: staging never changes a number, a check,
  or an outcome (#307's discipline; #180's cue law
  is about *declared effects*, and a register is
  explicitly not one). A steal staged darkly does
  not roll worse; a rescue staged warmly does not
  roll better. The moment a register bent math it
  would become a tell players optimise against,
  and the conscience layer's whole point is that
  the choice is moral, not tactical. Stated here
  because this is the spec where the temptation
  lives.
- **Guarding and refusal, staged.** The two
  counterplay beats inherit the theft register's
  room: #354's screen (the knock-aside) plays
  inside the same dropped quiet — one clean crack
  of contact in a silenced bed is the loudest sound
  in the package, and it is spent exactly once, on
  the block. A refusal (#351) stages as *nothing
  happening, pointedly*: the device speaks its
  line into ordinary room noise that never dropped,
  because no throw ever entered the air. The
  contrast is the lesson the staging teaches across
  a playthrough: silence means a crime was
  attempted; ordinary noise means the world said no
  before it could be.
- **The crowd is a witness, staged as one.** #242's
  chorus law holds entire (applause is never a
  mechanic; the crowd judges nothing) — but the
  crowd *reacts*, and in a theft attempt its
  reaction is cast from the same facts #272 counts:
  a crowded room that goes quiet is the witness set
  becoming visible to the player before any report
  exists. Staging and system show the same crowd
  from two sides; neither may contradict the other
  (a room staged empty of reaction while #272 counts
  twelve witnesses is a landing defect of the
  legibility kind, registered in STAGING step 7).

## Show lane

The show's version of this split is the package's
oldest promise restated: villain grammar versus
rescue grammar around one device (#276). Episode
staging takes the two registers as its own —
dark/quick/wrong against the held, warm read — and
adds the one thing the game cannot do: the
audience sees the *stripped* device's missing beat
(#349's tell) and learns to dread its silence.
Game and show registers are one vocabulary in two
media, keyed by the same attempt record (family +
register are on the animscript header in both
lanes, #307's grammar).
