# EPISODE PLANNING METHOD — researched, then made ours

How the shows we pull from actually plan and storyboard episodes
(researched 2026-10-27 from staff interviews, production books and
industry manuals), and the hybrid method Mochiichao adopts. Full
research notes: research folder `episode-planning-storyboard-methods`
(summary in show/production/research/INDEX.md chain).

## What each of them does (the evidence)

**Pokémon (OLM) — script-driven with a series composer.**
The series composition role (Shudō, then Tomioka, now Satō) owns
the season's shape. Shudō's own account: his construction plan was
a **one-line memo per episode**, taken to a script meeting with
director + producers + broadcasters. ~4 script meetings a month;
the featured Pokémon is fixed at the meeting so episodes don't
duplicate; writers build plots → drafts (2nd, 3rd) → final draft.
Then e-konte (board — a separate rotating credit), layout, genga
corrected by animation directors, dōga — with **BANK stock footage
reused by design**.

**SpongeBob — storyboard-driven, totally.**
Hillenburg's decision, inherited from Rocko: story editor + writers
meet daily; **one 2-page outline per week**; a 2-person board team
thumbnails the whole 11-minute episode in a week — the thumbnail IS
the script, dialogue and gags invented in boarding ("We don't write
scripts" — head writer Steven Banks). Board **pitch** to
Hillenburg/Drymon (Fri) → revisions → **pitch to Nickelodeon** (the
network gate is a performed board pitch) → 3 weeks clean-up → voice
record → animatic + timing director (1 week) — with real rewriting
happening in the animatic ("Clams" was almost completely reboarded
there). ~5 months per segment.

**Cartoon Network — outline → board pairs (except Gumball).**
Adventure Time: room story games / boarder proposals → 2–3 page
outline of important beats → board PAIRS (credit "Written &
Storyboard by"), 1 week thumbnails, showrunner notes, 1 week
clean-up → voices → animatic trimmed to length → overseas
animation. 8–9 months outline-to-air. Regular Show and Steven
Universe corroborate (SU's only "script" is transcribed FROM
finished boards, keyed to panel numbers). **Gumball is the
documented exception**: scripted by a writers' room, then worked
heavily at board stage — because its mixed 2D/3D/photo elements
needed a very detailed layout stage to register everything
(Bocquelet, Kidscreen 2016).

**Digimon Adventure — composition as fairness + growth law.**
From the official encyclopedia interviews (producer Seki, director
Kakudō, composer Nishizono): no mandated story beyond monsters +
kids empathising → partners. Kakudō's composition rules: **equal
shine and equal featured sequences for every child/partner pair,
distributed evenly** (visible in eps 1–13's rotation), and Seki:
**human growth tied to how the Digimon evolve**. Storyboard and
episode direction usually the same person; the series director
personally boarded the structural episodes.

**Japanese TV anime (industry default, AJA manual).**
Series composer splits the story into episodes → scenario
(~21–22 min) approved in script meetings → **e-konte**: templated
boards with cut numbers, sketches, staff notes, dialogue, and
**length in seconds per cut** (hundreds of pages; BANK marks) →
episode director checks a breakdown sheet → layout per cut →
genga + timesheet → dōga → backgrounds/composite → picture edit →
**afureko (voices recorded AFTER picture)** → dubbing. Reference
timings from board delivery: ~13 weeks + buffer; 250–350 cuts per
30-minute episode.

## The common pattern (synthesis)
1. A **short beat-level plan** owned by ONE role (series composer /
   story editor / showrunner) comes before everything.
2. The **locked visual document** (e-konte, or board + animatic) is
   where timing is truly fixed — and it is the real approval gate,
   defended live in a meeting or pitch.
3. Rotating small units work in parallel on a **formula that is
   really a scheduling device**.
4. Where voices sit relative to the animatic is the diagnostic:
   US cartoons record before animation; anime records after
   (afureko). Both work — you must pick one and build around it.

## Mochiichao's method (the hybrid we run)
- **Series composition = the show bible + Jamar.** Season shape
  and the species rotation are owned here (Digimon law: equal
  shine, featured sequences distributed evenly; Pokémon law: the
  featured species is fixed at planning, never duplicated by
  accident).
- **Step 1 — one-line memo** (Shudō): every episode starts as ONE
  line in the season grid. No memo, no episode.
- **Step 2 — beat sheet** on our engine (Pokémon showcase +
  6-beat garden formula), Jamar approves direction HERE, before
  any script (his standing rule).
- **Step 3 — shooting script** in Jamar's house format
  (WRITING-STANDARD.md): rules first, canon sources, VISUAL/
  ACTION/DIALOGUE/SUBTITLES/SFX/MUSIC blocks, open items listed.
- **Step 4 — boards in e-konte discipline**: cuts numbered,
  dialogue and SECONDS per cut, BANK marks for stock (walk cycles,
  gate, pond) — boarded heavily from the locked script, Gumball-
  style, because our mixed sources (game art + puppets + generated
  clips) need the registration a detailed layout gives.
- **Step 5 — board pitch = the gate.** The episode is pitched as
  boards (performed, SpongeBob-style) to Jamar before production.
  No pitch pass, no animation.
- **Step 6 — voices + animatic.** US-side recording (audio first),
  per Jamar's own dialogue-first pipeline: scratch voices → final
  → animatic timed to audio; the animatic is where final timing
  locks and last rewriting is allowed (the "Clams" rule).
- **Step 7 — production** per PIPELINE.md (puppets on 2s/3s,
  action beats get the motion budget), anim gate, clip index.
