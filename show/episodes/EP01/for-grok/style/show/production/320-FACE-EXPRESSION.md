# #320 — FACES & EXPRESSION ACROSS ANATOMIES → how a face you cannot fully see still acts

**The gap this fills.** Canon's faces now come in
four constructions — open faces in the family
grammar, Bastihound's muzzle-plate, Verdilance's
mouth-shield under an open crown, Wispkin's two dark
holes in a cracked skull — and every one of them has
to *act*: joy, threat, grief, mischief, in a show
whose creatures never speak. Without staging rules,
each board artist invents a solution per episode and
the covered faces quietly lose their inner life.
This spec writes the solutions down, per anatomy.

**The standing law over all of it:** creatures are
**cries and sounds only** — no word dialogue, no
name-saying (voice law). Expression is **drawn**,
never spoken; a covered face is never given a mouth
"for one scene" — that is an anatomy error of the
#317 class.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/face-expression/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

Expression is staged from a closed kit of carriers.
Every anatomy uses the carriers it physically has;
no anatomy borrows a carrier it lacks.

- **Open faces** (the family grammar): the species
  art's own construction — large tall ovals, one
  hard upper highlight, angled lids carrying
  temperament (the style spec's SS1.4 grammar).
  Mouth and lids both act. This is the baseline the
  covered faces are measured against, not the norm
  they apologise to.
- **Muzzle-plate faces** (Bastihound class, #317
  `muzzle-plate`): the mouth does not exist on
  screen. Expression rides on (1) the **eyes** —
  Bastihound's narrow wedge glare is an owner-ruled
  exception to the family eye grammar *for
  Bastihound only*, and its range is
  slant-and-highlight work, (2) the **brow ridge**
  above the plate, (3) **ears and head angle** — a
  knight's attention is shown by where the helm
  turns, and (4) the **body**: the tower-shield
  foreleg plants or lifts, and that is his loudest
  sentence. Rule: never draw a mouth line on the
  plate, never flex the plate; the plate is bone-
  still and everything around it moves.
- **Mouth-shield faces** (Verdilance class, #317
  `mouth-shield`): eyes, lids and brow act at full
  family-grammar range (the upper face is open);
  below them, **head tilt and the crown** carry what
  a mouth would — the wide rose crown bows, lifts,
  and turns with the head, so a Verdilance's
  feelings are read in the angle of the bloom
  against the light. The shield itself is still.
  Rule: the menpo never dents, grins, or opens.
- **Full-mask faces** (Wispkin — the hardest case,
  solved here as staging rules): the presented face
  is a white skull with **two dark holes and a
  crack**, and it emotes by:
  1. **Hole read** — the holes are drawn as fixed
     shapes; their *apparent* shape changes only
     through head angle and light (a tilt makes the
     holes narrow to slits against the light; a
     downward bow makes them loom). Artists change
     the angle, never the holes' drawn shape.
  2. **The crack as a light-line** — the crack
     catches light at certain angles; staging may
     use one catch per emotional beat (a glint on
     the crack is Wispkin's raised eyebrow). The
     crack never moves, widens, or heals (#317's
     protected flaw).
  3. **Smoke and hands** — the trailing smoke's
     density and curl, and the small black hands,
     carry tempo: agitation is in the smoke before
     it is anywhere else.
  4. **The whole body as the mouth** — float height,
     lean, and stillness. A Wispkin who goes
     perfectly still mid-air is shouting.
  Rule: no face may be drawn *under* the mask in any
  production material except the ruled pre-mask
  flashback form (#317) — the mask is the face the
  show presents.
- **The universal carriers** (every anatomy): posture,
  ear/crown/appendage angle, eye highlight placement,
  cry (sound carries what the face cannot — #155's
  layering and #229's dialects are the audio half of
  this spec), and **timing** — a beat held on a
  covered face reads stronger than the same beat on
  an open one, so covered faces get the held beats in
  staging, not fewer of them.

## Game lane

Battle and field presentation reuse the same
carriers at sprite scale: the read that matters
(status, bond cues under #180's declaration law —
a cue must be *visible*) is staged on the carriers
the individual's anatomy actually has. A bond cue
may never be drawn as a mouth expression on a
face-cover individual.

## Wiring

Staging rules seated beside #53's board pipeline and
the species style spec; keys off #317's forms; reads
#318 (a registered individual's carriers are theirs —
Doro's open face is staged as an open face, fully);
audio half joins #155/#229. Writes: nothing.

## Open slots

None that are mechanics. Per-species staging notes
(which carriers a given line leans on hardest) are
sheet/board-pass contents, written per line as it
enters production.

## Coverage — honest

- The four anatomy classes cover every face
  construction in canon at this build; a future
  anatomy with no carrier overlap is a design
  conversation at its sheet, not a fifth class
  founded here.
- Wispkin's rules are written from its canon read
  (mask, holes, crack, smoke, hands) as recorded in
  owner rulings; its species sheet was not re-read
  in this pass — registered in STAGING.
