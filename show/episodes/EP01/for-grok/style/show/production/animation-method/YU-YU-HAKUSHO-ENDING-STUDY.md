# YU YU HAKUSHO ENDING STUDY — what the ED reference actually is

**Commissioned by the owner (2026-10-28)** to answer, honestly:
what frame rate are the Yu Yu Hakusho ending credits at, and why
do they still read as industry-standard, production-grade work
when our ED-gag work — at similar drawing rates — read as
amateur? Researched from public sources only; no footage, art,
characters, or names were downloaded or used (construction-kit
rule: the method transfers, nothing else).

Grades: **DOCUMENTED** (a source states it) · **CONVENTION**
(documented general practice of the era, not YYH-specific) ·
**ANALYSIS** (labelled reading) · **GAP** (searched, not found —
never filled silently).

---

## 1. The headline correction

**No Yu Yu Hakusho TV ending is documented, anywhere found, as
containing chibi / SD characters walking in a line.** The
sequence as remembered does not appear to exist in the TV
endings as a single thing. What exists is two separate real
things, which the gag's format — correctly, as it turns out —
had already fused:

- **The line-walk shape is ED3.** "Anbaransu na Kiss o Shite"
  (eps 60–83) contains a genuine group walk — the main team
  walking through flames — **full-size and dramatic**, not chibi.
  (Best evidence is a fan description; no production-side source
  for ED3's visuals was found. Cited as DOCS, low grade — not
  upgraded.)
- **The chibi-comedy tone lives outside the endings** — in the
  *Eizou Hakusho* OVA releases and their *Nightmare Hakusho*
  comedy shorts (1993–96), and in the SD figure/merchandising
  tradition.

So the honest citation for the Mochiichao ED gag is: **walk shape
from the YYH ending tradition (ED3), chibi-comedy tone from the
YYH OVA-shorts tradition — and the fused "chibi party line-walk
ending" is an original Mochiichao construction.** It must never
be cited as a copy of one YYH sequence.

**Correction applied by this study:** the EP01 gag's v2 build
notes (§1) state that the YYH endings carry a chibi line-walk.
That sentence is unsupported. It is corrected by a dated note in
those build notes (original kept) and by this study wherever the
claim would otherwise be repeated.

## 2. The five endings, for the record (DOCUMENTED)

| ED | Song | Episodes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Homework ga Owaranai" | 1–29 |
| 2 | "Sayonara Bye Bye" | 30–59 |
| 3 | "Anbaransu na Kiss o Shite" | 60–83 |
| 4 | "Taiyō ga Mata Kagayaku Toki" | 84–102 |
| 5 | "Daydream Generation" | 103–111 |

(Episode 112 closes on the opening song. There are exactly five
endings and 112 episodes — a circulating guide inventing a sixth
ending is wrong and is not cited anywhere in our records.)

What the others do, at the grades available: ED1 is a
song-synced montage; **ED2 is a slow pan over a held tableau**
(one composition, a camera move, essentially no character
animation); ED4 is a quiet letter-writing tableau; ED5's visuals
are **undescribed** in everything searched (GAP) — its one hard
production fact is that Pierrot produced it with early digital
colouring in 1994, while ED1–ED4 were hand-painted cel.

Sources: YuYu Hakusho Wiki, *Ending Songs*
(yuyuhakusho.fandom.com/wiki/Ending_Songs); Wikipedia, *YuYu
Hakusho (1992 TV series)*; NamuWiki, *Cel animation* (ED5 digital
colouring).

## 3. Frame rate and timing — what is actually known

**YYH-ending-specific timing documentation: GAP.** No timing
sheet, exposure sheet, drawing count, or animator interview for
any YYH ending was found — including in the long Sakuga Blog
production history of the series, which treats episode animation
in depth and never the endings' timing. Anyone stating "the YYH
ED walk is on 3s with N drawings" as fact is inventing it.

**The documented convention those endings were produced inside:**

- Motion-picture film and anime timing base: **24 fps**
  (Anime News Network Lexicon, *Shot on threes* —
  animenewsnetwork.com/encyclopedia/lexicon.php?id=61).
- TV animation is commonly **"shot on threes"** — each cel held
  three frames, **8 drawings per second in effect** — with fours
  and more for quiet material and **twos or ones for action**;
  "more an average than a constant rule" (same entry).
- Frame-rate **modulation** is the named technique of the era:
  movement shifting between roughly **6, 8, 12, and rarely 24
  drawings per second** as the scene warrants (AniGamers, *The 12
  Principles of Anime*).
- Camera work carries held art: pans are stepped frame-by-frame
  over held cels and big backgrounds; layering lets one layer
  move while others hold (traditional cel production references;
  Sakuga Wiki, *E-konte*, for timing practice in seconds+frames
  at 24 fps).
- Walk construction, general craft: **4 key poses per step —
  contact, recoil/down, passing, high-point/up — mirrored for the
  other leg: an 8-pose cycle**; cycle length (16/24/32 frames) is
  a character dial (brisk/relaxed), not a quality dial
  (walk-cycle craft references incl. Careers360, Adobe,
  animationexplainers).

**Stated honestly for our records:** the YYH endings ran at a
24 fps master, most plausibly largely on 3s with 2s/1s accents
and long holds/pans — CONVENTION, near-certain for a 1992–94
Pierrot TV ending, unproven for these specific sequences.

## 4. Why they read as production-grade anyway

1. **Finished paintings, not placeholders** (ANALYSIS, grounded
   in the cel process): at 8 drawings/sec, each drawing was fully
   traced, painted, and photographed. ED2 is the extreme proof —
   one painting and a pan, remembered for thirty years.
2. **Contrast and colour discipline** (DOCUMENTED for the
   series): Sakuga Blog's production history documents the
   series' aggressive contrast direction and bold colour as its
   remembered signature; endings inherit that art direction even
   at low drawing counts.
3. **Timing contrast, designed** (CONVENTION → ANALYSIS): holds
   and 3s for calm, bursts at accents, movement speeding and
   slowing with the song. Uniform low rate reads cheap; *chosen*
   low rate reads intentional.
4. **The camera and background buy the motion** (DOCUMENTED
   technique): a pan over a large finished background, or a
   scroll under a looping walk, is real screen motion bought in
   the cheapest layer.
5. **The song is the engine** (ANALYSIS): cuts, pans, and walk
   loops land on musical phrases across a ~90-second song-length
   piece; the music supplies momentum the drawings don't have to.
6. **Held poses are the product** (CONVENTION/ANALYSIS): a
   drawing held 3+ frames must read instantly — silhouette and
   attitude carry it. The endings' held images are still
   circulating as GIFs and stills today, which is what a survived
   hold looks like.

**The one-sentence version:** the reference endings were never
"low-fps but somehow good." They are finished art, strong held
poses, deliberate timing contrast, and camera/background motion,
cut to a song. The low drawing count was the budget; everything
else was where the budget got spent.

## 5. What this sets for our ED (adopted into the gates)

Per instance (24 fps master, ~14 s gag format): default exposure
**on 3s** (8 unique drawings/sec) written on a chart before
drawing; **≥8 distinct drawings per stride** per walking
character (mirrored flips don't count for the lead); **timing
contrast is mandatory** — gag accents on 2s with at most 2–3
drawings on 1s at the exact accent; any hold ≥12 frames must be a
composed, silhouette-checked key pose with camera or secondary
motion over it; contacts planted, travel derived from stride;
one written personality dial per character (cycle length + one
timing/pose quirk); background a finished, registry-current
living background; one colour picture across cast and plate.
These sit alongside the standing drawn gates
(`WALK-DIAGNOSIS-LOG.md` §3) and the ED standard
(`METHOD-REGISTRY.md`). The remaining craft gaps this study
exposed in *our* pipeline — beat timing against the ED song
included — are logged as gaps 12–21 in
`WHAT-WE-DO-WRONG-ADDENDUM-2.md`.

## 6. What would raise this study's grade

Watching the clean endings on the official anniversary Blu-ray /
DVD extras (confirmed to exist) and logging them cut by cut —
a viewing task for a human with legal access, not a web task.
Until then, §§1–3 stand at the grades marked and must not be
quoted at a higher grade.

## 7. Owner-memory note (2026-10-28, appended after landing)

Recorded from the owner, who commissioned this study, on seeing
its findings — labelled as his memory, not as a citation:

- He remembers the team-walk **most strongly from a Yu Yu
  Hakusho game** (he also remembers it from the show, and
  believes the walk appeared in the game). Which game is **not
  identified and not researched** — this is a lead for a future
  check, never a source.
- The walk's lead figure, Yusuke Urameshi, is one of his
  favourite characters in the franchise — part of why a gag
  shaped around the lead walking with his group is the shape he
  reached for.
- His name for the ending walk's tone: **"reverse deadpan"** —
  a completely serious scene (flames, stakes, nobody joking)
  whose cute register makes it play as visual comedy; the comedy
  comes out backwards, from the seriousness played straight.
  That serious-vs-cute contrast is recorded as an ingredient of
  the Mochiichao gag's tone.
- He also recognised the chibi-comedy segment as the anime
  eyecatch / omake trope — short comedy cards and bonus segments
  outside the endings proper — matching §1's placement of the
  chibi material outside the EDs.
- His episode count for the ED3 walk was ~23 episodes; the
  documented range is eps 60–83 = 24 of 112.
