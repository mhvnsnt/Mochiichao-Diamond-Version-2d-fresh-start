## 1. THE SIZE LAW (production law, owner's words in substance)

**Characters are FULL-SIZE. Chibi is a device, not the picture.**

- Full-size characters carry the story: **~85–90% of character
  screen presence in every episode is full-size**, measured in
  character-appearance seconds inside story content.
- Chibi versions exist for the show's **in-between furniture and
  flair**: eyecatch cards, preview cards, transition/B-roll
  cards, credits bits, and the rare dramatic-comedic snap (§3).
- A chibi form is a **proportion variant of the canon full-size
  likeness** (§4) — never a separate character, never a separate
  likeness, never the default way a character looks.
- The band is a ceiling on chibi, not a quota to fill. An episode
  with **zero** chibi in its story is always legal; EP01 is
  nearly that (§6). The furniture slots (eyecatch, preview,
  credits) are where chibi lives, and they are seconds, not
  scenes.

## 2. THREE DEVICES, THREE JOBS — THE BOUNDARY

The show has three card-like devices. They are not
interchangeable, and no moment may be served by the wrong one.

| Device | What it is | What it carries | Governed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Silent-film intertitle** | A text moment *inside* the story | A creature's **meaning** — the one thing the audience must understand that cries cannot say | Subtitle law (Bible §5.2): rationed hard; EP01 has exactly five, all locked |
| **Eyecatch** | Slot furniture at the A-part/B-part join (Bible §6 slot shape) | The keeper-device check-in — the episode's door, not a scene | Bible §6; EP01's is scripted (V3-061) |
| **Chibi card** | Furniture art in chibi proportions | **Identity and flair** — the cast being charming, funny, or dramatic *as furniture*: a pose, a gag beat, a pointing-at-next-week | THIS document |

Boundary rules:

- **A chibi card never carries meaning.** If a moment needs the
  audience to *understand* a creature, that is an intertitle's
  job (and intertitles are rationed separately). Chibi cards
  carry no creature dialogue, no translated cries, no plot.
- **A chibi card never explains time.** No "later that night"
  text cards (the SpongeBob time-card mechanism is a different
  show's voice; Mochiichao marks time with light states and the
  Living World, per the craft doc). Card text, where any exists,
  is furniture text only: episode furniture words ("NEXT TIME",
  the episode title), never narration.
- **An intertitle moment is never chibi-fied.** The locked
  intertitle cuts play exactly as scripted, full-frame, in the
  episode's own grammar. Chibi never appears inside, before, or
  after them as decoration (§3, protected adjacencies).
- **The eyecatch is one slot, whoever furnishes it.** Whether
  the join is furnished by the graphic check-in (EP01 as
  scripted) or by an approved chibi variant of that same card,
  it is the same 8-second door — a variant replaces the
  furnishing, it never adds a second slot.

## 3. THE TAXONOMY — SLOTS, CAPS, AND PROTECTED MOMENTS

Frequencies are per episode (24-minute slot format), and are
**maxima**, not targets.

### C1 — Eyecatch card (the A/B join)
- **Slot:** the single eyecatch join in the Bible §6 slot shape
  (OP/story join furniture rides the OP's own tail and the title
  card, which stays the fixed identity-kit layout — full-size or
  graphic art only, never chibi).
- **Cap: 1 slot per episode** (an in-card and an out-card are
  the two halves of the one slot).
- **Form:** chibi cast posed around the keeper-device check-in
  graphic, Persona-pop treatment over the identity palette —
  the device stays the subject; the chibi cast are its
  attendants.
- **May never:** carry story, repeat a episode-unique story
  sound (EP01's Aeon ping is fenced to V3-043 and does NOT
  repeat in the eyecatch — scripted, and binding on any chibi
  variant), or run longer than the slot's scripted duration.

### C2 — Preview card ("next time")
- **Slot:** inside the next-episode preview / broadcaster
  buffer block (45s in the pilot format), never inside the ED.
- **Cap: 1 per episode.**
- **Form:** chibi cast pointing at, reacting to, or being
  dragged toward next episode's premise — a promise played as
  furniture. The preview's story clips themselves stay
  full-size; the chibi card is the frame, not the footage.
- **May never:** spoil a locked reveal, show a character in
  chibi who has not yet appeared full-size in the series
  (chibi is a *returning* joke — the audience must already know
  the true proportions), or use a form not yet built under §5.

### C3 — Transition / B-roll card
- **Slot:** act or cluster boundaries only — where the episode
  already breathes (a light-state change, a location change).
  Never inside a scene, never between locked spine cuts.
- **Cap: 2 per episode. EP01's cap is 0 (§6).**
- **Form:** a single chibi beat over an identity-palette field:
  the crew crossing the frame with the location's prop, the
  weather changing on a chibi keeper — B-roll energy at card
  scale.
- **May never:** interrupt a held silence, a colour-key set
  piece, or any beat the craft grammar marks as a hold; never
  substitute for an establishing shot the story needs full-size.

### C4 — Credits bit
- **Slot:** inside the ED (75s in the pilot format) only.
- **Cap: 1 gag beat per episode**, inside the ED's own runtime —
  it adds no seconds.
- **Form:** one small chibi gag in the credits field (the crew
  at miniature work: sweeping the credits away, stacking the
  name blocks like the berry pyramid — mechanisms, played in
  the cast's own gags, never a donor's).
- **May never:** cover or alter a credit, change the ED's
  music, or appear in the OP.

### C5 — The dramatic-comedic snap
- **What it is:** the story itself snaps to chibi for one beat
  — a full-size scene's emotion spikes (terror, pride, despair,
  triumph) and the character *pops* chibi for the spike, then
  snaps back. This is the only chibi that occurs inside story
  content, and it is the rarest device in this system.
- **Cap: 1 per episode, ≤2 seconds (≤48 frames at 24fps), one
  character per snap.** EP01's cap is 0 (§6).
- **Form:** same shot, same background, same light state,
  same sound bed — only the character's proportions pop. The
  snap is a performance accent, not a cutaway: the frame does
  not change, the world does not acknowledge it, and the
  character's cry does not change (§4).
- **May never:** occur in a battle's decisive beat (the
  autonomous-action doctrine plays fights straight), in a
  first-appearance scene, or as a reaction to another
  character's pain played for real.

### Protected moments — chibi may NEVER interrupt, adjoin, or
### decorate any of these
1. **The stinger** — the whole block, including its engineered
   absence (EP01: 46s at −51.9 LUFS by design). No chibi in,
   before as a lead-in gag, or after as relief.
2. **Any locked intertitle cut**, nor the cut immediately
   before or after it.
3. **A held silence** (EP01: N9's 10-second floor — Jack holds
   are floors, not ceilings, and nothing furnishes them).
4. **A scripted colour set piece** (EP01: the Frost Close-Up
   Colour Shift, V3-039 — shift and restore beats play clean).
5. **A fenced unique sound** (EP01: the Aeon device ping,
   V3-043 — once only; no card may echo it).
6. **A cameo frame** (EP01: Hex at the far fence, V3-002 and
   its rhyme V3-098 — cameos are presence rationed by the
   craft doc; chibi cameo forms exist for *cards* only, §5,
   and never enter story frames).

## 4. THE CHIBI MODEL SPEC

Chibi models are **derived from the canon full-size
likenesses** — the same repo art files that feed every other
build (Likeness Law, Bible §5.7). A chibi model is built from
the character's model sheet, passes the same likeness gate
against it, and is registered as a proportion variant of that
character. Nobody designs a chibi from a text description, and
nobody designs a *different, cuter character* and calls it
chibi.

### Proportions (derived, then locked per character)
- **Creatures: ~2 heads tall** — head ≈ 45–50% of total
  height; body compressed to a single mass; limbs shortened
  to stubs **except** where a limb IS the character (below).
- **Humans: ~2.5 heads tall** — Mochiko keeps her twin tails
  at full relative scale; they are silhouette, not hair.
- **What exaggerates:** head size; eye size (size only);
  hands/paws; the signature feature's share of the silhouette
  — Dorowani's shell and fin cheeks, Gabu's long arms (chibi
  Gabu's arms stay *long*: dragging knuckles are his read),
  Sproutle's leaf, Mochii's drop point, Wispkin's mask
  (full-size relative to the body — the mask does not shrink).
- **What does not change is §4's anchor list (below).** If a
  proportion pass moves an anchor, the model fails the gate.

### NEVER-CHANGE ANCHORS (gate-enforced)
- **Eye colour family** — eyes may grow; their colour may not
  shift family (Dorowani's red eyes stay red; Hex's amber
  stays amber; Mochii's eyes stay their canon colour).
- **Ink colour `#101010`** — line and shadow ink, unchanged.
- **Accent red `#a03436`** — stays exactly where canon puts it
  (Dorowani's fin cheeks are red at any size; the accent never
  spreads to new places in chibi).
- **Species palette families** — brand blues `#70b0d0 /
  #90b0d0 / #b0d0f0`; Dorowani `#30b0d0/#d0f0f0`; Mochii
  `#50b0f0`. No pastel re-grade: chibi colour is canon colour.
- **Wispkin's mask** — white, cracked, held onto the face by
  both small black hands, fat round black body under it,
  bottom dissolving into smoke. All of it, at card scale,
  unchanged. The mask is never removed, miniaturised, or made
  a face.
- **Kittember's tail-tip flame** — attached to the tail tip,
  always; never a floating orb, never moved to an ear or paw.
- **Silhouette read** — a chibi model blacked out must still
  read as *that character* (the silhouette test the gate
  already runs): Mochiko's twin tails, Gabu's arms, Sproutle's
  leaf, Dorowani's shell-and-fins.
- **Human anchors** — Mochiko: twin tails, teal eyes, navy
  jacket with teal trim. Hex: purple-streak hair, amber eyes,
  black/purple high-collar jacket. Aeon: grey afro and beard,
  round glasses, lab coat over teal tech suit.
- **Cries** — the voice law is untouched by proportion: a
  chibi creature makes the character's own cry, unpitched, no
  "cute" processing. A snap (C5) never changes the sound bed.

### Style binding (the fight-read law applies with force)
Chibi is the register at highest risk of the preschool read,
so the standing style law binds hardest here: the
**MOCHIICHAO art style** is the only render finish — hard cel
shadow, anime eyes with edge, assertive/angular accents, ink
`#101010`. **Soft, plush, pastel, rounded-toddler rendering is
an automatic failure even in chibi** — chibi Mochiichao are
small *fighters and keepers*, not plush toys: chibi Gabu still
reads bruiser, chibi Wispkin still reads eerie (a cute Wispkin
is a failed Wispkin), chibi Kittember keeps her attitude. **No
baked shadows** — card shadows are composite-stage work, same
as everywhere.

## 6. CARD DESIGN GRAMMAR

- **Backgrounds:** flat identity-palette fields — one dominant
  colour per card (Persona rule), brand blues as the home
  field, digicore pixel-cute interface motifs for device cards
  (check-in icons, battery hearts, the Mochiioteca's graphic
  language), JoJo-register graphic accents (speed-line and
  menace-typography *shapes* — as graphics, never as words a
  creature "says"). Backgrounds are graphic fields, not
  locations: a card never pretends to be a place in the world.
- **Typography:** Bungee for any display word on a card;
  Fredoka for small furniture text. Card words are limited to
  furniture vocabulary (episode title furniture, "NEXT TIME",
  credits). No card text is a subtitle; the subtitle law's
  ration is counted only on intertitles.
- **Timing (24fps):** eyecatch slot as scripted (EP01: 192
  frames / 8s — in-card and out-card are beats inside it);
  preview card inside its 45s block; transition card 36–48
  frames (1.5–2s); credits bit inside the ED's frames; snap
  ≤48 frames. A card never extends its block.
- **Sound — one signature sting per card type (SPEC ONLY; no
  audio is built by this document):**
  - **C1 eyecatch:** the T1 sting, graphic register (already
    scripted in EP01, V3-061).
  - **C2 preview:** a digicore check-in chirp arpeggio —
    three rising device notes over one T1 fragment.
  - **C3 transition:** a single gate-bell note, dry, no tail —
    the Garden's own sound at card scale.
  - **C4 credits bit:** a T1 stems fragment, work-register
    (the day-round motif, miniature).
  - **C5 snap:** no sting. One percussive accent tied to the
    gag beat, mixed *under* the unchanged scene bed.

## 8. BIBLE DRAFT — FOR OWNER LOCK

*The following is drafted for SHOW-BIBLE §5 as a new law
(numbered 11, following Laws 9–10 as added by the canon
sweep; renumber on landing if the sweep's numbering differs).
Marked DRAFT until the owner locks it.*

> **11. CHIBI CARD LAW (DRAFT — owner lock pending):**
> Characters are full-size — roughly 85–90% of character
> screen presence in every episode. Chibi versions are a
> device for the show's in-between furniture: eyecatch cards
> (one slot per episode), preview cards (one), transition /
> B-roll cards (max two), one credits bit inside the ED, and
> the dramatic-comedic snap (max one per episode, ≤2 seconds,
> one character). Chibi is a proportion variant of the canon
> full-size likeness, built from the same repo art and passed
> through the same likeness gate — never a separate likeness,
> never a style change: the MOCHIICHAO art style and the
> fight-read law bind chibi hardest, and no anchor ever moves
> (eye colour family, ink #101010, accent red #a03436,
> Wispkin's mask, Kittember's tail-tip flame, silhouette
> read). Chibi cards carry identity and flair only — never
> meaning (that is the intertitles' rationed job), never time,
> never plot. Chibi never interrupts or adjoins the stinger,
> a locked intertitle, a held silence, a colour set piece, a
> fenced unique sound, or a cameo frame. A creature's cry does
> not change with its proportions. (System of record:
> show/production/CHIBI-CARD-SYSTEM.md.)
