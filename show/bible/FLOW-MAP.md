## Part 1 — The grammar (Pokémon's show flow, abstracted)

**SF-1 · The show is the game's second screen.** World, creatures,
and places are locked in the game first; the show adapts them with
model-sheet fidelity and spends the game's locations as its sets.
*(GROUNDED — FIRST-START §1, the corpus's own production research.)*

**SF-2 · The journey episode loop.** Travel → arrival at a new
place → the creature of that place showcased → a local problem →
resolution that teaches the creature (and the audience) one true
thing → onward. The loop is the structure; individual episodes are
instances. *(GROUNDED at the level of the corpus's research —
FIRST-START §1 states this engine verbatim.)*

**SF-3 · The featured creature is fixed at planning.** One species
carries each episode; across a season the features distribute
evenly — nobody's favourite eats the season. *(GROUNDED — Show
Bible §4 states this as the "Pokémon meeting rule" and makes it
our rotation law.)*

**SF-4 · A home base the journey returns to.** Between journey
stretches, episodes come home (the lab, the hometown, the base) —
the return episodes do the emotional bookkeeping: what changed,
who grew, what the journey cost. *(DESIGN-GRADE — basis: the
Pokémon anime's well-known return-to-Pallet/lab rhythm across
seasons; our corpus does not document it episode-by-episode, so
it is held as design knowledge, not citation.)*

**SF-5 · Recurring faces on a cadence.** The rival and the villain
team recur on a rhythm the audience can feel: the rival every few
episodes with one new fact each time; the villains oftener, played
comic-menace at low stakes and straight only when the arc darkens.
Their defeats are routine; their *escalations* are events.
*(DESIGN-GRADE — basis: the Pokémon anime's long-running
rival/Team Rocket cadence, general structural knowledge; the
corpus's own cutting law (Bible §9) confirms the show's pacing
benchmark but not this cadence in so many words.)*

**SF-6 · Competition punctuation.** Tournaments, contests, and
festivals interrupt the journey loop at intervals — episodes that
exist to show the cast *playing* inside the world's rules, and to
let secondary characters win something. *(DESIGN-GRADE — basis:
the anime's tournament/contest arc structure across regions.)*

**SF-7 · The season darkens in the middle and resolves upward.**
Season shape: a bright opening run (choosing, first bonds) → a
broad middle (journey breadth, the featured-creature rotation) →
a darkening (the villain thread stops being funny; a two-parter
at arc stakes) → a climax at the season's institution (league /
tournament / the place the season has pointed at) → a quiet coda
that hooks the next landmass. *(DESIGN-GRADE — synthesis of
SF-2/5/6 against the anime's per-region season arcs; stated as a
shape, not a claim about any single season's episode list.)*

**SF-8 · The slot shape serves the loop.** Cold open, title card,
two acts with a mid-break device beat, a tag, and — when the
season calls for it — a stinger that belongs to the season's
shadow, not the episode's plot. *(GROUNDED as OURS — Show Bible
§6's pilot format is exactly this shape, built by the owner from
the donor grammar.)*

**Where ours deliberately differs — his canon wins:**
- *The home-base season.* Pokémon's show leaves home in episode
  one and mostly stays gone; ours spends Season 1 *learning one
  place* while a horror stands unlooked-at past the gate. That is
  the owner's thesis, not a defect. The fills therefore add ONE
  journey movement to S1, not a journey season.
- *Creatures never speak.* The anime's creatures vocalise too, but
  our law is absolute (cries only, no name-saying, subtitles
  rationed to important moments) — every fill's creature beats are
  performance and sound, never dialogue.
- *The Forest is the season's shadow, not its villain.* It does
  nothing, threatens nothing on screen, and is never explained.
  Fills may let it *register* (a stinger dividend of exactly one
  sound) and nothing more. What stopped moving stays unfilled —
  see the game map's closing note; the fence is identical here.

## Part 3 — The gap list (show)

**Season 1 (priority — fills in GAP-FILLS.md §B):**
- **SF-G1** No journey-out episode: S1 never leaves the Garden on screen (EP03's First Route is a toe-dip, not a journey).
- **SF-G2** Hex's cadence stops after EP04 — no scheduled reappearances, no new-fact rhythm.
- **SF-G3** The villain thread (Hacker cell → Syndicate) has no S1 episode at all, though game canon seats its first beat at the Stoneport docks.
- **SF-G4** No competition/festival punctuation episode.
- **SF-G5** No S1 shape past EP05: no darkening beat, no finale shape, no coda/hook; the EP01 stinger has no dividend scheduled.
- **SF-G6** The Goddgachuu presence-sign grammar (canon for the game) has never been used in the show — where restraint matters most, one sign per season is the dose the grammar implies.
- **SF-G7** The dojo ladder — the game's spine (8 dojos, Elite Four, Champion) — has no show mapping: EP04 is a dojo-*flavoured* episode at the Garden, but no episode yet *is* a dojo episode, and no grid maps dojos to seasons.
