# #343 — HERO/DARK CAMPAIGNS ON THE LADDER → two full ways through one world, and an ending that needs both

**OWNER'S DIRECTION (Jamar, 2026-10-27):** SA2's
Hero/Dark campaigns **mix with the entire morale
system** — the dark campaign is the ladder walked
down; the finale is shared (Last Story grammar).
List 29's #278 built the anti-hero *route* (the
thief's way through, content parity as law). This
spec builds the **campaign structure** the route
lives inside: two campaign tracks over the one
world, their facts interlocking, and the shared
finale gate above both.

**What was studied.** Sonic Adventure 2's
documented structure (SONIC-CATALOGUE, List 30):
two campaigns — Hero and Dark — played in either
order, each complete, each telling its half of one
story in one world; completing both unlocks the
**Last Story**, a shared finale neither campaign
contains. The kit taken: parallel completeness,
order freedom, interlocking halves, and a finale
that is *earned by both sides having been lived* —
not by either side winning.

## Native spec — `mochiichao/hero-dark-campaigns/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **One world, two tracks.** A campaign is a
  **progression track over #146's one-world save**,
  not a save file and not a restart: the world
  payload is shared (places, dex states, world
  facts), and each track keeps its own story
  progress, its own lead's records, and its own
  List 28 standing. Switching tracks switches
  *whose story you are in* — the world does not
  reset, forget, or look away (#286's transition
  discipline at campaign scale; #122's closed NG+
  ruling stands untouched: a new track is a new
  *lead in the same world*, exactly #277's part
  grammar wearing a game seat).
- **The ladder is both campaigns' spine.** There
  is one morale system (List 28: #269's deed
  ledger, #270's per-group tallies, #271's 50
  steps) and the campaigns are its two directions
  made playable:
  - The **hero track** is the ladder walked up —
    its authored beats assume and reward the
    respectable world's doors (#278's parity law
    applies to it symmetrically).
  - The **dark track** is the ladder walked down —
    its spine is #278's route beats (befriend →
    steal → seen or not → fence or keep → fall or
    turn), with #299's faction membership (the
    Syndicate) as its institutional home.
  Neither track has a private morality: a deed in
  either is a deed on the same ledger, read by the
  same world. The dark track's lead can climb
  (#275) and the hero track's lead can fall — the
  tracks name the *authored direction*, the ladder
  keeps the *actual* standing, and a campaign whose
  lead walks the other way is the system working,
  not a broken save.
- **Facts interlock.** What one track does, the
  other track's world *contains*: the dark track's
  thefts surface in the hero track as #298 ambient
  events, #224 chronicle items, and #272 wanted
  facts with the dark lead's face on them; the hero
  track's rescues and restitutions surface in the
  dark track as the law's tightening and the
  underworld's grievances (#274's info market
  trades in both directions). Interlock is read
  through the existing world-fact systems — this
  spec founds **no cross-campaign messaging
  layer**: the world is the message, as it already
  is between #277's parts.
- **Each campaign ends whole.** Per #278's parity
  law, each track has its own complete ending
  (the hero's arrival; the dark track's fall or
  redemption fork on #275). No ending is a demo
  for the other, and neither is gated on the other
  track existing.
- **The shared finale gate.** Above both endings
  sits a **finale content class** — the Last Story
  seat — gated on a single read: **both tracks'
  completion facts exist in the one world.** The
  gate reads *completion*, never virtue: a dark
  track that ended in the fall and one that ended
  redeemed both count as *lived* (what the lead
  became inside the finale is the finale's own
  content, and the ladder will be read there —
  by the fiction, not by the gate). Order is
  free; a player who runs the dark track first
  meets the same gate. In the **show**, the same
  structure is a season shape under #82's arc
  planning: the shared finale is *aired* only
  after both sides' arcs have run, and #286's
  handover record carries each side's closing
  facts into it quoted whole.
- **What the finale IS is canon.** This spec
  builds the gate, the tracks, and the interlock.
  The finale's story — the threat or truth both
  sides are needed for, who stands where, what it
  costs — is a **story/canon fact, the owner's,
  entire and OPEN here.** Machinery is deliberately
  content-blind: the gate would work for any
  finale canon writes into the seat.

## Show lane

The season-level mirror is the point of the
system: hero arc and dark arc as interleaved
halves of one season (or sister seasons under
#277's rotation), the audience holding facts each
side lacks, and a finale that pays off *both* —
the SA2 structure the owner named, run on #82's
planning grammar with #276's morale staging
(reputation as arrival, morale as the unseen
meter) as its episode-level language.
