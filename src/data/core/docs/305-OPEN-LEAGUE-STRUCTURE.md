# #305 — OPEN LEAGUE STRUCTURE → the Fragment path bends; it does not break

**The tension this spec exists to resolve.** The
league as built is a spine: #45's circuit, #100's
gauntlet, #126's record at the capstone — and #143
instances Region 1's shape honestly from canon
(eight dojos, Fragments, a linear circuit, the
threshold of eight carried as flagged structural
inference). But the owner's essence statement names
Skyrim, whose guilds stand in a row and let you walk
to any of them; and #139 already ruled that early
arrival may not "complete a circuit out of its own
region's order." An open world needs its league
answer written down before a landing improvises one.
Here it is: **the system permits open order; each
region's authored shape decides how open its league
actually is; and exactly one sequence rule is hard —
the Champion gate.**

**What was studied.** League/guild ordering in the
named kin, as publicly documented: the Pokémon base's
own drift across its history (the classic linear
circuit; later games' gyms attempted in flexible
order with authored level bands standing in for
locks — the documented proof that the base itself
treats order as content, not machinery), Skyrim's
guild rows (any door, any order, consequences in the
world rather than in a lock), and the programme's
spine law (#52/#139: strength is authored, never
scaled — so a league that lets you walk anywhere
must let you *lose* anywhere, honestly).

## Native spec — `mochiichao/open-league/v1` (fragment beside this doc)

- **Attempts are free; Fragments are earned; the
  spine is story.** Three different verbs, and the
  confusion of them is what this spec prevents:
  - **Attempt** — any dojo the Ranger can *reach*
    (#297's terrain + #127's discovery) may be
    attempted, in any order the reaching allows.
    A dojo does not scale down to its challenger
    (#52/#139, quoted as law below): its test is
    its authored strength, and a first-week Ranger
    who walks to the eighth dojo meets the eighth
    dojo. Order becomes *strategy* — where you go
    first is a decision about your party, not a
    permission you hold.
  - **Earn** — a Fragment is earned by passing the
    dojo's own test under its own rules (#45's
    circuit mechanics + #133's trial framings as
    they apply per dojo). Earning writes the one
    progress fact the league counts (#12/#98's
    Fragment case renders from exactly these facts —
    #213's presentation reads them, unchanged).
  - **The spine** — a region's *story* beats around
    its league (its #45 spine) keep their own
    prerequisites, in their own records, per #139's
    story-locked class. Open order governs attempts;
    it does not rewrite a story that has an order.
    Where a region authors a dojo as story-gated,
    the gate is a #297-class gate with an in-world
    reason, declared in that dojo's record — never
    a silent level wall wearing a league badge.
- **The one hard rule: the Champion gate.** The
  gauntlet (#100) opens on the full Fragment count
  of the region — every Fragment, whatever order
  they were earned in. It is the league's only
  sequence law, it is canon's own (#143's threshold
  shape), and it is *counting*, not ordering: the
  case (#213) fills in any order, and the gate reads
  the fill. The Elite Four and Champion beyond it
  keep #100's own order — the capstone is a spine,
  and this spec does not bend spines.
- **Region 1's linearity is data, not machinery —
  and it stands.** #143 instanced Region 1's circuit
  as linear from canon. Under this spec that record
  is read as Region 1's *authored shape*: its dojos
  stand where they stand, its story gates say what
  they say, and its threshold of eight is the
  Champion gate's local number. Nothing in #143
  needs editing: the open-order *system* landing
  does not re-author Region 1, and a future region
  whose data declares open order (Regions 2–5 stand
  null in #143, free to be authored so) uses the
  same machinery with different facts. STAGING
  registers this reading so the two packages are
  never quoted against each other.
- **Losing is information, and it is free of
  theatre.** A failed attempt writes an attempt
  record (the dojo's own history of you — #173's
  rematch machinery reads exactly such records) and
  nothing else: no Fragment, no lock-out, no scaling
  mercy on the rematch. A Ranger may attempt again
  when they judge themselves ready — readiness is
  the player's strategy, the dojo's honesty is the
  system's. (#253's prize/loss arithmetic runs the
  money; this spec runs the door.)

## Show lane

Open order is the show's routing freedom: the cast's
dojo order can follow the story's geography and the
season's shape (#82) instead of a corridor — while
the Champion gate keeps the finale's arithmetic
honest on screen exactly as in the game. A dojo met
too early is the show's oldest gift: a loss the cast
carries.

## Wiring

Attempts ride #297 (reaching) + #127 (discovery);
tests ride #45 + #133; Fragments render in #98/#213;
the gate reads #100; rematch reads #173; regional
shapes are #143's records (unmodified). The
no-scaling law is #52's, restated in #139 — this
spec adds ordering law, not strength law.

## Open slots

Each region's league shape — how open, which dojos
story-gated and why, its Fragment count — is that
region's authored content (#143's slots, exactly as
#143 left them; Regions 2–5 entire). Region 1's
shape is canon's and closed. No mechanic is open:
attempt/earn/spine, the Champion gate, and the loss
rule are filled in full.
